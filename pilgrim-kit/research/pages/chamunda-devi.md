## 2. Facts
- Names: Shri Chamunda Nandikeshwar Dham / श्री चामुंडा नंदिकेश्वर धाम; Chamunda Devi / चामुंडा देवी — data + https://kangratemples.hp.gov.in/shri-chamunda-mata-temple/ (2026-10-14).
- Location: **Padar (Dadh)**, Dharamshala tehsil, **Kangra district**, Himachal Pradesh, on the **Baner river** (Baner Khad) below the Dhauladhar; 32.1483, 76.4192 (32°8′54″N 76°25′9″E) — Wikipedia + hpkangra.nic.in (2026-10-14).
- Distances (sources disagree, print "about"): Dharamshala **~15 km** (district page; trust says 14–15) · Palampur **~19 km** · McLeod Ganj ~24 km (trust) · Kangra town 10 km (Wikipedia) vs 30 km (trust) → don't print Kangra town km; Brajeshwari (Kangra Devi) ~22 km by road (OSRM, data) · Ghumarwin (Suzu office) ~113 km (OSRM, data).
- Deities: **Chamunda** (fierce form of Durga/Chandika) and **Shiva as Nandikeshwar** in a cave shrine behind the temple (marble steps down to a cave with a natural linga) — hence "Nandikeshwar Dham"; idols of **Hanuman and Bhairav** flank the sanctum door; the idol is draped mostly in red cloth — trust page + hpkangra (2026-10-14).
- Classical status: **not in the 51/52 classical Shakti Peeth lists**; one of Himachal's **five major Devi shrines** by local tradition (with Naina Devi, Chintpurni, Jwala Ji, Brajeshwari) and one of the **Saat Behna / Nau Devi** — data + shaktipeeth-notes (README §2 trap). Local lore calls it a Shakti Peeth — say so neutrally.
- Legend (own words, as tradition — Devi Mahatmya / Durga Saptashati ch. 7, Markandeya Purana): In the Devi's war with the asuras Shumbha and Nishumbha, their generals **Chanda and Munda** marched against her. From the frowning brow of the Goddess (Kaushiki/Ambika) sprang a fearsome, dark warrior goddess who destroyed them both; the Goddess then named her **Chamunda**, after the two she had overcome. In Kangra, a local tale adds that about four centuries ago a king and a priest prayed for leave to bring the Devi down from her remote, snowbound hilltop shrine. She appeared to the priest in a dream and showed where her old image lay buried; the king's men could not move it, but the priest lifted it alone after bathing and coming in clean clothes with reverence. (≈130 words) — data + trust page (2026-10-14).

## 4. Questions people ask (short answers)
1. **What are Chamunda Devi temple timings?** Summer 5 am–10 pm, winter 6 am–9 pm, closed 12–1 pm for bhog; aarti 8 am and about 7:10 pm (official trust page).
2. **How far is Chamunda Devi from Dharamshala?** About 15 km on the Palampur road; Palampur is about 19 km.
3. **Is Chamunda Devi a Shakti Peeth?** Not in the classical 51/52 lists; it is one of Himachal's five major Devi shrines by local tradition.
4. **Why is she called Chamunda?** Because, in the Devi Mahatmya, she slew the demon generals Chanda and Munda.
5. **What is Aadi Himani Chamunda?** The original hilltop shrine at about 3,000 m, a 9–13 km trek from Jia/Jadrangal, open mid-March to mid-November.
6. **Nearest airport and railway station?** Gaggal (Kangra) airport ~25 km; Kangra narrow-gauge station ~14 km; Pathankot ~90 km for broad gauge.
7. **Is there free food?** Yes, the trust runs a free langar, 12–2 pm and 7–9 pm daily.
8. **Why is it called Nandikeshwar Dham?** Shiva is worshipped as Nandikeshwar in a cave shrine behind the temple, so Shiva and Shakti are honoured together.
