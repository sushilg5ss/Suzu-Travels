## 2. Facts
- Names: Gurdwara Sri Hemkunt Sahib / **हेमकुंड साहिब** (Gurmukhi ਹੇਮਕੁੰਟ ਸਾਹਿਬ — hf fonts have no Gurmukhi → Devanagari only, as done for golden-temple) — data; Wikipedia https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gurdwara_Hemkund_Sahib (2026-10-29). Spellings "Hemkund"/"Hemkunt" both used → use "Hemkund Sahib" (search form), mention "Hemkunt" once.
- Location: Bhyundar valley, **Joshimath tehsil, Chamoli district, Uttarakhand**, Garhwal Himalaya; 30.7002 N, 79.6160 E (Wikipedia 30°42′0.78″N 79°36′57.54″E) — (2026-10-29).
- Altitude — sources differ: **4,632 m (15,197 ft)** Uttarakhand portal; **4,572 m (15,000 ft)** Wikipedia (Survey of India); "about 15,000 ft" PTI 2026 → print "**about 4,600 m (around 15,000 ft)**". Portal: "one of the highest gurudwaras in the world" → never "highest in the world".
- Setting: white-marble gurdwara (portal calls it pentagonal; Wikipedia describes star-like plan — print neither shape) beside the glacial **Hemkund (Lokpal) lake**, ringed by **seven snow peaks**, each with a Nishan Sahib — portal; Wikipedia (2026-10-29).
- Tradition / scripture (own words, ≈120 words): In the **Bachittar Natak** section of the **Dasam Granth**, Guru Gobind Singh, the tenth Sikh Guru, describes a former life of deep meditation at a place called Hemkunt, among seven peaks in the Himalaya, before he was called to be born into the world. Generations later, Sikh seekers set out to find the lake that matched these words; in the 1930s **Sant Sohan Singh** identified this lake in the Bhyundar valley, and a small gurdwara rose here in **1936**. Hindus have long revered the same shore as **Lokpal**, where **Lakshman** is believed to have done penance — the **Lokpal Lakshman temple** stands beside the lake, and both close together each winter. — Wikipedia; data (paraphrased). Write "according to the Dasam Granth" / "it is believed"; no claim about the exact identification beyond "identified in the 1930s".
- History (dated): identified 1930s (Sant Sohan Singh); first gurdwara **1936**; present structure begun **mid-1960s** after Maj. Gen. Harkirat Singh's visit, designed by architect **Manmohan Singh Siali** (MES) — Wikipedia (2026-10-29). Exact years for the present building beyond "1960s" UNVERIFIED → don't print one.
- **2026 season (held):** opened **23 May 2026** (data, Republic World); **2.38 lakh+ by 10 Aug** (Republic World 10 Aug 2026); **closed Sat 10 Oct 2026** — closing rites from 10 am, doors closed **1 pm**; **3.84 lakh+ pilgrims** in 2026; Lokpal Lakshman temple closed the same day — ThePrint/PTI 10 Oct 2026 https://theprint.in/india/uttarakhand-doors-of-hemkund-sahib-closed-for-winter-over-3-84-lakh-pilgrims-visited-this-year/3068040/ (2026-10-29).
- **2027:** opening date **not announced yet** (portal: "to be declared", 2026-10-29). Usual pattern: opens in the **second half of May** after the army/sevadars clear snow and do kar seva on the path; closes **around 10 October** (2024, 2025, 2026 all closed ~10 Oct — ETV Bharat 2024/2025 headlines, PTI 2026). Inaccessible Oct–Apr (Wikipedia).
- Darshan/rules: gurdwara timings **not published officially** (portal "to be declared") → "confirm locally"; **no overnight stay at Hemkund** — pilgrims return to Ghangaria the same day, leave the lake by about **2 pm** (Wikipedia; data); daily recitation of Guru Granth Sahib and **langar** for all (portal); head covered, shoes off, no tobacco/alcohol (standard gurdwara maryada — UNVERIFIED specific notice → phrase as general gurdwara etiquette); many bathe in the icy sarovar (portal) — optional, cold.
- **Registration (official):** mandatory, on the Uttarakhand portal **registrationandtouristcare.uk.gov.in** (same as Char Dham) / Tourist Care Uttarakhand app; verification at Govindghat/Pulna (data; portal "official portal for pilgrimage registration"). No fee printed. Fake-site warning.
- **Helicopter:** Govindghat ↔ Ghangaria only (portal); bookable only on **heliyatra.irctc.co.in** (data; yatras-notes FACT TRAP 4 — needs registration number). From Ghangaria to the lake: trek, pony/mule or palki only (portal). No ropeway yet.
- **Ropeway:** Govindghat–Hemkund Sahib ropeway (**12.4 km**) approved by Union Cabinet **5 Mar 2025** with Sonprayag–Kedarnath (combined outlay quoted in rupees — don't print) — Business Today 5 Mar 2025 https://www.businesstoday.in/latest/economy/story/cabinet-approves-sonprayag-kedarnath-govindghat-hemkund-sahib-ji-ropeway-projects-for-rs-6811-crore-466796-2025-03-05 . 12.4 km length from memory of the PIB release — **UNVERIFIED today**; construction status 2026 UNVERIFIED → say "a ropeway has been approved; not yet running".
- **Route** (portal + Wikipedia, 2026-10-29):
  - Rishikesh → Govindghat by road: **270 km**, ~7 h (OSRM 2026-10-29; portal "about 280 km"; Haridwar ~300 km, Dehradun ~330 km per portal)
  - Govindghat → Pulna ~4 km by shared taxi, then Pulna → **Ghangaria** ~9 km on foot/pony — **13 km** Govindghat–Ghangaria in all (portal 13 km; Wikipedia 4 + 9)
  - Ghangaria → **Hemkund Sahib 6 km**, steep stone path, ~**1,100 m** climb (Wikipedia; portal "steep") — 4–5 h up (data)
  - Total trek **about 19 km** one way (portal); typical **4–5 days ex Rishikesh**, +1 day for Valley of Flowers (data).
- How to reach: nearest airport **Jolly Grant, Dehradun**; railheads **Rishikesh / Haridwar / Dehradun** (portal). OSRM from Govindghat (2026-10-29): **Joshimath 19 km**, **Badrinath 25 km** (portal "about 25 km"), Rishikesh 270 km, **Ghumarwin (Suzu office) 604 km** (~10.5 h drive time, real time longer on hill roads). Jolly Grant OSRM 351 km looks like a detour → don't print; say "airport near Rishikesh".
- Nearby: **Valley of Flowers** (UNESCO World Heritage Site, from Ghangaria) — portal; **Lokpal Lakshman temple** at the lake; **Badrinath** 25 km; Govindghat gurdwara at the road head.
- Accessibility for elders: steep, high-altitude; ponies/palkis and the Ghangaria helicopter reduce walking but the last 6 km is by foot, pony or palki only; acclimatise a night at Ghangaria; health advisory on the portal. Elders/heart patients → consult a doctor; Char Dham health advisory applies (data/yatras-notes).

## 3. Disputes & fact traps
- **Altitude** 4,572 / 4,632 m (and Google's 4,160) → "about 4,600 m / ~15,000 ft".
- **"World's highest gurudwara"** — popular claim, not verifiable (Gurudongmar/ others are cited elsewhere) → "one of the highest".
- **2026 closed 10 Oct** — any page saying "open now" is wrong until the 2027 notice. **2027 opening not announced.**
- **Helicopter is only Govindghat–Ghangaria**, not to the lake; only IRCTC HeliYatra. Fake helicopter sites are common.
- **No overnight stay at the lake** — blogs suggesting camping there are wrong.
- Sikh and Hindu traditions at one lake: present both respectfully; do not say the gurdwara "replaced" the Lokpal temple.
- Distances 13 vs 14 km (Govindghat–Ghangaria) and 19 km total circulate → "about".
- Not the same as "Hemkund" in Valley of Flowers park boundary claims — the gurdwara is outside the park core (UNVERIFIED → don't state either way).
- Ropeway: approved, not built — never write it is operational.

## 4. Questions people ask (short answers)
1. **When does Hemkund Sahib open in 2027?** Not announced yet. It usually opens in the second half of May; in 2026 it opened on 23 May.
2. **When did Hemkund Sahib close in 2026?** On 10 October 2026 at 1 pm, after more than 3.84 lakh pilgrims visited (PTI).
3. **How high is Hemkund Sahib?** About 4,600 m (around 15,000 ft); sources give 4,572–4,632 m.
4. **How long is the trek?** About 19 km one way: 13 km Govindghat–Ghangaria (first ~4 km by shared taxi to Pulna), then a steep 6 km to the lake.
5. **Is registration required?** Yes — on registrationandtouristcare.uk.gov.in or the Tourist Care Uttarakhand app; it is checked on the way.
6. **Is there a helicopter?** Only Govindghat–Ghangaria, booked on heliyatra.irctc.co.in; the last 6 km is on foot, pony or palki.
7. **Can I stay overnight at Hemkund Sahib?** No — stay at Ghangaria and return the same day, leaving the lake by about 2 pm.
8. **Can I do Valley of Flowers with Hemkund?** Yes, both start from Ghangaria; add a day (the valley's own season and permits apply).
(Plus: "Who identified Hemkund?" → Sant Sohan Singh in the 1930s; "Is there a Hindu temple there?" → yes, Lokpal Lakshman temple.)
