## 2. Facts
- Names: Bhimakali Temple (also Bhima Kali, originally **Bhimadevi** temple) — **भीमाकाली मंदिर** (bhaktibharat Hindi title; Wikipedia Sarahan) — Wikipedia https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarahan (2026-10-25).
- Location: **Sarahan (Sarahan Bushahr), Rampur tehsil area, Shimla district, Himachal Pradesh** — on the hill above Jeori and the Satluj; the Satluj is ~7 km below (17 km by road); "gateway to Kinnaur" — Wikipedia Sarahan; Incredible India https://www.incredibleindia.gov.in/en/himachal-pradesh/shimla/sarahan (2026-10-25).
- Coordinates: **use Sarahan town ~31.52, 77.80** (Wikipedia Sarahan). Altitude **~2,313 m (7,589 ft)** (Wikipedia Sarahan, town average).
- Deity: **Goddess Bhimakali**, a form of Durga/Devi; **kuldevi (family deity) of the Bushahr rulers** and presiding deity of the former Bushahr state — HPTDC https://hptdc.hp.gov.in/attraction-detail/7; Wikipedia temple (2026-10-25). Upper storey: Bhimakali; storey below: Parvati (Live History India; Inditales notes the floors confusingly → keep to "the goddess is enshrined on the upper floor of the tower").
- Legend (own words, as tradition — ≈130 words): Two stories meet here. In the **Shakti Peeth** tradition, when Shiva wandered carrying Sati's body after Daksha's sacrifice and Vishnu's discus divided it, **Sati's ear is believed to have fallen at Sarahan**, so the goddess is worshipped here as Bhimakali. Older local lore makes the hill the realm of the asura king **Banasura**, whose daughter **Usha** dreamed of **Aniruddha**, Krishna's grandson; her friend Chitralekha brought him to her, Krishna came to war, and Shiva stood with Banasura. Tradition says Banasura's head lies buried under the raised platform before the entry gate, and that Krishna's son **Pradyumna** ruled afterwards — the Bushahr kings traced their line to him. A later king raised the shrine to Bhimakali. — HPTDC; Wikipedia temple; Inditales (paraphrased; present as "tradition says").
- History (dated; keep modest):
  - Commonly called **about 800 years old** / 13th century (Wikipedia, HPTDC, Incredible India) — Live History India says **no record of when it was built** → print "believed to be about 800 years old".
  - Some stone images may date to the **Kushan era (c. 1st–3rd c. CE)** — Wikipedia Sarahan ("may" — keep the hedge).
  - Bushahr state founded **1412** (Live History India); Sarahan was the **summer capital**, Rampur Bushahr the winter capital (Wikipedia Sarahan, Incredible India).
  - **1905 Kangra earthquake** tilted the old tower; tradition/sources say a later tremor straightened it (Wikipedia, Incredible India) → say "is said to have".
  - **1920s** rebuilding with Raja Padam Singh's support; gate inscription **1927** (Wikipedia); Incredible India: "a new temple replaced the old one in 1927". Inditales says the present tall tower was built in the 1940s and the idol moved there in **1962** → dates conflict → print "rebuilt in the 20th century after the 1905 earthquake", no single year.
  - **2022** part of the foundation collapsed; **2023** analysis committee with the **Bhimakali Temple Trust, Rampur**; **Oct 2024** reports said IIT Mandi experts would study the sinking portion (Wikipedia, citing news) — current status UNVERIFIED → mention only as "conservation work has been under study" or skip.
- Architecture: twin **towers** in **kath-kuni** style (alternate courses of deodar wood and stone, slate roofs); three courtyards on rising levels — **Narsingh temple** (1st), **Raghunath temple** (2nd), the **twin towers** (3rd); four gateways — gold-plated main gate, silver-foil carved second gate, third to Raghunath, fourth **Shri Dwar** in stone; carved silver doors, wooden wind chimes; Hindu and Vajrayana-Buddhist motifs side by side (trade route to Tibet); a small museum of utensils, weapons and lamps; the old Bushahr palace next door — Wikipedia (both); Live History India https://livehistoryindia.com/story/amazing-india/bhimakali-temple-jewel-of-the-himalayas; Inditales https://inditales.com/?p=8683 (2026-10-25).
- Administration: **Bhimakali Temple Trust, Rampur** (named in Wikipedia/news, 2023) — official website not found → don't link one.
- Darshan: **"opens at sunrise and closes at sunset"** — Incredible India (official, 2026-10-25). No official aarti times found → **"confirm locally"**. Aggregator clock times (6 am–8 pm etc.) not official → don't print.
- Rules: **head must be covered — caps are handed out in the complex**; **no leather items, no bags; no photography inside the shrine** (photos allowed in the outer courtyards) — Inditales (first-hand, not an official notice → "visitors are given a cap…, leather and bags are not allowed inside — confirm at the gate"). Phones: not stated → "keep it in your bag / follow the gate notice".
- Festivals: **Navratri and Dussehra** (Wikipedia infobox). Dates: Sharad Navratri 2026 was 11–19 Oct (README) → past; **Chaitra Navratri 2027 starts 7 Apr** (README); Sharad Navratri 2027 not checked → Page Builder: "Navratri (Chaitra from 7 Apr 2027)". Any Sarahan fair dates UNVERIFIED.
- Season: open all year; snow possible Dec–Feb at ~2,300 m (general); search demand peaks May–Jun (Keyword Planner).

## 3. Disputes & fact traps

The classical lists (e.g. Peetha Nirnaya) differ and many don't include Sarahan → say "revered locally as a Shakti Peeth where Sati's ear is believed to have fallen; it is not on every classical list". ## 4. Questions people ask (short answers)
1. **Where is Bhimakali temple?** In Sarahan, Shimla district, Himachal Pradesh, at about 2,300 m above the Satluj valley, on the road to Kinnaur — roughly 165–180 km from Shimla.
2. **What are Bhimakali temple timings?** Officially it opens at sunrise and closes at sunset; aarti times aren't published — confirm locally.
3. **Why is Bhimakali temple famous?** For its twin wood-and-stone (kath-kuni) towers, carved silver doors and its role as family deity of the Bushahr kings; it is also revered as a Shakti Peeth.
4. **Which body part of Sati fell at Sarahan?** Tradition says her ear — though Sarahan isn't on every classical Shakti Peeth list.
5. **Is there a dress code at Bhimakali?** Visitors cover their heads (caps are given in the complex); leather items and bags are not allowed inside and photography is not allowed in the shrine — check the gate notice.
6. **How old is Bhimakali temple?** It is believed to be about 800 years old; the towers were repaired and rebuilt in the 20th century after the 1905 earthquake.
7. **How to reach Sarahan?** By road: Shimla → Narkanda → Rampur → Jeori, then 17 km uphill; nearest broad-gauge railhead Kalka, airports Shimla (limited) and Chandigarh.
8. **What is the best time to visit Sarahan?** All year; spring and autumn are clear, May–June is busiest, and Dec–Feb can bring snow; Navratri and Dussehra are the main festivals.
