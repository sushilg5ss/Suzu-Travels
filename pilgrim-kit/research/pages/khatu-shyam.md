## 2. Facts
- Names: **Shri Khatu Shyam Ji** — **श्री खाटू श्याम जी** (also "Baba Shyam", "Sheesh ke Daani" — Daily Jagran 22 Feb 2026); temple managed by the **Shri Shyam Mandir Committee** — Patrika (30 Dec 2025).
- Location: **Khatu (Khatoo) village, Danta Ramgarh tehsil, Sikar district, Rajasthan** — Nominatim/OSM (2026-11-03); ~**27.37 N, 75.40 E** (approx.).
- Deity: **Barbarika (Barbareek) worshipped as Shyam**, a form of Krishna's name; the temple enshrines the **head (sheesh)** — Patrika (23 Jul 2025), Navbharat Live, yatradham (2026-11-03).
- Legend (own words, as tradition, ≈120 words): According to tradition drawn from Mahabharata-related lore (the Skanda Purana's tellings are often cited), **Barbarika, a grandson of Bhima** (Patrika; Ghatotkacha's son in most tellings — UNVERIFIED today), was a mighty archer who vowed to fight for whichever side was losing. Krishna, seeing that such a vow would leave no one standing, came to him disguised and asked for a gift; Barbarika offered his own **head (sheesh daan)**. Moved by the sacrifice, Krishna blessed him that **in the Kali Yuga he would be worshipped as Shyam**, Krishna's own name — Patrika (23 Jul 2025), Daily Jagran (22 Feb 2026). Tradition adds that the head watched the whole war from a hilltop and was later found at **Shyam Kund** in Khatu (Shyam Kund ~1 km from the temple — Navbharat Live; the "found at the kund" story UNVERIFIED → "it is believed"). Devotees call him "Haare ka Sahara" — support of the defeated (common usage, UNVERIFIED; may omit).
- History: popularly dated **1027 CE (Roop Singh Chauhan & Narmada Kanwar)** and a **1720 rebuild** — **UNVERIFIED today (not in any fetched page)** → print "an old shrine, rebuilt in the 18th century (as local tradition records)" only if Page Builder confirms; otherwise "centuries-old shrine, rebuilt over time". ## 3. Disputes & fact traps
- **Not** the Khatu Shyam temples in Agra/Delhi etc. (Nominatim returns an Agra temple first) — main temple is Khatu, Sikar.
- Barbarika's parentage: most tellings — son of Ghatotkacha (grandson of Bhima); some regional tellings differ → "grandson of Bhima" is safe.
- Tradition vs history: the 1027 CE date is from temple tradition, not epigraphy (UNVERIFIED).
- Phalgun Mela dates differ by source (18–28 vs 21–28 Feb 2026) — print 2027 only once the committee announces.
- "Wishes fulfilled", "Haare ka Sahara" — belief wording only; no guarantees.
- No pay-for-VIP endorsement; no touts.
- Barbarika was **not** at Kurukshetra as a combatant in tradition — he gave his head before the war.

## 4. Questions people ask (short answers)
1. **What are Khatu Shyam Ji darshan timings?** Roughly 4:30 am–noon, 1:30–4:30 pm and 5:30–9 pm, with five aartis from Mangla (~5:30 am) to Shayan (~10 pm); times shift between summer and winter.
2. **Who is Khatu Shyam Ji?** Barbarika, a grandson of Bhima, who gave his head to Krishna before the Mahabharata war and was blessed to be worshipped as Shyam.
3. **When is the Phalgun (Lakkhi) Mela 2027?** Not announced yet; it falls in Phalgun Shukla (late Feb–March), peaking on Ekadashi a few days before Holi.
4. **Is online booking needed?** Not for general darshan; follow only the Shri Shyam Mandir Committee's notices and never pay touts for VIP darshan.
5. **What is the Nishan yatra?** Devotees walk about 17 km from Ringas to Khatu carrying saffron flags (nishan) to offer to Baba Shyam.
6. **Which is the nearest railway station?** Ringas (Reengus) Junction, about 17 km; Jaipur Junction is about 78 km.
7. **How far is Khatu Shyam from Jaipur airport?** About 90 km by road (around 2 hours).
8. **What is Shyam Kund?** A sacred tank about 1 km from the temple where, it is believed, the sheesh was found.
