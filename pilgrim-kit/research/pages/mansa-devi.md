## 2. Facts
- Names: Shri Mata Mansa Devi Mandir — श्री माता मनसा देवी मंदिर, पंचकूला — data; Shrine Board https://mansadevi.org.in/ (2026-10-21).
- Location: Mansa Devi Complex (MDC), Sector 4, **Bilaspur village, Panchkula district, Haryana** 134114, on the **Shivalik foothills** near Mani Majra — **30.7653, 76.8269** — Wikipedia https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mata_Mansa_Devi_Mandir (2026-10-21). Complex ~**100 acres** (Wikipedia; Shrine Board history page).
- Deity: **Mansa Devi**, a form of Shakti — the one who fulfils the wishes (*mansa*) of the mind (data); worshipped as **three pindis** (stones) — Shrine Board history https://mansadevi.org.in/mansa-devi-temple.php (2026-10-21).
- Legend (own words, as tradition — Shrine Board history; ≈110 words): Long ago a cow is said to have climbed a Shivalik hilltop every day and let its milk flow over three stones lying side by side. Villagers noticed, began to worship the stones, and the shrine came to be honoured as a place of Sati — the Shrine Board's history says the three pindis were recognised as **Sati's forehead**. Mani Majra's ruler, Raja **Gopal Singh**, was so devoted that, by local legend, he went to the goddess every day with his queen through a **tunnel about 3 km long** from his fort. He built the present temple, and the hill shrine grew into one of the region's great Navratri gatherings. — Shrine Board; data legend_summary.
- History (dated):
  - **1811–1815** — present main temple built by **Raja Gopal Singh (Gopal Das Singh) of Mani Majra** (enthroned 1783) — Shrine Board, Wikipedia.
  - **1840** — **Patiala Shivalaya** built by **Maharaja Karam Singh of Patiala**, ~200 m from the main temple — Shrine Board, Wikipedia.
  - After the princely states merged into PEPSU, patronage ended and the temple was neglected (no year) — Shrine Board.
  - **1991** — Haryana enacted the **Shri Mata Mansa Devi Shrine Act (Haryana Act No. 14 of 1991)**; a Shrine Board runs the temple — Wikipedia. Chief Administrator = Panchkula Deputy Commissioner (UNI, 9 Oct 2026).
  - Art: **38 panels of wall paintings** (frescoes) with floral ceilings — Wikipedia places them in the **Patiala Shivalaya**; the Shrine Board summary reads as the main temple → page: "fine 19th-century wall paintings in the temple complex" (don't assign).
- Darshan (Shrine Board home page, 2026-10-21 — same as data as_of 2026-10-08):
  - **Summer 4 am – 10 pm; winter 5 am – 9 pm.**
  - **Aarti — summer 4:30–5 am & 7–7:30 pm; winter 5:30–6 am & 6–6:30 pm.**
  - **Bhog 11–11:15 am** daily.
  - Changeover dates summer↔winter **UNVERIFIED** → "summer/winter hours — confirm locally"; Navratri mela hours may be extended (UNVERIFIED).
- Festivals & dates:
  - Two **nine-day Navratra melas** a year (Chaitra and Ashvin), organised by the Shrine Board; lakhs attend — Wikipedia; Shrine Board.
  - **Ashvin (Sharad) Navratri 2026 = 11–19 Oct** (data) — done; mela programme had a Ramlila on 11 Oct and devotional evenings 12–19 Oct (UNI 9 Oct 2026 https://www.uniindia.com/north-india/religion-mansa-devi-shrine-navratri-celebrations/208257).

## 3. Disputes & fact traps
- **Two Mansa Devis** — Panchkula (Haryana, Shivalik foothills, Shrine Board) ≠ **Haridwar** (Bilwa Parvat, Uttarakhand, reached by the Udan Khatola ropeway). The Nau Devi Yatra uses **Panchkula** (shaktipeeth-notes FACT TRAP 9). Put a short "Not the Haridwar temple" note near the top.
- **Shakti Peeth?** — the Shrine Board says Sati's forehead is worshipped here; it is **not** in the classical 51/52 tables → "a revered Shakti shrine; local tradition links it to Sati's forehead, but it is not in the classical Shakti Peeth lists". It is one of the **Saat Behna / Nau Devi** goddesses.
- **Builder's name** — Gopal Singh / Gopal Das Singh / "King Gopaldas" → "Raja Gopal Singh of Mani Majra".
- **Date** — Wikipedia converts Vikram Samvat 1870 to "813 CE" (wrong; VS 1870 ≈ 1813 CE) → 1811–1815.
- **Frescoes location** — Patiala Shivalaya (Wikipedia) vs main temple (Shrine Board summary) → don't assign.
- **"Mansa Devi is in Himachal"** — it's in Haryana; our keyword plan grouped it with Himachal temples. Don't list it as a Himachal temple.
- **Shrine Board notice dates** — the 2026 e-rickshaw notice for the *Ashvin* mela repeats the *Chaitra* dates (19–27 Mar 2026) — a copy error on the board's site; don't reproduce it.
- No wish-granting claims ("it is believed devotees' wishes are fulfilled" only as belief); no prices of puja/prasad.

## 4. Questions people ask (short answers)
1. **What are Mansa Devi temple Panchkula timings?** Summer 4 am–10 pm, winter 5 am–9 pm, per the Shrine Board; bhog 11–11:15 am.
2. **What is the Mansa Devi aarti time?** Summer 4:30–5 am and 7–7:30 pm; winter 5:30–6 am and 6–6:30 pm (Shrine Board).
3. **Is Mansa Devi Panchkula a Shakti Peeth?** Local tradition and the Shrine Board link it to Sati's forehead, but it is not in the classical 51 Shakti Peeth lists.
4. **Who built Mansa Devi temple?** Raja Gopal Singh of Mani Majra, between 1811 and 1815; the nearby Patiala Shivalaya was added in 1840.
5. **How far is Mansa Devi from Chandigarh?** About 10 km from the Chandigarh bus terminus, ~13 km from Chandigarh railway station and ~19 km from the airport.
6. **Is Mansa Devi Panchkula the same as Mansa Devi Haridwar?** No — Panchkula is in Haryana's Shivalik foothills; Haridwar's temple is on Bilwa Parvat in Uttarakhand with a ropeway.
7. **When is the Navratri mela at Mansa Devi?** Twice a year, for the nine days of Chaitra and Ashvin Navratri; next starts 7 April 2027.
8. **Which Mansa Devi is in the Nau Devi Yatra?** The Panchkula temple — usually the first or second stop after Vaishno Devi in the traditional nine.
