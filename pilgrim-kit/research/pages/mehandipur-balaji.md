## 2. Facts
- Names: Mehandipur Balaji Temple / Shri Mehandipur Balaji Dham (Wikipedia; bhaktibharat); spellings vary (Mehndipur, Mahendipur). Devanagari **मेहंदीपुर बालाजी** (bhaktibharat) (as_of 2026-11-23).
- Location: Mehandipur village, **Sikrai tehsil, Dausa district, Rajasthan** (PIN 321610) — yatradham + bhaktibharat; Dausa district also Incredible India + Wikipedia. Lat/lon 26.9468, 76.7956 (bhaktibharat; consistent with Wikipedia). (as_of 2026-11-23)
- Deities: **Hanuman worshipped as Balaji** (child form), with **Pret Raj Sarkar** and **Bhairav (Kotwal Kaptan)** — Incredible India (summary), bhaktibharat, yatradham. Wikipedia names only Hanuman.
- Legend (our words, as tradition): Devotees believe the image of Balaji was not carved by any sculptor but appeared on its own among the rocks of the Aravalli hills. Local tradition tells of a mahant who saw Hanuman in a dream, together with two other divine figures, and was asked to begin their worship; when he searched the hillside he found the self-manifested images and the worship that continues today began. Over time the shrine became known across north India as a place where families come seeking relief from troubles they believe are caused by spirits or black magic. (Self-manifested: Wikipedia, bhaktibharat, yatradham; dream of the mahant: Boldsky 2022 only → "a local tradition".)
- History: **no founding date** in any source; ages quoted range from "centuries" (Incredible India) to "1,000 years" (Boldsky) to "thousands of years" (Wikipedia) → print "the shrine's exact age is not recorded; tradition places it centuries back". The temple has been studied by doctors/researchers for its healing rituals (1981 psychiatric study; 2013 international study — Wikipedia) → optional one neutral line.
- Darshan timings — **no official timetable found**; sources differ: Incredible India 6 am–8 pm; bhaktibharat 7 am–9 pm, aarti ~6:15 am & ~7:15 pm; yatradham morning aarti ~6:15–6:45 (summer)/~6:25–6:55 (winter), evening aarti ~7:15 pm (summer)/~6:35 pm (winter), open ~7:30 am–8:30 pm; Boldsky summer 5:30 am–8 pm / winter 6:30 am–7:30 pm. → **Print: "Open roughly from early morning (6–7 am) to about 8–9 pm, with a morning aarti around 6–6:30 am and an evening aarti around sunset (6:30–7:30 pm, earlier in winter) — confirm locally."**
- Busy days: **Tuesday and Saturday** (Hanuman's days) — large crowds (Incredible India; bhaktibharat). **Hanuman Jayanti** and Holi are major festivals (bhaktibharat; yatradham). Hanuman Jayanti 2027 date **not verified today** → "Chaitra Purnima (around Apr 2027) — confirm". Waits: ~15–45 min normally, up to ~2 hrs on special days (yatradham).
- Best time: **Oct–Feb** (yatradham) — Rajasthan summers are very hot.
- Offerings/customs (as reported by devotional sites — print as "devotees traditionally…", never as rules enforced by the temple unless confirmed):
  - **Darkhast/durkhast** — laddus offered in front of each deity, part placed in the fire (bhaktibharat; Boldsky).
  - **Arzi** (application offering) — urad dal, rice and laddus offered to Pret Raj Sarkar and Bhairav (bhaktibharat; Boldsky) — no quantities/prices on our page.
  - **Savamani** — a vow offering on Tuesday or Saturday when a wish is fulfilled (bhaktibharat; Boldsky).
  - Widely followed beliefs: **don't take prasad/food home**, **don't look back** when leaving, don't talk to or touch strangers inside, avoid onion/garlic/non-veg before and after the visit (yatradham; bhaktibharat; Boldsky — details differ, keep general).
- Entry: **no entry fee** (yatradham). Dress: decent/modest (yatradham). Phones/photography: **no source states an official rule**; Boldsky advises not photographing → "photography inside is discouraged — follow the gate notice". Pickpocket caution in crowds (Boldsky).

## 3. Disputes & fact traps
- District: some sites say **Karauli** or **Todabhim** — today's sources all say **Dausa (Sikrai tehsil)**; the village sits near the Dausa–Karauli boundary → print Dausa.
- No official timings → never print minute-exact times.
- Exorcism content: blogs describe chains, beatings, boiling water — unverified, sensational → **do not repeat**. No claims of cures; no medical promises.
- "Don't look back / don't take prasad home" are devotee beliefs, not temple-posted rules (none verified on an official notice).
- Age claims ("thousands of years") = belief.
- Not the same as Salasar Balaji (Churu) or Balaji (Tirupati) — disambiguate in intro.

## 4. Questions people ask
1. *What are Mehandipur Balaji timings?* Roughly 6–7 am to 8–9 pm, aartis around sunrise and sunset; confirm locally.
2. *Where is Mehandipur Balaji?* Mehandipur village, Dausa district, Rajasthan, near the Jaipur–Agra highway.
3. *How far is Mehandipur Balaji from Jaipur?* About 110 km by road (about 2.5 hrs).
4. *How far is it from Delhi?* About 260 km by road.
5. *Nearest railway station?* Bandikui Junction, about 40 km.
6. *Why shouldn't you take prasad home from Mehandipur Balaji?* It is a long-held devotee belief that offerings stay at the temple.
7. *Which days are most crowded?* Tuesdays and Saturdays, and Hanuman Jayanti.
8. *Can we visit Khatu Shyam and Salasar Balaji on the same trip?* Yes, many pilgrims combine the three by car over 2–3 days.
