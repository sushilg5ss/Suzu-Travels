## 2. Facts
- Names: **Adi Kailash** — **आदि कैलाश**; also Chhota (Chota) Kailash, Shiva Kailash, Baba Kailash, **Jolingkong / Jonglingkong peak** — Wikipedia https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adi_Kailash (2026-10-26). **Om Parvat** — **ॐ पर्वत** — Wikipedia https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Om_Parvat (2026-10-26).
- Location: **Vyas valley / Kuthi Yankti valley, Dharchula tehsil, Pithoragarh district, Uttarakhand**, near the India–Tibet (China) border — Wikipedia Adi Kailash (2026-10-26); data/yatras.json.
- Adi Kailash: **5,945 m (19,505 ft)**, **30.3192, 80.6325** (30°19′09″N 80°37′57″E) — Wikipedia (2026-10-26). Pilgrim point Jolingkong ~4,500 m (data/yatras.json `altitude_m`, as_of 2026-10-08; Wikipedia gives no figure → print "about 4,500 m").
- Om Parvat: **5,590 m (18,340 ft)**, **30.1967, 81.0325** — Wikipedia (2026-10-26); seen from **Nabhidhang** camp near Lipulekh pass; it lies in a different direction from Adi Kailash (people often confuse the two) — Wikipedia Om Parvat. Pithoragarh district site describes it as holy to Hindus and Buddhists, visible from Nabhidhang — https://pithoragarh.nic.in/?p=28786 (2026-10-26).
- Sacred lakes: **Gauri Kund (Jolingkong lake)** at the base of Adi Kailash with the **Shiva temple** on its bank; **Parvati Tal/Parvati Sarovar** also named — Wikipedia Adi Kailash. (Pilgrim sites mix "Parvati Kund" / "Gauri Kund" → print "the sacred lake at Jolingkong (Parvati Sarovar / Gauri Kund)".)
- Deity/meaning: Shiva and Parvati; considered **second most sacred of the Panch Kailash** after Mount Kailash (others: Shrikhand, Kinnaur/Kinner Kailash, Manimahesh — all Himachal) — Wikipedia (2026-10-26). Good internal-link hook to `manimahesh-yatra`, `shrikhand-mahadev-yatra`.
- Legend (own words, as tradition — ≈120 words): Devotees believe Adi Kailash — "the first Kailash" — was a home of Shiva and Parvati in the Himalaya, a peak whose form recalls Mount Kailash across the border, which is why it is also called Chhota Kailash. The still lake at its foot is said to mirror the mountain, and pilgrims pray at the small Shiva–Parvati temple on its shore. A short drive away, snow settles on Om Parvat in a pattern that looks like the sacred syllable ॐ; the Shiva Purana describes Om as existing before creation, and local lore counts this as one of the natural Om forms of the world. The valley carries the name of sage Ved Vyas, and the old pilgrim and trade path to Mansarovar runs through it to Lipulekh. — data/yatras.json legend_summary; Pithoragarh district site; Wikipedia (paraphrased; "devotees believe / lore says").

## 3. Disputes & fact traps
- **Adi Kailash ≠ Om Parvat ≠ Kailash Mansarovar.** Adi Kailash (5,945 m) is reached via Kuti/Jolingkong; Om Parvat (5,590 m) is seen from Nabhidhang near Lipulekh; Mount Kailash is in Tibet (China). Many sites mix the three.
- **Om Parvat height:** the district site wording suggests "5,900 m" next to Nabhidhang — that reads as garbled; use **Wikipedia 5,590 m** and don't give Nabhidhang's altitude.
- **"Parvati Kund" vs "Gauri Kund" vs "Parvati Tal":** naming varies → use "the sacred lake at Jolingkong (Parvati Sarovar / Gauri Kund)".
- **"Trek":** older blogs describe a long trek; since 2020 it is mostly a drive — say so.
- **Permit fee / age:** only reported figures (10–70, fee range) → print age as "reported 10–70, confirm", no fee figure.
- **Snow 'Om' fading** (2024 viral reports) — not verified today → don't print.
- **Nepal claims Kalapani/Lipulekh** — political; **don't mention** (no border content, no map outline).
- No prices; no "Adi Kailash grants moksha" claims; ILP only via official channels.

## 4. Questions people ask (short answers)
1. **Where is Adi Kailash?** In the Vyas valley of Dharchula, Pithoragarh district, Uttarakhand, close to the Tibet border; the pilgrim point Jolingkong is about 36 km beyond Gunji.
2. **Is Adi Kailash the same as Om Parvat?** No. They are two different peaks in the same border region — Adi Kailash (5,945 m) is seen from Jolingkong, Om Parvat (5,590 m) from Nabhidhang; most yatras visit both.
3. **Do I need a permit for Adi Kailash?** Yes — an Inner Line Permit, applied online and verified in person (with a medical check) at the SDM office in Dharchula.
4. **When does the Adi Kailash yatra open?** In 2026 it opened on 1 May; permits paused for the monsoon from 2 July and resumed in late September. 2027 dates are not announced yet.
5. **What is the best time to visit Adi Kailash?** May–June and late September–October, outside the monsoon; check permit status first.
6. **Is Adi Kailash a trek?** Not any more for most pilgrims — the road reaches Jolingkong and Nabhidhang; there are short walks at high altitude.
7. **How many days does the Adi Kailash yatra take?** About 6–9 days from Kathgodam or Tanakpur, including a night at Gunji to acclimatise.
8. **Can senior citizens go to Adi Kailash?** Reported age limit is 70; the altitude (~4,500 m) and medical check make it suitable only for fit elders with a doctor's clearance.
