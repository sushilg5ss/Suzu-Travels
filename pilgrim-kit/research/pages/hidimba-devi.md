## 2. Facts
- Names: Hidimba Devi Temple — हिडिम्बा देवी मंदिर; also **Hadimba Temple** / Hadimba Mata Mandir; locally **Dhungri (Dhungari / Dhoongri) temple** — Incredible India https://www.incredibleindia.gov.in/en/himachal-pradesh/manali/hidimba-temple ; Wikipedia https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hidimba_Devi_Temple (as_of 2026-10-21).
- Location: Dhungri, in the deodar (cedar) forest **Dhungiri Van Vihar**, **about 2 km north-west of central Manali**, **Kullu district**, Himachal Pradesh — **32.2423, 77.1874** — Wikipedia; Lonely Planet https://www.lonelyplanet.com/points-of-interest/hidimba-devi-temple/1292087 (2026-10-21). Altitude **UNVERIFIED** (don't print).
- Deity: **Hidimba (Hidimbi) Devi**, worshipped locally as **kuldevi and gram devi** (family and village goddess) — Incredible India. **No idol in human form**: the shrine is built over a large rock; a **footprint of the goddess carved on a stone block** is worshipped, with a **small brass image (about 7.5 cm / 3 inches)** — Incredible India, Wikipedia, CPREEC https://ecoheritage.cpreec.org/dhoongri-temple/ (2026-10-21).
- Legend (own words, as tradition — from the **Mahabharata** and local Kullu tradition; ≈120 words): In the Mahabharata, the Pandavas, escaping the burning lac house, rested in a forest ruled by the rakshasa **Hidimb**. His sister **Hidimbi** was sent to lure them but fell in love with **Bhima**. Bhima killed Hidimb in a duel, and with Kunti's blessing Hidimbi married him; their son was **Ghatotkacha**, who later gave his life for the Pandavas at Kurukshetra. Kullu tradition carries the story on: when the Pandavas moved on, Hidimbi stayed in these mountains, handed over the kingdom to her son and **meditated on a rock in the forest**, and through her penance she came to be worshipped as a goddess (Incredible India: blessed by Durga). The temple stands over that rock. — Incredible India, CPREEC, Wikipedia. (Mahabharata part = scripture; the penance/goddess part = local tradition — label it.)
- History (dated):
  - **1553 CE** — temple built by **Raja (Maharaja) Bahadur Singh** of Kullu — Incredible India, Wikipedia, National Culture Fund https://ncf.nic.in/projects/ongoing-projects/himachal-pradesh (2026-10-21). Wikipedia/Incredible India say "Maharaja" → page: "Raja Bahadur Singh of Kullu".
  - **15 Jul 2008** — MoU between ASI, National Culture Fund and UCO Bank to improve tourist amenities at the temple — NCF page. (ASI-protected-monument status **UNVERIFIED** → don't print "ASI protected".)
- Architecture: **pagoda-style**, about **24 m** high; **three square roofs of timber tiles** topped by a **fourth, cone-shaped brass roof**; whitewashed mud-plastered stone base; richly **carved wooden doorway** — Durga, animals, foliage, dancers, scenes of Krishna's life and the **Navagrahas** — Incredible India, Wikipedia, CPREEC. Outer walls hung with **antlers and ibex horns** (Lonely Planet). Inside, a stone once used for animal sacrifice (Lonely Planet: "once") — mention only neutrally if at all ("old customs"); **do not** state current practice.
- Darshan: **8 am – 6 pm** — Incredible India (2026-10-21) → page: "about 8 am to 6 pm (Incredible India) — confirm locally; hours may be shorter in winter". Aarti times **UNVERIFIED**. ## 3. Disputes & fact traps
- **"Hidimba is a Shakti Peeth / a form of Durga"** — not a Shakti Peeth in any classical list; she is a Mahabharata figure revered as a goddess in Kullu tradition. Incredible India's "Durga blessed her" is local tradition → say "it is believed".
- **Builder title** — "Maharaja" (Wikipedia/Incredible India) vs "Raja" (CPREEC) → "Raja Bahadur Singh of Kullu, 1553".
- **"Cave temple"** — CPREEC/NCF call it a cave temple; it is a timber pagoda built **over a rock**, not in a cave → never caption it a cave.
- **Brass image size** — 7.5 cm (Wikipedia) = 3 inches (Incredible India) — consistent; don't call it an "idol of Hidimba" as the main deity — the rock/footprint is the focus.
- **Three roofs or four?** — three timber-tiled square roofs + a brass cone on top ("four-tiered" in CPREEC) → write "three tiers of wooden roofs crowned by a brass cone".
- **Dhungri Mela date** — 14 May (CPREEC) vs "mid-May, several days" (Lonely Planet) → "around 14 May".
- **Kullu Dussehra 2026 dates** — 21–27 Oct (DD India) vs 20–26 Oct (travel sites).
- **Animal sacrifice** — Lonely Planet says slaughter "once" took place; don't describe or imply current sacrifice; HP High Court banned animal sacrifice at temples in 2014 (UNVERIFIED today — don't print).
- **Hidimba vs Hidimbi spelling** — the Mahabharata calls her Hidimbā/Hidimbi; locals say Hadimba. Use "Hidimba (Hadimba)".
- Don't confuse with the **Ghatotkacha** sacred tree being a separate "temple building".

## 4. Questions people ask (short answers)
1. **What are Hidimba Devi temple timings?** About 8 am to 6 pm (Incredible India); confirm locally — winter hours can be shorter.
2. **What is inside the Hidimba Devi temple?** There is no large idol: the shrine is built over a big rock, where a footprint of the goddess carved in stone and a small brass image are worshipped.
3. **Who built the Hidimba Devi temple?** Raja Bahadur Singh of Kullu, in 1553.
4. **Who was Hidimba?** In the Mahabharata, the sister of the rakshasa Hidimb; she married Bhima and was the mother of Ghatotkacha. Kullu tradition says she later meditated here and became a goddess.
5. **How far is Hidimba temple from Manali?** About 2 km from central Manali, in the Dhungri deodar forest.
6. **Is there an entry fee?** No ticket is known to be charged; confirm locally.
7. **When is the Hidimba Devi mela?** The Dhungri Mela, her birthday fair, is held around 14 May every year.
8. **What is near Hidimba temple?** A Ghatotkacha shrine (sacred tree) about 70 m away; Vashisht hot springs, Manu temple and Old Manali nearby.
