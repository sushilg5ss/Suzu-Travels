## 2. Facts
- Names: Baijnath Temple / Vaidyanath (Shiv) Temple — बैजनाथ शिव मंदिर (data/yatras.json) — Kangra district https://hpkangra.nic.in/tourist-place/shiv-temple-baijnath/ (2026-10-23).
- Location: **Baijnath town, Kangra district, Himachal Pradesh** (PIN 176125), at the foot of the **Dhauladhar**, on the **Binwa river** (tributary of the Beas); old name of the town **Kiragrama** — Kangra district (2026-10-23). On **NH 154 (Pathankot–Mandi/Manali)** between Palampur and Jogindernagar — Wikipedia https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baijnath_Temple (2026-10-23).
- Coordinates: **use the town, ~32.052, 76.648** (OSRM snaps here; matches 16–17 km from Palampur). **Wikipedia's temple coordinates (32.0836, 76.9664) are wrong** — they route 170 km from Palampur (FACT TRAP, already noted in jyotirlinga-notes.md row 20). Visual Studio/Page Builder: don't use the Wikipedia pair. Exact temple lat/lon UNVERIFIED.
- Deity: **Shiva as Vaidyanath, "Lord of Physicians / healers"**; Shiva lingam in the sanctum; tradition also calls it an **Ardhanarishwara** form — district; Wikipedia (2026-10-23). Smaller shrines incl. Ganesha, Harihara, Kalyanasundara (utsav.gov.in, district).
- Legend (own words, as tradition — Puranic-style local telling; ≈120 words): Ravana is said to have pleased Shiva with fierce penance on Kailash and to have been allowed to carry Shiva, in the form of a lingam, to Lanka — on one condition: it must not touch the ground on the way. The gods feared what Lanka would become. Near Kiragrama, tradition says, Ravana had to stop and handed the lingam to a passing shepherd (in some tellings Vishnu or Ganesha in disguise); it was set down and could never be moved again. Another strand says Ravana offered his heads in penance and Shiva healed him — hence "Vaidyanath", the divine healer. Versions differ; tell them as "it is believed". — district, utsav.gov.in, Wikipedia (2026-10-23).
- History (dated):
  - **1204 CE** — present stone temple built by two local merchants **Ahuka and Manyuka**, per two long Sanskrit inscriptions in the hall (**Sharada script**; also Takri/Pahari text) naming the builders, architects and the ruling king **Jaya Chandra**; the inscriptions show an earlier Shiva temple stood here — district; utsav.gov.in (SDM Baijnath); Wikipedia (2026-10-23). One inscription calls the district **Nagarakot**.
  - **1786** — Raja **Sansar Chand** (Kangra) had it repaired: spire and outer roof rebuilt — Wikipedia (2026-10-23).
  - **4 Apr 1905** Kangra earthquake — main temple survived with minor damage; smaller shrines (Jamadagni, Bhairava, Narmadeshwar, Murli Manohar) and the roof were damaged — Wikipedia (date of quake is well known; "4 Apr" UNVERIFIED in today's sources → print only "1905").
  - **Protected monument of the Archaeological Survey of India (ASI)** — district; Wikipedia (2026-10-23).
  - Style: North-Indian **Nagara** with a tall curved shikhara, pillared mandapa with balconies, antarala, garbhagriha; carved niches; Nandi at the porch — district (it notes Odisha-style influence, rare in HP).
  - **Do not print "804 AD"** (rural.tourism.gov.in says 804 — contradicts every inscription-based source; trap) and don't print "9th century" (ETV) as the build date.
- Dussehra custom: **Dussehra is traditionally not celebrated in Baijnath** — no Ravana effigy burning, no Ramlila — out of respect for Ravana's devotion to Shiva (district; Wikipedia). ETV Bharat (23 Oct 2020) reports it was held on the temple grounds from the 1970s and stopped after an accident involving the organisers; locals believe burning the effigy here brings misfortune; the town reportedly has **no goldsmith shop** (belief that gold turns black) — https://www.etvbharat.com/english/state/himachal-pradesh/baijnaths-tradition-of-not-celebrating-dussehra/na20201023223243942 → print as "local belief", no claims of curses as fact.
- Healing water: devotees believe the water of the nearby stream/spring is medicinal — district (belief only).
- Darshan: **open all year** (data). **Daily hours and aarti times are not published officially → "confirm locally".** (Aggregator times vary — don't copy.)
- Festivals: **Maha Shivratri — state-level fair at Baijnath** (hpkangra.nic.in/?p=25713), organised by **SDM Baijnath** (utsav.gov.in; venue Indira Gandhi Stadium; cultural evenings) → **Mahashivratri 2027 = 6 Mar 2027** (kit FACT TRAP); fair programme 2027 **not announced yet**. ## 3. Disputes & fact traps
- **Baijnath (HP) ≠ Baidyanath/Baba Baidyanath Dham (Deoghar, Jharkhand)** — and ≠ Parli Vaijnath (Maharashtra). Disambiguation box at top; link `baidyanath` page.
- **Is it a Jyotirlinga?** utsav.gov.in and rural.tourism.gov.in say it "hosts one of the twelve Jyotirlingas"; the classical Dwadasha Jyotirlinga stotra's "Vaidyanatham" is most widely identified with Deoghar, with Parli as the other main claim; Baijnath HP is a **minority/local tradition** (see research/pages/baidyanath.md). Page wording: "Some local tradition counts it as the Vaidyanath Jyotirlinga; most lists place Vaidyanath at Deoghar." Never state it as one of the 12 as fact.
- **Build date** — 1204 CE (inscriptions) vs "804 AD" (rural.tourism.gov.in) vs "9th century" (ETV). Use 1204 CE + "an earlier shrine stood here".
- **Coordinates** — Wikipedia temple coords are ~30 km east of the town (trap, see §2).
- **Dussehra** — custom of not celebrating; don't say "banned" or "cursed"; the accident story is reported, not verified.
- **Who swapped the lingam** — Vishnu / Ganesha / a shepherd — traditions differ.
- No wish-granting or cure claims ("believed to be healing" only).

## 4. Questions people ask (short answers)
1. **Where is Baijnath temple?** In Baijnath town, Kangra district, Himachal Pradesh, on the Binwa river below the Dhauladhar, about 16 km from Palampur on the Pathankot–Mandi highway.
2. **Who built Baijnath temple?** Two local merchants, Ahuka and Manyuka, in 1204 CE, according to Sanskrit inscriptions inside; an older Shiva shrine stood on the site before.
3. **Is Baijnath in Himachal a Jyotirlinga?** Some local tradition says so, but most Jyotirlinga lists place Vaidyanath at Deoghar (Jharkhand); Parli Vaijnath in Maharashtra is the other main claim.
4. **Why is Dussehra not celebrated in Baijnath?** Because of the belief that Ravana was a great devotee of Shiva here — so no Ravana effigy is burnt.
5. **What are Baijnath temple timings?** Open every day of the year; daily hours and aarti times are not officially published — confirm locally.
6. **When is the Baijnath Shivratri fair?** Around Maha Shivratri, a state-level fair; Mahashivratri 2027 falls on 6 March 2027 (fair programme not announced yet).
7. **How do I reach Baijnath temple?** Fly to Gaggal (Kangra) ~55 km or drive via Palampur; the Kangra Valley toy train stops at Baijnath Paprola when running.
8. **What to see near Baijnath?** Bir-Billing (~12 km), Palampur tea gardens (~17 km), Chamunda Devi (~35 km) and Kangra Devi (~51 km).
