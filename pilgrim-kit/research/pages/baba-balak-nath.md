## 2. Facts
- Names: Sidh Baba Balak Nath, shrine "Deotsidh / Deothsidh / Deot Sidh" — बाबा बालक नाथ, देवसिद्ध (दियोटसिद्ध) — Wikipedia https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baba_Balak_Nath (2026-10-22). Devanagari of the place: दियोटसिद्ध is the common Hindi spelling (UNVERIFIED official form) → page uses "बाबा बालक नाथ".
- Location: hilltop natural cave in **Chakmoh village, Barsar (Badsar) tehsil, Hamirpur district, Himachal Pradesh**, near the Hamirpur–Bilaspur border — Hamirpur district https://hphamirpur.nic.in/?p=22706 ; trekking.hp https://trekking.hp.gov.in/trekdetail/177 (2026-10-22). Altitude ~1,110 m, Mid Shivaliks; **31.4551, 76.5411** — Wikipedia https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deothsidh (2026-10-22).
- Deity: Baba Balak Nath, a **Siddha** worshipped as an eternal child-yogi — believed by devotees to be a form of Shiva in Kali Yuga (Wikipedia Deothsidh) and linked in popular tradition with **Kartikeya** (peacock mount) — Devdutt Pattanaik https://devdutt.com/child-sage-balak-nath/ (2026-10-22). Shown as a boy with a yogi's staff, riding a peacock. Mantra "Hari Om Shri Siddhaye Namah" (Wikipedia). Worshipped across HP, Punjab, Haryana, Chandigarh, J&K (district).
- Legend (own words, as tradition — popular tellings; ≈130 words): A boy-yogi is said to have come to the Shivalik hills and agreed to graze an old woman's cows (in local tellings, **Mai Ratno**) in return for roti and lassi. Years later, when villagers complained that the cows had damaged their fields — and the woman felt she had fed him for nothing — he struck a tree and brought out all the bread and buttermilk she had ever given him, then left for the hills, owing no one. Another story tells how **Guru Gorakhnath** came to test him and make him a disciple; the child-yogi answered every test by miracle, flew away on his peacock and said his guru was **Dattatreya**. He is believed to have meditated in the Deotsidh cave, where a lamp ("dhuna"/flame) is said to have burned since. — Devdutt; HP Tourism https://himachaltourism.gov.in/destination/hamirpur/ (flame "about 500 years", as belief). "Mai Ratno" and the Shahtalai link are folk tradition — say "in local tellings".
- History (dated):
  - Flame lit "about 500 years ago" — belief only (HP Tourism). No founding date found → don't print one.
  - **16 Jan 1987** — HP government took over administration under the **HP Hindu Public Religious Institutions and Charitable Endowments Act, 1984**; run by **Shri Baba Balak Nath Temple Trust, Deotsidh**, headed by the Temple Trust Commissioner = **Deputy Commissioner Hamirpur** — district page (2026-10-22).
  - The first priest is remembered as **Pandit Banarasi Das**; his descendants hoist the red flag that opens the Chaitra mela; Mahant (2025) **Rajinder Giri** — PTI/The Week, 14 Mar 2025 https://www.theweek.in/wire-updates/national/2025/03/14/hed1-hp-bbn-mela.html.
  - **Oct 2025** — Rs 65 crore ADB-funded renovation under way (≈2–2.5 years): multi-storey parking (~250 cars), new sarai, library, museum, Ayurvedic hospital, ramps — PTI/Tribune 18 Oct 2025 https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/himachal/himachals-baba-balak-nath-temple-to-overhaul-sop-after-alleged-irregularities/amp → page: "the complex is being upgraded; expect construction" (don't print money).
- Darshan: **open all year**; during the mela the temple stays open **round the clock except aarti** (PTI 2025). **Daily hours and aarti times are not published officially → "confirm locally"** (district page lists none). **Sunday** is Baba's day — biggest crowds on Sundays and weekends; also Holi and Navratri (district; trekking.hp). Temple Officer phone on district page: +91-1972-286354 (print only as "temple office" if Page Builder wants; Suzu number stays the CTA).
- Women's darshan: by long local custom women do **not enter the cave**; they take darshan from a **platform about 5 m in front of it** (trekking.hp, Wikipedia; HP Tourism: "a special platform inside the cave for women"). In **Apr 2016** the trust said the restriction was never official and told staff to let women in (Tribune 16 Apr 2016 https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/archive/community/deot-sidh-baba-balak-nath-temple-opens-doors-to-women-222823); current official pages still describe the platform → page: state both neutrally, "most women follow the custom of darshan from the platform; ask the temple staff".
- Offerings: **rot/roat** (sweet thick bread of flour, ghee, jaggery) is the classic offering (HP Tourism; Tribune 2025: money, ghee, flour, roats). Goats offered alive to the shrine are a known folk practice — UNVERIFIED in official sources → don't print.
- Festivals: **Chaitra Mela, 14 March – 13 April every year** (district page; PTI 2025 "month-long", started 14 Mar 2025) → next **14 Mar – 13 Apr 2027** (fixed solar-month dates — confirm close to time). ~75–80 lakh visitors a year (PTI 2025, temple estimate). Many pilgrims from Punjab; NRI devotees in the mela.
- Shahtalai: ~**6 km** below Deotsidh by road (OSRM); many pilgrims walk up from the **Shahtalai** shrine chanting (trekking.hp). Its legend role (Baba's meditation spot / Mai Ratno's village) = folk tradition, UNVERIFIED.
- Easy walk: Deotsidh → Gaoosala and back, **60–80 min**, forest, old samadhis and a stepwell, villages Jabli and Loharli (trekking.hp).
- How to reach:
  - Road: Hamirpur town **~45 km** (district, trekking.hp; OSRM 40) · Bilaspur **~60 km** (HP Tourism; OSRM 46) · Una **~64 km** (HP Tourism) · Ghumarwin ~33 km (OSRM). Buses run to Deotsidh bus stand; taxis from Hamirpur/Una/Bilaspur.
  - Rail: **Una** broad gauge, ~60–65 km (Wikipedia 60, HP Tourism 64) — OSRM gives 123 km (likely a routing quirk) → print "about 60–65 km (confirm route)". Amb-Andaura also on the Una line (UNVERIFIED km).
  - Air: **Chandigarh ~150–170 km** (Wikipedia 172; OSRM 153) or **Gaggal (Kangra) ~117 km** (OSRM; district: Gaggal is 128 km from Hamirpur).
- Facilities: langar, sarais, library (Wikipedia; Tribune 2025). Construction under way (above).
- Nearby: Shahtalai ~6 km · Naina Devi (Bilaspur) — OSRM 92 km via Bilaspur (district/road reality may be shorter, "about 90 km") · Chintpurni ~99 km (OSRM) · Bhakra/Gobind Sagar visible from the hilltop (Wikipedia).
- Accessibility for elders: hilltop shrine with steps; ramps are part of the renovation (Tribune 2025); queues on Sundays and in Chaitra are long → go on a weekday morning.

## 3. Disputes & fact traps
- **Not a Devi / Shakti Peeth** — he is a Siddha (Nath/Siddha tradition), not a goddess; don't list him as a Shakti Peeth or in Nau Devi.
- **Identity** — "form of Shiva" (Wikipedia/district wording), "linked with Kartikeya" (peacock), Siddha of the Nath line, disciple of Dattatreya not Gorakhnath — present as "devotees believe…" and say traditions differ.
- **Birth** — Wikipedia: born to a Gaur Brahmin family at **Girnar (Kathiawar)**; other tellings place his origin elsewhere → "traditions differ"; don't print a birthplace as fact.
- **Women's entry** — custom vs 2016 trust order (above). No judgemental wording.
- **Spelling** — Deotsidh / Deothsidh / Deot Sidh / Diyot Sidh — all one place; use "Deotsidh" + mention others once.
- **District** — it's Hamirpur district (Barsar tehsil), not Bilaspur, though it sits on the border.
- **Distances** — Una 60 vs 64 vs OSRM 123; Naina Devi OSRM 92 → always "about".
- **Mela dates** — fixed 14 Mar–13 Apr (Chaitra solar month), not Chaitra Navratri dates.
- No wish-granting claims; no prices of rot/prasad; no goat offering text.

## 4. Questions people ask (short answers)
1. **Where is Baba Balak Nath temple?** At Deotsidh, a hilltop cave in Chakmoh village, Barsar tehsil, Hamirpur district, Himachal — about 45 km from Hamirpur town.
2. **Can women enter the Baba Balak Nath cave?** By local custom women take darshan from a platform about 5 m in front of the cave; the trust said in 2016 the bar was never official — ask the staff on the day.
3. **When is the Baba Balak Nath Chaitra mela?** Every year from 14 March to 13 April; the 2027 mela is due 14 Mar–13 Apr 2027.
4. **What are Baba Balak Nath temple timings?** Open all year; daily hours and aarti times are not published officially — confirm locally. In the mela it stays open round the clock except aarti.
5. **Which day is special for Baba Balak Nath?** Sunday — the biggest crowds come on Sundays.
6. **What is offered to Baba Balak Nath?** Mostly rot (roat), a sweet thick bread of flour, ghee and jaggery.
7. **Which is the nearest railway station to Deotsidh?** Una (broad gauge), roughly 60–65 km; nearest airports are Chandigarh (~150–170 km) and Gaggal-Kangra (~120 km).
8. **What is the story of Baba Balak Nath?** Tradition says a child-yogi grazed an old woman's cows for roti and lassi, then returned all of it by miracle and went to meditate in the Deotsidh cave; he also out-tested Guru Gorakhnath.
