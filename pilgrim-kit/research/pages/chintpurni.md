- Legend (own words, as tradition — Mahavidya / Shakta tradition, as told by the temple trust): After a fierce battle, the Mother's two attendants, Jaya and Vijaya, were still burning with hunger and thirst. Out of compassion she struck off her own head so that streams of her blood could nourish them — the form devotees call Chhinnamastika, the goddess who gives even herself. Tradition holds that her seat would be a place ringed by Shiva on every side, and this hill in Una, with Shiva shrines at all four points, is believed to be that place. Much later a devotee, Pandit Mai Das from the old Patiala state, is said to have received her darshan at Chhaproh village. Because she lifted his worries (chinta), she became known as Chintpurni, "she who fulfils and ends worries", and his descendants still serve as the temple's priests. ## 3. Disputes & fact traps
- **Body part disputed** (foot / forehead / consciousness) — never state one as fact; say "traditions differ; the temple itself tells the Chhinnamasta story".
- Classical 51 status contested — "counted in many popular lists".
- Darshan/aarti clock times: no official figures — many travel sites invent "4 am – 10 pm"-type tables. Don't copy.
- Mai Das founding date — no dated record; avoid "built in 15xx".
- Don't mix with Chinnamasta temple at Rajrappa (Jharkhand) — a different shrine of the same goddess.
- Sharad Navratri 2026 is 11 Oct (not 22 Sep).
- No caste or regional labels in the founder story.

## 4. Questions people ask (with short answers)
1. **What are Chintpurni temple timings?** The temple trust does not publish clock times; there are three daily aartis (morning, midday, evening). Check matashrichintpurni.com or confirm locally; hours extend in Navratri.
2. **How to book Sugam Darshan / Chintpurni online booking?** Paid Sugam Darshan is bookable only on the official site matashrichintpurni.com — beware look-alike sites.
3. **Which body part of Sati fell at Chintpurni?** Traditions differ — foot (compiled 51 lists), forehead (local lore); the temple tells the Chhinnamasta story.
4. **Why is the goddess called Chintpurni?** Because she is believed to remove devotees' worries (chinta); the village was earlier Chhaproh.
5. **Who is Chhinnamastika?** One of the ten Mahavidyas — the goddess who cut off her own head to feed her hungry attendants Jaya and Vijaya.
6. **When is the Chintpurni Navratri mela 2026?** Sharad Navratri mela 11–20 Oct 2026; Sawan Ashtami mela is in Jul–Aug (2027 dates not announced yet).
7. **How far is Chintpurni from Jwala Ji / Una?** About 31 km from Jwala Ji and about 40 km from Una town.
8. **Is Chintpurni one of the 51 Shakti Peeths?** It is counted in many popular lists, but scholars and texts disagree.
