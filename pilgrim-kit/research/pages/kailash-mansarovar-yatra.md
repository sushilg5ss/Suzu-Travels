## 2. Facts
- Names: **Kailash Mansarovar Yatra** — **कैलाश मानसरोवर यात्रा** (data/yatras.json); Mount Kailash — Sanskrit Kailāsa, Tibetan **Gang Rinpoche** ("snow jewel"), Chinese Kangrinboqê; Lake Manasarovar — Tibetan **Mapam Yumtso** — Wikipedia https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Kailash ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Manasarovar (2026-10-26).
- Location: **Burang (Purang) County, Ngari Prefecture, Tibet Autonomous Region, China**, near the China–India–Nepal tripoint — Wikipedia Lake Manasarovar (2026-10-26). Print "in Tibet (China)"; no border/claims content.
- Mount Kailash: **6,638 m (21,778 ft)**, **31.0667, 81.3125** — Wikipedia (2026-10-26). **Never climbed**; climbing is prohibited by China for religious reasons — Wikipedia.
- Parikrama (kora): **about 53 km** (Wikipedia; many sites say 52) from and back to **Darchen (4,670 m)**; highest point **Dolma/Drölma La ~5,650 m** (Wikipedia; data/yatras.json has 5,630 → print "about 5,630–5,650 m") — Wikipedia (2026-10-26).
- Lake Manasarovar: **~4,600 m**, **30.65, 81.45** (30°39′N 81°27′E), **320 km²**, circumference **88 km**, max depth ~100 m; overflows via the Ganga Chhu channel into salt-water **Rakshastal** — Wikipedia (2026-10-26).
- Rivers: **Indus, Sutlej, Brahmaputra (Yarlung Tsangpo) and Ghaghara (Karnali)** rise within ~60 km of the mountain — Wikipedia Mount Kailash.
- Faiths (one line each, neutral): Hindus — Shiva's abode with Parvati; Jains — **Ashtapada**, where the first Tirthankara **Rishabhadeva** attained liberation; Buddhists — Mount Meru / seat of Demchok (Chakrasamvara); **Bon** — sacred axis; Hindus, Buddhists and Jains walk the kora **clockwise**, Bon followers **anticlockwise** — Wikipedia (both pages).
- Legend (own words, as tradition — ≈120 words): For Hindus, Kailash is the throne of Shiva, where he sits in meditation with Parvati, Ganesha and Kartikeya. The lake below is named from *manas* (mind) and *sarovar* (lake): the Ramayana tradition says Brahma created it from his mind, so it is "the lake of the mind". Pilgrims believe a dip in its water and a walk around the mountain wash away the burden of many lifetimes. The same mountain is holy to three other faiths — Jains remember it as Ashtapada, where Rishabhadeva was freed; Buddhists see Mount Meru and the seat of Demchok; Bon followers honour it as their sacred centre — so pilgrims of different faiths walk the same circuit, some clockwise, some the other way. — Wikipedia (both); data/yatras.json (paraphrased; "tradition says / pilgrims believe").
- History (dated):
  - MEA-organised yatra **suspended from 2020** (COVID-19, then the eastern Ladakh standoff); **resumed 2025** after nearly five years — PTI via Travel Trade Journal 1 May 2026 https://traveltradejournal.com/kailash-mansarovar-yatra-2026-to-take-place-between-june-and-august-mea/ (2026-10-26).
  - The Adi Kailash yatra started in 1981 alongside it (PTI/ThePrint 14 Sep 2026).
- **2026 season (dated, season over):**
  - Announced by MEA **30 Apr 2026**; applications on **kmy.gov.in** till **19 May**; can choose one or both routes with priority; **computerised, random, gender-balanced draw** — PTI/TTJ 1 May 2026; draw ~21 May, **1,000 selected** (The Statesman, data/yatras.json).
  - **20 batches × 50**: 10 via **Lipulekh (Uttarakhand)**, 10 via **Nathu La (Sikkim)** — PTI/TTJ.
  - Nathu La first batch **20 Jun 2026** (Amar Ujala/IANS, data); Lipulekh first batch flagged off from **Tanakpur 5 Jul** (IANS, data; News On AIR says the yatra "began 4 Jul").
  - Concluded **23 Aug 2026** (Nathu La, final 10th batch back in Gangtok) and **24 Aug** (Lipulekh: last batch of 44 crossed back ~9:20 am, taken straight to Dharchula because rain damaged the road); **941 completed** — **464 Nathu La, 477 Lipulekh (incl. 154 women)** — DD News 24 Aug 2026 https://ddindia.co.in/2026/08/kailash-manasarovar-yatra-concludes-final-batch-of-pilgrims-returns-to-india-via-lipulekh-pass/ ; News On AIR 24 Aug 2026 https://newsonair.gov.in/kailash-mansarovar-yatra-through-sikkims-nathu-la-route-concludes/ (2026-10-26). KMVN is the Indian-side nodal agency for Lipulekh; Sikkim Tourism Development Corporation for Nathu La (DD News).
- **2027:** **not announced yet** (searched 26 Oct 2026). Pattern: MEA announces in **late April/May**, applications ~3 weeks on kmy.gov.in, batches **June–August** (yatras-notes.md; 2026 timeline).
- **Eligibility (MEA rules as recorded in data/yatras.json, as_of 2026-10-08):** **Indian citizens only**, **age 18–70**, valid Indian passport, **BMI 25 or less**, mandatory medical tests in Delhi (DHLI/ITBP); **OCI holders and foreigners not eligible**. Selected yatris pay a **non-refundable confirmation fee** (amount: see kmy.gov.in). Re-check on kmy.gov.in when 2027 opens.
- Duration & cost: **~22 days (Lipulekh)**, **~21 days (Nathu La, mostly by vehicle)** — data/yatras.json. Per-route cost exists in reports — **don't print amounts**; "MEA lists the cost for each route on kmy.gov.in".
- Routes (data/yatras.json):

  - **Nathu La:** Delhi → Gangtok → Nathu La → Kangma/Lazi → Mansarovar → Kailash (mostly by vehicle; easier for elders, longer distance).
- Private routes via **Nepal** (Kathmandu / Hilsa / Lhasa) run by Nepal-registered operators with Chinese group permits — separate from MEA; 2026 groups did travel (Republic World 9 Sep 2026 headline on a private group; Telangana Today 28 Aug 2026 on Andhra pilgrims crossing Nepal floods after the yatra) — rules/fees set by China/Nepal → **UNVERIFIED for 2027**; say "private Nepal-route tours exist; Suzu does not sell them; check rules carefully and use licensed operators".
- Official sites: **https://kmy.gov.in/** (only place to apply) · https://www.mea.gov.in/ · KMVN https://www.kmvn.in/ (Indian-side logistics, Lipulekh). Warn: **no agent can "book" an MEA seat** — selection is by draw only.
- Accessibility for elders: hard — parikrama crosses ~5,630–5,650 m; strict medical screening, upper age 70 → "Nathu La route is mostly by vehicle, but altitude and fitness tests still apply; elders who want an India-side alternative can consider Adi Kailash & Om Parvat (motorable, permit-based)".

## 3. Disputes & fact traps
- **Adi Kailash (India) ≠ Kailash Mansarovar (Tibet).** Keep them separate; Om Parvat is seen in India (Nabhidhang), not in Tibet.
- **Kora length 52 vs 53 km; Dolma La 5,630 vs 5,650 m; lake 4,590 vs 4,600 m** → print "about".
- **"Kailash Mansarovar package" sellers:** Suzu cannot sell the MEA yatra; MEA seats come only through kmy.gov.in draw — say clearly; warn about agents promising MEA seats.
- **2026 numbers:** 1,000 selected vs **941 completed** (DD News) — don't write "1,000 pilgrims went".
- Lipulekh start: 4 Jul (News On AIR) vs 5 Jul flag-off at Tanakpur (IANS) → "early July".
- **Politics:** no India–China/Nepal border commentary, no map outline, no "reopened thanks to X" framing beyond "resumed in 2025 after a pause since 2020".
- Don't describe Kailash as "unclimbable because of energy" or other mystery claims; don't claim the yatra "washes away sins" as fact — "pilgrims believe".

## 4. Questions people ask (short answers)
1. **When is Kailash Mansarovar Yatra 2027?** Not announced yet. MEA usually opens applications on kmy.gov.in in late April or May for batches between June and August.
2. **Who can apply for Kailash Mansarovar Yatra?** Indian citizens aged 18–70 with a valid Indian passport and a BMI of 25 or less who pass the medical tests; OCI holders and foreigners can't.
3. **How are pilgrims selected?** By a computerised, random, gender-balanced draw run by the MEA — no agent can book a seat.
4. **What are the routes?** Lipulekh pass (Uttarakhand, about 22 days, with walking) and Nathu La (Sikkim, about 21 days, mostly by vehicle).
5. **How many pilgrims went in 2026?** 941 completed the yatra between June and August 2026 — 477 via Lipulekh and 464 via Nathu La.
6. **What is the Kailash Mansarovar Yatra cost?** The MEA lists the cost of each route on kmy.gov.in when applications open; selected yatris also pay a non-refundable confirmation fee.
7. **How long is the Kailash parikrama?** About 52–53 km from Darchen, crossing the Dolma La pass at around 5,600 m; most pilgrims take three days.  *(three days = common practice, UNVERIFIED today → Page Builder may write "usually 2–3 days" or drop.)*
8. **Can I see Kailash from India?** Not Mount Kailash itself from the usual pilgrim points; the India-side yatra many choose instead is Adi Kailash & Om Parvat in Pithoragarh, which needs only an Inner Line Permit.  *(Om Parvat view point is in India — verified; "Kailash view from Lipulekh" claims exist but are UNVERIFIED → keep wording as written.)*
