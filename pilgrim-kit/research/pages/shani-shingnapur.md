## 2. Facts
- Names: **Shri Shaneshwar Devasthan / Shri Shanaishwar Devasthan, Shani Shingnapur**; Devanagari **शनि शिंगणापूर** (Marathi spelling; Hindi often शनि शिंगणापुर) — Lokmat/Navbharat headlines (as_of 2026-11-06). Use **शनि शिंगणापूर** for `deva`.
- Location: Shingnapur village, **Nevasa taluka, Ahilyanagar (formerly Ahmednagar) district, Maharashtra**; about **6–10 km from Ghodegaon** on the Ahilyanagar–Chhatrapati Sambhajinagar (Aurangabad) road — OSM https://www.openstreetmap.org (Nominatim "NH160C, Shingnapur, Nevasa, Ahilyanagar District") (as_of 2026-11-06); myoksha (6 km from Ghodegaon).
- Deity: **Shani Dev (Saturn)** as a **svayambhu black stone, about 5.5 ft (≈1.7 m) high, on an open-air platform (chauthara) with no roof and no shrine walls** — myoksha https://myoksha.com/shani-shingnapur-temple/ (2022) (as_of 2026-11-06). Wikipedia not loaded today.
- Legend (own words, as tradition, ≈120 words; no scripture names it — it is a **village tradition**): Villagers say a large black slab once came to rest near the village (popular versions: washed down by a flood in the local stream). When a shepherd touched it with a pointed stick, the stone is said to have bled. That night Shani Dev appeared in a dream to the village elder, said the stone was his own form and asked to be installed in the village — but under the open sky, with no roof, because the whole sky is his canopy. He promised to protect the village, and so, the story goes, Shingnapur's houses traditionally have **door frames but no doors**. — myoksha (shepherd/rod, open-sky wish, no doors) (as_of 2026-11-06). **The flood/Panasnala-stream detail is UNVERIFIED** (Wikipedia blocked) → print "according to local tradition" and keep the flood optional.
- "No doors" tradition: houses traditionally have door frames, no doors; myoksha says no theft was reported until 2010 — print as tradition, **no "zero crime" claim** (thefts were reported later; UCO Bank 'lockless branch' 2011 and police station 2015 are Wikipedia-only → UNVERIFIED, omit).
- **Women's entry:** after protests (Bhumata Brigade, Jan 2016) and a **Bombay High Court** ruling that places of worship cannot bar women, the trust **allowed women onto the platform on 8 April 2016 (Gudi Padwa)**, ending a tradition said to be ~400 years old — Business Standard 8 Apr 2016 https://www.business-standard.com/article/pti-stories/shani-shingnapur-temple-lifts-curb-on-women-116040800516_1.html ; Hinduism Today 9 Apr 2016 https://www.hinduismtoday.com/hpi/2016/04/09/shani-temple-lets-in-women-devotees/ (as_of 2026-11-06). (myoksha's 2022 "only men on the platform" line is OUTDATED — trap.)
- **Management (2025–26):** Maharashtra brought the Shri Shaneshwar Devasthan Trust Act 2018 into force (22 Sep 2025) and removed the trustee board after alleged irregularities; the Bombay HC (Aurangabad) restored the old board on **12 Dec 2025**; the **Supreme Court stayed that on 19 Dec 2025** and made the **Nashik Divisional Commissioner interim administrator** until a statutory committee is formed — LawChakra 20 Dec 2025 https://lawchakra.in/supreme-court/shingnapur-temple-nashik-commissioner ; Devdiscourse 19 Dec 2025 ; Lokmat Times 13 Dec 2025 (as_of 2026-11-06). - **Darshan (secondary — confirm locally):** temple premises about **4:00 am – 10:30 pm** (gokshetra quoting the Devasthan homepage, mod. 29 Sep 2026); older sites say "open 24 hours" (myoksha 2022) → print "about 4 am to 10:30 pm; older guides say round the clock — confirm locally". ## 3. Disputes & fact traps
- "Only men may climb the platform" — **outdated** (women allowed since 8 Apr 2016).
- "Open 24 hours" vs "4 am–10:30 pm" — sources conflict; "confirm locally".
- "No crime / no theft ever" — not true today; present doors as tradition only. Lockless bank branch / police station facts unverified → omit.
- Legend versions differ (flood-borne slab vs shepherd's rod) and no Purana is the source → "local tradition".
- Trust litigation 2025 (HC vs Supreme Court, interim administrator) and the 2025 staff-dismissal controversy — **communal/legal, do not mention**.
- Not a Jyotirlinga; not one of the "Navagraha temples" of Tamil Nadu (different circuit). Not Shani Dham Delhi / Shani temples elsewhere.
- District: Ahilyanagar = Ahmednagar (renamed) — give both. Sometimes spelt Shingnapur / Shinganapur / Shingnapur — use "Shani Shingnapur".
- No claims that offerings "remove sade sati" or "grant" anything — say "devotees come to seek Shani Dev's blessings, especially during sade sati, according to belief".
- No ₹ (abhishek/puja fees exist; never print).

## 4. Questions people ask
1. **What are Shani Shingnapur temple timings?** About 4 am to 10:30 pm daily (the Devasthan's posted hours as quoted in 2026); some older guides say it is open round the clock — confirm locally.
2. **What are the aarti timings?** About 4:30 am, 12 noon and at sunset.
3. **Can women go on the Shani platform?** Yes — since 8 April 2016 women worship on the platform alongside men.
4. **How far is Shani Shingnapur from Shirdi?** About 70–80 km by road, roughly 1.5–2 hours.
5. **Why are there no doors in Shingnapur?** Local tradition says Shani Dev protects the village, so houses traditionally have door frames but no doors.
6. **Which oil can I offer?** Since March 2025 only packaged, ISI-marked edible oil is allowed for the abhishek.
7. **Can I book puja online?** The Devasthan says it has not authorised any app or website for online puja — do it at the temple.
8. **When is Shani Jayanti 2027?** Friday, 4 June 2027 (Jyeshtha Amavasya).
