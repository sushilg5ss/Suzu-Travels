## 2. Facts
- Names: Sri Bhramaramba Mallikarjuna Swamy Varla Devasthanam, Srisailam — मल्लिकार्जुन ज्योतिर्लिंग (Telugu శ్రీశైలం; also "Srisaila", "Sri Parvatam") — data (2026-10-08).
- Place in lists: **2nd** in the Shiva Purana (Koti Rudra Samhita) list and the popular smaranam ("श्रीशैले मल्लिकार्जुनम्") — data; FACT TRAP #3 (say which list).
- Location: Srisailam town, **Nandyal district** (formed 2022 from Kurnool), Andhra Pradesh; on the Nallamala hills above the **Krishna** river (called **Patala Ganga** here, reached by **852 steps**) — 16.0742, 78.8681 — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mallikarjuna_Temple,_Srisailam (data 2026-10-08). Incredible India still files it under Kurnool → write "Nandyal district (formerly Kurnool)".
- Deity: Shiva as **Mallikarjuna**, with the goddess **Bhramaramba** in the same complex — data.
- **Jyotirlinga + Shakti Peeth:** Bhramaramba is counted among the **18 Maha Shakti Peethas** (Adi Shankara-attributed Ashtadasha list) — Wikipedia (data). Say "counted among the 18 Maha Shakti Peethas"; don't claim a body part (lists differ).
- Legend (own words, as tradition — Shiva Purana, Koti Rudra Samhita): Shiva and Parvati asked their sons to race around the world; whoever returned first would marry first. Kartikeya set off on his peacock. Ganesha walked around his parents instead, saying they were his whole world, and was declared the winner and married. When Kartikeya came back he felt cheated and left for the Krauncha mountain. Shiva and Parvati, missing him, followed and settled on the Srisailam hill nearby. It is believed Shiva shines here as a Jyotirlinga, with Parvati as Bhramaramba. A popular tradition adds that Shiva comes as Arjuna on new-moon days and Parvati as Mallika on full-moon days — hence "Mallikarjuna". (≈115 words) — data legend_summary.
  - Other local tales (Chandravati and the jasmine/mallika; Bhramaramba as the bee-goddess who killed Arunasura) exist — mention at most as "other local stories", no detail.

## 3. Disputes & fact traps
- **Timings** — Incredible India (6–15:30, 18–22) vs news (4:30–20:30 in slots). Never print one as official.
- **District** — Nandyal, not Kurnool (since 2022).
- **Legend** — Incredible India mentions Shiva's Tandava against Tarakasura and the Markandeya story; these are not the Shiva Purana Mallikarjuna legend → don't use.
- **Name** — the "Mallika (jasmine) + Arjuna" etymology is popular tradition, not scripture → "tradition says".
- **Shakti Peeth** — counted in the 18 Maha Shakti Peethas, but not uniformly in the 51 lists; body part disputed → don't name one.
- **List order** — 2nd in the Shiva Purana list (FACT TRAP #3).
- **Booking sites** — many "Srisailam online booking / VIP darshan" sites are private agents → only srisailadevasthanam.org.
- Sparsha weekend rule changed in Aug 2026 — older blogs still show three slots every day.

## 4. Questions people ask (short answers)
1. **What are Srisailam temple timings?** Darshan runs in slots from early morning to night with ritual breaks; timings change with crowds, so check srisailadevasthanam.org before you go.
2. **What is Sparsha darshan at Srisailam?** A paid darshan, booked online only, in which devotees may touch the Jyotirlinga. Since August 2026 it runs only at night on Saturdays, Sundays and Mondays; other days have three slots.
3. **Is Srisailam a Jyotirlinga and a Shakti Peeth?** Yes — Mallikarjuna is the second Jyotirlinga (Shiva Purana list) and Bhramaramba Devi is counted among the 18 Maha Shakti Peethas.
4. **Which state and district is Srisailam in?** Andhra Pradesh, Nandyal district (earlier Kurnool), in the Nallamala hills on the Krishna river.
5. **How far is Srisailam from Hyderabad?** Roughly 200–215 km by road, through the Nallamala forest.
6. **Nearest railway station to Srisailam?** Markapur Road (about 85 km); Nandyal and Kurnool are about 180 km.
7. **What is the story of Mallikarjuna?** The Shiva Purana says Shiva and Parvati followed their hurt son Kartikeya to the Krauncha hills and stayed at Srisailam to be near him.
8. **When is Srisailam most crowded?** Kartika month (10 Nov – 8 Dec 2026, especially Mondays and Kartika Pournami), Mahashivratri (6 Mar 2027) and long weekends.
