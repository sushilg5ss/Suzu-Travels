## 2. Facts
- Names: Guruvayur Sri Krishna Temple; deity **Guruvayurappan** (Incredible India; yatradham). Epithet "Bhooloka Vaikuntham" (Vishnu's abode on earth) — Incredible India + blog (as_of 2026-11-23). Devanagari: **गुरुवायुर** (title) / गुरुवायूरप्पन (standard transliteration — our rendering, not from a source). Malayalam name not verified → don't print.
- Location: Guruvayur town, **Thrissur district, Kerala** — Incredible India https://www.incredibleindia.gov.in/en/kerala/thrissur/guruvayur-sri-krishna-temple (as_of 2026-11-23).
- Deity form: standing **four-armed Vishnu** holding conch (Panchajanya), Sudarshana chakra, mace and lotus, worshipped as the child Krishna (Balakrishna/Unnikrishnan) — Incredible India (summary) (as_of 2026-11-23). Idol said to be of "Patala Anjana" stone — same source; print as "said to be".
- Legend (our words, as tradition): It is believed that the image now worshipped at Guruvayur was once honoured at Dwaraka. When Dwaraka was about to be swallowed by the sea, tradition says the idol was entrusted to Brihaspati, the guru of the gods, and Vayu, the wind god, who searched for a fitting place to install it. They chose a spot beside a lotus lake where Shiva himself is said to have welcomed them, and the place took its name from the two — **Guru + Vayu = Guruvayur**. Devotees therefore call the shrine "the Dwaraka of the South". Brihaspati+Vayu founding — Incredible India (summary); "Dwaraka of the South" tagline — Incredible India. The Dwaraka-flood/Shiva-at-Mammiyur details are **traditional lore not confirmed by today's sources** → keep to "it is believed" and keep short; Mammiyur not verified.
- History: belief it is **very ancient** ("at least 5,000 years" — tradition only, print as belief); present structure **believed rebuilt in the 16th century under the Zamorins of Calicut** (Incredible India summary). Dated events (Dutch 1716, Tipu 1789, 1970 fire) **NOT verified today → do not print**.
- Darshan timings: opens **3:00 am** (Nirmalyam), noon closing then reopens **about 4:00–4:30 pm**, closes **about 9:00–9:15 pm**. Sources differ on the noon break: Incredible India/yatradham 2024 = 3 am–12:30 pm & 4:30–9:15 pm; Kerala Kaumudi (Thulam-season note, year unclear) = 3 am–3 pm & 4–9 pm in the busy season vs 3 am–2 pm & 4:30 pm normally. → **Print: "Opens 3 am; closes for a few hours after the noon puja (about 12:30–3 pm, varies by season); reopens about 4–4:30 pm; closes about 9:15 pm — confirm on guruvayurdevaswom.in."** (as_of 2026-11-23)
- Puja order (yatradham 2024, secondary — label "typical order", no minute-exact times): Nirmalyam darshan ~3 am → oil abhishekam, vaka charthu, shankhabhishekam → Usha Pooja → Seeveli (procession) morning → Ucha Pooja (noon) → evening Seeveli → Deeparadhana at dusk → Athazha Pooja → Thrippuka, close.

## 4. Questions people ask
1. *What are Guruvayur temple timings?* Opens 3 am, closes for a few hours after the noon puja, reopens about 4–4:30 pm, closes about 9:15 pm; confirm on the Devaswom site.
2. *What is the dress code at Guruvayur?* Men: mundu/dhoti, no shirt; women: saree, churidar or salwar kameez; no jeans.
3. *Can non-Hindus enter Guruvayur temple?* No, entry to the inner temple is for Hindus only.
4. *Are mobile phones allowed?* No; deposit them in the lockers before entering.
5. *When is Guruvayur Ekadasi?* On Ekadasi of Vrishchikam month (Nov–Dec); in 2026 it fell on 21 Nov.
6. *Which is the nearest railway station / airport?* Thrissur railhead (Guruvayur has a small station); Cochin International Airport.
7. *What is Punnathur Kotta?* The temple's elephant camp, near the town.
8. *Why is it called Guruvayur?* Tradition says Guru (Brihaspati) and Vayu installed the idol here.
