## 2. Facts
- Names: Shree Kshetra Bhimashankar Temple / भीमाशंकर ज्योतिर्लिंग — data (2026-10-08)
- Place in lists: **6th** in both the Shiva Purana (Koti Rudra Samhita) order and the popular smaranam ("डाकिन्यां भीमशङ्करम्") — FACT TRAP #3; the "Dakini" word is the root of the Assam dispute (#10).
- Location: Bhorgiri village area, Khed taluka, Pune district, Maharashtra; Sahyadri (Western Ghats) crest; source of the **Bhima (Bhimarathi)** river, a tributary of the Krishna; 19.072, 73.536 — Wikipedia (data 2026-10-08); official site: "about 50 km north-west of Khed, about 125 km from Pune" — https://shreebhimashankar.com/en/about-temple/ (2026-10-17). Wikipedia/data say ~110 km → page: "about 110–125 km from Pune, depending on route".
- Deity: Shiva as Bhimashankar (jyotirlinga); temple surrounded by Bhimashankar Wildlife Sanctuary (Malabar giant squirrel, the state animal "shekru") — data.
- Legend (own words, as tradition — Shiva Purana, Koti Rudra Samhita): Bhima, son of Kumbhakarna and the demoness Karkati, grew up in these hills after his father fell to Rama. Angry, he did fierce penance, won a boon of great strength from Brahma and set out to defeat the gods and righteous kings. He threw Sudakshina, the Shiva-devoted king of Kamarupa, into prison, yet the king kept worshipping a clay linga in his cell. When Bhima raised his sword to smash that linga, Shiva burst out of it and destroyed him. At the gods' request Shiva stayed on as Bhimashankar. A separate Maharashtra tradition ties the name to Shiva's battle with Tripurasura, saying the sweat of that fight became the Bhima river. (≈125 words) — data legend_summary

## 3. Disputes & fact traps
- **Assam vs Maharashtra** (#10): Shiva Purana says "Dakini" and features the king of Kamarupa (Assam). Most pilgrims/the western-India tradition = Pune site (forest called Dakini vana); Assam govt and devotees = Bhimeswar Dham, Dakini hill, Pamohi, Guwahati (26.0971, 91.6961; Wikipedia) — Assam CM, 20 Feb 2023. Some also point to Bhimashankar near Kashipur (Uttarakhand) — UNVERIFIED, omit. Present both main traditions neutrally; no politics.
- "Open as usual" (#9): 2026 closure 9 Jan–14 Jun; limited hours since; many 2026 blogs and agency sites ignore this or still sell "VIP passes" (VIP entry discontinued 15 Jun 2026).
- Distance from Pune: 110 km (Wikipedia) vs 125 km (official) → give the range.
- Bell year: 1739 presentation (Wikipedia) vs 1721 inscription (official) — print only the 1739 presentation by Chimaji Appa.
- Bhimashankar ≠ Bhimakali (Sarahan, HP; row 36) and ≠ Bhimeswar Dham — disambiguate.

## 4. Questions people ask (short answers)
1. **Is Bhimashankar temple open now?** Yes — it reopened on 15 Jun 2026; the official site currently shows darshan 7–11 am while works continue. Confirm before you go.
2. **Do I need an online pass?** It was compulsory from 15 Jun 2026 (photo ID, daily cap). Check shreebhimashankar.com for the current rule.
3. **Is VIP darshan available?** No — VIP and recommendation entry were discontinued in June 2026; everyone follows the same queue.
4. **How far is Bhimashankar from Pune?** About 110–125 km by road via Rajgurunagar and a ghat section, roughly 3–4 hours.
5. **Which is the nearest airport and railway station?** Pune for both (about 110–120 km).
6. **Is the real Bhimashankar in Assam?** It is a tradition dispute: the Pune temple is the most widely accepted; Assam identifies Bhimeswar Dham near Guwahati.
7. **Which river starts at Bhimashankar?** The Bhima (Bhimarathi), a tributary of the Krishna.
8. **What is the best time to visit?** October to February; the monsoon is beautiful but foggy and slippery on the ghat road.
