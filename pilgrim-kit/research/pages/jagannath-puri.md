## 2. Facts
- Names: Shree Jagannath Temple / Srimandir, Puri — **श्री जगन्नाथ मंदिर, पुरी**; Odia ଶ୍ରୀ ଜଗନ୍ନାଥ ମନ୍ଦିର (hf fonts: Devanagari only) — Wikipedia https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jagannath_Temple,_Puri (2026-10-29).
- Location: **Puri town, Puri district, Odisha**, on the Bay of Bengal coast; **19.80472 N, 85.81833 E** — Wikipedia (2026-10-29).
- Deities: **Jagannath** (a form of Vishnu/Krishna) with elder brother **Balabhadra** and sister **Subhadra**, plus **Sudarshan**, Madhaba, Sridevi and Bhudevi on the ratna bedi; wooden images — Wikipedia; data (2026-10-29).
- Legend (own words, as tradition, ≈130 words): Temple tradition — told in the Skanda Purana's Purushottama Kshetra Mahatmya and in Odia retellings — speaks of **King Indradyumna**, who longed to see Vishnu worshipped as **Nila Madhaba**. Guided by a dream, he was told that the Lord would come as a log of sacred wood drifting ashore at Puri. A mysterious old carpenter, believed to be **Vishwakarma** (or Vishnu himself), agreed to carve the images on one condition: no one must open the workshop door until he finished. When the queen grew anxious and the door was opened early, the carver had vanished, leaving the images with unfinished hands — and they are worshipped in that form to this day. — Wikipedia; data (paraphrased). Variants differ on the carver's identity and on the Savara (tribal) Nila Madhaba episode → "traditions differ".
- **Nabakalebara**: the wooden images are ritually renewed in certain years with an extra Ashadha month (every **8, 12 or 19 years**); last **2015** (3 million+ visitors est.); first recorded **1575 CE** — Wikipedia (2026-10-29). Next year **UNVERIFIED** (some sources say 2034/2035) → "not announced; the next falls in a coming adhika-Ashadha year".
- History (dated): present temple built from the **12th century**, begun by **Anantavarman Chodaganga Deva** (Eastern Ganga dynasty) after 1112 CE; donation inscription **1134–35 CE**; infobox completion **1161 CE**; Rath Yatra reportedly instituted **c. 1150 CE** — Wikipedia. Incredible India: "built in the 12th century AD, additions into the 16th century" (2026-10-29). → print "12th century, Eastern Ganga king Anantavarman Chodaganga; additions continued for centuries". Indradyumna = legend, not history.
- Architecture: main deula about **65 m (214 ft)** high; complex **10+ acres** (>37,000 m²), **120+ shrines**; outer wall **Meghanada Pacheri** ~6.1 m high, inner wall **Kurma Bedha**; **four gates** — **Singhadwara** (Lion, east, main), **Hathidwara** (Elephant, north), **Vyaghradwara** (Tiger, south), **Ashwadwara** (Horse, west) — Wikipedia (2026-10-29). **Nilachakra** (eight-metal wheel) on the spire; flag changed daily at sunset by a servitor climbing the spire (Patitapabana bana) — Incredible India (2026-10-29). Four gates **reopened to devotees 13 Jun 2024** (news, UNVERIFIED today → don't print the date).
- **Darshan timings (official):** temple open **about 5:00 am – 10:00 pm** — Incredible India https://incredibleindia.gov.in/en/odisha/puri/sri-jagannath-temple (2026-10-29). Day ritual sequence (Mangala Alati early morning, Sakala Dhupa, Madhyahna Dhupa, Sandhya Dhupa, Bada Singhara at night) is fixed by tradition, but **clock times vary daily and on festival days → "confirm locally"**; darshan pauses during bhogas. Special "Parimanik darshan" / paid darshan details UNVERIFIED → omit.
- **Queue / slot booking (2026):** queue-based "**Dhadi darshan**" trial **put on hold 2 Jan 2026** after a structural glitch (Organiser 3 Jan 2026); Law Minister said **slot booking will be rolled out "shortly"** as an experiment alongside the queue (Organiser 16 Apr 2026). - **Rules:** **only Hindus** may enter the main temple (Jains, Sikhs, Buddhists are generally admitted per Wikipedia; foreigners/non-Hindus not) — present neutrally, as the temple's long-standing rule; non-Hindus can view the temple and festivities from outside, and see the deities on the chariots at Rath Yatra (data legend). **Mobile phones banned inside since 1 Jan 2023**, deposit at **Singhadwara / Dakshina Dwara** (OdishaBytes 15 Dec 2022 — SJTA decision). **Dress code enforced from 1 Jan 2024** — no shorts/half-pants, torn jeans, skirts or sleeveless/revealing clothes; traditional/"decent" dress (Tribune, Oct–Dec 2023 https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/india/puri-jagannath-temple-makes-dress-code-mandatory-for-devotees-577307 ). - **Mahaprasad**: cooked daily in earthen pots in the temple kitchen (Rosaghara), all vegetarian, no onion/garlic (Wikipedia); offered first to Jagannath and to **Goddess Vimala** before it becomes Mahaprasad (data/shaktipeeth Vimala); sold at **Ananda Bazar** inside the complex (Wikipedia; Deccan Herald). ## 3. Disputes & fact traps
- **Non-Hindu entry** — state the rule plainly and respectfully; no commentary on the debate, no named incidents.
- **Indradyumna "built the temple"** = legend; the dated temple is 12th-century Eastern Ganga.
- **"Temple casts no shadow", "flag flies against the wind", "no birds fly over", "sea sound stops inside Singhadwara"** — popular claims (Incredible India even repeats "no shadow"). **Never state as fact**; if used, "a popular belief says…", or skip.
- **Kitchen "largest in the world"** — sources disagree → avoid superlatives.
- **Rath Yatra 2027** — 5 Jul is panchang-based; the temple announces the detailed niti schedule closer to the day.
- **Slot booking** — announced repeatedly (2024, Apr 2026) but not live as of today; no "book darshan online" links.
- **Nearest railway** = Puri (Incredible India's "Balugaon" is wrong for pilgrims).
- **Char Dham** — Puri belongs to the all-India Char Dham, not the Uttarakhand Chota Char Dham.
- **Ratna Bhandar** — no claims about contents, value or secret chambers.
- Vimala's Shakti Peeth status — "counted among the Adi Shakti Peethas in several lists".

## 4. Questions people ask (short answers)
1. **What are Puri Jagannath temple timings?** Open roughly 5 am to 10 pm (Incredible India); darshan pauses during rituals and timings shift on festival days — confirm locally.
2. **When is Rath Yatra 2027?** Monday 5 July 2027 per panchang (Ashadha Shukla Dwitiya); the temple's official schedule is not announced yet.
3. **Can non-Hindus enter the Jagannath temple?** No — only Hindus are allowed inside; others can see the temple from outside and the deities on the chariots during Rath Yatra.
4. **Can I take my phone inside?** No. Phones have been banned since 1 January 2023; deposit them at the gate counters.
5. **Is there a dress code?** Yes, since 1 January 2024 — no shorts, torn jeans or revealing clothes; wear traditional or modest dress.
6. **Is there online darshan booking?** Not yet — a slot-booking trial has been announced but no official online booking was live as of 29 Oct 2026; avoid sites selling "VIP darshan".
7. **How far is Puri from Bhubaneswar airport?** About 59 km, 1–1.5 hours by road; Puri railway station is about 5 km from the temple.
8. **What is Mahaprasad?** The food offered to Jagannath (and Goddess Vimala), cooked in earthen pots in the temple kitchen and sold at Ananda Bazar inside the temple.
(Plus: "Why are the idols unfinished?" → Indradyumna/carver legend; "What is Nabakalebara?" → renewal of the wooden images, last in 2015.)
