## 2. Facts
- Names: Shri Mahakaleshwar Temple, Ujjain / महाकालेश्वर ज्योतिर्लिंग (श्री महाकालेश्वर मंदिर, "बाबा महाकाल") — data (2026-10-08)
- Place in lists: 3rd in the Shiva Purana (Koti Rudra Samhita) list and in the popular stotra line "उज्जयिन्यां महाकालम्" — jyotirlinga-notes §1 (2026-10-08); FACT TRAP #3
- Location: Ujjain (ancient Avanti/Avantika), Ujjain district, Madhya Pradesh; near the Shipra (Kshipra) river; 23.1828, 75.7683 — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahakaleshwar_Jyotirlinga (as_of 2026-10-08)
- Deity: Shiva as Mahakala, lord of time and death; svayambhu (self-manifested) linga; **dakshinamukhi (south-facing) — the only one of the 12** — data faqs + notes Q8 (2026-10-08)
- Temple layout (UNVERIFIED today — Wikipedia-level): three levels — Mahakaleshwar (lowest), Omkareshwar (middle), **Nagchandreshwar (top, opened only on Nag Panchami)** — Nag Panchami one-day opening is in data (Wikipedia, 2026-10-08); the three-level detail → "according to the temple tradition…".
- Legend (own words, as tradition — Shiva Purana, Koti Rudra Samhita): In Avanti lived a learned Brahmin, Vedapriya, whose four sons worshipped an earthen linga every day. A demon named Dushana, made strong by a boon from Brahma, came down from the Ratnamala hills and began stopping prayers and harassing the priests. The family would not give up their worship. As the demon rushed at them, the earth beside their linga split and Shiva rose in his fearsome Mahakala form, and with a single roar turned Dushana to ash. The devotees asked him to stay and protect the city for ever, and it is believed he remained here as the self-born, south-facing Mahakaleshwar. Another story in the same tradition tells of King Chandrasena and a young cowherd, Shrikhar, whose devotion protected Ujjain. (≈130 words) — data legend_summary (2026-10-08)

## 3. Disputes & fact traps
- Ash from pyres → no longer (#7). Offline/"tatkal" Bhasma Aarti queues sold by agents → unverified; only official online booking (#8).
- "Only south-facing Jyotirlinga" — tradition, widely stated; say "the only south-facing one among the 12".
- Shravan dates: Ujjain uses the Purnimanta calendar, unlike Somnath (#16).
- Iltutmish 1234–35 raid: state date plainly; no "destroyed X times" counts.
- Omkareshwar shrine on the temple's middle level ≠ the Omkareshwar Jyotirlinga on the Narmada (140 km) — disambiguate.
- Harsiddhi as Shakti Peeth: lists differ → "counted as a Shakti Peeth in many traditions".
- Official website confusion: .mp.gov.in vs .com, plus many agency look-alikes (ujjaindarshan.com etc. rank for booking terms) — never link agencies.

## 4. Questions people ask (short answers)
1. **What time is the Mahakal Bhasma Aarti?** It starts at about 4 am daily (MP Tourism); devotees report much earlier — confirm on your pass.
2. **How do I book the Bhasma Aarti?** Online only, on the temple's official website, about a month ahead; one booking per mobile number every 90 days since June 2026.
3. **What is the dress code for the Bhasma Aarti?** Men wear a dhoti and women a saree for worship at the sanctum.
4. **Is the Bhasma Aarti ash from cremation pyres?** Not today — it is prepared from cow dung (MP Tourism).
5. **What are Mahakaleshwar darshan timings?** Roughly from 4 am until late night; exact hours aren't published on a source we could verify — confirm locally.
6. **Why is Mahakaleshwar south-facing?** South is the direction of death; as Mahakala, lord of time and death, Shiva here faces south — the only such Jyotirlinga.
7. **Can I carry my phone inside?** No — mobiles and cameras go in lockers.
8. **How far is Omkareshwar from Ujjain?** About 140 km by road via Indore (Omkareshwar Trust).
