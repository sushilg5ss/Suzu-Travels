## 2. Facts
- Names: **Shakti Peeth / शक्ति पीठ** (pl. Shakti Peethas, also Shakta Pithas); "51 Shakti Peeth" = **५१ शक्तिपीठ** — data.
- **Legend** (own words, as tradition — Shiva Purana / Devi Bhagavata / Kalika Purana retellings, per Wikipedia): Daksha held a great sacrifice and left out his daughter **Sati** and her husband **Shiva**. Sati went anyway, was humiliated before the gathering, and gave up her body — by fire, by yogic power, or (in the Mahabhagavata) through a shadow form, depending on the text. Shiva's grief and anger produced **Virabhadra**, who wrecked the sacrifice; Daksha was later restored with a goat's head. Shiva then wandered the worlds carrying Sati's body. To end his grief, **Vishnu's Sudarshan Chakra** divided it, and wherever a part or ornament fell, a **Shakti Peeth** arose — each with its Devi and a guardian **Bhairava**. (~120 words.)
- **Why the count differs** (notes §2, Wikipedia 16 Oct 2026):
  - **4 Adi peethas** — Kamakhya, Vimala (Puri), Kalighat, Tara Tarini (Devi Bhagavata / Kalika Purana per Wikipedia).
  - **18 Maha (Ashtadasha)** — the Ashtadasha Shakti Peetha Stotram, *attributed* to Adi Shankara ("citation needed", trap 7); Kularnava Tantra also counts 18.
  - **51** — **Pithanirnaya (Mahapithanirupana)**, attributed to the Tantra Chudamani; Mahabhagavata Purana; often linked to the 51 letters of the Sanskrit alphabet.
  - **52** — a Bengal variant; D.C. Sircar attributes it to confusion in copying.
  - **64** — Brahmanda Purana ("citation needed"); **108** — Devi Bhagavata Purana (Vishalakshi named first) and Matsya Purana. Others: Kalika Purana 7, Kubjika Tantra 42.
  - Compiled tables disagree on places, body parts, Devi and Bhairava names; Wikipedia's own table ("pithas identified in different textual and local traditions") has **69 rows** and the article is flagged with multiple issues → print "one widely used list; traditions differ".
- **Countries** (Wikipedia, Pithanirnaya-based): most in India; **7 in Bangladesh**, several in **Nepal**, **2 in Pakistan** (Hinglaj, Balochistan; Sharada Peeth ruins near the LoC — the latter in the 18 list), one each in **Tibet** (Manasa, near Mansarovar), **Sri Lanka** (Shankari, Trincomalee — "historically unknown / recent claims" note) and Bhutan.

## 3. Disputes & fact traps
- "Exactly 51, all in India" — wrong (trap 6): counts 4/18/51/52/64/108; peethas in Pakistan, Bangladesh, Nepal, Tibet, Sri Lanka (Bhutan in some lists).
- Body parts and places differ between lists (Chintpurni foot vs forehead; Kangra vs Jalandhar left breast; Naina Devi Bilaspur vs Nainital eyes — trap 8; Ujjain Harsiddhi vs Mahakali). Print "as per one common list" and note variants.
- Vaishno Devi, Chamunda, Mansa Devi, Meenakshi, Baglamukhi ≠ classical 51 (traps 1, 2, 9, 14).
- Adi Shankara authorship of the 18-stotram = attribution only (trap 7).
- Sri Lanka Shankari identification is recent/uncertain.
- Don't map disputed borders; no India outline; place Sharada/Hinglaj in text, neutrally ("in Pakistan / Pakistan-administered Kashmir"), no political comment.
- Kamakhya "closes during Navratri" — wrong; it closes for Ambubachi in June (trap 15).

## 4. Questions people ask (short answers)
1. **What are the 51 Shakti Peeths?** Shrines where, by tradition, parts or ornaments of Sati fell when Vishnu's chakra divided her body; each has a Devi and a guardian Bhairava.
2. **Are there 51 or 52 Shakti Peeths?** 51 is the most quoted count (Pithanirnaya); 52 is a Bengal variant, and other texts give 4, 18, 64 or 108.
3. **Which are the 18 Maha Shakti Peethas?** The seats named in the Ashtadasha Shakti Peetha Stotram, from Shankari (Sri Lanka) and Kamakshi (Kanchipuram) to Kamakhya, Jwalamukhi, Vishalakshi and Sharada.
4. **Which Shakti Peeths are in Himachal Pradesh?** Naina Devi, Jwalamukhi and Kangra (Bajreshwari) Devi; Chintpurni is counted in some lists; Chamunda belongs to the local five-temple circuit.
5. **Is Vaishno Devi one of the 51 Shakti Peeths?** No — it is counted in the broader 64/108 traditions.
6. **Which Shakti Peeths are outside India?** Hinglaj and Sharada (Pakistan), several in Bangladesh and Nepal, Manasa near Mansarovar (Tibet) and Shankari (Sri Lanka).
7. **Which is the most important Shakti Peeth?** Traditions differ; Kamakhya (Assam) is among the four Adi peethas and is especially revered in Tantric practice.
8. **Why is a Bhairava worshipped at each peetha?** Tradition holds Shiva guards every peetha as a Bhairava; pilgrims often visit his shrine to complete the darshan.
