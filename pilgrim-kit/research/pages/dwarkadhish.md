## 2. Facts
- Names: Dwarkadhish Temple, also **Jagat Mandir** ("universal shrine") and Trilok Sundar — **द्वारकाधीश मंदिर** (Sanskrit Dvārakādhīśa, "Lord of Dwarka") — district site https://devbhumidwarka.nic.in/tourist-place/dwarkadhish-temple/ ; Wikipedia https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dwarkadhish_Temple (as_of 2026-10-27).
- Location: **Dwarka town, Devbhumi Dwarka district, Gujarat**, on the **Gomti creek** where it meets the Arabian Sea; coords **22.2379, 68.9676**; ~12 m above sea level; temple faces west — Wikipedia; district site (2026-10-27).
- Deity: **Krishna as Dwarkadhish, king of Dwarka** — Wikipedia (2026-10-27).
- Status: one of the four **Char Dham** of the all-India circuit linked with **Adi Shankaracharya** (with Badrinath, Puri, Rameswaram); counted among the **Sapta Puri** (seven sacred cities) and as a Divya Desam (Wikipedia marks both "citation needed" → say "counted by tradition among…"); the **Dvaraka (Sharada) Pitha** math of the Shankaracharya tradition is nearby — Wikipedia; data/yatras.json (2026-10-27).
- Legend (own words, as tradition — ≈130 words): Tradition holds that Krishna left Mathura with the Yadavas after repeated attacks on the city, and that the divine architect Vishwakarma raised a golden city for him on the western sea — Dwarka, "the gateway". Krishna ruled here as king, which is why he is worshipped as Dwarkadhish rather than as the child of Vrindavan. The Mahabharata and the Puranas say that after Krishna left the world the sea swallowed the city. It is believed that **Vajranabha, Krishna's great-grandson,** built the first shrine over Krishna's palace (Hari-griha). A local story says the old image was carried to **Dakor** by a devotee, and a new image was later installed. Rukmini's separate temple outside town is explained by **Sage Durvasa's curse** (tradition). — district site; Incredible India; Wikipedia (paraphrased).
- History (dated; keep modest, conflicts noted):
  - Archaeology: earliest temple **not before c. 200 BCE** (Wikipedia, citing excavations); Incredible India "originally built in 200 BCE"; district site says the Archaeology Dept dates it to **about 1,200 years** AND repeats the 2,500-year Vajranabha tradition → print "the site's earliest temple is dated by archaeologists to around the 2nd century BCE; the present building is mostly 15th–16th century".
  - **1473** — destroyed by Mahmud Begada (Wikipedia); rebuilt and enlarged **15th–16th c.** (Wikipedia; Incredible India "current form 16th century").
  - **1559** — present image installed by Aniruddhashrama Shankaracharya (Wikipedia).
  - Tradition: image taken to Dakor **1156 CE** (Wikipedia, local tradition — label as tradition).
- Architecture: **Maru-Gurjara** style; **five storeys** of limestone/sandstone on **72 pillars**; 29 × 23 m — Wikipedia; Incredible India confirms 5 storeys, 72 pillars. **Spire height conflicts**: 78 m / 256 ft (Wikipedia, Incredible India) vs **~43 m** shikhar (district site, official) → print "the tower rises more than 40 m" (safe) — never "78 m". Two gates: **Swarga Dwar** (entry, south side) and **Moksha Dwar** (exit, north, facing the market); **56 steps** lead down from Swarga Dwar to the **Gomti ghats** — district site; Wikipedia. Sudama Setu (footbridge) crosses the Gomti from the temple's base to the beach — Incredible India.
- **Dhwaja (flag)**: made from **52 yards** of cloth, seen up to ~10 km away (district site; Incredible India); bears sun and moon symbols (Incredible India); **changed several times a day — Incredible India says five, Wikipedia says four** → print "changed several times every day". Flag offering is sponsored by devotees (booked far ahead — no official booking URL verified → "ask the temple office"; no prices).
- Darshan (official tourism, Incredible India, read 2026-10-27): **6:30 am – 1:00 pm and 5:00 pm – 9:30 pm**. Detailed aarti schedule (YatraDham, 29 Aug 2026 — private, internally inconsistent): Mangla aarti ~6:30 am, Shringar aarti ~10:30 am, Sandhya aarti ~7:30 pm, Shayan aarti ~8:30 pm, with short closures (e.g. ~8–9 am abhishek). → Page: print the official two windows; aarti times as "usually around … — confirm locally"; timings change on Janmashtami.

## 3. Disputes & fact traps
- **Mathura's Dwarkadhish temple (1814)** and **Kankroli (Rajasthan) Dwarkadhish** are different temples — our Holi page links the Mathura one; never mix timings (YatraDham has separate pages; Amar Ujala Mar 2026 timing change = Mathura).
- **Height**: 78 m (Wikipedia/Incredible India) vs ~43 m (district, official) — use "more than 40 m".
- **Flag changes per day**: 5 (Incredible India) vs 4 (Wikipedia) → "several times a day".
- **Age**: 2,500 years (tradition, district) vs ~1,200 years (Archaeology per district) vs 200 BCE earliest (Wikipedia) vs present structure 15th–16th c. → present as tradition + "present building largely 15th–16th century".
- **Mahmud Begada**: 1473 (Wikipedia) — some sites say 1472; print 1473 or "in the 15th century".
- **Char Dham**: Dwarka is one of the **all-India** Char Dham (Badrinath, Dwarka, Puri, Rameswaram) — not the Uttarakhand Chhota Char Dham. Our Char Dham page already states this.
- Underwater "Dwarka city found" claims — marine archaeology finds are debated; **don't claim** the sunken city has been proven.
- Nageshwar's Jyotirlinga status is disputed (Aundha Nagnath, Jageshwar) — keep the nageshwar pack's neutral line.
- No prices, no hotel names, no "the Lord grants…".

## 4. Questions people ask (short answers)
1. **What are Dwarkadhish temple timings?** Officially 6:30 am – 1 pm and 5 pm – 9:30 pm (Incredible India); aarti times vary — confirm locally, especially on Janmashtami.
2. **Why is the flag on Dwarkadhish changed so often?** The 52-yard dhwaja is offered by devotees and changed several times a day; it carries sun and moon symbols, believed to say Krishna will stay as long as they shine.
3. **Is Dwarka one of the Char Dham?** Yes — of the all-India Char Dham linked with Adi Shankaracharya, with Badrinath, Puri and Rameswaram.
4. **How far is Bet Dwarka from Dwarkadhish temple?** About 31 km by road to Okha, then across Sudarshan Setu (2.3 km, opened Feb 2024) to the island; boats from Okha also run.
5. **How far is Nageshwar Jyotirlinga from Dwarka?** About 16–18 km on the Okha road, 25–35 minutes by car.
6. **Why is Rukmini's temple outside Dwarka?** Tradition says Sage Durvasa's curse separated Rukmini from Krishna; her temple is about 2 km away.
7. **Which is the nearest airport and station?** Porbandar (~107 km) or Jamnagar (~130 km); Dwarka railway station is about 2 km from the temple.
8. **How many days for Dwarka?** One full day covers Dwarkadhish, Rukmini, Nageshwar and Bet Dwarka; add a day for Somnath (~240 km).
