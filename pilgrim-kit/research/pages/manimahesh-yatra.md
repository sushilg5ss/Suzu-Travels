## 2. Facts
- Names: Manimahesh Kailash Yatra / Manimahesh Lake (Manimahesh Dal) — **मणिमहेश कैलाश यात्रा** · peak also called **Chamba Kailash** — data (2026-10-08); Wikipedia https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manimahesh_Lake (2026-10-24).
- Location: glacial lake below **Manimahesh Kailash** in the **Pir Panjal / Dhauladhar** area, **Bharmour subdivision, Chamba district, Himachal Pradesh**; lake **32.395, 76.637**; lake altitude **about 4,080 m** (Wikipedia text 4,080; infobox 4,190; Tribune 4,085 → print "about 4,080 m"); peak **about 5,650 m** (Wikipedia 5,656; our Mountains pages 5,653 → print "about 5,650 m (5,653 m on our peaks list)") — Wikipedia (2026-10-24); Tribune 20 Sep 2026.
- Deity / sacred form: Shiva as **Manimahesh** (Lord of the jewel), the peak itself as his Kailash; small shikhara shrine at the lake with a brass Mahishasuramardini image and a Chaumukha Shiva — Wikipedia (2026-10-24).
- Lake parts: **Shiv Karotri** (larger, colder — men's dip by custom) and **Gauri Kund** (smaller; women's bath by custom; Wikipedia places it about a mile short of the lake on the trail) — Wikipedia. Pilgrims take the **"nhaun"** (holy dip) and circle the lake — Wikipedia.
- Legend (own words, as tradition — local Gaddi / Chamba tradition, not one named scripture; ≈130 words): The Gaddi shepherds of Bharmour call these valleys **Shiv Bhumi**, Shiva's own land, and believe Manimahesh Kailash is one of his homes. It is said that Shiva meditated here for ages and that the lake formed from water flowing out of his matted hair. Another story says Shiva hid behind the Dhancho waterfall to escape the demon Bhasmasura, who had won the power to burn anyone he touched, until Vishnu tricked the demon into destroying himself — so the falls too are held sacred. On rare clear dawns or full-moon nights, a glow on the summit is believed to be the **mani**, the jewel on Shiva's crown (Wikipedia notes it may be light reflecting off the glacier). Gaddi belief also holds that Shiva leaves Kailash on Janmashtami and returns around Mahashivratri. — Wikipedia (2026-10-24); data legend_summary (2026-10-08). Paraphrased.
- History / institutions:
  - Declared a **state-level pilgrimage** by the HP government — Wikipedia.
  - The **holy chhari** (sacred staff of Guru Charpatnath) sets out from the **Lakshmi Narayan temple / Dashnami Akhara, Chamba** — Wikipedia.
  - **Chaurasi temple complex, Bharmour** (~84 shrines; central **Manimahesh temple** with a large Shiva linga; Lakshana Devi temple the oldest; often dated ~7th century but uncited → print "centuries-old") — Wikipedia "Bharmour" (2026-10-24). Bharmour 32.4428, 76.5329. Many pilgrims pray here before the trek (common practice; UNVERIFIED as a rule → "many pilgrims stop here first").
  - 2025: cloudbursts and flash floods damaged the route and stranded 15,000+ pilgrims → 2026 caps and e-pass — Tribune 20 Sep 2026; Amar Ujala 6 Aug 2026.
- Yatra season & dates:
  - Pattern: official yatra in **Bhadon (Aug–Sep)**; **"Chhota Nhaun"** (small bath) on **Krishna Janmashtami**, **"Bada / Shahi Nhaun"** (royal bath) on **Radha Ashtami**, 15 days later — Wikipedia; AIR 26 Aug 2024 (2024: Janmashtami 26 Aug → Radhashtami 11 Sep) https://newsonair.gov.in/hi/shri-krishna-janmashtami-world-famous-manimahesh-yatra-of-chamba-district-officially-starts-from-today/
  - **2026**: official window **25 Aug – 20 Sep 2026** (Amar Ujala 6 Aug 2026); Janmashtami bath **4 Sep**, Radha Ashtami royal bath and conclusion **19 Sep 2026**; **about 80,000 pilgrims** (lower than earlier years under NGT rules; final official figure not released) — Tribune 20 Sep 2026 https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/himachal/himanimahesh-yatra-culminates-with-2nd-royal-bath-draws-80k-pilgrims/ ; Dainik Jagran 19 Sep 2026.   - **2027: not announced yet.** Pattern only: Radha Ashtami **8 Sep 2027** (Drik Panchang, New Delhi) https://www.drikpanchang.com/festivals/radha-ashtami/radha-ashtami-date-time.html?year=2027 ; Janmashtami ~15 days earlier (late Aug 2027, not checked). DC Chamba notifies the window ~July–Aug.
- Registration (2026 rule): **online registration / e-pass mandatory**, opened **5 Aug 2026**; **5,000 pilgrims per day** may go beyond **Hadsar**, except on the two Nhaun days (cap not applied) — Amar Ujala 4 & 6 Aug 2026 https://www.amarujala.com/himachal-pradesh/chamba/online-registration-for-the-manimahesh-yatra-has-begun-chamba-news-c-88-1-ssml1004-192405-2026-08-06 ; official portal **https://manimaheshyatra.hp.gov.in/** (data). - Route (Bharmour route, the main one):
  - Chamba → **Bharmour ~59 km** (OSRM, ~2.5 h on hill road) · Bharmour → **Hadsar** road head **~13 km** (data).
  - **Hadsar → Dhancho → Gauri Kund → lake: about 13–14 km** on foot (data; AIR 2024 "13 km"; Wikipedia gives 12 + 7 km on older trail — sources differ → "about 13–14 km"); most pilgrims halt at **Dhancho**; langars at ~3,600 m — Wikipedia.
  - Other traditional routes: **Kugti** (from Lahaul side over Kugti pass), **Tyari–Holi** from Kangra/Mandi over Jalsu/Karwarsi pass, and the 9-day walk from Chamba — Wikipedia. Kugti parikrama route **closed to most pilgrims in 2026** for safety (data 2026-10-08, not re-checked today → "check the current order").
- On-site options: ponies/porters from Hadsar (data); **helicopter Bharmour → Gauri Kund has run in past seasons** (AIR 2024: from 22 Aug 2024) — 2026 operation **UNVERIFIED** → "check each year; book only through the district-notified operator". Langars, medical camps, NDRF/SDRF on routes (AIR 2024).
- How to reach:
  - Rail: **Pathankot** (broad gauge) → Chamba **~125 km** (OSRM) → Bharmour total **~184 km**, ~5–6 h.
  - Air: **Kangra (Gaggal)** → Bharmour **~200 km** by road via Chamba (OSRM 203 km) · Pathankot airport (limited flights; km not checked).
  - Bus: HRTC to Bharmour (common; schedule UNVERIFIED).
  - From Suzu's Ghumarwin office: long drive via Kangra–Chamba (OSRM routed 381 km via Pathankot — odd; don't print a figure).
- Rules & safety: e-pass printout + ID; daily cap; **no night trekking**; NGT plastic ban (data); avoid trekking in heavy rain — route was hit by 2025 floods. Medical/age rules: none officially published that we found → don't invent; advise fitness/doctor check.
- Accessibility for elders: hard — 13–14 km steep climb to ~4,080 m; ponies or (if running) helicopter; altitude sickness risk; at least one night halt.
- Best time: only during the official yatra window (late Aug–Sep); lake frozen roughly Oct–June (Wikipedia); outside the yatra = trekking with permits/guides only.
- Nearby: Chaurasi temples, Bharmour (start) · Bharmani Mata temple above Bharmour (km UNVERIFIED) · Chamba town (Lakshmi Narayan temple, Chamba Chaugan) · Dalhousie/Khajjiar en route from Pathankot.

## 3. Disputes & fact traps
- **Altitude**: lake 4,080 vs 4,085 vs 4,190 m; peak 5,653 vs 5,656 m → "about".
- **Trek length**: 13 vs 13–14 vs 19 km (old Hadsar–Dhancho 12 + 7) → "about 13–14 km from Hadsar".
- **2026 dates**: official window 25 Aug–20 Sep vs Janmashtami 4 Sep start of baths vs conclusion 19 Sep — say "the official window ran 25 Aug–20 Sep; baths on 4 Sep and 19 Sep".
- **"Never climbed"** claims about the peak — don't print; "first ascent not recorded" (our Mountains pages) and climbing is avoided out of reverence (tradition).
- Not the same as **Kinner Kailash** (Kinnaur) or **Shrikhand Mahadev** (Kullu/Shimla) — the "Panch Kailash" list (Manimahesh, Kinnaur, Shrikhand, Adi Kailash, Mount Kailash) — Wikipedia.
- Jewel-light: present as belief + reflection explanation side by side.
- Helicopter: never say "available" without a current notice; never quote prices.
- No fake booking sites — only manimaheshyatra.hp.gov.in.

## 4. Questions people ask (short answers)
1. **When is Manimahesh Yatra 2027?** Not announced yet; it follows Janmashtami to Radha Ashtami (8 Sep 2027 per panchang). In 2026 the window ran 25 Aug–20 Sep.
2. **Is registration compulsory for Manimahesh?** Yes, since 2026 an online e-pass on manimaheshyatra.hp.gov.in, with about 5,000 pilgrims a day allowed beyond Hadsar.
3. **How long is the Manimahesh trek?** About 13–14 km one way from Hadsar via Dhancho and Gauri Kund; most people halt a night at Dhancho.
4. **How high is Manimahesh Lake?** About 4,080 m; the peak above it is about 5,650 m.
5. **How do I reach Manimahesh?** Train to Pathankot or fly to Kangra (Gaggal), drive to Chamba and Bharmour (~59 km from Chamba), then 13 km to Hadsar where the trek starts.
6. **Is there a helicopter for Manimahesh?** A Bharmour–Gauri Kund service has run in past seasons; check the district notice each year.
7. **What is Chhota and Bada Nhaun?** The two holy baths in the lake — the small one on Janmashtami and the royal one on Radha Ashtami.
8. **Can I visit Manimahesh outside the yatra?** The lake is frozen roughly Oct–June; outside the official window it is a trek only, with guides and local permission.
