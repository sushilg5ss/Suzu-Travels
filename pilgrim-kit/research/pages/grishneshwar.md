## 2. Facts
- Names: Shri Grishneshwar / Ghrishneshwar / Ghushmeshwar Temple — घृष्णेश्वर ज्योतिर्लिंग (Marathi घृष्णेश्वर; local "Ghushneshwar") — data (2026-10-08).
- Place in lists: **12th and last** in the popular smaranam ("घुश्मेशं च शिवालये"); also 12th in the Shiva Purana list — data; say "traditionally the twelfth" (FACT TRAP #3).
- Location: Verul village (Ellora), Khuldabad taluka, **Chhatrapati Sambhajinagar district** (formerly Aurangabad), Maharashtra; beside the **Shivalaya Teerth** tank — 20.025, 75.1699 — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghrishneshwar_Temple (data 2026-10-08). **~1.5 km from the Ellora Caves**.
- Deity: Shiva as Ghushmeshwara ("lord of compassion" in popular renderings — UNVERIFIED etymology, don't print). Red-stone (basalt) temple with carved panels — Wikipedia (data).
- Legend (own words, as tradition — Shiva Purana, Koti Rudra Samhita): The Brahmin Sudharma and his wife Sudeha had no children, so Sudeha asked him to marry her younger sister Ghushma. Ghushma was devoted to Shiva: each day she shaped 101 clay lingas, worshipped them and let them dissolve in a nearby pond. She had a son, and Sudeha's jealousy grew until she took the young man's life and left him in that pond. Ghushma did not stop her worship; by Shiva's grace her son returned alive. Shiva wanted to punish Sudeha, but Ghushma asked him to forgive her sister and to stay there for the good of all. He remained as Ghushmeshwar. (≈115 words) — data legend_summary. Keep the death off-screen ("took his life" at most; no method).
- History (state plainly, no communal framing):
  - **13th–14th c.** — the temple was destroyed in the Delhi Sultanate period and damaged again during the Mughal–Maratha wars — Wikipedia (data). Page: "destroyed and rebuilt several times".
  - **16th c.** — restored by **Maloji Bhosale**, grandfather of Chhatrapati Shivaji — Wikipedia (data).
  - **18th c.** — present structure: Wikipedia credits **Gautama Bai Holkar (1729)**; popular accounts say Ahilyabai Holkar — print "rebuilt by the Holkars in the 18th century" (FACT TRAP #12).
- Darshan (no official site; **Incredible India** = data): **5:30 am – 9:30 pm**; aartis 5:30 am, 12 noon, 7 pm, Shayan 9:30 pm. A private guide (YatraDham, 2 Oct 2026) lists 5:30 am–9 pm with a 12–1 pm break and aartis 5 am/12/8 pm → conflict → page: "about 5:30 am to 9:30 pm (Incredible India); aarti times vary — confirm locally".
- Customs: men enter the sanctum **bare-chested** (shirts/vests off); devotees may **touch the linga** (sparsh darshan) — Incredible India (data). On peak days the administration has closed the sanctum and given darshan from outside — **Mahashivratri 2026 (15 Feb)**: garbhagriha darshan closed for the first time; phones and cameras banned in the premises; parking banned within 1 km — decided by the **Shri Grishneshwar Devasthan Trust** with the district administration — ETV Bharat Marathi, 4 Feb 2026 https://www.etvbharat.com/mr/state/preparations-for-mahashivratri-in-ghrishneshwar-jyotirlinga-chhatrapati-sambhajinagar-temple-administration-took-big-decision-maharashtra-news-mhs26020401472 (2026-10-19). Whether the phone ban is permanent: **UNVERIFIED** → "phones may not be allowed inside — keep them in the car/locker".
- Abhishek in Shravan was stopped in a past Shravan (News18 Marathi headline; year not checked) → don't print.

## 3. Disputes & fact traps
- **Who rebuilt it:** Gautama Bai Holkar (1729, Wikipedia) vs Ahilyabai Holkar (popular) → "the Holkars, 18th century" (#12).
- **"Closed for six months from 1 Nov 2026"** — viral; not confirmed; the Collector has said no six-month closure. Never print the closure as fact; never print it as "fully open as usual" either.
- **Timings** — Incredible India 5:30–21:30 vs private guides (9 pm close, noon break, different aarti times).
- **VIP/online darshan booking** — no official online booking found; "VIP darshan ticket" sites are private → warn, don't link.
- Name spellings: Grishneshwar / Ghrishneshwar / Ghushmeshwar / Ghushneshwar — same temple.
- "Grishneshwar is inside the Ellora Caves" — no; the temple is ~1.5 km away (Ellora Cave 16 has the Kailasa temple — different).
- Aurangabad was renamed **Chhatrapati Sambhajinagar** (2023) — use the new name, "formerly Aurangabad" once.

## 4. Questions people ask (short answers)
1. **What are Grishneshwar temple timings?** About 5:30 am to 9:30 pm (Incredible India); aarti times vary, so confirm locally.
2. **Is Grishneshwar temple closed in November 2026?** A six-month closure was reported, but the district collector has said the temple won't close for six months; works are on, so check local news before you go.
3. **Why do men remove their shirts at Grishneshwar?** By temple custom men enter the sanctum bare-chested; devotees may touch the linga.
4. **Which is the 12th Jyotirlinga?** Grishneshwar near Ellora is traditionally listed twelfth and last.
5. **How far is Grishneshwar from Ellora Caves?** About 1.5 km — most visitors see both the same day.
6. **How far is it from Chhatrapati Sambhajinagar (Aurangabad)?** About 30 km by road; the airport is roughly 30–37 km away.
7. **What is the story of Grishneshwar?** The Shiva Purana tells of Ghushma, whose faith brought her son back to life and who asked Shiva to forgive her jealous sister; Shiva stayed as Ghushmeshwar.
8. **Can I carry a phone inside?** Phones and cameras were banned in the premises on Mahashivratri 2026; rules on other days aren't published — be ready to leave them outside.
