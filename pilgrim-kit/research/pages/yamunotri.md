## 2. Facts
- Names: **Yamunotri** · यमुनोत्री · Yamunotri Dham; dedicated to goddess **Yamuna** — official portal (as_of 2026-11-11)
- Location: **Uttarkashi district, Uttarakhand**; temple at **3,293 m** — official portal (as_of 2026-11-11)
- Temple: "originally built by **Maharaja Pratap Shah of Tehri Garhwal**" (official portal; no year). Later rebuilds (Jaipur royals, 19th c., earthquake/flood damage) — **NOT verified today** → don't print.
- Deity & belief (tradition, our words): Yamuna is the daughter of Surya (the Sun) and the sister of **Yama**, lord of death; it is believed that bathing in her waters protects from an untimely death (official portal). Bhai Dooj (Yama Dwitiya) — the day the shrine closes — is linked to Yamuna's love for her brother; Garhwal Post (2025) also notes local belief that **Shani Dev** of Kharsali is her brother and comes for the closing puja. Sage **Asit Muni** is traditionally said to have lived here (blog only — phrase "tradition associates"). Black-marble idol: **not verified**.
- **Surya Kund**: natural hot spring by the temple where pilgrims cook rice and potatoes tied in cloth as an offering/prasad — official portal + yatradham (as_of 2026-11-11)
- **Divya Shila**: sacred rock pillar near the temple, worshipped before entering the shrine — official portal. (Blog: linked to a sage's penance — don't print.)
- Other: Tapt Kund (hot bathing pool), Mukharvind spout, Saptarishi Kund (high, difficult trek, not for most pilgrims); Yamuna's source is the **Champasar glacier** a little above the temple (blog, approx. 2 km — "said to be"). Kalind Parvat name — not verified.
- **Road head & trek**: last motorable point **Janki Chatti**; **6 km** trek to the temple (official portal + data/yatras.json), usually 2–3 hours up (portal) — blog says 2–4 h.
- **On-site options**: walk, **pony, palki/doli, pitthu** (official portal + blog). Helicopter: chartered helicopters from Dehradun mentioned by the portal (Kharsali helipad not detailed) → only "charters exist; no scheduled shuttle to the shrine" (unverified detail — keep vague). **Ropeway (Kharsali–Yamunotri, Parvatmala)**: NOT mentioned officially → **not operational; do not promise** (BACKLOG note).
- **Season 2026**: opened **19 Apr 2026, 12:35** (Akshaya Tritiya) — official portal (19 Apr) + data/yatras.json; closing **11 Nov 2026 (Bhai Dooj)**, announced 20 Oct 2026 — data/yatras.json (muhurat 10:28 am per one source only → "late morning, confirm"). In 2025 it closed 23 Oct at 12:30 pm (News On AIR, Garhwal Post). **2026 closing not yet reported in news at research time** (today) — Page Builder: re-check.
- **2027 opening**: Akshaya Tritiya 2027 — **not announced yet**.
- **Winter seat**: goddess's doli goes to **Kharsali** (Khushimath), near Janki Chatti, where she is worshipped for ~6 months; Kharsali also has the **Shani temple** — official portal + News On AIR 2025 (as_of 2026-11-11)
- **Darshan/aarti timings**: official portal says opening/closing & aarti times "will be announced soon" → **"confirm locally"**. (Blog: 6 am–8 pm with a midday break; aarti ~6:30 am & ~6:30 pm — unofficial, don't print as fact.)

- **How to reach (official portal, approx.)**: Dehradun **~180 km** by road + 6 km trek; Rishikesh **~210 km**; Haridwar **~220 km**; Uttarkashi **~125 km**; route Dehradun → Barkot → Janki Chatti (Barkot km not verified). Nearest railheads Dehradun / Rishikesh / Haridwar; airport **Jolly Grant, Dehradun** (blog ~199 km — print "about 200 km", approx.). Mussoorie ~141 km (blog).

## 3. Disputes & fact traps
- **Garhwal Post wrote "opened on Akshaya Tritiya, 30 April"** — that is the **2025** season (30 Apr 2025). 2026 opening was **19 Apr** (portal). Don't mix.
- **Recycled closing dates**: 23 Oct (2025) is all over blogs; 2026 = 11 Nov (Bhai Dooj).
- **Ropeway**: many blogs say "ropeway coming/ready" — not operational per anything official → don't print as available.
- **Timings**: blogs contradict each other (6–8 pm straight vs 12–2 break); official not published → "confirm locally".
- **Builder**: Tehri king Pratap Shah (official); "Maharani Guleria of Jaipur rebuilt it in the 19th century" is a common blog line — unverified.
- Source of the Yamuna: Champasar glacier / Saptarishi Kund — say "rises in the glaciers above the shrine", traditions vary.
- Shani as Yamuna's brother — local belief; keep as "locally believed".

## 4. Questions people ask
1. How long is the Yamunotri trek? — 6 km from Janki Chatti, usually 2–3 hours uphill.
2. Can I go by pony or palki? — Yes, ponies, palkis/dolis and pitthus are available at Janki Chatti.
3. When does Yamunotri open in 2027? — On Akshaya Tritiya 2027; the date is not announced yet.
4. When did Yamunotri close in 2026? — On Bhai Dooj, 11 Nov 2026; the goddess moves to Kharsali for winter.
5. What is Surya Kund? — A hot spring beside the temple where pilgrims cook rice and potatoes as prasad.
6. Where is Yamuna worshipped in winter? — At Kharsali village near Janki Chatti.
7. How far is Yamunotri from Dehradun? — About 180 km by road to Janki Chatti, then the 6 km trek.
8. Is there a ropeway to Yamunotri? — Not in operation as of 2026; plan to walk or take a pony/palki.
