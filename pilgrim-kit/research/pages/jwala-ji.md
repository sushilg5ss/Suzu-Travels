- Legend (own words, as tradition — Shakta tradition / local lore): When Sati gave up her body at Daksha's yagna and Shiva carried her through the worlds in grief, Vishnu's chakra divided the body; tradition says her tongue fell in these Kangra hills, and the Goddess shows herself here not as a statue but as living flame. A cowherd is said to have followed a mysterious girl who drank his cow's milk, and later a king, guided by a dream, found the fire burning on the hillside and built the first shrine. A famous later story tells that the Mughal emperor Akbar tried to put out the flames with water and an iron cover, failed, and offered a golden chhatra (canopy) that, devotees say, turned into a strange unknown metal. (≈130 words; Akbar story = legend) — data file; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jwala_(goddess) (2026-10-08)

## 4. Questions people ask (with short answers)
1. **Why does the flame at Jwala Ji never go out?** Devotees see it as the Goddess herself; geologically the flames are natural gas seeping through rock — ONGC drilled exploratory wells here from 1957.
2. **Is there an idol in Jwalamukhi temple?** No — the flames (usually counted as nine) are worshipped directly.
3. **Which body part of Sati fell at Jwala Ji?** Her tongue, by Shakti Peeth tradition.
4. **What are Jwala Ji temple timings?** Roughly early morning to about 10 pm with a short midday break (official pages differ) — confirm locally, especially in Navratri.
5. **What is the Akbar story at Jwalamukhi?** Legend says Akbar tried to douse the flames, failed and offered a golden chhatra that turned into an unknown metal.
6. **How to reach Jwala Ji?** Nearest airport Gaggal (~46–50 km); narrow-gauge Jawalamukhi Road (Ranital) ~20 km; broad gauge Pathankot ~120–140 km; road from Kangra ~30 km.
7. **How far is Jwala Ji from Chintpurni / Kangra?** About 31 km from Chintpurni and ~36 km from Brajeshwari (Kangra Devi).
8. **Where to stay at Jwala Ji?** Simple hotels, guest houses and dharamshalas in Jawalamukhi town; Kangra/Dharamshala for wider choice — book early for Navratri.
