## 2. Facts
- Names: Shri Trimbakeshwar Temple / त्र्यंबकेश्वर ज्योतिर्लिंग (also Tryambakeshwar) — data (2026-10-08)
- Place in lists: Shiva Purana order **8th**; the popular smaranam lists "Tryambakam Gautami-tate" later — say which list (FACT TRAP #3).
- Location: Trimbak town, Nashik district, Maharashtra; foot of the **Brahmagiri** hills; source of the **Godavari (Gautami)** — ritual source at **Kushavarta Kund** in town; 19.9322, 73.5308 — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trimbakeshwar_Temple (data 2026-10-08).
- Deity: Shiva as Tryambaka ("three-eyed"). The linga sits in a hollow with **three small faces**, believed to stand for Brahma, Vishnu and Shiva — data (Wikipedia).
- Legend (own words, as tradition — Shiva Purana, Koti Rudra Samhita): Sage Gautama and Ahalya lived on Brahmagiri, where Varuna gave them a pond that never dried, even in drought. Jealous sages plotted to drive Gautama out: they sent a weak cow into his fields, and when he waved it away with a blade of grass it fell dead. Branded guilty of killing a cow, Gautama prayed to Shiva for a way to atone. Pleased, Shiva released the Ganga here; she flows on as the Godavari, also called Gautami after the sage. At the request of Gautama and the gods, Shiva agreed to stay as Tryambakeshwar. (≈110 words) — data legend_summary
- History (state plainly):
  - **18th c.** — present black-basalt temple commissioned by **Peshwa Balaji Bajirao (Nanasaheb)** after an earlier shrine was destroyed under Aurangzeb — Wikipedia (data). State without communal framing: "rebuilt in the 18th century under Peshwa Balaji Bajirao".
  - **1817–18** — the Nassak Diamond, once on the deity, taken by the British in the Third Anglo-Maratha War — Wikipedia (data).
  - **Crown (mukut)**, said by tradition to date from the Pandava era, shown **Mondays 4–5 pm** — Wikipedia (data); trust home page shows crown images, no timing → "confirm locally".
  - **1 Jul 2026** — ASI found a stone Shivling at the bottom of the temple's **Amrit Kund** during desilting — https://www.deccanherald.com/india/maharashtra/maharashtra-asi-discovers-shivling-from-amrit-kund-in-trimbakeshwar-4059651 (2026-10-18).

## 3. Disputes & fact traps
- **Simhastha end date:** PTI = flag lowered **24 Jul 2028**; Wikipedia's infobox says the event runs "31 Oct 2026 – 24 Jul 2027". Print the snan dates + "the Simhastha period closes in July 2028 (PTI)" or just the snan dates.
- **Which snan is at Trimbak:** some blogs list all three Amrit Snans "at Trimbakeshwar". PTI/FPJ split the final one: 11 Sep Nashik, 12 Sep Trimbakeshwar; the first two are not clearly located by PTI (FPJ says Nashik). Page: "2 Aug and 31 Aug 2027 (main Amrit Snans), 11 Sep (Nashik) and 12 Sep (Trimbakeshwar)".
- **Evening puja time** — official typo (#20); never print "7 am evening puja".
- **Kaal Sarp "guaranteed results"/"best pandit" claims** — commercial pandit sites dominate search; no claims, no names, no prices; warn about online-ad fraud (FPJ Sep 2026).
- Trimbakeshwar ≠ Tryambakeshwar elsewhere; Godavari "source" = Brahmagiri (ritual source Kushavarta Kund) — say both.
- Simhastha Nashik (2026–28) ≠ Simhastha Ujjain (2028, Mahakaleshwar page) — disambiguate.
- Non-Hindu-entry/ritual controversies (e.g. 2023 news) — communal, **omit**.

## 4. Questions people ask (short answers)
1. **What are Trimbakeshwar temple timings?** 5:30 am to 9 pm (trust); hours change on festival days.
2. **How can I skip the long queue?** Use the trust's paid donation darshan, booked on trimbakeshwartrust.com or at its counter; VIP passes can be suspended on peak days (as in Shravan 2026).
3. **Why does the Trimbakeshwar linga have three faces?** Tradition says the three small faces stand for Brahma, Vishnu and Shiva.
4. **Where does the Godavari start?** On the Brahmagiri hills above Trimbak; its ritual source is Kushavarta Kund in town.
5. **When is the Nashik–Trimbakeshwar Kumbh?** Flag hoisting 31 Oct 2026; main Amrit Snans 2 Aug and 31 Aug 2027, then 11 Sep (Nashik) and 12 Sep 2027 (Trimbakeshwar).
6. **How far is Trimbakeshwar from Nashik?** About 28–30 km from Nashik city and 40 km from Nashik Road station.
7. **Can I book Kaal Sarp puja online?** The trust site books only its own pujas (Rudrabhishek, Laghurudra); for other rituals arrange in person in Trimbak — local priests have warned about fake online offers.
8. **What is the best time to visit?** July to February; avoid Simhastha bathing dates and Shravan Mondays if you want a calm darshan.
