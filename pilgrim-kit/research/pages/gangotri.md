## 2. Facts
- Names: **Gangotri** · गंगोत्री · Gangotri Dham; seat of goddess **Ganga** — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gangotri (as_of 2026-11-11)
- Location: Gangotri town, **Uttarkashi district, Uttarakhand**, on the Bhagirathi; town at **~3,100 m** (10,200 ft) — Wikipedia; FPJ also "around 3,100 metres" (as_of 2026-11-11). Bhagirathi at Gangotri ≈ 3,049 m (Wikipedia Bhagirathi River).
- Temple: built by the Gorkha (Nepali) general **Amar Singh Thapa**, later restored in the 19th century (Wikipedia). FPJ says "18th century", white granite. Print: "built by the Gorkha commander Amar Singh Thapa (late 18th/early 19th century, per most accounts) and restored later" — avoid a single exact year.
- Legend (tradition, our words): King **Bhagiratha** did long penance so that the heavenly river would come down and free the souls of his ancestors (the sons of Sagara); Brahma agreed, and Shiva caught her fierce fall in his matted hair, releasing her gently. Near the river a stone is pointed out as the place of Bhagiratha's penance; the river here is called **Bhagirathi** after him. A second tradition says the Pandavas performed a great yajna here after the Mahabharata war. (Ramayana, Mahabharata, Puranas — Wikipedia Bhagirathi River / Gangotri, as_of 2026-11-11). "Bhagirath Shila" name and the "submerged shivling" story were NOT found in today's sources → don't print as fact (or "locally it is believed", only if Page Builder finds a source).
- River: Bhagirathi rises at **Gaumukh** (snout of the Gangotri Glacier) and meets the **Alaknanda at Devprayag**, from where the river is called the Ganga; Bhagirathi length ~205 km — Wikipedia Bhagirathi River (as_of 2026-11-11)
- **Gaumukh**: ~**18 km** trek from Gangotri; **Bhojbasa/Bhojwasa 14 km**; snout at **~4,023 m** (13,200 ft); last ~4.5 km over moraine; snout has retreated ~1.5 km since the 1950s; Tapovan beyond Gaumukh — Wikipedia Gomukh + GMVN (as_of 2026-11-11)
- **Permits**: Gaumukh/Tapovan lies in **Gangotri National Park**; **150 permits per day**; permit from the DFO Uttarkashi / online via the state's Pathik portal — GMVN + Outlook Traveller 4 Apr 2026. Park open **1 Apr – 30 Nov**, winter break to 31 Mar; checkposts from 6 am (Outlook Traveller). Fee exists — **no ₹ on our page**.
- **Season 2026**: opened **19 Apr 2026, 12:15** (Akshaya Tritiya) — data/yatras.json; closed **10 Nov 2026 (Annakut/Govardhan Puja)**, scheduled ~12:05 pm (FPJ 22 Oct 2026; one source → print "around noon") — data/yatras.json updated today. Custom: closes on Annakut after Diwali, reopens on Akshaya Tritiya (Wikipedia; data).
- **2027 opening**: Akshaya Tritiya 2027 — **exact date & muhurat not announced yet** (usually fixed by the temple committee; re-check Feb–Apr 2027).
- **Winter seat**: the Ganga idol goes in a doli to **Mukhba village near Harsil** (halt overnight at the Markandeya temple on the way per News On AIR 22 Oct 2025; enshrined next day); winter darshan at the Ganga temple, Mukhba — News On AIR (2025) + FPJ (2026) + Wikipedia (as_of 2026-11-11)
- **Darshan/aarti timings**: **NOT published officially** in sources checked today → print "confirm locally" (GMVN mentions an evening aarti at the temple without time).
- **Registration**: Char Dham yatris register on the official Uttarakhand portal https://registrationandtouristcare.uk.gov.in/ (README §1; helpline toll-free **1364**, 0135-3520100 per portal page read today). Warn about fake sites.
- **How to reach**: road to the temple (no trek to the shrine). Rishikesh–Gangotri **~280 km** by road (GMVN); Uttarkashi–Gangotri **99 km** (Wikipedia); airport **Jolly Grant, Dehradun**; railheads **Rishikesh / Dehradun**; road via NH34 through Uttarkashi; helicopter charters Sahastradhara (Dehradun)–Harsil exist (Outlook Traveller). Jolly Grant/Dehradun km — **not verified** (print without km).
- On-site: motorable to Gangotri; temple a short walk from the parking/market. No ropeway/pony needed for the main shrine. Gaumukh = proper high-altitude trek (permit, fitness).
- Elders: no trek to the shrine (good for elders); altitude ~3,100 m — acclimatise (Uttarkashi/Harsil night), cold mornings; Gaumukh not for elders/unfit.
- Nearby (names only unless km verified): Harsil & Mukhba, Bhaironghati (~2,650 m on the Bhagirathi, Wikipedia), Uttarkashi (Vishwanath temple), Gaumukh–Tapovan; Yamunotri is the other Uttarkashi dham (route via Barkot — km not verified today).
- Footfall: Gangotri set a record of **8.41 lakh** pilgrims by late Sept 2026 (DD India 29 Sep 2026, research/yatras-notes.md).

## 3. Disputes & fact traps
- **Builder/date**: Wikipedia "built by Amar Singh Thapa, restored in the 19th century"; FPJ "18th century". Many blogs say "early 18th century" or credit the Jaipur royals for rebuilding — **unverified**; use the soft wording above.
- **Closing day**: Wikipedia says "closes on Diwali" — 2026 actual closing was **Annakut (10 Nov)**, the day after Diwali-week Govardhan Puja; use data.
- **Recycled dates**: blogs show 2025's 22 Oct closing; 2027 opening "dates" online are guesses → "not announced yet".
- **Gaumukh = source of the Ganga** — strictly the Bhagirathi; it becomes "Ganga" at Devprayag. Say both.
- Snout retreat/climate — state the ~1.5 km figure neutrally, no alarmism.
- Swarajya headline (2026) on a temple-committee entry policy appeared in search results — **not checked, communal topic → do not print** (README §1.6).
- Timings on blogs (6 am–2 pm / 3–9:30 pm etc.) — not official → "confirm locally".

## 4. Questions people ask
1. When does Gangotri open in 2027? — On Akshaya Tritiya 2027; the exact date and time are not announced yet (usually fixed in spring).
2. When did Gangotri close in 2026? — 10 Nov 2026, on Annakut, around noon; the idol went to Mukhba for winter.
3. Where is Goddess Ganga worshipped in winter? — At the Ganga temple in Mukhba village near Harsil.
4. How far is Gaumukh from Gangotri? — About 18 km on foot (Bhojwasa at 14 km); permit needed, 150 per day.
5. Is there a trek to Gangotri temple? — No, the road reaches Gangotri; the temple is a short walk.
6. How far is Gangotri from Rishikesh? — About 280 km by road (GMVN); from Uttarkashi 99 km.
7. Who built the Gangotri temple? — The Gorkha general Amar Singh Thapa; restored later.
8. Is registration required? — Yes, register on the official Uttarakhand Char Dham portal before the yatra (helpline 1364).
