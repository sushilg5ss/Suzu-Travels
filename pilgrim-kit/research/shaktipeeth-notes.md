*Compiled 8 Oct 2026 for suzutravels.com. Companion to `shaktipeeth.json` (28 shrines). All timings and dates are as of 2026-10-08. `null` / "not verified" means no official source was found. Do not publish those values as fact.*

## 1. The story: Sati, Daksha's yagna, Shiva's tandava and Vishnu's chakra (own words)

Sati was the daughter of Prajapati Daksha, and she chose the ascetic Shiva as her husband against her father's wishes. Daksha later held a grand yagna and invited every god except Shiva. Sati went anyway, uninvited. In front of the assembled guests her father ridiculed Shiva as an unfit, inauspicious outsider. Sati could not bear the insult and gave up her body. Some texts say she entered the sacrificial fire. Others say she released her life through yogic power. In his fury Shiva created Virabhadra, who wrecked the yagna and beheaded Daksha. Daksha was later revived with a goat's head.

Shiva lifted Sati's body onto his shoulder and wandered the worlds in grief. His dance of mourning (tandava) threatened to unmake creation. To end it, Vishnu released his Sudarshan Chakra and cut the body into pieces. Each place where a limb, or in some accounts an ornament, fell became a *peetha*, a seat of the Goddess. In each one she is worshipped in a local form, guarded by a form of Shiva called a Bhairava. Free of the burden, Shiva withdrew into meditation, and the sacred geography of the Shakti Peethas was born.

Sources: [Wikipedia: Shakta pithas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shakta_pithas), [Wikipedia: Daksha yajna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daksha_yajna), [HP Kangra district: Kangra Pilgrimage](https://hpkangra.nic.in/kangra-pilgrimage/).

| **64** | Brahmanda Purana (Wikipedia flags "citation needed") | | Vaishno Devi is placed in the 64/108 traditions |
| **108** | Devi Bhagavata Purana / Matsya Purana | Vishalakshi is named first | |

Other counts also exist: Kalika Purana 7, Kularnava Tantra 18, Kubjika Tantra 42. Compiled tables (Wikipedia, temple sites, travel blogs) often mix these lists and sometimes disagree on body parts, Devi names and Bhairavas. Wikipedia's own article is tagged "Disputed". Treat any single "official 51 list" as one tradition among several.

**Outside India (exclude from India packages but mention):** Hinglaj Mata (Balochistan, Pakistan; Brahmarandhra). Sharada Peeth (PoK, in ruins; a new Sharada temple was consecrated in Kupwara district in 2023). Guhyeshwari and Muktinath (Nepal). Seven sites in Bangladesh, such as Sugandha, Jeshoreshwari and Chattal Bhawani. Mansarovar/Kailash (Tibet: "Manasa" Dakshayani, right hand). Shankari (Trincomalee, Sri Lanka).

Sources: [Wikipedia: Shakta pithas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shakta_pithas); D.C. Sircar, *The Śākta Pīṭhas* (Motilal Banarsidass, 1998), as cited there.

## 3. Nau Devi Yatra (नौ देवी दर्शन): which nine?

### 3a. What the sources say
- **Core folk tradition: "Saat Behna" (seven sisters).** Vaishno Devi (elder sister), Jwala Ji, Naina Devi, Chintpurni, Kangra/Vajreshwari, Chamunda and Mansa Devi (Panchkula). Wikipedia says modern Hindi pamphlets extend these seven to *nine* to match the nine nights of Navratri, usually by **adding Shakumbhari and Kalika (Kalka)**. ([Wikipedia: Saat Behna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saat_Behna_(seven_sister_goddesses)))
- The book *Call of Nine Goddesses* (listed on exoticindia.com) gives the same nine: Naina Devi, Chintpurni, Jwalamukhi, Vajreshwari-Kangra, Chamunda, Vaishno Devi, Mansa Devi, Kalika Devi and Shakumbhari.
- **Commercial packages** ([indianholiday.com](https://www.indianholiday.com/tours-of-india/nau-devi-darshan.html), [tourtravelworld 1](https://www.tourtravelworld.com/packages/nau9-devi-darshan-tour-130002.html), [tourtravelworld 2](https://www.tourtravelworld.com/packages/th-nau9-devi-darshan-8days-210668.html)) **drop Kalka and Shakumbhari**. They add **Sheetla Devi** (between Naina Devi and Chintpurni; the exact temple is not identified in those pages) and **Baglamukhi (Bankhandi, Kangra)**. Their routes run Delhi → Chandigarh → Kangra → Dharamshala → Katra → Amritsar, over 7N/8D.
- Do not confuse this with the "Nau Devi" cave near Katra (SMVDSB), which holds pindis of the **Navadurga** (Shailaputri … Siddhidatri). That shrine is unrelated to the circuit.

### 3b. List settled for Suzu content
**Traditional nine (primary):** 1 Vaishno Devi · 2 Mansa Devi (Panchkula) · 3 Kali Mata (Kalka) · 4 Shakumbhari Devi (Saharanpur) · 5 Naina Devi · 6 Chintpurni · 7 Jwala Ji · 8 Brajeshwari (Kangra Devi) · 9 Chamunda Devi.
**Variant to note on the page:** many packages swap Kalka and Shakumbhari for Baglamukhi and Sheetla Devi.

### 3c. Practical route (Delhi start, traditional nine)
Road distances were computed with OSRM (OpenStreetMap router, router.project-osrm.org) on 2026-10-08. They are approximate shortest-time routes. Real hill driving takes 30–60% longer than OSRM's time estimates, so drive times below are realistic estimates, not OSRM output.

| Day | Route | Approx. km | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Delhi → **Shakumbhari Devi** (Behat, Saharanpur) | 210 | Visit Bhura Dev first. Overnight Saharanpur/Yamunanagar side |
| 2 | Shakumbhari → **Mansa Devi**, Panchkula → **Kali Mata, Kalka** | 156 + 25 | Mansa Devi: summer 04:00–22:00, winter 05:00–21:00 (Shrine Board) |
| 3 | Panchkula → **Naina Devi** | 110 | Cable car to the hilltop. Overnight Naina Devi / Anandpur Sahib |
| 4 | Naina Devi → **Chintpurni** | 112 | Via Una. Overnight Chintpurni/Bharwain |
| 5 | Chintpurni → **Jwala Ji** → Kangra | 31 + 36 | Optional: Baglamukhi, Bankhandi (on NH503) |
| 6 | **Brajeshwari** (morning) → **Chamunda** → Dharamshala | 22 (+15) | |
| 7 | Chamunda → Pathankot → **Katra** | 241 | Pathankot leg 99 km + Pathankot–Katra 149 km. Register for the RFID card on arrival or online |
| 8 | **Vaishno Devi** yatra + Bhairon | trek ~12 km each way | Or helicopter Katra–Sanjichhat; ropeway Bhawan–Bhairon 09:00–17:00 |
| 9 | Katra → Delhi | ~649 by road | Or train from Shri Mata Vaishno Devi Katra station |

- **Realistic duration:** 9 days for the traditional nine. **7 days** if you start from Chandigarh and skip Shakumbhari and Kalka (Mansa Devi → Naina Devi → Chintpurni → Jwala Ji → Kangra → Chamunda → Katra). This matches the 7N/8D commercial pattern.
- **Reverse option:** start with Vaishno Devi from Jammu (Jammu–Katra ~50 km per Shrine Board, or OSRM 41 km from Jammu city centre) and end at Chandigarh or Delhi.
- **Best season:** March–June and September–November. Kangra district suggests March–June. Incredible India suggests March–October for Naina Devi. Avoid peak monsoon (Jul–Aug): an Adhkuwari landslide on 26 Aug 2025 killed 35 pilgrims ([Wikipedia: Vaishno Devi Temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vaishno_Devi_Temple)). The Shrine Board says not to trek during weather alerts. Winter (Dec–Feb) is cold but workable. In 2012 Chintpurni had its first snowfall in 52 years.
- **Navratri crowd note:** Sharad Navratri 2026 starts on **11 Oct 2026**, three days after these notes; Vijayadashami is 20 Oct. Chaitra Navratri 2027 starts on **7 Apr 2027**. Expect the heaviest crowds at every shrine on these dates and during the Shravan Ashtami melas (Naina Devi and Chintpurni, Jul–Aug). Vaishno Devi caps darshan at about 30–35k people a day. Above that, registration stops and pilgrims "wait" at Katra (SMVDSB). In 2008, 123+ people died in a stampede at Naina Devi during Shravan.

### 3d. Vaishno Devi yatra registration (official, maavaishnodevi.org)
- An **RFID Yatra Access Card is compulsory and free**, one per pilgrim. Get it at the Yatra Registration Counter near Katra bus stand, at YRC-II on Udhampur road (about 1 km away), or online at online.maavaishnodevi.org. The Shrine Board is the only authorised issuer.
- You must cross the **Banganga checkpost within 6 hours** of the card being issued, or the card is confiscated. Pilgrims without a card are turned back.
- Registered yatris are covered by accident insurance, per the SMVDSB registration page.
- **Helicopter:** helipads at Katra, Sanjichhat and Panchi; bookings on the official portal. **Battery cars:** Bhawan–Himkoti–Ardhkuwari, with quotas for senior citizens and disabled pilgrims (Wikipedia). **Ropeway:** Bhawan–Bhairon, 09:00–17:00.
- Helplines: 1800-180-7212 (toll-free), 01991-234804; WhatsApp 99060 19494.

### 3e. What to carry
Valid photo ID (needed for Vaishno Devi registration) · light bag; the Shrine Board says to carry only water, snacks, medicines, ID and yatra slip · shoes with good grip · rain poncho (Shrine Board) · warm layer for Katra and Bhawan nights and Kangra winters · cash/UPI for prasad and parking · a printout of hotel bookings for the Navratri rush.

### 3f. Rules: leather, photography, conduct (verified items only)
- **Vaishno Devi:** shoes, belts, combs and other items are **not allowed in the holy cave**. Deposit them at the free cloak room opposite Gate 1 at Bhawan. The official cloak-room page (2023) says **video cameras and mobile phones are "not allowed on the track for security reasons"**. Enforcement in practice may differ, so confirm before travel. Hazardous and sharp items are banned (Do's & Don'ts).
- **Leather:** no official "no leather" rule was found for the Himachal temples. The only verified restriction is the belt and shoe deposit at Vaishno Devi. Removing footwear is standard everywhere; at Kamakhya, shoes must be left at camps on the approach road.
- **Chintpurni (official Do's):** queue only with a darshan *parchi* (slip) from the temple guard. No back-door or bribe darshan. Don't feed monkeys. No smoking or drinking. Don't throw prasad on the floor.
- **Photography inside sanctums:** no official policy page was found for the Himachal shrines. Advise "no photography in the garbhagriha" as general etiquette and check notices on site. Do not present this as a published rule.

## 4. Himachal angle: "5 Shakti Peeth Yatra"

- **The five usually marketed:** Naina Devi (Bilaspur), Chintpurni (Una), Jwala Ji (Kangra), Brajeshwari/Kangra Devi (Kangra) and Chamunda Devi (Kangra). Wikipedia's Chintpurni article and the Kangra district site both use this grouping.
- **Classical status:** Jwalamukhi (tongue), Brajeshwari (left breast) and Naina Devi (eyes) appear in the compiled 51-peetha tables. Chintpurni appears with a disputed body part (foot vs forehead). **Chamunda is not a classical peetha.** Its place in the five comes from local tradition (Wikipedia; Kangra district).
- **Suzu already has a page:** "5 Shakti Peeth Yatra, Himachal" (5D/4N, price on request), <https://suzutravels.com/tours/5-shakti-peeth-tour-package-himachal-5-days-4-nights/>. Its route covers Naina Devi, Chintpurni, Jwala Ji, Brajeshwari and Chamunda. Found via https://suzutravels.com/?s=shakti on 2026-10-08. The search returned the full package list rather than a filtered search page. The closest related page is "Kashmir with Vaishno Devi" (<https://suzutravels.com/tours/panoramic-kashmir-vaishnodevi-economy/>). **There is no Nau Devi page and no standalone Vaishno Devi yatra page.** That is a content gap.

## 5. Twelve FAQs people search (answers verified as above)

1. **How many Shakti Peeths are in Himachal Pradesh? (हिमाचल में कितने शक्तिपीठ हैं?)** Five are commonly cited: Naina Devi, Chintpurni, Jwala Ji, Brajeshwari and Chamunda. Strictly, only Jwala Ji, Brajeshwari and Naina Devi (plus Chintpurni in some lists) are classical peethas.
2. **How many days does the Nau Devi Yatra take? (नौ देवी यात्रा कितने दिन की होती है?)** 7–8 days from Chandigarh/Delhi for the common package. About 9 days if you include Shakumbhari and Kalka.
3. **Which Shakti Peeth is where the tongue fell?** Jwalamukhi (Jwala Ji), Kangra.
4. **Where did Sati's eyes fall?** Naina Devi, Bilaspur (HP); Wikipedia specifies the right eye. Naina Devi in Nainital is a separate shrine with its own "eyes" tradition.
5. **Which nine Devis are in the Nau Devi Yatra?** Traditionally Vaishno Devi, Mansa Devi, Kalka, Shakumbhari, Naina Devi, Chintpurni, Jwala Ji, Kangra Devi and Chamunda. Packages often swap in Baglamukhi and Sheetla Devi.
6. **Is Vaishno Devi a Shakti Peeth?** It is not in the classical 51 lists. It is counted in the 64/108 traditions, and whether the skull or the right arm fell there is disputed.
7. **Is RFID registration needed for Vaishno Devi?** Yes. It is free and you must cross Banganga within 6 hours of issue.
8. **When is Navratri 2026?** Chaitra: 19–27 Mar 2026 (past). Sharad: 11–19 Oct 2026, Dussehra 20 Oct. Chaitra 2027 starts 7 Apr 2027.
9. **Why does the Jwala Ji flame burn forever?** Devotees see it as the Goddess. Geologically it is natural gas seepage, and ONGC drilled exploratory wells in the area from 1957.
10. **Is there a ropeway at Naina Devi?** Yes, a cable car from the base to the hilltop. The Anandpur Sahib–Naina Devi ropeway had not been built as of Aug 2024.
11. **Which Shakti Peeth is closest to Chandigarh?** Of the classical peethas, Naina Devi (~100–106 km). Mansa Devi in Panchkula is right next to Chandigarh but is a local-tradition shrine.
12. **Best time for the 5 Shakti Peeth / Nau Devi trip?** Mar–Jun and Sep–Nov. Avoid peak monsoon. Expect very heavy crowds during Navratri and the Shravan Ashtami melas.

## 8. Method
- Official and government sources were fetched first: maavaishnodevi.org, mansadevi.org.in, matashrichintpurni.com, maakamakhya.org, ambajitemple.in, kangratemples.hp.gov.in, hpkangra.nic.in, hpbilaspur.nic.in and incredibleindia.gov.in. Wikipedia raw wikitext came next, then Tribune / Outlook / panchang sites for dates.
- Road km come from the OSRM public router over OpenStreetMap, computed 2026-10-08 using the JSON coordinates. Treat them as approximate.
- All legend text in the JSON is paraphrased and was checked against the fetched sources. Details that could not be confirmed are listed in each entry's `unverified` array.
