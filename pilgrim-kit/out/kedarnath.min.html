Where is Kedarnath temple and how does the yatra work?

Kedarnath temple stands at about 3,583 m at the head of the Mandakini valley in Rudraprayag district, Uttarakhand. It is one of the 12 Jyotirlingas. Pilgrims register free, drive to Sonprayag, take a shuttle to Gaurikund and walk about 16 km — or fly from Guptkashi, Phata or Sersi, booked only on IRCTC HeliYatra. The temple is open from late April to around Bhai Dooj.

Kedarnath is the most demanding of the Jyotirlingas to reach, and for many families the most moving. The temple sits in a wide glacial bowl below snow peaks, with the Mandakini rising nearby. It is managed by the Shri Badarinath-Kedarnath Temple Committee (BKTC). The doors open each spring and close for winter around Bhai Dooj, when the deity's doli moves down to the Omkareshwar temple at Ukhimath.

Two things are easy to mix up: the Omkareshwar temple at Ukhimath is Kedarnath's winter seat, not the Omkareshwar Jyotirlinga on the Narmada; and the highest Shiva temple in the world is Tungnath, while Kedarnath is the highest Jyotirlinga. Kedarnath is 5th in the Shiva Purana list and 11th in the popular smaranam stotra ("हिमालये तु केदारं").

The legend

The legend of Kedarnath

The Mahabharata cycle and the Shiva Purana tell how Shiva came to stay in the Garhwal Himalaya. This is how tradition tells it.

  1. The Pandavas seek forgivenessAfter the Kurukshetra war, the five brothers went looking for Shiva, hoping to be freed from the sin of killing their own kin.
  2. A bull among the herdsShiva did not wish to be found so easily. It is believed he hid in the Garhwal hills as a bull among grazing cattle.
  3. Bhima stands across the valleyBhima planted his feet on two hillsides so the herd had to pass beneath him. One bull refused — and the brothers knew it was the Lord.
  4. The hump remainsAs the bull sank into the earth, Bhima caught its hump. Tradition says it remained at Kedarnath, worshipped as a rock form rather than a carved idol.

Tradition places the other parts of the bull at the other four Panch Kedar shrines — Tungnath, Rudranath, Madhyamaheshwar and Kalpeshwar — though the lists of which part fell where vary by source. The Shiva Purana adds that Shiva chose to dwell here as a Jyotirlinga at the prayer of the sages Nara and Narayana. These are stories of faith, told as tradition rather than history.

Route

The route from Haridwar to Kedarnath

Most pilgrims drive from Haridwar or Rishikesh to Sonprayag in a day or two, then cover the last stretch by shuttle and on foot. Distances are approximate.

  1. Start
    Haridwar / Rishikeshहरिद्वार / ऋषिकेश

    Registration counters, railheads and the start of the road journey.

  2. Road
    Rudraprayagरुद्रप्रयाग

    Where the Mandakini meets the Alaknanda; the road turns up the Mandakini valley.

  3. Road
    Guptkashiगुप्तकाशी

    Vishwanath temple; helipads at Guptkashi, Phata and Sersi lie along this stretch.

  4. Road
    Sonprayagसोनप्रयाग

    Parking; shared shuttles run about 5 km to Gaurikund.

  5. ~5 km
    Gaurikundगौरीकुंड

    The last motorable point and the start of the trek.

  6. ~16 km
    Kedarnathकेदारनाथ

    About 6–8 hours uphill on foot, or by pony, palki or pithoo.

Old "14 km" trek figures are from before 2013; the present path is about 16 km (IRCTC). Weather or landslides can pause the yatra for a few hours even in October.

How to reach

How to reach Kedarnath

By air

Jolly Grant Airport, Dehradun, is the nearest — about 240 km by road to Gaurikund.

By rail

Yog Nagari Rishikesh station is about 215 km from Gaurikund; Haridwar has more trains.

By road

Haridwar or Rishikesh → Rudraprayag → Guptkashi → Sonprayag, then the shuttle to Gaurikund.

By helicopter

Shuttles fly from Guptkashi, Phata and Sersi. Book only on heliyatra.irctc.co.in, after Char Dham registration — up to 2 tickets per user ID, 6 pilgrims per ticket.

Sample itinerary

A sample three-day Kedarnath yatra from Haridwar

  1. Day 1
    Haridwar to Guptkashi or Sonprayag

    Drive up via Rishikesh and Rudraprayag along the Alaknanda and Mandakini. Stop at Guptkashi's Vishwanath temple and rest early.

  2. Day 2
    Trek to Kedarnath

    Start early from Sonprayag, shuttle to Gaurikund and walk about 16 km (or fly from your helipad). Evening darshan and a night near the temple.

  3. Day 3
    Darshan and descent

    Early darshan, visit Bhairavnath temple and the Adi Shankaracharya Samadhi, then walk down to Gaurikund and drive back towards Guptkashi.

Before you go

Before you go

  1. Register first — it is freeRegistration is compulsory and free on registrationandtouristcare.uk.gov.in, the Tourist Care Uttarakhand app or helpline 1364, with offline counters at Haridwar and Rishikesh. Any site charging a "registration fee" is fake.
  2. Helicopter: only IRCTCBook only on heliyatra.irctc.co.in. Police have warned about cloned helicopter sites and WhatsApp sellers offering "confirmed seats" — ignore them. Fares change each season; see the official notice.
  3. Health check for eldersUttarakhand advises a check-up for anyone over 50 or with heart, lung or blood-pressure conditions. Acclimatise on the way, use the health kiosks, and come down at the first signs of altitude sickness.
  4. Ponies, palki and pithooAvailable from Gaurikund for those who cannot walk the full climb. Pay only the official rates at the counter.
  5. Best monthsMay–June and mid-September to October. July–August brings landslides and weather pauses, though the temple itself stays open.
  6. Pack warmNights are cold even in summer. Carry rain gear, warm layers and good walking shoes.
Shankaracharya to Bhim Shila

A short history of Kedarnath temple

According to tradition, Adi Shankaracharya revived the temple in the 8th century CE; the BKTC credits the present stone structure to him, beside an earlier Pandava-era shrine. His samadhi lies behind the temple, where a 12-ft statue of the saint was unveiled on 5 November 2021.

In the floods of 16–17 June 2013 the town was devastated but the temple survived: a large boulder behind it diverted the debris. Pilgrims now worship it as Bhim Shila, believed to have shielded the shrine.

FAQ

Questions pilgrims ask

When will Kedarnath close in 2026?

Not announced yet. By custom the temple closes on Bhai Dooj (around 10–11 November); the BKTC announces the muhurat on Vijayadashami, 20 October 2026. See our Char Dham closing dates 2026 post.

When will Kedarnath open in 2027?

Not announced yet. The date is fixed on Mahashivratri, 6 March 2027, at Ukhimath — usually it falls in late April or early May.

How do I book a Kedarnath helicopter?

Register for the Char Dham yatra first, then book only on heliyatra.irctc.co.in for Guptkashi, Phata or Sersi. Ignore WhatsApp or agent offers of "confirmed tickets".

How long is the Kedarnath trek?

About 16 km one way from Gaurikund, usually 6–8 hours uphill. Ponies, palki and pithoo are available for those who need them.

Is registration compulsory for Kedarnath?

Yes, and it is free — on registrationandtouristcare.uk.gov.in, the Tourist Care Uttarakhand app or helpline 1364.

How do I reach Kedarnath from Haridwar or Delhi?

Drive via Rishikesh, Rudraprayag and Guptkashi to Sonprayag, take the shuttle to Gaurikund, then trek or fly. The nearest airport is Dehradun and the railhead is Rishikesh.

What are Kedarnath darshan timings?

The BKTC does not publish daily hours: the doors open early in the morning and close at night, so confirm locally. Pujas are booked on the BKTC website.

Where is Kedarnath worshipped in winter?

At the Omkareshwar temple in Ukhimath, from the closing until the spring reopening.
