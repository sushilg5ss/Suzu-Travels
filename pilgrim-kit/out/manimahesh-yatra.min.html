What is the Manimahesh Yatra and when is it in 2027?

The Manimahesh Yatra is Himachal's pilgrimage to Manimahesh Lake, about 4,080 m up below the peak of Manimahesh Kailash in Bharmour, Chamba district. It is held in Bhadon (Aug–Sep), from Janmashtami to Radha Ashtami. The 2027 dates are not announced yet. Since 2026 every pilgrim needs an online e-pass, and the trek from Hadsar is about 13–14 km.

Manimahesh — the jewel of Mahesh — is a high glacial lake in the Bharmour subdivision of Chamba district, Himachal Pradesh. Above it rises Manimahesh Kailash, also called Chamba Kailash, which the Gaddi shepherds of these valleys revere as one of Shiva's homes. A small shikhara shrine stands on the shore with a brass image of Mahishasuramardini and a four-faced Shiva. The Himachal government has declared the yatra a state-level pilgrimage, and it is counted among the Panch Kailash with Kinner Kailash, Shrikhand, Adi Kailash and Mount Kailash.

Pilgrims take the nhaun, the holy dip, in the lake and walk around it. By custom men bathe in the larger, colder Shiv Karotri and women at Gauri Kund, a little short of the lake on the trail. Many pilgrims first pray at the centuries-old Chaurasi temples of Bharmour. If you are coming from the Kangra side, you can visit Chamunda Devi and Kangra Devi on the way. The legend

The legend of Manimahesh

These are the local traditions of the Gaddi people and of Chamba, retold in our own words — beliefs held with love, not history.

  1. Shiv BhumiThe Gaddi shepherds of Bharmour call these valleys Shiv Bhumi, Shiva's own land, and believe Manimahesh Kailash is one of his homes.
  2. A lake from his hairIt is said that Shiva meditated here for ages, and that the lake formed from water flowing out of his matted hair.
  3. Behind the waterfallAnother story says Shiva hid behind the Dhancho waterfall from the demon Bhasmasura, until Vishnu tricked the demon into destroying himself — so the falls too are held sacred.
  4. The jewel on the peakOn rare clear dawns or full-moon nights, a glow on the summit is believed to be the mani, the jewel on Shiva's crown. Some explain it as light reflecting off the glacier.

Gaddi belief also holds that Shiva leaves Kailash on Janmashtami and returns around Mahashivratri. Out of reverence, people avoid climbing the peak itself; no first ascent is recorded.

Route

The Bharmour route to Manimahesh Lake

Distances are approximate. Most pilgrims drive to Bharmour, continue to the road head at Hadsar and walk up via Dhancho.

  1. ~59 km
    Chamba to Bharmourभरमौर

    About 2.5 hours on a hill road. Pray at the Chaurasi temples, the ~84 shrines of Bharmour.

  2. ~13 km
    Bharmour to Hadsarहड़सर

    The road head and the e-pass check point. Ponies and porters start here.

  3. Halt
    Dhanchoधन्छो

    Most pilgrims spend the night here, by the sacred waterfall. Langars run in the season.

  4. ~13–14 km in all
    Gauri Kund to the lakeमणिमहेश झील

    Past Gauri Kund to Manimahesh Lake at about 4,080 m — the holy dip and the walk around the lake.

Other traditional routes come over high passes from Kugti (Lahaul side) and from the Kangra–Mandi side; the Kugti parikrama route was closed to most pilgrims in 2026 for safety, so check the current order.

How to reach

How to reach Manimahesh

By train

Pathankot is the nearest broad-gauge station. Chamba is about 125 km by road, and Bharmour about 184 km — 5 to 6 hours.

By air

Kangra (Gaggal) airport is about 200 km from Bharmour by road via Chamba. Pathankot airport has limited flights.

By road

Drive or take an HRTC bus to Chamba and Bharmour (about 59 km from Chamba), then 13 km on to Hadsar where the walk begins.

Ponies and helicopter

Ponies and porters work from Hadsar. A Bharmour–Gauri Kund helicopter has run in past seasons — check the district notice each year; there is no guarantee it runs.

Before you go

Before you go

  1. CarryYour e-pass printout and photo ID; a rain shell, warm layers, gloves and cap; grippy shoes and a stick; personal medicines and a torch.
  2. Altitude and fitnessThe lake is at about 4,080 m and the climb is steep. Walk slowly, drink water, and see your doctor first if you have heart, breathing or blood-pressure problems.
  3. EldersThis is a hard trek for elders. Plan a night at Dhancho, use a pony, and ask us about the helicopter only if the district has announced it for that year.
  4. WeatherThe route was hit by floods in 2025. Do not trek in heavy rain, never walk at night, and follow police and district advisories.
  5. Keep it cleanPlastic is banned on the route. Carry your waste back down and use the langars and camps responsibly.
  6. Fake sitesRegister only on manimaheshyatra.hp.gov.in. Never pay unknown sellers for passes or helicopter seats.
Compulsory since 2026

Manimahesh Yatra registration and e-pass

After cloudbursts and flash floods hit the route in 2025 and stranded more than 15,000 pilgrims, the district made online registration compulsory in 2026. Registration opened on 5 August 2026 on the official portal manimaheshyatra.hp.gov.in, and about 5,000 pilgrims a day were allowed beyond Hadsar — the cap did not apply on the two holy-bath days.

  1. Register online on the official Himachal portal once the Deputy Commissioner, Chamba announces the season (usually in July–August).
  2. Carry a printout of your e-pass and photo ID; they are checked on the route.
  3. Start early from Hadsar — night trekking is not allowed.

The 2027 rules are not announced yet; check the DC Chamba notice before you travel. Register only on manimaheshyatra.hp.gov.in and ignore anyone who sells a "confirmed pass". If a helicopter service runs, book it only through the operator the district notifies.

FAQ

Questions pilgrims ask

When is the Manimahesh Yatra in 2027?

The dates are not announced yet. The yatra follows the Janmashtami-to-Radha Ashtami pattern, and Radha Ashtami falls on 8 September 2027 by panchang. In 2026 it began on 25 August and ended with the royal bath on 19 September.

Is registration compulsory for the Manimahesh Yatra?

Yes. Since 2026 every pilgrim needs an online e-pass from the official portal, manimaheshyatra.hp.gov.in. In 2026 about 5,000 pilgrims a day were allowed beyond Hadsar.

How long is the Manimahesh trek?

About 13–14 km one way from Hadsar, via Dhancho and Gauri Kund. Most pilgrims spend a night at Dhancho.

How high is Manimahesh Lake?

About 4,080 m above sea level. The Manimahesh Kailash peak above it is about 5,650 m.

How do I reach Manimahesh?

Take a train to Pathankot or fly to Kangra (Gaggal), drive to Chamba and on to Bharmour (about 59 km), then 13 km to Hadsar, where the trek starts.

Is there a helicopter for Manimahesh?

A Bharmour–Gauri Kund helicopter has run in past seasons, but it is not guaranteed. Check the Chamba district notice each year and book only with the notified operator.

What are the Chhota and Bada Nhaun?

The two holy baths in the lake: the small bath (Chhota Nhaun) on Janmashtami, and the royal bath (Bada or Shahi Nhaun) on Radha Ashtami, about 15 days later.

Can I visit Manimahesh outside the yatra?

The lake is frozen roughly from October to June. Outside the official window it is a trek only, with guides and local permission.
