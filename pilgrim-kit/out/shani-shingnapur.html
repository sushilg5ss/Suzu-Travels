Where is Shani Shingnapur and what makes it special?

Shani Shingnapur is a village in Nevasa taluka of Ahilyanagar (formerly Ahmednagar) district, Maharashtra, about 70–80 km from Shirdi. Shani Dev is worshipped here as a self-manifested black stone, about 5.5 ft high, on an open-air platform with no roof or walls. Local tradition says he protects the village, so its houses traditionally have door frames but no doors.

The shrine is run by the Shri Shaneshwar Devasthan, Shani Shingnapur, near Ghodegaon on the Ahilyanagar–Chhatrapati Sambhajinagar road. There is no temple building around the deity: Shani Dev stands as a dark stone on a raised platform, under the open sky, and devotees pour oil over it as their offering. Saturdays, and above all Shani Amavasya (a new moon that falls on a Saturday), bring the biggest crowds.

Many pilgrims come here after darshan in Shirdi, and some go on to the Jyotirlingas of Grishneshwar near Ellora or Trimbakeshwar near Nashik. See all our pilgrimage guides.

The legend

The story and history of Shani Shingnapur temple

Told in our own words, as the village tells it. This is a local tradition — no scripture names Shingnapur, and the details vary from teller to teller.

  1. A black stone comesAccording to local tradition, a large black slab once came to rest near the village — many versions say it was carried down by a flood in the local stream.
  2. The stone that bledWhen a shepherd touched it with a pointed stick, the stone is said to have bled. The villagers gathered, unsure what they had found.
  3. A dream under the open skyThat night Shani Dev appeared in a dream to the village elder: the stone was his own form. He asked to be installed in the village, but without a roof — the whole sky is his canopy.
  4. A village without doorsHe promised to protect Shingnapur. And so, the story goes, the village's houses traditionally have door frames but no doors.

Today the open-air platform, the chauthara, is still the heart of the shrine. For centuries only men climbed it; after a Bombay High Court ruling in 2016, the Devasthan opened the platform to women on 8 April 2016 (Gudi Padwa), and today men and women both worship there. The "no doors" custom is a living tradition and a matter of faith — it is not a claim that the village has never known crime.

How to reach

How to reach Shani Shingnapur (and the distance from Shirdi)

Shani Shingnapur to Shirdi

About 70–80 km by road, roughly 1.5–2 hours. Most pilgrims combine the two in a day or two.

By air

Shirdi airport is about 64 km away; Chhatrapati Sambhajinagar (Aurangabad) airport about 88 km (1.5–2 hours).

By train

Rahuri station is about 25 km away and Ahilyanagar (Ahmednagar) station about 39 km (around 45 minutes).

By road

Ghodegaon is about 10 km away. Pune is about 160 km (3.5–4 hours) and Mumbai about 290 km (around 6 hours). Buses and shared taxis run from Shirdi and Ahilyanagar.

Before you go

Before you go

  1. Go on a weekday morningSaturdays and Shani Amavasya draw huge crowds. A weekday morning is calmer, especially with elders.
  2. Bring the right oilOnly sealed, ISI-marked edible oil is accepted for the abhishek.
  3. Puja only at the templeNo app or website is authorised for online puja — ignore such offers.
  4. Elders and accessThe campus is open and flat; for wheelchair or other help, ask at the Devasthan counter.
Oil, platform and online puja

Oil abhishek, platform customs and fake puja sites

The main offering at Shani Shingnapur is oil poured over the stone. From 1 March 2025 the Devasthan accepts only branded, ISI-marked edible oil in sealed packs, because loose and non-edible oils were said to be damaging the stone. Carry a sealed pack or buy one near the temple.

Traditionally, devotees who go up on the platform bathe first and go in wet clothes with the head uncovered, using water from the temple well. Customs can change, so ask the temple staff for today's rule before you climb.

Beware of online puja offers. The Devasthan says it has not authorised any app or website to book or perform puja or abhishek on your behalf. Do your puja in person at the temple, and never pay for "online Shani puja" over WhatsApp or UPI.

Questions pilgrims ask

What are Shani Shingnapur temple timings?

About 4 am to 10:30 pm every day, according to the Devasthan's posted hours as quoted in 2026. Some older guides say the shrine is open round the clock, so confirm locally.

What are the aarti timings at Shani Shingnapur?

About 4:30 am, 12 noon and at sunset. Times can change on festival days, so check at the temple.

Can women go on the Shani platform?

Yes. Since 8 April 2016 women worship on the platform alongside men.

How far is Shani Shingnapur from Shirdi?

About 70–80 km by road, roughly 1.5–2 hours. Many pilgrims visit Shani Shingnapur on the morning after their Shirdi darshan.

Why are there no doors in Shani Shingnapur?

Local tradition says Shani Dev protects the village, so its houses traditionally have door frames but no doors. It is a matter of faith and custom.

Which oil can I offer to Shani Dev here?

Since 1 March 2025 only packaged, ISI-marked edible oil is accepted for the abhishek. Loose or non-edible oil is turned away.

Can I book Shani Shingnapur puja online?

No. The Devasthan says it has not authorised any app or website for online puja or abhishek — do your puja in person at the temple.

When is Shani Jayanti 2027?

Friday, 4 June 2027 (Jyeshtha Amavasya). Expect very large crowds that day.
