Where is Baglamukhi temple in Kangra and why do people visit?

Baglamukhi temple stands at Bankhandi, near Dehra in Kangra district, Himachal Pradesh, on the Chandigarh–Dharamshala highway (NH503) — about 24 km from Jwala Ji. Devotees come to the yellow-robed goddess, the eighth Mahavidya, for her protection, offering yellow flowers and sweets and performing havans. Darshan is generally about 5 am – 9:30 pm.

Maa Baglamukhi — also written Bagalamukhi, and called Pitambara Maa, the goddess clad in yellow — is the eighth of the ten Mahavidyas, the wisdom goddesses of the Shakta tradition. She is linked with stambhana, the power to halt and to silence: devotees ask her to still false speech, quarrels and obstacles. She is often shown holding a club. At Bankhandi her sanctum is bright yellow, and shrines of Shiva, Bhairava and Hanuman stand close by.

Not the Datia or Nalkheda temple. India has three famous Baglamukhi shrines: this one at Bankhandi in Kangra, Himachal, the Pitambara Peeth at Datia and the temple at Nalkheda — both in Madhya Pradesh. There is also a smaller Baglamukhi temple in Una district. Bankhandi is revered as a Siddha Peeth, but it is not one of the classical 51 Shakti Peeths; many families still visit it on the Himachal Devi circuit, between Jwala Ji, Kangra Devi and Chintpurni.

The legend

The story of Baglamukhi temple, Kangra

Retold in our own words from the traditions the Kangra district and temple keep — told as belief, not history.

  1. A storm over the worldLong ago, it is said, a terrible storm rose that threatened to destroy all of creation, and even the gods could not calm it.
  2. The lake of turmericAt Vishnu's prayer the goddess rose from a lake of turmeric, dressed in yellow, and stilled the storm. That is why everything at her temple is yellow.
  3. Rama's havanLocal tradition holds that Rama, guided by Hanuman, worshipped Baglamukhi here before the war with Ravana and received the Brahmastra. A havan kund is pointed out as the place of his havan.
  4. Raised in one nightAnother local belief says the Pandavas built a temple here in a single night during their exile. Devotees still come to her for protection and strength.

The recorded story is younger. The present shrine is believed to have been built in the early 1800s by the former rulers of Guler, devotees of the goddess, after they lost Kotla fort to Maharaja Ranjit Singh (The Tribune, 2026). Today it is listed on the Himachal government's Kangra Temples portal and draws its biggest crowds on Baglamukhi Jayanti and in Navratri.

How to reach

How to reach Baglamukhi temple, Bankhandi

By road

The temple is right on NH503 (Chandigarh–Dharamshala). About 16 km from Dehra, about 23 km from Kangra town, about 24 km from Jwala Ji and about 36 km from Chintpurni. Buses and taxis run from Kangra, Dharamshala and Pathankot.

By air

Gaggal (Kangra) airport is about 35–40 km by road.

By rail

Kangra Mandir on the narrow-gauge Pathankot–Joginder Nagar line is about 25–40 km away (confirm the route). The nearest broad-gauge station is Pathankot, about 98 km.

From our office

Ghumarwin, where our office is, is about 102 km away — an easy day on a Himachal Devi yatra.

Before you go

Before you go

  1. Wear something yellowNot required, but most devotees do — and yellow flowers and besan laddoos are the usual offering.
  2. Havans through the templeIf you want a havan, book it only at the temple counter. Be wary of anyone promising results or quoting prices on the roadside or online.
  3. Beat the crowdsJayanti and Navratri bring very long queues. A weekday morning is the calmest darshan, especially with elders.
  4. Easy for eldersIt is a roadside temple on the highway with a short walk from parking — one of the easier Devi darshans in Kangra.

FAQ

Questions pilgrims ask

Where is Baglamukhi temple in Himachal?

At Bankhandi near Dehra in Kangra district, right on the Chandigarh–Dharamshala highway (NH503) — about 23 km from Kangra town.

What are Baglamukhi temple Kangra timings?

Generally about 5 am to 9:30 pm, according to the Kangra district site. Aarti times are not published officially, so please confirm locally.

Why is everything yellow at Baglamukhi temple?

Yellow — pitambara — is the goddess's colour. In tradition she rose from a lake of turmeric dressed in yellow, so devotees wear yellow and offer yellow flowers, turmeric and besan laddoos.

Why do people perform havan at Baglamukhi?

Devotees perform havans, often at night, seeking the goddess's protection and success in disputes and court cases. It is a matter of faith; book a havan only through the temple.

Is Baglamukhi Kangra a Shakti Peeth?

It is revered as a Siddha Peeth of one of the ten Mahavidyas, but it is not in the classical 51 Shakti Peeth lists. It is also not one of the traditional nine Devis, though many 9-Devi trips add it.

How far is Baglamukhi temple from Jwala Ji?

About 24 km by road. Kangra town and Brajeshwari (Kangra Devi) are about 23 km away and Chintpurni about 36 km.

Which are the three main Baglamukhi temples in India?

Bankhandi in Kangra (Himachal Pradesh), the Pitambara Peeth at Datia and the temple at Nalkheda — both in Madhya Pradesh.

When is Baglamukhi Jayanti?

On Vaishakh Shukla Ashtami, in April–May; in 2026 it fell in late April. Check the 2027 date with the temple or a panchang.
