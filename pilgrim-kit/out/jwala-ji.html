Where is Jwalamukhi temple and why is it sacred?

Shri Jwalamukhi (Jwala Ji) stands in Jawalamukhi town, Kangra district, Himachal Pradesh, about 30 km from Kangra and 31 km from Chintpurni. It is one of the Shakti Peeths of Himachal: tradition says Sati's tongue fell here. There is no idol — small natural flames rising from the rock, usually counted as nine, are worshipped as the Goddess.

Most temples hold an image of the deity. Jwalamukhi holds fire. Inside the sanctum, small flames rise from fissures in the rock and are worshipped directly as Jwala, the Goddess in her form as flame — usually counted as nine. Pilgrims come for a glimpse of the jyoti, offer prasad and leave with the feeling of having stood before something alive.

Jwala Ji is the third stop on the five Shakti Peeths of Himachal circuit, after Naina Devi and Chintpurni, and before Brajeshwari (Kangra Devi) and Chamunda. It is also one of the nine shrines of the Nau Devi Yatra. Please note that this is the Kangra temple — other shrines called Jwalamukhi or Jwala Mai (in Uttar Pradesh, or at Muktinath in Nepal) are different places.

The legend

The story of Jwala Ji

Shakta tradition and local lore tell of a fallen tongue, a mysterious girl and an emperor's offering. This is how the stories are told.

  1. Sati's tongue fallsWhen Sati gave up her body at Daksha's yagna and Shiva carried her through the worlds in grief, Vishnu's chakra divided her body. Tradition says her tongue fell in these Kangra hills.
  2. Flame, not stoneHere the Goddess is believed to show herself not as a statue but as living flame, rising from the rock itself.
  3. The cowherd and the girlA cowherd is said to have followed a mysterious girl who drank his cow's milk. Later a king, guided by a dream, found the fire burning on the hillside and built the first shrine.
  4. Akbar's chhatraA famous later legend says the Mughal emperor Akbar tried to put out the flames with water and an iron cover, failed, and offered a golden chhatra (canopy) — which, devotees say, turned into a strange, unknown metal.

These are stories of faith, told as tradition rather than history. There is also a scientific account of the flames, which we give below — many pilgrims find that knowing both only deepens the wonder.

The temple is open all year. The best months to visit are March–June and September–November; roads can be slippery in the monsoon. Navratri brings the biggest crowds — elders and small children are more comfortable on a weekday outside the fairs.

How to reach

How to reach Jwalamukhi temple

By air

Gaggal (Kangra/Dharamshala) airport is about 46–50 km away. Chandigarh (about 200 km) has more flights, and a Chandigarh airport taxi can drive you on from there; Shimla is about 160 km.

By rail

The narrow-gauge Kangra Valley Railway stops at Jawalamukhi Road (Ranital), about 20 km away. The nearest broad-gauge station is Pathankot, about 120–140 km.

By road

About 30–34 km from Kangra, 54–56 km from Dharamshala, 31 km from Chintpurni and about 480 km from Delhi. From Ghumarwin (Bilaspur) it is about 80 km.

The last stretch

From the town bazaar a short climb of steps leads up to the temple. No ropeway, pony or palki service is published — confirm locally if anyone in your group needs help with steps.

Before you go

Before you go

  1. Go early in NavratriQueues build from dawn during the Navratri fairs. Keep bags small, carry water and keep children close in the crowd.
  2. Respect the sanctumModest clothes, shoes off at the gate, and no photography inside the sanctum. Follow the temple's notices.
  3. Where to stayJawalamukhi town has simple hotels, guest houses and dharamshalas; Kangra and Dharamshala have wider choice. Book early for Navratri — or ask us on WhatsApp.
  4. Elders and stepsThere are steps up to the sanctum and long queues in Navratri. Facilities for wheelchairs are not published, so confirm locally.
  5. See the other shrinesTara Devi, Ashtabhuja and Bhairav temples are in the same complex and town.
  6. Combine the DevisChintpurni is about 31 km away and Brajeshwari (Kangra Devi) about 36 km — easy to join in a day or two.
Faith and geology

Why do the flames at Jwala Ji keep burning?

For devotees, the flame is the Devi herself, and that is the heart of the visit. Geologists explain the flames as natural combustible gas seeping up through fissures in the rock. The question is old: Abul Fazl's Ain-i-Akbari put the fire down to a sulphur mine, and in 1957 ONGC began drilling deep exploratory wells near Jawalamukhi — the first showed some gas pressure and the second was dry, as a Lok Sabha answer of March 1964 records.

We share both views side by side, with respect. The flames are neither "only a gas leak" nor "beyond any explanation" — for millions of pilgrims they are where faith and the earth's own fire meet.

FAQ

Questions pilgrims ask

Why does the flame at Jwala Ji never go out?

Devotees see the flame as the Goddess herself. Geologically, the flames are natural gas seeping up through fissures in the rock; ONGC drilled exploratory wells near Jawalamukhi from 1957. We share both views with respect.

Is there an idol in Jwalamukhi temple?

No. There is no idol — the small natural flames rising from the rock, usually counted as nine, are worshipped directly as the Goddess Jwala.

Which part of Sati fell at Jwala Ji?

Her tongue, according to the Shakti Peeth tradition. That is why Jwalamukhi is counted among the classical Shakti Peeths and the five Shakti Peeths of Himachal.

What are Jwalamukhi temple timings?

Roughly early morning to about 10 pm with a short midday break, but the official pages differ, and hours change in Navratri. Please confirm locally before you go.

What is the Akbar story at Jwalamukhi?

Legend says the Mughal emperor Akbar tried to douse the flames with water and an iron cover, failed, and offered a golden chhatra (canopy) that devotees say turned into an unknown metal. It is told as devotional legend.

How to reach Jwala Ji?

The nearest airport is Gaggal (Kangra), about 46–50 km away. The narrow-gauge Jawalamukhi Road (Ranital) station is about 20 km away and the broad-gauge Pathankot station about 120–140 km. By road it is about 30 km from Kangra.

How far is Jwala Ji from Chintpurni and Kangra?

About 31 km by road from Chintpurni and about 36 km from Brajeshwari (Kangra Devi) temple.
