Where is Chamunda Devi temple and why is it sacred?

Chamunda Devi temple (Shri Chamunda Nandikeshwar Dham) stands at Padar on the Baner river in Kangra district, Himachal Pradesh, about 15 km from Dharamshala on the Palampur road. It honours Chamunda, the fierce form of the Devi who slew Chanda and Munda, with Shiva as Nandikeshwar in a cave behind. It is one of Himachal's five great Devi shrines.

Mata Chamunda's temple sits low in the valley, beside the rushing Baner river, with the snow wall of the Dhauladhar rising behind it. The sanctum holds the Devi, draped mostly in red, with Hanuman and Bhairav on either side of the door. Behind the temple, a short flight of marble steps leads down to a cave where Shiva is worshipped as Nandikeshwar in a natural linga — which is why the shrine is called Chamunda Nandikeshwar Dham, a place where Shiva and Shakti are honoured together.

Chamunda is the last stop of the five Shakti Peeths of Himachal circuit, after Naina Devi, Chintpurni, Jwala Ji and Brajeshwari, and one of the shrines of the Nau Devi Yatra. Local lore counts it a Shakti Peeth, though it is not in the classical 51 or 52 lists. Please note: this guide is about the Kangra temple — Chamundeshwari on Chamundi Hills (Mysuru), Chamunda Mata at Mehrangarh (Jodhpur) and Chamunda Tekri (Dewas) are different shrines.

The legend

The story of Chamunda Devi

The Devi Mahatmya tells how the Goddess got her name, and Kangra remembers how she came down from the snows. This is how the stories are told.

  1. A power from the browIn the Devi's war against the asuras Shumbha and Nishumbha, their generals Chanda and Munda marched against her. It is said that from her frowning brow sprang a dark, fearsome warrior goddess.
  2. Chanda and Munda fallThe warrior goddess overcame both generals, and the Devi named her Chamunda after them. Some retellings link this fierce form with Kali.
  3. A priest's dreamIn Kangra, tradition says that about four centuries ago a king and a priest prayed for leave to bring the Devi down from her remote, snowbound hilltop shrine. She showed the priest in a dream where her old image lay buried.
  4. Down to the riverThe king's men could not move the image, but the priest, after bathing and coming in clean clothes with reverence, lifted it alone — and the shrine was set up here by the Baner river.

The story comes from the Devi Mahatmya (Durga Saptashati, chapter 7) in the Markandeya Purana, and from Kangra's own temple tradition. We retell it in our own words, as faith and tradition rather than history.

How to reach

How to reach Chamunda Devi temple

By air

Kangra Airport at Gaggal (Dharamshala) is about 25 km away, with flights to Delhi and Chandigarh.

By rail

The narrow-gauge Kangra Valley Railway stops at Kangra, about 14 km away. The nearest broad-gauge station is Pathankot, about 90 km.

By road

The temple is on the Dharamshala–Palampur road: about 15 km from Dharamshala, 19 km from Palampur and 24 km from McLeod Ganj. HRTC and private buses and taxis run from all three. From our office at Ghumarwin it is about 113 km.

At the temple

The road reaches the gate and the temple stands on level ground by the river; the steps down to the Nandikeshwar cave are short marble stairs.

Before you go

Before you go

  1. Go early in NavratriQueues build from early morning during the Navratri fairs. Keep bags small and children close.
  2. Plan around bhogThe sanctum closes from 12 noon to 1 pm for bhog. The 8 am and 7:10 pm aartis are the loveliest times to be there.
  3. Respect the shrineModest clothes, shoes off at the gate, and follow the temple staff on photography.
  4. Good for eldersRoad to the gate, parking and a level temple by the river make Chamunda one of the easier Devi darshans. Save the Aadi Himani trek for the fit.
  5. Book only on the official siteDarshan slips and prasad are offered online only on kangratemples.hp.gov.in. Don't pay agents who claim special darshan.
  6. Combine the DevisBrajeshwari (Kangra Devi) is about 22 km away; Dharamshala, McLeod Ganj and the Palampur tea gardens are close by.
The original shrine

Aadi Himani Chamunda: the trek to the Devi's first seat

High on the Chandar Bhan ridge above the valley stands Aadi Himani Chamunda, the hilltop shrine that tradition calls the Devi's original seat — the snowbound temple from the legend. It sits at about 3,000 m (sources differ) and is reached by a trek of about 9–13 km one way, roughly 5–7 hours, from Jia village or from Jadrangal / Kardiana, the last points a vehicle can reach.

The trail is rated moderate to challenging. The shrine is open from about 15 March to 15 November and closed in winter snow; April–June and September–October are the best months. The old temple was damaged by fire in 2014 and has since been rebuilt, and there is a basic rest house. This trek is not suitable for elders or anyone with heart or knee problems — the main temple by the river is the easy darshan.

FAQ

Questions pilgrims ask

What are Chamunda Devi temple timings?

In summer 5 am to 10 pm and in winter 6 am to 9 pm, closed from 12 noon to 1 pm for bhog. Aarti is at about 8 am and 7:10 pm. These are the official trust timings; confirm the seasonal changeover locally.

How far is Chamunda Devi from Dharamshala?

About 15 km on the Dharamshala–Palampur road. Palampur is about 19 km and McLeod Ganj about 24 km away.

Is Chamunda Devi a Shakti Peeth?

It is not in the classical 51 or 52 Shakti Peeth lists, though local lore counts it as one. It is revered as one of Himachal's five great Devi shrines, with Naina Devi, Chintpurni, Jwala Ji and Brajeshwari.

Why is the Goddess called Chamunda?

In the Devi Mahatmya, a fierce form of the Devi slew the asura generals Chanda and Munda, and the Goddess named her Chamunda after them.

Why is it called Nandikeshwar Dham?

Because Shiva is worshipped as Nandikeshwar in a cave shrine with a natural linga behind the temple — Shiva and Shakti are honoured together.

What is Aadi Himani Chamunda?

The original hilltop shrine at about 3,000 m on the Chandar Bhan ridge, reached by a 9–13 km trek from Jia village or Jadrangal. It is open from about mid-March to mid-November.

What is the nearest airport and railway station?

Kangra (Gaggal) airport is about 25 km away. Kangra station on the narrow-gauge line is about 14 km; Pathankot, about 90 km, is the nearest broad-gauge station.

Is there free food at Chamunda temple?

Yes. The temple trust runs a free langar every day from 12 to 2 pm and 7 to 9 pm, and all day during Navratri.
