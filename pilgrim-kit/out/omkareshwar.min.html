Where is Omkareshwar Jyotirlinga and why is it sacred?

Shri Omkareshwar temple stands on Mandhata island in the Narmada, in Khandwa district, Madhya Pradesh — about 77 km from Indore airport. It is the fourth of the 12 Jyotirlingas, where Shiva is worshipped as the Lord of Om. Tradition treats Omkareshwar and Mamleshwar on the south bank as one Jyotirlinga, so pilgrims visit both. Darshan runs from 5 am to 10:30 pm.

Omkareshwar is a temple town on the Narmada, where the river wraps around Mandhata (Shivapuri) island near its meeting with the Kaveri. The island is believed to resemble the sacred syllable Om — and so Shiva here is Omkareshwar, the Lord of Omkara. Pilgrims cross by footbridge or boat, take darshan in the island temple, then visit Mamleshwar (also called Amaleshwar) on the south bank. The temple is open all year and is managed by the Shri Omkareshwar Mandir Trust.

Two things are easy to confuse: the Omkareshwar temple at Ukhimath in Uttarakhand is Kedarnath's winter seat, and the small Omkareshwar shrine on the middle level of Mahakaleshwar temple in Ujjain is also a different shrine. The Jyotirlinga is the one on the Narmada.

The legend

The legend of Omkareshwar

The Koti Rudra Samhita of the Shiva Purana tells how Shiva came to dwell on the Narmada in two forms. This is how tradition tells it.

  1. Vindhya's wounded prideThe sage Narada once praised Mount Meru in front of the Vindhya mountain. Hurt, Vindhya longed for a greatness of his own.
  2. Penance by the NarmadaThe mountain went to the banks of the Narmada, shaped a linga of earth and worshipped Shiva with long, single-minded devotion.
  3. Shiva in two formsIt is believed Shiva appeared and granted Vindhya's wish — and, at the request of the gods and sages, agreed to remain here as Omkareshwar and Amaleshwar.
  4. The island of OmBecause the island, circled by the Narmada, is said to resemble the syllable Om, Shiva here is worshipped as the Lord of Omkara.

Another tradition remembers Mandhata, a king of the Ikshvaku line, whose devotion gave the island its name. According to tradition, Adi Shankaracharya met his guru Govinda Bhagavatpada here. These are stories of faith, told as tradition rather than history. The Shiva Purana names the shrine "ओङ्कारे परमेश्वरम्", while the popular Dwadasha Jyotirlinga smaranam says "ओङ्कारममलेश्वरम्" — Omkareshwar and Amaleshwar together.

The temple is open all year. October to March is the most comfortable season; in the monsoon (July–September) a high Narmada can affect boats and ghats. Mondays, Shravan and Mahashivratri bring the biggest crowds.

How to reach

How to reach Omkareshwar

By air

Devi Ahilyabai Holkar Airport, Indore, is about 77 km away; taxis run from the airport and city.

By rail

Sanawad station is about 12 km away; Khandwa Junction, with more trains, is about 72 km.

By road

Taxis run from Indore and Khandwa. Mortakka bus stand, about 12 km away, has buses to Indore, Ujjain, Khargone and Khandwa.

From Ujjain

Mahakaleshwar in Ujjain is about 140 km away — most pilgrims pair the two Jyotirlingas.

Before you go

Before you go

  1. Book Shighra Darshan only on the Trust siteThe paid fast darshan is sold only on shriomkareshwar.org, on the same day. Carry the printed ticket and a photo ID. Ignore anyone selling "VIP darshan" passes.
  2. See both shrinesPlan time for Mamleshwar on the south bank as well as the island temple — tradition counts them as one Jyotirlinga.
  3. Narmada Aarti at duskThe evening aarti at Kotitirth Ghat is free to watch; time it for dusk and confirm locally.
  4. OfferingsBilva leaves, flowers or coconut may at times not be allowed inside the garbhagriha — follow the staff's instructions.
  5. EldersThe island has steps, ghats and crowded lanes. Ask the Trust helpline about wheelchair help before you travel.
  6. Walk the parikramaThe Omkar Parikrama circles Mandhata island over a few kilometres, past the Narmada–Kaveri sangam and Ekatma Dham — confirm the route locally.
One Jyotirlinga, two shrines

Omkareshwar or Mamleshwar — which is the Jyotirlinga?

Both. Tradition and the Dwadasha Jyotirlinga stotra treat Omkareshwar on Mandhata island and Mamleshwar (Amaleshwar) on the south bank of the Narmada as one Jyotirlinga in two aspects. The Shiva Purana's list says "Omkare Parameshwaram"; the smaranam says "Omkaram Amaleshwaram".

Pilgrims therefore take darshan at both. Mamleshwar is about 1 km from the island temple, across the river by footbridge or boat.

Paramaras to the Holkars

A short history of Omkareshwar temple

The temple was built in the 11th century under the Paramara kings of Malwa, and later came under Chauhan rulers. In the 18th century the Holkars rebuilt it — the work was begun under Gautama Bai Holkar and completed by Ahilyabai Holkar.

On 21 September 2023 a 108-ft "Statue of Oneness" of Adi Shankaracharya was unveiled at Ekatma Dham on Mandhata island.

FAQ

Questions pilgrims ask

What are Omkareshwar darshan timings?

Darshan runs from 5 am to 10:30 pm with breaks for bhog and shringar. Mangal Aarti is at 4:30–5 am and Shayan Aarti at 9:30–10 pm, according to the Trust time table.

Is Omkareshwar or Mamleshwar the real Jyotirlinga?

Tradition treats them as one Jyotirlinga in two forms — Omkareshwar on Mandhata island and Mamleshwar (Amaleshwar) on the south bank. Pilgrims visit both.

How do I book Shighra (VIP) darshan at Omkareshwar?

Only on shriomkareshwar.org, on the same day, for a 2-hour slot. Carry the printed ticket and photo ID. Normal darshan is free.

How do I reach Omkareshwar from Indore?

It is about 77 km by taxi or bus. The nearest railway station is Sanawad (12 km); the bigger junction is Khandwa (72 km).

How far is Omkareshwar from Mahakaleshwar, Ujjain?

About 140 km by road, according to the Shri Omkareshwar Mandir Trust. Many pilgrims visit both Jyotirlingas in two days.

Is there a dress code at Omkareshwar temple?

There is no official dress code, but modest clothing is recommended.

Why is it called Omkareshwar?

The Narmada wraps Mandhata island in a shape believed to resemble the syllable Om, so Shiva here is worshipped as the Lord of Omkara.

What is the Statue of Oneness at Omkareshwar?

A 108-ft statue of Adi Shankaracharya at Ekatma Dham on Mandhata island, unveiled on 21 September 2023.
