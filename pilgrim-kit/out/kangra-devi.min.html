Where is Kangra Devi temple and why is it sacred?

Kangra Devi temple — Shri Brajeshwari (Bajreshwari) Devi — stands in the Nagarkot area of Kangra town, Himachal Pradesh, near Kangra Fort and about 8 km from Kangra (Gaggal) airport. It is a Shakti Peeth: tradition holds that Sati's left breast fell here. The Devi is worshipped as a pindi, and every Makar Sankranti it is covered in butter.

Kangra Devi's temple sits in the bazaar lanes of Kangra town, behind a stone wall that makes the complex look like a small fort, with a Nagarkhana (drum house) over the main gate. Inside, the Devi is worshipped as Vajreshwari — the Durga who wields the thunderbolt (vajra) — in the form of a pindi. A small Bhairav temple stands in the courtyard, and an image of Dhyanu Bhagat, the devotee who by tradition offered his head to the Devi in Akbar's time, faces the main shrine. Pilgrims know her by many names: Brajeshwari, Bajreshwari, Vajreshwari, Kangra Devi and Kangrewali Mata — all one temple.

Brajeshwari is one of the five Shakti Peeths of Himachal, with Naina Devi, Chintpurni, Jwala Ji and Chamunda, and a stop on the Nau Devi Yatra. Unlike some Himachal Devi shrines, it does appear in the classical lists of 51 Shakti Peeths. Please note: this guide is about the Kangra temple — the Vajreshwari temple near Mumbai, in Maharashtra, is a different shrine.

The legend

The story of Kangra Devi

The Shakta tradition tells how Nagarkot became a seat of the Devi, and Kangra keeps its own memories of the Goddess. This is how the stories are told.

  1. Shiva's griefAfter Sati gave up her life at her father Daksha's yajna, Shiva wandered the world carrying her body. To end his grief, Vishnu's Sudarshan chakra divided it, and wherever a part fell a Shakti Peeth arose.
  2. NagarkotAt Nagarkot, the old name of Kangra, Sati's left breast is believed to have fallen — and the Devi has been worshipped here ever since.
  3. The thunderboltLocal tradition says the Devi appeared to the Pandavas in a dream and asked for a shrine at Nagarkot, and that she struck down a demon with a thunderbolt — which is why she is called Vajreshwari.
  4. Wounds soothed with gheeIt is believed that after the Devi defeated Mahishasura, her battle wounds were soothed with ghee. Each Makar Sankranti the priests remember this by covering her pindi in butter.

These stories come from the Devi Bhagavata and the wider Shakta tradition, and from the Kangra temple's own lore. We retell them in our own words, as faith and tradition rather than history.

How to reach

How to reach Kangra Devi temple

By air

Kangra Airport at Gaggal is only about 8 km away. Chandigarh airport has more flights and is a longer drive.

By rail

The narrow-gauge Kangra Valley Railway stops at Kangra station, a short drive from the temple. The nearest broad-gauge station is Pathankot, about 90 km.

By road

Kangra town is on the Pathankot–Mandi highway, about 20 km from Dharamshala, 36 km from Palampur and 30 km from McLeod Ganj. HRTC buses and taxis run from Dharamshala, Pathankot and Chandigarh. From our office at Ghumarwin it is about 115 km.

At the temple

The temple is in Kangra's bazaar lanes, so the last stretch is a short walk on foot. There is no steep climb, which makes it an easier darshan for elders.

Before you go

Before you go

  1. Plan around bhogThe sanctum closes from 12 noon to 12:30 pm for bhog. The early mangal aarti and the evening aarti are the loveliest times to be there.
  2. Expect festival crowdsIn Navratri and at Makar Sankranti the queues are long. Go early, keep bags small and children close.
  3. Respect the shrineModest clothes, shoes off at the gate, and follow the temple staff on photography.
  4. Book only on the official siteOnline darshan slips and havan booking go only through kangratemples.hp.gov.in and the temple office. Don't pay agents who promise special darshan.
  5. Walk to Kangra FortKangra Fort is close to the temple; the Tara Devi shrine and Achhra Kund are in or near the complex.
  6. Combine the DevisChamunda is about 22 km away and Jwala Ji about 35 km — both fit easily into a two-day Kangra trip.
Makar Sankranti

Ghrit Mandal Parv: when Brajeshwari Devi is clad in butter

Kangra Devi's best-known festival is the Ghrit Mandal Parv at Makar Sankranti in mid-January. The priests cover the Devi's pindi in butter offered by devotees — hundreds of kilos, about two tonnes in recent years. The butter stays on the pindi for about a week, with a jagran and a Shobha Yatra through the Kangra bazaar, and is then shared out as prasad; it is believed to help skin and joint ailments.

Makar Sankranti falls on Friday 15 January 2027. The temple's 2027 programme is not announced yet — plan for winter cold and festival crowds, and arrive early in the day.

Nagarkot through the centuries

History of the Brajeshwari temple

Nagarkot's temple was once famed for its wealth. Mahmud of Ghazni plundered it from 1009 CE, Firoz Shah Tughlaq damaged it again in 1360, and Akbar later restored it. The 1905 Kangra earthquake brought the temple down; it was rebuilt by devotees in the years after and completed around 1920, with the fort-like stone wall and Nagarkhana you see today.

The Himachal government is preparing a master plan for the shrine, and further upgrades — including more online services — are planned.

FAQ

Questions pilgrims ask

What are Kangra Devi temple timings?

Per the temple trust, in summer 5 am to 9 pm and in winter 5:30 am to 8 pm, closed from 12 noon to 12:30 pm for bhog. Confirm the seasonal changeover and festival-day hours locally.

Is Kangra Devi a Shakti Peeth?

Yes. Tradition holds that Sati's left breast fell here at Nagarkot, and the temple appears in the classical 51 Shakti Peeth lists.

Is Brajeshwari the same as Kangra Devi?

Yes. Brajeshwari, Bajreshwari, Vajreshwari, Kangra Devi and Kangrewali Mata are names of the same temple in Kangra town. It is different from the Vajreshwari temple near Mumbai.

What is the butter festival at Kangra Devi?

The Ghrit Mandal Parv at Makar Sankranti (15 January 2027): the Devi's pindi is covered in butter for about a week and then shared as prasad. The 2027 programme is not announced yet.

How far is Kangra Devi from Jwala Ji and Chamunda?

About 35 km to Jwala Ji and about 22 km to Chamunda. Chintpurni is about 70 km and Naina Devi about 175 km.

What is the nearest airport and railway station?

Kangra (Gaggal) airport is about 8 km away. Kangra station on the narrow-gauge Kangra Valley line is a short drive; Pathankot, about 90 km, is the nearest broad-gauge station.

Is there free food at Kangra Devi temple?

Yes. The temple trust runs a free langar every day from 12:30 to 2:30 pm and 7:30 to 9 pm, with longer hours in Navratri.

Who built the Kangra Devi temple?

By tradition the Pandavas raised the first shrine. The present temple was rebuilt after the 1905 Kangra earthquake and completed around 1920.
