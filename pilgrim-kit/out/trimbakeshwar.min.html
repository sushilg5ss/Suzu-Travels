Where is Trimbakeshwar temple and what are its timings?

Trimbakeshwar is in Trimbak town, Nashik district, Maharashtra, about 28–30 km from Nashik at the foot of the Brahmagiri hills, where the Godavari river begins. It is one of the 12 Jyotirlingas. The temple trust gives darshan hours of 5:30 am to 9 pm, with changes on festival days.

Trimbakeshwar is unlike most Jyotirlingas. The linga sits in a small hollow, and inside it are three tiny faces that tradition reads as Brahma, Vishnu and Shiva — the Trimurti in one shrine, which is why Shiva is worshipped here as Tryambaka, the three-eyed Lord. The black-basalt temple you see today was rebuilt in the 18th century under Peshwa Balaji Bajirao (Nanasaheb).

In the Shiva Purana order it is the eighth Jyotirlinga; the popular Jyotirlinga stotram remembers it as "त्र्यम्बकं गौतमीतटे" — Tryambaka on the bank of the Gautami, the old name of the Godavari. The river's source lies on the Brahmagiri hills above the town, and its ritual source is Kushavarta Kund in Trimbak itself.

The legend

The legend of Trimbakeshwar

The Shiva Purana (Koti Rudra Samhita) tells how a sage's penance brought a river to these hills. This is how the story is told.

  1. A pond that never driedSage Gautama and his wife Ahalya lived on Brahmagiri. Varuna, the lord of waters, gave them a pond that stayed full even in drought, and the sage shared its water with all.
  2. The jealous sagesOther sages grew jealous and plotted to drive him away. They sent a weak cow into his fields; when Gautama waved it off with a blade of grass, it fell and died, and he was branded guilty.
  3. Gautama's penanceSeeking a way to atone, Gautama prayed to Shiva with deep devotion. Pleased, Shiva released the Ganga on Brahmagiri, and she flowed on as the Godavari — also called Gautami, after the sage.
  4. Shiva stays as TryambakaAt the request of Gautama and the gods, Shiva agreed to remain here as Tryambakeshwar, and the Ganga stayed as the Godavari.

These are stories of faith, told as tradition rather than history. Pilgrims honour the legend by bathing at Kushavarta Kund before darshan, and the hardy climb Brahmagiri to the river's source.

How to reach

How to reach Trimbakeshwar

By air

Nashik (Ozar) airport is about 55 km away. Mumbai airport, about 180 km, has far more flights — roughly 4 to 5 hours by road.

By rail

Nashik Road station, about 40 km away, is the railhead; taxis and buses run on to Trimbak.

By road

From Nashik city it is about 28–30 km. MSRTC buses and shared taxis run frequently from Nashik's bus stands.

In town

The temple is at town level, a short walk from parking — no steps or ropeway to the shrine. Brahmagiri is a separate, steep climb.

Before you go

Before you go

  1. Go early or lateReach at opening (5:30 am) or in the evening on weekdays; avoid Mondays, Shravan and Simhastha snan days for a calm darshan.
  2. Use only the trust siteDonation darshan and temple pujas are on trimbakeshwartrust.com. Ignore ads promising cheap rituals or "VIP" entry.
  3. Brahmagiri is steepThe climb to the Godavari's source (Gangadwar) is long stone steps — not for elders with heart or knee problems.
  4. Elders and queuesQueues can mean standing for hours. Carry water, and consider the donation darshan for elders.
Kaal Sarp, Narayan Nagbali, Tripindi

Kaal Sarp puja at Trimbakeshwar — what to know

Tradition associates Trimbakeshwar with several family rituals — Kaal Sarp puja, Narayan Nagbali and Tripindi shraddha among them. They are performed in Trimbak by local, hereditary priests (purohits), as they have been for generations. We describe them only as tradition; no ritual "guarantees" a result.

Beware of online offers. In September 2026 the Trimbakeshwar Purohits' Association complained to the police, for the second time in two months, that devotees were being cheated by online ads and apps offering cheap rituals by unauthorised people. Book temple services only on trimbakeshwartrust.com; arrange other rituals in person in Trimbak, and never pay an unknown website or WhatsApp number in advance.

Basalt, a diamond and a hidden linga

A short history of Trimbakeshwar temple

The present black-basalt temple was rebuilt in the 18th century under Peshwa Balaji Bajirao (Nanasaheb). The famous Nassak Diamond once adorned the deity; it was taken by the British in the Third Anglo-Maratha War of 1817–18. The temple's jewelled crown (mukut) is said by tradition to date from the time of the Pandavas.

On 1 July 2026, while desilting the temple's Amrit Kund, the Archaeological Survey of India found a stone Shivling at the bottom of the tank — a reminder of how old worship here is.

FAQ

Questions pilgrims ask

What are Trimbakeshwar temple timings?

The temple trust gives darshan hours of 5:30 am to 9 pm. The morning puja is 7–8:30 am and the mid-day puja 10:45 am–12:30 pm; timings change on festival days, so confirm locally.

How can I skip the long queue at Trimbakeshwar?

Use the trust's paid donation darshan, booked on trimbakeshwartrust.com or at its counter. VIP passes can be suspended on peak days, as they were in Shravan 2026.

Why does the Trimbakeshwar linga have three faces?

The linga sits in a hollow with three small faces, which tradition says stand for Brahma, Vishnu and Shiva. That is why Shiva is worshipped here as Tryambaka.

Where does the Godavari river start?

On the Brahmagiri hills above Trimbak. Its ritual source is Kushavarta Kund in the town, where pilgrims bathe before darshan.

When is the Nashik–Trimbakeshwar Kumbh?

The Simhastha flag is hoisted on 31 Oct 2026. The main Amrit Snans are on 2 Aug and 31 Aug 2027, then 11 Sep (Nashik) and 12 Sep 2027 (Trimbakeshwar).

How far is Trimbakeshwar from Nashik?

About 28–30 km from Nashik city and about 40 km from Nashik Road station. Mumbai is about 180 km away.

Can I book Kaal Sarp puja online?

The trust's website books only its own pujas, such as Rudrabhishek and Laghurudra. Arrange other rituals in person in Trimbak — local priests have warned about fake online offers.

What is the best time to visit Trimbakeshwar?

July to February: the monsoon turns Brahmagiri green and October to February is pleasant. Avoid Simhastha bathing days and Shravan Mondays for a calm darshan.
