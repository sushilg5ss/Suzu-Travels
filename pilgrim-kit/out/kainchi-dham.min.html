Where is Kainchi Dham and what are its timings?

Kainchi Dham is Neem Karoli Baba's Hanuman temple and ashram on the Nainital–Almora road in Nainital district, Uttarakhand — 9 km from Bhowali and about 17 km from Nainital. Darshan is open from about 6 am to 7 pm (confirm locally). Entry is free and, as of early November 2026, no registration is needed; a daily cap is only proposed.

Kainchi Dham — कैंची धाम — is the ashram of Neem Karoli Baba, lovingly called Maharaj-ji, built around a temple of Hanuman beside the small Shipra stream in the Kumaon hills. The place is said to take its name from the two hairpin bends of the road here, shaped like a pair of scissors (kainchi). It is run by the Shri Kainchi Hanuman Mandir and Asram Trust.

Besides Hanuman, the ashram has shrines of Lakshmi-Narayan, Parvati, Ganesha, Kartikeya and Vindhyavasini Devi, and a Shiva linga. It is a short, level walk across the bridge from the road, so elders manage it easily — the real challenge today is traffic and queues, especially on weekends and long weekends.

The legend

The story of Kainchi Dham

This is how devotees remember the ashram's beginning — a story of a saint, a riverbend and a temple for Hanuman.

  1. A pause by the ShipraIn 1962 Neem Karoli Baba stopped at this bend of the Shipra, where sadhus are said to have performed yagyas long before, and asked for a simple platform to be built.
  2. A temple for HanumanTwo years later, in June 1964, a Hanuman temple was consecrated here. 15 June is still kept as the ashram's foundation day.
  3. Love, serve, rememberDevotees sum up his teaching in a few words: love everyone, serve everyone, remember God — and feed people.
  4. Seekers from afarHe spent much of his last decade at Kainchi. Western seekers such as Ram Dass, who met him in 1967, carried his name abroad, and visitors from around the world still come.

Neem Karoli Baba passed away in September 1973 in Vrindavan, where his samadhi stands; Kainchi keeps an urn of his ashes. Devotees share many stories of his grace — we tell them as faith, not as claims.

How to reach

How to reach Kainchi Dham

By rail

Kathgodam is the nearest railway station, about 38–40 km away — roughly 30 km to Bhowali and 9 km more on the Almora road.

By air

Pantnagar is the nearest airport; Bareilly is another option. From either, continue by road via Haldwani and Kathgodam.

By road

Kainchi is on the Nainital–Almora highway: 9 km from Bhowali and about 17 km from Nainital. Taxis run from Kathgodam, Haldwani, Bhimtal and Nainital.

At the ashram

A short, flat walk across the Shipra bridge — no climb, ropeway or pony. On rush days, park where the police direct and use the shuttle.

Before you go

Before you go

  1. Pick a weekday morningBest months are March–June and September–November. Avoid long weekends and 13–16 June unless you are coming for the fair.
  2. Stay nearbyThe ashram does not host visitors overnight. Bhowali, Bhimtal and Nainital have plenty of stays — book early for holidays.
  3. Respect the ashramDress modestly, keep silence inside, don't bathe in the Shipra, and follow the volunteers' instructions on photography.
  4. Avoid fake bookingsThere is no paid darshan pass or room booking. Rely only on notices from the trust and Nainital district.
Crowds, registration and shuttles

Is registration required for Kainchi Dham?

Not today. There is no registration and no entry fee as of early November 2026. In October 2026 the district administration proposed an online registration system, like the Char Dham one, with a daily limit of about 20,000–25,000 visitors; the Kumaon Commissioner agreed in principle, and the Nainital DM and SSP are to prepare a plan for state government approval. It would take about a month to build after approval, with weekend trials first.

The reason is the crowd: about 24.34 lakh visitors came between January and June 2026, against roughly 8 lakh in all of 2025, and 35,000–50,000 a day on the 2 October long weekend. Already in force: no bathing in the Shipra, rate lists at shops and hotels, the Almora highway kept clear, and separate routes and shuttles on rush days. Check the latest notice from Nainital district before a weekend or holiday visit, and follow our Kainchi Dham registration news for updates.

Maharaj-ji

Who was Neem Karoli Baba?

Neem Karoli Baba was a saint of the bhakti tradition and a lifelong devotee of Hanuman. He was born Lakshman Narayan Sharma around 1900 in Uttar Pradesh and took his name from Neem Karoli village in Farrukhabad district, where he spent his early years as a sadhu. He founded the Kainchi ashram in the 1960s and passed away in September 1973 in Vrindavan, where his samadhi is.

His teaching was simple — love, serve and remember God — and it travelled far through his devotees in India and abroad. Many people come to Kainchi today because they first read about him; at the ashram, the quiet way to honour him is darshan of Hanuman, a moment of silence and prasad.

FAQ

Questions pilgrims ask

What are Kainchi Dham timings?

About 6 am to 7 pm (Incredible India). Confirm locally, especially on weekends and festival days; aarti times are not published officially.

Is registration required for Kainchi Dham?

Not as of early November 2026. Online registration with a daily limit of about 20,000–25,000 visitors has been proposed and awaits state government approval.

How to reach Kainchi Dham?

By road on the Nainital–Almora highway: 9 km from Bhowali and about 17 km from Nainital. The nearest railway station is Kathgodam (about 38–40 km) and the nearest airport is Pantnagar.

When is Kainchi Dham foundation day?

Every 15 June — the Hanuman temple was consecrated in June 1964. Around the fair, private vehicles may be stopped and shuttles run from Bhimtal, Nainital and Haldwani.

Who was Neem Karoli Baba?

A Hanuman devotee and saint (about 1900–1973) who founded the Kainchi ashram. He taught love, service and remembering God; his samadhi is in Vrindavan.

Can we stay at Kainchi Dham ashram?

The ashram does not offer overnight stays to visitors. Most people stay in Bhowali, Bhimtal or Nainital.

Is there an entry fee at Kainchi Dham?

No. Darshan is free, and there is no paid darshan pass — avoid websites that sell one.

What is the best time to visit Kainchi Dham?

March–June and September–November, on a weekday morning. Avoid long weekends and 13–16 June unless you are coming for the foundation day fair.
