What is Sabarimala and when is it open?

Sabarimala is the hill temple of Lord Ayyappa (Sree Dharma Sastha) in Pathanamthitta district, Kerala, inside the Periyar Tiger Reserve. It opens only for the Mandala–Makaravilakku season (mid-November to January), the first five days of every Malayalam month and a few festivals. For 2026-27 the nada is expected to open on 16 November 2026. Darshan needs a free virtual queue booking.

Sabarimala — the hill of Sabari — is one of the great living pilgrimages of India. Every season around fifty lakh devotees, dressed in black, blue or saffron and chanting Swamiye Saranam Ayyappa, walk up through the forest to the shrine of Ayyappa, whom tradition knows as Dharma Sastha, Hariharaputra and Manikandan. The temple is run by the Travancore Devaswom Board (TDB).

What makes Sabarimala different is the preparation. Pilgrims traditionally keep a 41-day vratham of simple, disciplined living, wear a mala, and carry the two-part irumudi kettu on the head — only those with an irumudi climb the Pathinettampadi, the eighteen holy steps to the sanctum. The legend

The legend of Ayyappa

Retold in our own words from Kerala tradition and later Puranic-style tellings — as belief, not history. Versions differ from place to place.

  1. A baby by the PambaTradition says Ayyappa was born of Shiva and Mohini, Vishnu's enchanting form, to end the demoness Mahishi. The childless king of Pandalam found a baby boy beside the Pamba river, a bell at his neck, and raised him as Manikandan.
  2. Tigress's milkAs the boy grew, the queen, jealous for her own son, feigned illness and asked for the milk of a tigress as her cure. Young Manikandan went alone into the forest to fetch it.
  3. Mahishi defeatedIn the forest, it is believed, he fought and slew Mahishi, and came home riding a tiger, with the forest's tigers behind him. The court saw then that he was no ordinary prince.
  4. A seat on the hillAyyappa is said to have shot an arrow to mark where his shrine should stand, and took his seat at Sabarimala as a celibate yogi. His companion Vavar is honoured too, at Erumeli and on the hill.

Tellings of Vavar differ — a warrior, a reformed robber or a saint — and pilgrims of many backgrounds stop at his shrine at Erumeli on the way. Historians see the worship of Ayyappa as layered, joining a local forest deity with the Puranic story; for devotees it is simply Swami's own hill.

Each season ends with Makaravilakku on Makara Sankranti. Three days before, the sacred ornaments (Thiruvabharanam) leave the Valiyakoikkal temple at Pandalam in procession and reach the shrine on the festival day, and a sacred lamp is lit on Ponnambalamedu, the hill across the valley. Every night the sanctum closes to the lullaby Harivarasanam.

How to reach

How to reach Sabarimala

By train

Chengannur is the main railhead, about 85 km from Pamba; KSRTC buses run round the clock in the season. Kottayam is about 95 km.

By air

Kochi airport is about 155 km and Thiruvananthapuram about 170 km from Pamba. There is no airport at Sabarimala yet.

By road to Pamba

Private vehicles park at the Nilakkal base camp; KSRTC chain buses shuttle between Nilakkal and Pamba. Pilgrims from Bengaluru, Chennai and Hyderabad usually drive or take a train to Chengannur or Kottayam.

The climb

From Pamba it is about 5 km uphill on foot via Neelimala, Appachimedu, Sabari Peedam and Saramkuthi to Sannidhanam. The traditional Erumeli forest path takes several days (about 45–60 km).

From Himachal and the north

Fly to Kochi or Thiruvananthapuram, or take a train to Chengannur. We can arrange the transfers to Nilakkal and back.

Before you go

Before you go

  1. Vratham and irumudiThe traditional 41-day vow means simple living, vegetarian food and celibacy. Only pilgrims carrying an irumudi climb the eighteen holy steps; others take the side path.
  2. Elders and childrenThe climb from Pamba is steep. Rest often, carry your medicines, and confirm dolly service locally. Girls under 10 and women over 50 make the pilgrimage with their families.
  3. Women aged 10–50Traditionally women of this age group do not make the pilgrimage. The question has been before the Supreme Court; follow the rules the Devaswom Board applies when you travel.
  4. Avoid plasticPlastic is banned in the forest. Carry cloth bags and steel bottles.
  5. Pick your daysEarly Mandala weekdays are calmest. The Makaravilakku week is the most crowded and daily caps can be cut — book early.
  6. PhotographyPhotography is restricted near the sanctum. Follow the staff's instructions.
Free — and only on the official portal

How to book the Sabarimala virtual queue

Darshan at Sabarimala runs on a virtual queue managed for the Devaswom. For the 2026-27 season booking opened at 10 am on 29 October 2026 and it is free. Up to 80,000 pilgrims a day are allowed, including spot booking. Online slots are split by route: about 60,000 a day for Nilakkal–Pamba, 3,000 for the Erumeli forest path and 3,000 for the Vandiperiyar–Sathram side.

  1. Go to sabarimalaonline.org — the only official portal — and register with your photo ID.
  2. Pick your route, date and time slot.
  3. Print or save the confirmation and carry it with the same ID.
  4. Show it at the check points; pilgrims without a valid booking are not let through.

Limited spot booking is available at base centres such as Nilakkal, but on busy days it fills fast. TDB rooms at Sannidhanam are also booked online only.

Beware of fake sites. Booking is free. Ignore look-alike websites and WhatsApp sellers offering "VIP darshan" or "confirmed virtual queue tickets" for money.

FAQ

Questions pilgrims ask

When does Sabarimala open for Mandala 2026?

The nada is expected to open on the evening of 16 November 2026, with the Mandala season from 17 November. Confirm on the Travancore Devaswom Board's notice.

How do I book the Sabarimala virtual queue?

Only on sabarimalaonline.org. It is free; booking for 2026-27 opened on 29 October 2026. Carry the printout and the same photo ID you booked with.

How many pilgrims are allowed per day?

Up to 80,000 a day in 2026-27, including limited spot booking at base centres such as Nilakkal.

When are Mandala Pooja and Makaravilakku?

Mandala Pooja is expected on 27 December 2026 and Makaravilakku on 14 January 2027 (Makara Sankranti). The season is expected to close around 20 January 2027.

How far is the temple from Pamba?

About 5 km uphill on foot via Neelimala and Appachimedu to Sannidhanam.

Which is the nearest railway station and airport?

Chengannur station is about 85 km from Pamba and Kottayam about 95 km. The nearest airports are Kochi (about 155 km) and Thiruvananthapuram (about 170 km).

What are the 41-day vratham and the irumudi?

The vratham is a 41-day period of simple, disciplined living before the pilgrimage. The irumudi is the two-part offering bundle carried on the head; only pilgrims with it climb the eighteen holy steps.

Can women visit Sabarimala?

Girls under 10 and women over 50 make the pilgrimage. Entry for women aged 10–50 has been before the Supreme Court; follow the Devaswom Board's rules when you travel.
