The 12 Jyotirlingas: names, places and states

The 12 Jyotirlingas are Somnath (Gujarat), Mallikarjuna (Andhra Pradesh), Mahakaleshwar and Omkareshwar (Madhya Pradesh), Kedarnath (Uttarakhand), Bhimashankar (Maharashtra), Kashi Vishwanath (Uttar Pradesh), Trimbakeshwar (Maharashtra), Vaidyanath (Jharkhand), Nageshwar (Gujarat), Rameshwaram (Tamil Nadu) and Grishneshwar (Maharashtra). Each marks a place where Shiva is worshipped as a pillar of light.

Four of the twelve are in Maharashtra and Madhya Pradesh's Malwa plateau, two on the Gujarat coast, two in the deep south, two in the north and one in the east — which is why the full yatra is planned region by region.

The legend

Why "pillar of light"? The story of the first Jyotirlinga

The Shiva Purana tells of a quarrel at the beginning of time, between Brahma the creator and Vishnu the preserver.

  1. Who is greater?Brahma and Vishnu argue over which of them is supreme.
  2. A pillar of lightAn endless column of fire appears between them, splitting earth and sky.
  3. No top, no endVishnu dives as a boar to find its base; Brahma flies up as a swan. Neither finds an end.
  4. Shiva emergesShiva appears from the pillar — the light worshipped at the twelve Jyotirlingas.

The scene is carved in temples across India as the Lingodbhava. Each Jyotirlinga then has its own story — the Moon's curse at Somnath, the demon Dushana at Ujjain, the Vindhya mountain at Omkareshwar, the Pandavas at Kedarnath. You'll find them temple by temple below.

Route plan

How to plan the 12 Jyotirlinga yatra

The temples are spread over seven states, so pilgrims group them and fly between the groups. A comfortable pace is 18–22 days; many families split the circuit over two or three trips.

  1. North · 5–7 days
    Kedarnath & Kashi Vishwanathकेदारनाथ · काशी

    Kedarnath only between late April and November (registration compulsory); Kashi all year — combine with Ayodhya and Prayagraj.

  2. Madhya Pradesh · 2–3 days
    Mahakaleshwar & Omkareshwarमहाकाल · ओंकारेश्वर

    Fly to Indore; Ujjain and Omkareshwar are a drive apart. Book the Bhasma Aarti well in advance.

  3. Maharashtra · 4–5 days
    Trimbakeshwar, Grishneshwar & Bhimashankarत्र्यंबकेश्वर · घृष्णेश्वर · भीमाशंकर

    Nashik, Ellora and the Sahyadri forests. Add Parli Vaidyanath and Aundha Nagnath if you follow those traditions.

  4. Gujarat · 2–3 days
    Somnath & Nageshwarसोमनाथ · नागेश्वर

    The Saurashtra coast, with Dwarkadhish temple next to Nageshwar.

  5. South · 3–4 days
    Mallikarjuna & Rameshwaramमल्लिकार्जुन · रामेश्वरम्

    Srisailam in the Nallamala hills; Rameshwaram on Pamban island — add Madurai and Kanyakumari.

Jyotirlinga 1 of 12 · Gujarat

Somnath सोमनाथ

Shree Somnath Temple (Somnath Mahadev Mandir) · Prabhas Patan (near Veraval), Gir Somnath district

According to the Shiva Purana (Koti Rudra Samhita) and the Prabhas Khanda of the Skanda Purana, the Moon god Chandra married the twenty-seven daughters of Daksha Prajapati but showered his love on Rohini alone. The neglected sisters complained, and when Chandra ignored Daksha's warnings, the angry father cursed him to waste away. As the Moon's glow faded, the worlds suffered. On Brahma's advice Chandra came to Prabhas on the Saurashtra coast and worshipped Shiva with deep devotion. Shiva softened rather than cancelled the curse: the Moon would wane for one fortnight and grow again in the next. Grateful, Chandra, also called Soma, begged Shiva to remain at Prabhas, and the shrine came to be known as Somnath, the Lord of Soma.

Jyotirlinga 2 of 12 · Andhra Pradesh

Mallikarjuna मल्लिकार्जुन

Sri Bhramaramba Mallikarjuna Swamy Varla Devasthanam, Srisailam · Srisailam, Nandyal district

The Shiva Purana (Koti Rudra Samhita) relates that Shiva and Parvati set their sons a race around the world, the winner to marry first. Kartikeya sped away on his peacock, but Ganesha simply walked around his parents, calling them his whole universe, and was declared the winner and married. When Kartikeya returned and learned what had happened, he felt wronged and withdrew to the Krauncha mountain. His grieving parents followed and settled close by on the Srisailam hill so they could stay near him. It is believed Shiva shines here as a Jyotirlinga alongside Parvati as Bhramaramba. Popular tradition adds that Shiva visits as Arjuna on new-moon days and Parvati as Mallika on full-moon days, giving the name Mallikarjuna.

Jyotirlinga 3 of 12 · Madhya Pradesh

Mahakaleshwar महाकालेश्वर

Shri Mahakaleshwar Temple, Ujjain · Ujjain, Ujjain district

According to the Shiva Purana (Koti Rudra Samhita), a learned Brahmin named Vedapriya lived in Avanti, today's Ujjain, with four devout sons who worshipped an earthen linga every day. The demon Dushana, empowered by a boon from Brahma, swept down from the Ratnamala hills, tormenting priests and stopping sacred rites. The family refused to abandon their prayers. As the demon closed in on them, the ground beside their linga split open and Shiva emerged in his terrifying Mahakala form, reducing Dushana to ashes with a mere roar. The grateful devotees begged him to stay and guard the city forever, and it is believed he remained as the self-manifested, south-facing Mahakaleshwar. Another tradition links the shrine to King Chandrasena and the young cowherd Shrikhar.

Jyotirlinga 4 of 12 · Madhya Pradesh

Omkareshwar ओंकारेश्वर

Shri Omkareshwar Mandir (with Mamleshwar / Amaleshwar on the south bank) · Omkareshwar (Mandhata island), Khandwa district

The Shiva Purana (Koti Rudra Samhita) relates that the sage Narada once praised Mount Meru in the presence of the Vindhya mountain, wounding Vindhya's pride. Seeking greatness, the mountain deity went to the banks of the Narmada, shaped a linga from earth and worshipped Shiva with long, single-minded penance. Pleased, Shiva appeared and granted his wish, and at the request of the gods and sages agreed to stay here in two aspects, Omkareshwar and Amaleshwar (Mamleshwar). Another tradition credits Mandhata, a king of the Ikshvaku line, whose devotion gave the island its name. The island, wrapped by the Narmada, is believed to resemble the sacred syllable Om, which is why Shiva here is hailed as the Lord of Omkara.

Jyotirlinga 5 of 12 · Uttarakhand

Kedarnath केदारनाथ

Shri Kedarnath Dham · Kedarnath, Rudraprayag district

Tradition drawn from the Mahabharata cycle and the Shiva Purana holds that the Pandavas, burdened by the bloodshed of the Kurukshetra war, set out to find Shiva and seek his forgiveness. Shiva avoided them and hid in the Garhwal Himalaya disguised as a bull among a grazing herd. Bhima stood astride a valley so the cattle had to pass beneath his legs; one bull refused, and the brothers knew it was Shiva. As the bull sank into the earth, Bhima grabbed its hump, which remained at Kedarnath, while other parts appeared at the four other Panch Kedar shrines. The Shiva Purana also says Shiva settled here at the request of the sages Nara and Narayana, rewarding their long penance.

Jyotirlinga 6 of 12 · Maharashtra

Bhimashankar भीमाशंकर

Shree Kshetra Bhimashankar Temple · Bhimashankar, Pune (Khed taluka) district

The Shiva Purana (Koti Rudra Samhita) tells of Bhima, son of Kumbhakarna and the demoness Karkati, who grew up in the hills after his father fell to Rama. Learning this, he performed fierce penance, won a boon of great strength from Brahma and set out to conquer the gods and righteous kings. He imprisoned Sudakshina, the Shiva-devoted king of Kamarupa, who kept worshipping a clay linga inside his prison. When Bhima raised his sword to smash that linga, Shiva burst forth and destroyed him. At the gods' plea Shiva remained there as Bhimashankar. A separate Maharashtra tradition links the name to Shiva's battle with the demon Tripurasura and says the sweat of that fight became the Bhima river.

Jyotirlinga 7 of 12 · Uttar Pradesh

Kashi Vishwanath काशी विश्वनाथ

Shri Kashi Vishwanath Temple (Vishweshwar) · Varanasi (Kashi), Varanasi district

The Shiva Purana (Koti Rudra Samhita) honours Kashi as Avimukta, the place Shiva never leaves. It is believed that at the beginning of creation Shiva and Shakti shaped this city as their own home, and that it is held aloft on the tip of Shiva's trident, so it survives even the great dissolution of the universe. Tradition also links Vishweshwara with the endless column of light that appeared when Brahma and Vishnu quarrelled over who was greater; neither could find its top or base. Here Shiva is the Lord of the Universe, and devotees believe that anyone who dies in Kashi hears from him the liberating Taraka mantra, making the city a doorway to moksha.

Jyotirlinga 8 of 12 · Maharashtra

Trimbakeshwar त्र्यंबकेश्वर

Shri Trimbakeshwar Temple · Trimbak, Nashik district

The Shiva Purana (Koti Rudra Samhita) recounts that Sage Gautama and his wife Ahalya lived on the Brahmagiri hills, where Varuna had granted them an ever-full pond during a drought. Envious sages schemed to drive him away: a frail cow was sent into his fields, and when Gautama shooed it with a blade of grass it fell dead. Blamed for killing a cow, Gautama turned to Shiva for atonement. Pleased by his penance, Shiva released the Ganga here, who flows on as the Godavari, also called Gautami, and agreed to remain as Tryambaka, the three-eyed lord. The linga's three small faces are believed to represent Brahma, Vishnu and Shiva together.

Jyotirlinga 9 of 12 · Jharkhand

Vaidyanath वैद्यनाथ

Baba Baidyanath Dham · Deoghar, Deoghar district

It is believed, following the Shiva Purana's account of Vaidyanath, that the demon-king Ravana performed fierce penance to win Shiva's grace, offering his heads one by one. Shiva restored him, which earned the Lord the title Vaidyanath, 'Lord of Physicians', and granted Ravana a linga to install in Lanka, on condition that it would stay wherever it first touched the ground. Fearing Ravana's growing power, the gods arranged that he was forced to halt on the way. A cowherd, identified with Vishnu and remembered locally as Baiju, held the linga and set it down. Ravana could not lift it again, pressed it with his thumb in anger, and the linga remained forever at Deoghar.

Jyotirlinga 10 of 12 · Gujarat

Nageshwar नागेश्वर

Shri Nageshwar Jyotirlinga Temple (Dwarka) · Near Dwarka (on the Dwarka-Okha/Bet Dwarka route), Devbhumi Dwarka district

The Shiva Purana (Koti Rudra Samhita) tells of a demon named Daruka and his demoness consort who ruled the forest of Darukavana beside the western sea and preyed on travellers. Among their captives was Supriya, a merchant devoted to Shiva, who kept leading his fellow prisoners in chanting Shiva's name inside the demons' dungeon. When the demons rushed to kill him, Shiva appeared in a blaze of light, destroyed the attackers and blessed his devotee. At the request of those he had rescued, Shiva remained there as Nageshwara, 'Lord of the Serpents', with Parvati as Nageshwari. Devotees believe that prayers at this shrine protect against poison of every kind, including snakebite.

Jyotirlinga 11 of 12 · Tamil Nadu

Rameshwaram रामेश्वरम्

Arulmigu Ramanathaswamy Temple, Rameswaram · Rameswaram (Pamban island), Ramanathapuram district

The Shiva Purana (Koti Rudra Samhita) says that Rama, camped on the southern shore before crossing to Lanka, shaped a linga and prayed to Shiva for victory. Shiva appeared, blessed him and, at Rama's request, stayed as Rameshwara, the Lord worshipped by Rama. A widely told temple tradition adds that after the war Rama wished to cleanse himself of having slain Ravana, a learned Brahmin. Hanuman was sent to Kailash for a linga but was delayed, so Sita fashioned one from sand, which Rama consecrated as the Ramalingam. To honour Hanuman's effort, Rama decreed that the Vishwalingam brought from Kailash be worshipped first, a custom still followed in the temple.

Jyotirlinga 12 of 12 · Maharashtra

Grishneshwar घृष्णेश्वर

Shri Grishneshwar (Ghushmeshwar) Temple, Verul · Verul (Ellora), Chhatrapati Sambhajinagar (formerly Aurangabad) district

The Shiva Purana (Koti Rudra Samhita) tells of the Brahmin Sudharma and his wife Sudeha, who had no children. Sudeha urged her husband to marry her younger sister Ghushma, a devoted worshipper who shaped and worshipped 101 clay lingas every day before immersing them in a nearby pond. Ghushma bore a son, and Sudeha's jealousy grew until she killed the young man and threw his body into the same pond. Ghushma calmly continued her worship, and by Shiva's grace her son came back to life. Shiva wished to punish Sudeha, but Ghushma begged him to forgive her sister and to stay at that spot for the good of all. He remained as Ghushmeshwar.

परल्यां वैद्यनाथं च डाकिन्यां भीमशंकरम् ।
सेतुबन्धे तु रामेशं नागेशं दारुकावने ॥
वाराणस्यां तु विश्वेशं त्र्यम्बकं गौतमीतटे ।
हिमालये तु केदारं घुश्मेशं च शिवालये ॥

Recited daily by devotees, the stotram lists the twelve in its own order: Somnath in Saurashtra, Mallikarjuna on Shrishaila, Mahakal in Ujjain, Omkareshwar (with Amaleshwar), Vaidyanath "at Parli", Bhimashankar "in Dakini", Rameshwaram at the Setu, Nageshwar in the Daruka forest, Vishwanath in Varanasi, Trimbakeshwar on the Gautami's bank, Kedar in the Himalaya and Ghushmeshwar at Shivalaya. Chanting it, tradition says, washes away the sins of seven lifetimes.

FAQ

12 Jyotirlinga: questions people ask

What are the names of the 12 Jyotirlingas?

Somnath, Mallikarjuna, Mahakaleshwar, Omkareshwar, Kedarnath, Bhimashankar, Kashi Vishwanath, Trimbakeshwar, Vaidyanath, Nageshwar, Rameshwaram and Grishneshwar — the order of the Shiva Purana.

Which is the first Jyotirlinga?

Somnath, at Prabhas Patan on the Gujarat coast, is traditionally counted first. Its legend tells of the Moon (Soma), cursed to fade, who regained his light by worshipping Shiva here.

Which Jyotirlinga is the highest?

Kedarnath, at about 3,583 m in Uttarakhand's Rudraprayag district. It is open only from late April or May to around Bhai Dooj in November, and is reached by a walk of about 16 km from Gaurikund, by pony or palki, or by helicopter.

Which Jyotirlingas are in Maharashtra?

Three by the most common list — Trimbakeshwar (Nashik), Bhimashankar (Pune district) and Grishneshwar (near Ellora). Other traditions add Parli Vaidyanath and Aundha Nagnath, which makes five.

Which Jyotirlinga is in South India?

Mallikarjuna at Srisailam (Andhra Pradesh) and Rameshwaram (Ramanathaswamy) in Tamil Nadu. Mallikarjuna is also a Shakti Peeth, as Bhramaramba Devi.

Can I visit all 12 Jyotirlingas in 15 days?

Only at a rush with several flights. A comfortable circuit takes 18–22 days because the temples are spread over seven states; we group them by region so you fly between groups and drive within them.

When is Mahashivratri 2027?

Saturday, 6 March 2027 — the busiest day at every Jyotirlinga, so book hotels and darshan passes early.

Is the Bhasma Aarti at Mahakaleshwar open to everyone?

Yes, with an advance booking through the temple. Since June 2026 one booking per mobile number is allowed every 90 days, and the aarti begins around 4 am.
