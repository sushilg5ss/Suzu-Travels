Where is the Jagannath Puri temple and why is it so sacred?

The Shree Jagannath Temple (Srimandir) stands in Puri town, Odisha, close to the Bay of Bengal, about 59 km from Bhubaneswar airport. Jagannath, a form of Vishnu and Krishna, is worshipped here with Balabhadra and Subhadra as wooden images. Puri is the eastern dham of Adi Shankara's Char Dham tradition. The temple is open roughly 5 am to 10 pm; only Hindus may enter.

Srimandir is a whole sacred town within walls: a complex of more than ten acres with over 120 shrines, enclosed by the outer wall called Meghanada Pacheri and an inner wall, Kurma Bedha. The main entrance is the Singhadwara (Lion Gate) on the east, facing the Bada Danda, the grand road down which the chariots roll every Ashadha. On top of the main spire is the Nilachakra, a wheel of eight metals, and a servitor climbs the spire every evening to change the flags.

The present temple was begun in the 12th century by the Eastern Ganga king Anantavarman Chodaganga Deva, and additions continued for centuries. Inside the complex is also the shrine of Goddess Vimala, counted among the Adi Shakti Peeths in several lists — see our 51 Shakti Peeth guide. Puri completes the all-India Char Dham with Badrinath, Dwarka and Rameswaram. See all our pilgrimage guides.

The legend

The story of Jagannath and the unfinished images

Told in our own words, as tradition tells it — in the Skanda Purana's praise of Purushottama Kshetra and in many Odia retellings. The versions differ in their details.

  1. A king's longingKing Indradyumna longed to see Vishnu, worshipped in the forests as Nila Madhaba. In a dream he was told that the Lord would come to Puri in a new form.
  2. A log from the seaAs the dream foretold, a great log of sacred wood drifted ashore at Puri. No craftsman could work it.
  3. Behind a closed doorAn old carpenter — believed to be Vishwakarma, or Vishnu himself — offered to carve the images, on one condition: nobody must open the door until he was done.
  4. Images left unfinishedWhen the queen grew anxious and the door was opened early, the carver had vanished, leaving the images with unfinished hands. Jagannath, Balabhadra and Subhadra are worshipped in that form to this day.

Traditions differ on who the carver was, and some tellings add an episode with the Savara people who first worshipped Nila Madhaba. Because the images are made of wood, they are ritually renewed in the ceremony called Nabakalebara, held in certain years with an extra month of Ashadha — every 8, 12 or 19 years. The last one was in 2015; the next has not been announced.

The three chariots — Nandighosa (Jagannath), Taladhwaja (Balabhadra) and Darpadalana (Subhadra) — are built new every year and pulled about 3 km along the Bada Danda to the Gundicha temple. Book stays early; Puri fills up weeks before the festival.

How to reach

How to reach Jagannath Puri temple

By air

Biju Patnaik International Airport, Bhubaneswar, is about 59 km away — around 1–1.5 hours by road.

By train

Puri railway station is about 5 km from the temple and has direct trains from many Indian cities. Bhubaneswar station is about 59 km away.

By road

Good highways link Puri with Bhubaneswar. Konark Sun Temple is about 36 km along the coast by Marine Drive.

Nearby

The Gundicha temple, where the chariots go at Rath Yatra, is about 3 km away; Konark Sun Temple about 36 km; the Lingaraj temple in Bhubaneswar about 60 km.

Sample itinerary

A simple 2-day Puri, Konark and Bhubaneswar plan

  1. Day 1
    Bhubaneswar → Puri

    Land at Bhubaneswar and drive about 1.5 hours to Puri. Evening darshan at Srimandir, Mahaprasad at Ananda Bazar, then a walk on the beach at Swargadwar.

  2. Day 2
    Puri → Konark → Bhubaneswar

    Early darshan, then along Marine Drive to the Konark Sun Temple (about 36 km), and on to Bhubaneswar for the Lingaraj temple before your flight or train.

Before you go

Before you go

  1. Leave the phone behindPhones and cameras are not allowed inside — keep them at the hotel or deposit them at the gate.
  2. Dress modestlyTraditional or modest dress; shorts, torn jeans and sleeveless tops are turned away.
  3. Elders and crowdsThe town is flat, but queues are long. Avoid Rath Yatra day and holiday weekends with elders, and ask at the Singhadwara counter for help.
  4. Best timeOctober to March is comfortable. June–July is hot and humid, but it is Rath Yatra season.
Food of the Lord

Mahaprasad and Ananda Bazar

Every day the temple kitchen cooks vegetarian food, without onion or garlic, in stacked earthen pots — it is one of the largest temple kitchens in India. The food is offered first to Jagannath and then to Goddess Vimala, and only then does it become Mahaprasad.

Pilgrims buy and share Mahaprasad at Ananda Bazar, the market inside the temple complex.

Beware of fake sites. There is no official online darshan booking or "VIP darshan" sold by agents. Do not pay anyone online for darshan, and follow only notices from the temple administration and the Odisha government.

FAQ

Questions pilgrims ask

What are the Puri Jagannath temple timings?

The temple is open roughly 5 am to 10 pm. Darshan pauses during the food offerings and times change on festival days, so confirm locally before you go.

When is Rath Yatra 2027?

Monday, 5 July 2027 according to the panchang (Ashadha Shukla Dwitiya). The temple's official programme has not been announced yet.

Can non-Hindus enter the Jagannath temple?

No. By the temple's long-standing rule only Hindus may go inside. Others can see the temple from outside and the deities on the chariots during Rath Yatra.

Can I take my mobile phone inside?

No. Phones have been banned inside the temple since 1 January 2023. Deposit them at the counters near the gates.

Is there a dress code at the Jagannath temple?

Yes, since 1 January 2024. Shorts, torn jeans, skirts and sleeveless or revealing clothes are not allowed; wear traditional or modest dress.

Can I book Jagannath darshan online?

Not yet. A slot-booking system has been announced, but no official online booking was live when we checked in November 2026. Avoid sites selling VIP darshan.

Why are the images of Jagannath unfinished?

Tradition says the divine carver asked that his door stay shut until he finished; when it was opened early he vanished, leaving the images with unfinished hands.

How far is Puri from Bhubaneswar airport?

About 59 km, around 1–1.5 hours by road. Puri railway station is about 5 km from the temple.
