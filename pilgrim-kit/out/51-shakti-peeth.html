What are the 51 Shakti Peeths?

The 51 Shakti Peeths are shrines of the Goddess where, by tradition, parts or ornaments of Sati fell after Vishnu's Sudarshan Chakra divided her body to end Shiva's grief. Each has its Devi and a guardian Bhairava. The best-known list of 51 comes from the Pithanirnaya; other texts count 4, 18, 52 or 108. See the full list.

51in the Pithanirnaya, the most quoted list
18Maha Peethas in the Ashtadasha stotram
4Adi Peethas, led by Kamakhya
108seats in the Devi Bhagavata Purana

Most peethas are in India, but the lists follow the old map of the subcontinent — there are five to seven in Bangladesh (depending on the list), several in Nepal, two in Pakistan, one in Tibet and one or two in Sri Lanka. In South India the same shrines are often called Shakti Peetha or Shakthi Peetham.

Three of the peethas are in Himachal — Naina Devi, Jwala Ji and Kangra Devi — and Suzu Travels is based in Bilaspur, Naina Devi's own district. Start with the legend, then the list.

The legend

The story of the Shakti Peeths

It begins with a father's pride and ends with the Goddess present all across the land.

  1. Daksha's sacrificeDaksha held a great yajna and invited every god — except his daughter Sati and her husband Shiva.
  2. Sati's choiceSati went anyway, was humiliated before the gathering, and gave up her body. The texts tell it differently: by fire, by yogic power, or through a shadow form in the Mahabhagavata.
  3. Shiva's griefFrom Shiva's grief and anger came Virabhadra, who wrecked the sacrifice; Daksha was later restored with a goat's head. Shiva then wandered the worlds carrying Sati.
  4. The Peethas ariseTo end his grief, Vishnu's Sudarshan Chakra divided her body. Wherever a part or an ornament fell, a Shakti Peeth arose.

It is believed that at every peetha the Devi is worshipped in her own form, and Shiva stands guard as a Bhairava — so many pilgrims visit both shrines.

This is a sacred tradition, retold here in our own words from the Shiva Purana, Devi Bhagavata and Kalika Purana retellings — not a historical record.

Why are there 51 Shakti Peeths?

The number 51 comes from the Pithanirnaya (Mahapithanirupana), a list attributed to the Tantra Chudamani. Tradition links the 51 seats to the 51 letters of the Sanskrit alphabet. Other texts count differently: 4 Adi Peethas, 18 Maha Peethas, 52 in a Bengal variant and 108 in the Devi Bhagavata Purana.

State by state

How many Shakti Peeths are in India?

Most of them. Of the 51 seats in the Pithanirnaya-based lists, about 40 are in present-day India, depending on the list; the rest are in Bangladesh, Nepal, Pakistan, Tibet and Sri Lanka. West Bengal has the most, with 15 entries in the table on this page. Counts below are rows in that table, which also holds the 18 Maha and 4 Adi Peethas.

North India · 13

Uttar Pradesh (3), Himachal Pradesh (4), Rajasthan (2), and one each in Punjab, Haryana, Jammu & Kashmir and Uttarakhand. Naina Devi, Jwala Ji and Kangra Devi in Himachal are among the most visited.

East India · 21

West Bengal alone has 15, the most of any state, including Kalighat and Tarapith. Odisha has 3, Bihar 2 and Jharkhand 1 (Jaya Durga at Deoghar).

North-East India · 3

Kamakhya in Assam, Jayanti in Meghalaya and Tripura Sundari in Tripura.

West and Central India · 9

Maharashtra (3), Madhya Pradesh (3), Gujarat (2) and Chhattisgarh (1).

South India · 8

Andhra Pradesh (3), Tamil Nadu (3), Telangana (1) and Karnataka (1); most are in the 18 Maha Peetha list.

Why the number differs

4, 18, 51, 52 or 108 — how many Shakti Peeths are there?

4 Adi Peethas

Kamakhya (Assam), Vimala (Puri), Kalighat (Kolkata) and Tara Tarini (Odisha) — the "first" four in Devi Bhagavata and Kalika Purana traditions.

18 Maha Peethas

The Ashtadasha Shakti Peetha Stotram (attributed to Adi Shankara) and the Kularnava Tantra.

51 Peethas

The Pithanirnaya (Mahapithanirupana), attributed to the Tantra Chudamani, and the Mahabhagavata Purana. The number is often linked to the 51 letters of the Sanskrit alphabet.

52 Peethas

A Bengal variant; the scholar D.C. Sircar put the extra one down to confusion in copying the lists.

64 and 108

The Brahmanda Purana is said to give 64; the Devi Bhagavata and Matsya Puranas give 108 seats, with Vishalakshi of Varanasi named first.

And others

The Kalika Purana names 7 and the Kubjika Tantra 42. Compiled tables of all these traditions run to about 70 places.

So there is no single "correct" list. When someone says "51 Shakti Peeth", they usually mean the Pithanirnaya list — but even its compiled tables disagree on some places, body parts and Bhairava names. We print one widely used list and say where traditions differ.

Ashtadasha Shakti Peethas

The 18 Maha Shakti Peethas list

The Ashtadasha Shakti Peetha Stotram — a short Sanskrit hymn traditionally attributed to Adi Shankaracharya (the attribution is not certain) — names 18 seats of the Goddess, from Lanka in the south to Kashmir in the north. The Kularnava Tantra also counts eighteen. In the South these are the Ashtadasha (Astadasa) Shakthi Peethas, and many families try to visit all of them over the years.

Often asked

Which is the most important Shakti Peeth?

Traditions differ, but Kamakhya in Guwahati, Assam, is the most widely revered. It is one of the four Adi (first) Peethas and the centre of Tantric worship of the Goddess. The four Adi Peethas are:

  1. Kamakhya, Guwahati (Assam)
  2. Kalighat, Kolkata (West Bengal)
  3. Vimala, Puri (Odisha)
  4. Tara Tarini, Brahmapur (Odisha)

Which is the first Shakti Peeth?

The lists do not number the peethas in one fixed order. In the Devi Bhagavata's list of 108 seats, Vishalakshi of Varanasi is named first. In the Ashtadasha stotram of 18 Maha Peethas, Shankari of Sri Lanka comes first.

Where did Sati's head fall?

Lists differ. The compiled tables link Hinglaj in Balochistan, Pakistan, to the Brahmarandhra (the crown of the head) and Muktinath in Nepal to the head. In Himachal, popular belief links Chintpurni to Sati's forehead, and the Mansa Devi shrine board links its pindis to the forehead too.

Beyond today's borders

Shakti Peeths outside India

The peethas follow the old map of the subcontinent, not today's borders. By the Pithanirnaya-based lists:

Bangladesh

Five to seven peethas, depending on the list — the largest group outside India.

Nepal

Several peethas are in Nepal.

Pakistan

Hinglaj in Balochistan, and the ruins of Sharada Peeth near the Line of Control in Pakistan-administered Kashmir (the latter is in the 18 list).

Tibet

Manasa, near Lake Mansarovar.

Sri Lanka

One or two, depending on the list: Shankari at Trincomalee, first in the 18 list (its identification is debated), and, in some lists, Indrakshi at Nainativu.

Often asked

Famous Devi temples that are not in the 51

Many of India's most loved Devi temples are not in the classical 51 lists. That takes nothing away from their sanctity — they belong to other traditions, such as the broader 64 and 108 counts or a regional circuit:

  • Vaishno Devi (Jammu & Kashmir) — counted in the 64/108 traditions; which part of Sati is linked to it is contested.
  • Chamunda Devi (Kangra) — one of Himachal's local five Shakti temples, not a classical peetha.
  • Mansa Devi (Panchkula) — the shrine board speaks of Sati's forehead, but it is not in the standard tables.
  • Meenakshi Amman (Madurai) — not in the classical lists.
  • Baglamukhi (Kangra) — a Mahavidya siddha peetha rather than one of the 51.

Local traditions add others too, such as Tuljabhavani and Pavagadh.

Is Vaishno Devi a Shakti Peeth?

Not in the classical lists. Vaishno Devi is not one of the Pithanirnaya's 51; it is counted in the broader 64 and 108 traditions and is one of India's most visited Devi shrines. Many families add it to a Devi yatra — see our 6- and 7-day Kashmir trips with Vaishno Devi or the Vaishno Devi yatra guide.

Before you go

Visiting a Shakti Peeth: what pilgrims should know

  1. Visit the Bhairava tooTradition says Shiva guards every peetha as a Bhairava, and many pilgrims visit his shrine to complete the darshan.
  2. Check timings locallyEach temple sets its own hours and aarti times, and they change on festivals. Our temple guides list what is published; otherwise confirm with the temple.
  3. Plan around NavratriQueues at every Devi temple are longest in Chaitra and Sharad Navratri — go early in the day or pick the days just before.
  4. Kamakhya and AmbubachiKamakhya closes for about three days in June for Ambubachi; time an Assam trip around it.
  5. Respect local customsModest clothes, shoes off at the gate, and follow each shrine's photography rules.
  6. Check the weatherHill evenings are cool and July–August brings heavy rain. See the weather at Naina Devi, Chintpurni weather and Kangra weather before you pack.

FAQ

Questions pilgrims ask

What are the 51 Shakti Peeths?

Shrines where, by tradition, parts or ornaments of Sati fell when Vishnu's Sudarshan Chakra divided her body. Each peetha has its own Devi and a guardian Bhairava.

Are there 51 or 52 Shakti Peeths?

51 is the most quoted count, from the Pithanirnaya. 52 is a Bengal variant — the scholar D.C. Sircar explained it as a copying confusion — and other texts give 4, 18, 64 or 108.

Which are the 18 Maha Shakti Peethas?

The seats named in the Ashtadasha Shakti Peetha Stotram, from Shankari (Sri Lanka) and Kamakshi (Kanchipuram) to Kamakhya, Jwalamukhi, Vishalakshi and Sharada in Kashmir. The full list of 18 is on this page.

Which Shakti Peeths are in Himachal Pradesh?

Naina Devi (Bilaspur), Jwalamukhi and Bajreshwari (Kangra Devi) are in the tables; Chintpurni is counted in some lists. Chamunda belongs to Himachal's local five-temple circuit.

Is Vaishno Devi in the 51 Shakti Peeth list?

No. Vaishno Devi is not in the classical 51 lists; it is counted in the broader 64 and 108 traditions.

Which Shakti Peeths are outside India?

Hinglaj and Sharada Peeth (Pakistan), five to seven in Bangladesh depending on the list, several in Nepal, Manasa near Lake Mansarovar (Tibet) and one or two in Sri Lanka, including Shankari at Trincomalee, whose identification is debated.

Which is the most important Shakti Peeth?

Traditions differ. Kamakhya in Assam is one of the four Adi Peethas and is especially revered in Tantric practice.

Why is a Bhairava worshipped at each Shakti Peeth?

Tradition holds that Shiva guards every peetha as a Bhairava. Pilgrims often visit his shrine as well to complete the darshan.

When is the best time to visit the Shakti Peeths of Himachal?

The temples are open all year. March–June and October–November are the most comfortable months. Navratri is the most auspicious and the most crowded: Chaitra Navratri 2027 runs 7–15 April and Sharad Navratri 2027 starts on 30 September. In July–August, monsoon rain can slow the hill roads.

How many days do I need for the 5 Shakti Peeths of Himachal?

Five days from Chandigarh covers Naina Devi, Chintpurni, Jwala Ji, Kangra Devi and Chamunda; add a day from Delhi. Adding Mansa Devi and Vaishno Devi (the Nau Devi route) takes about 7 days from Chandigarh or 9 from Delhi.

Is the Himachal Shakti Peeth yatra suitable for senior citizens?

Yes, with planning. No drive between two temples is longer than about 115 km, Naina Devi has a ropeway from the base, and we plan darshan early in the day to avoid long queues. Some shrines have steps and slopes, so tell us about mobility needs when you enquire.
