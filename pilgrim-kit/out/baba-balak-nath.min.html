Where is Baba Balak Nath temple and when should you visit?

Baba Balak Nath temple is a hilltop cave shrine at Deotsidh (also spelt Deothsidh or Deot Sidh) in Chakmoh village, Barsar tehsil, Hamirpur district, Himachal Pradesh — about 45 km from Hamirpur town. It is open all year; Sundays and the Chaitra mela (14 March – 13 April) draw the biggest crowds, so weekday mornings are calmest.

Sidh Baba Balak Nath — बाबा बालक नाथ — is a Siddha, a perfected yogi of the Siddha tradition, worshipped as a child who never grows old. Devotees picture him as a boy with a yogi's staff, riding a peacock. Many believe he is a form of Shiva in the Kali Yuga; in popular tradition the peacock also links him with Kartikeya. Traditions differ, and all of them meet at one cave on a Shivalik hilltop.

Not a Devi temple. Deotsidh sits in the same hills as the great Devi shrines, and many families visit it on a Himachal Shakti Peeth yatra — but Baba Balak Nath is a Siddha, not a goddess, so the shrine is not a Shakti Peeth. For us it is close to home: Deotsidh is about 33 km by road from Ghumarwin, after Naina Devi the nearest famous shrine to our office.

The legend

The story of Baba Balak Nath

Popular tellings, retold in our own words — told as tradition, as devotees and storytellers keep it.

  1. The cowherd yogiA boy-yogi, it is said, came to the Shivalik hills and agreed to graze an old woman's cows — Mai Ratno, in local tellings — in return for roti and lassi.
  2. Nothing owedYears later, when villagers blamed the cows for ruined fields and she felt she had fed him for nothing, he struck a tree and brought out every roti and every drop of buttermilk she had given.
  3. Gorakhnath's testAnother story says Guru Gorakhnath came to make the child his disciple. The boy met every test with a miracle, flew off on his peacock, and said his guru was Dattatreya.
  4. The cave and the flameHe is believed to have meditated in the Deotsidh cave, where devotees say a sacred flame, the dhuna, has burned for about 500 years.

Where he was born is told differently — some accounts name Girnar in Gujarat, others other places — so we leave that open. What is recorded: on 16 January 1987 the Himachal government took over the shrine's administration under the HP Hindu Public Religious Institutions and Charitable Endowments Act, 1984. It is run by the Shri Baba Balak Nath Temple Trust, Deotsidh, with the Deputy Commissioner of Hamirpur as Temple Trust Commissioner. The first priest is remembered as Pandit Banarasi Das; his descendants still raise the red flag that opens the Chaitra mela.

The mela follows the Chaitra solar month, not the Chaitra Navratri dates. It is the busiest month of the year, with many pilgrims from Punjab and NRI families — book stays early.

How to reach

How to reach Baba Balak Nath temple, Deotsidh

By road

About 45 km from Hamirpur town, about 60 km from Bilaspur, about 64 km from Una and about 33 km from Ghumarwin. Buses run to the Deotsidh bus stand; taxis from Hamirpur, Una or Bilaspur.

By rail

Una (broad gauge) is the nearest major station — roughly 60–65 km by road (confirm the route).

By air

Gaggal (Kangra) airport is about 120 km away; Chandigarh airport about 150–170 km.

Shahtalai

About 6 km below Deotsidh. Many pilgrims start at the Shahtalai shrine and walk up to the cave chanting.

Before you go

Before you go

  1. Avoid Sunday crowdsSundays and the mela month bring long queues. A weekday morning is the calmest darshan, especially with elders.
  2. Steps on the hilltopThe shrine sits on a hilltop with steps. The complex is being upgraded — new parking, sarai and ramps — so expect some construction work.
  3. Respect the customsDress modestly, remove footwear, and follow the staff's guidance on the women's platform and photography inside the cave.
  4. Walk to GaoosalaIf you have an hour, the easy forest walk from Deotsidh to Gaoosala and back (60–80 minutes) passes old samadhis and a stepwell.

FAQ

Questions pilgrims ask

Where is Baba Balak Nath temple?

At Deotsidh, a hilltop cave shrine in Chakmoh village, Barsar tehsil, Hamirpur district, Himachal Pradesh — about 45 km from Hamirpur town, near the Bilaspur border.

Can women enter the Baba Balak Nath cave?

By long local custom women take darshan from a platform about 5 m in front of the cave. In 2016 the temple trust said the restriction was never official. Most women follow the custom; ask the staff on the day.

When is the Baba Balak Nath Chaitra mela?

Every year from 14 March to 13 April. The 2027 mela is due from 14 March to 13 April 2027 — confirm close to the time.

What are Baba Balak Nath temple timings?

The temple is open all year, but daily hours and aarti times are not published officially, so confirm locally. During the Chaitra mela it stays open round the clock except during aarti.

Which day is special for Baba Balak Nath?

Sunday is Baba's day, and the shrine is most crowded on Sundays and weekends.

What is offered to Baba Balak Nath?

Mostly rot (roat) — a thick sweet bread made of flour, ghee and jaggery.

Which is the nearest railway station to Deotsidh?

Una (broad gauge), roughly 60–65 km by road. The nearest airports are Gaggal (Kangra), about 120 km, and Chandigarh, about 150–170 km.

Is Baba Balak Nath a Shakti Peeth?

No. Baba Balak Nath is a Siddha, a child-yogi, not a goddess, so Deotsidh is not a Shakti Peeth — though many families visit it on the Himachal Devi circuit.
