What is the Panch Kedar and which five temples are in it?

The Panch Kedar are five Shiva temples in the Garhwal Himalaya of Uttarakhand: Kedarnath, Tungnath, Rudranath, Madhyamaheshwar and Kalpeshwar. By tradition, parts of Shiva's bull form appeared at each after he hid from the Pandavas. Four close for winter; Kalpeshwar stays open all year. Seeing all five takes about 12–16 days from Rishikesh, with several mountain treks.

Kedarnath is the best known of the five and a Jyotirlinga — read our full Kedarnath Yatra guide. The other four sit in quieter valleys: Tungnath above the meadows of Chopta, Madhyamaheshwar at the end of a long walk from Ransi, Rudranath in high alpine grassland above Gopeshwar, and Kalpeshwar in the green Urgam valley. Three are in Rudraprayag district (Kedarnath, Tungnath, Madhyamaheshwar) and two in Chamoli (Rudranath, Kalpeshwar).

The Panch Kedar are a different circuit from the Panch Badri and the Panch Prayag, though many pilgrims combine them with Badrinath or the full Char Dham Yatra. Note that the Omkareshwar temple at Ukhimath, where Kedarnath is worshipped in winter, is not the Omkareshwar Jyotirlinga on the Narmada in Madhya Pradesh. The legend

The Panch Kedar legend: Shiva and the Pandavas

This is the tradition of the Mahabharata cycle and the Kedar Khand of the Skanda Purana, retold in our own words — a belief held with love, not history.

  1. Seeking pardonAfter the Kurukshetra war, the Pandavas set out to ask Shiva's forgiveness for the killing of their own kin.
  2. Hidden KashiShiva did not wish to be found so easily. He left Kashi and hid in the Garhwal hills as a bull — the place near which he hid is called Guptkashi, the hidden Kashi.
  3. Bhima holds onThe brothers recognised him in a herd. Bhima stood astride two hills so the cattle had to pass below, and as the bull sank into the earth he caught hold of its back.
  4. Five shrinesThe hump stayed at Kedarnath. It is believed the arms appeared at Tungnath, the face at Rudranath, the navel at Madhyamaheshwar and the matted hair at Kalpeshwar — and the Pandavas built a shrine at each.

One tradition adds that the head appeared at Pashupatinath in Nepal. Lists of the parts differ a little from one telling to another — Madhyamaheshwar is sometimes called the middle or belly — so read them as tradition, not a fixed record.

Route

The Panch Kedar route by road and on foot

Road distances are approximate and hill roads are slow. Traditionally the order is Kedarnath, Tungnath, Rudranath, Madhyamaheshwar, Kalpeshwar; many pilgrims follow the road order below instead.

  1. ~216 km
    Rishikesh to Ukhimathऊखीमठ

    Five to six hours of hill driving up the Alaknanda and Mandakini valleys. Ukhimath is the base and the winter seat of Kedarnath and Madhyamaheshwar.

  2. ~42 km
    Ukhimath to Sonprayagकेदारनाथ

    Shuttle to Gaurikund, then about 16 km on foot to Kedarnath (pony, palki or IRCTC helicopter).

  3. Ransi
    Madhyamaheshwarमध्यमहेश्वर

    Drive from Ukhimath to the road head at Ransi and walk about 16 km via Gaundar to the temple.

  4. ~26 km
    Ukhimath to Choptaतुंगनाथ

    About 3.5 km on a short but steep path to Tungnath; Chandrashila peak is about 1.5 km further.

  5. ~44 km
    Chopta to Gopeshwarरुद्रनाथ

    From Sagar near Gopeshwar, about 18–20 km up through forest and high meadows to Rudranath — the hardest walk.

  6. ~55 km
    Gopeshwar to Helangकल्पेश्वर

    Turn into the Urgam valley; Kalpeshwar is a short walk from the road head.

Rudranath and Madhyamaheshwar are multi-day treks for fit walkers with a guide. Avoid the monsoon (July–August), when landslides often block these roads.

How to reach

How to reach the Panch Kedar

By air

Jolly Grant airport, Dehradun, is the nearest. Rishikesh is a short drive away, and the hill journey to Ukhimath starts there.

By train

Rishikesh and Haridwar stations (Haridwar–Rishikesh about 24 km). Most trips start from either town early in the morning.

By road

Rishikesh to Ukhimath is about 216 km. Chopta, Sonprayag, Ransi and Gopeshwar are all reached from there — see the route above. For a car, our Rishikesh taxi page has options.

Helicopter and ponies

A helicopter runs only for Kedarnath — book it only on heliyatra.irctc.co.in. Ponies and porters work on most of the trails; confirm rates at the counters.

Sample itinerary

A sample 13-day Panch Kedar plan from Rishikesh

  1. Day 1
    Rishikesh → Ukhimath

    About 216 km. Evening darshan at the Omkareshwar temple.

  2. Day 2
    Ukhimath → Gaurikund → Kedarnath

    Drive to Sonprayag, shuttle to Gaurikund and walk about 16 km to Kedarnath.

  3. Day 3
    Kedarnath → Sonprayag

    Morning darshan, walk down and drive back towards Ukhimath.

  4. Day 4
    Ransi → Gaundar

    Drive to Ransi and walk to the halt at Gaundar.

  5. Day 5
    Madhyamaheshwar

    Walk up to the temple for darshan and stay the night.

  6. Day 6
    Madhyamaheshwar → Ukhimath

    Walk back to Ransi and drive to Ukhimath or Chopta.

  7. Day 7
    Tungnath and Chandrashila

    Early walk from Chopta to Tungnath, on to Chandrashila for the sunrise peaks, and back.

  8. Day 8
    Chopta → Gopeshwar

    About 44 km. Darshan at the Gopinath temple.

  9. Day 9
    Sagar → Rudranath trail

    Start the long walk from Sagar with a guide and halt on the way.

  10. Day 10
    Rudranath

    Darshan at Rudranath, where Shiva is worshipped as the face.

  11. Day 11
    Rudranath → Gopeshwar

    Walk down to Sagar and drive back to Gopeshwar.

  12. Day 12
    Kalpeshwar

    Drive about 55 km to Helang, into the Urgam valley, and walk to Kalpeshwar.

  13. Day 13
    Return

    Long drive back towards Rishikesh, or keep it as a spare day for weather.

Before you go

Before you go

  1. RegistrationKedarnath needs the Uttarakhand Char Dham registration on registrationandtouristcare.uk.gov.in. No separate registration is reported for the other four — check the Uttarakhand portal and temple committee notices before you go. Ignore anyone selling "confirmed" passes or helicopter tickets.
  2. Which ones suit eldersKedarnath (with pony or helicopter), Tungnath (short but steep) and Kalpeshwar (a short walk) are realistic for most families. Rudranath and Madhyamaheshwar are for fit trekkers.
  3. AltitudeFour of the five temples are above about 3,400 m. Walk slowly, drink water and see a doctor first if you have heart, breathing or blood-pressure problems.
  4. Best timeMay–June and September–October. In winter you can still pray at the winter seats — Ukhimath, Makkumath, Gopeshwar — and at Kalpeshwar, all by road.
  5. CarryPhoto ID and your Kedarnath registration; warm layers, a rain shell, grippy shoes and a stick; a torch, personal medicines and some cash, as networks are patchy.

FAQ

Questions pilgrims ask

What are the Panch Kedar temples?

Five Shiva temples in the Garhwal Himalaya: Kedarnath, Tungnath, Rudranath, Madhyamaheshwar and Kalpeshwar. By tradition, parts of Shiva's bull form appeared at each.

What is the Panch Kedar story?

According to tradition, the Pandavas sought Shiva's pardon after the Kurukshetra war. He hid from them as a bull, sank into the earth when found, and parts of his body appeared at five places.

Which Panch Kedar temple is open all year?

Kalpeshwar, in the Urgam valley of Chamoli district. The other four close for winter.

Which is the highest Panch Kedar temple?

Tungnath, at about 3,680 m — widely regarded as the highest Shiva temple in the world. The walk from Chopta is about 3.5 km.

Which Panch Kedar trek is the hardest?

Rudranath, about 18–20 km from Sagar near Gopeshwar, followed by Madhyamaheshwar, about 16 km from Ransi.

When did the Panch Kedar temples close in 2026?

Rudranath closed in mid-October, Tungnath on 6 November and Kedarnath on 11 November; Madhyamaheshwar closes on 16 November 2026. Kalpeshwar stays open.

How many days does the Panch Kedar Yatra take?

About 12–16 days from Rishikesh for all five, depending on weather, fitness and the order you choose.

Where are the Panch Kedar deities worshipped in winter?

Kedarnath and Madhyamaheshwar at the Omkareshwar temple, Ukhimath; Tungnath at Makkumath; Rudranath at the Gopinath temple, Gopeshwar.
