What are the Dwarkadhish temple timings, and why is it so sacred?

Per Incredible India, the Dwarkadhish temple in Dwarka, Gujarat is open 6:30 am–1 pm and 5–9:30 pm; aarti times vary, so confirm locally, especially on Janmashtami. Here Krishna is worshipped as king of Dwarka, and the temple is one of the four all-India Char Dham, with Badrinath, Puri and Rameswaram.

The Dwarkadhish temple (द्वारकाधीश मंदिर), also called Jagat Mandir, the "universal shrine", stands in Dwarka town, Devbhumi Dwarka district, Gujarat, on the Gomti creek where it meets the Arabian Sea. The deity is Krishna as Dwarkadhish, the Lord of Dwarka. The temple faces west, towards the sea.

Tradition counts Dwarka among the four dhams of the all-India Char Dham linked with Adi Shankaracharya, and among the Sapta Puri, the seven sacred cities. This is a different circuit from the Himalayan Char Dham yatra of Uttarakhand, which shares only Badrinath. It is also not the Dwarkadhish temple of Mathura — a separate shrine with its own timings.

Most families make Dwarka a full day: darshan at Dwarkadhish, the Rukmini temple, Nageshwar Jyotirlinga and Bet Dwarka — and many continue along the coast to Somnath.

The legend

The story of Dwarka and Dwarkadhish

Told in our own words, as tradition and the Puranas remember it — faith, not a history lesson.

  1. A golden city on the seaTradition holds that Krishna left Mathura with the Yadavas after repeated attacks, and that the divine architect Vishwakarma raised a golden city for him on the western sea — Dwarka, "the gateway".
  2. Krishna as kingKrishna ruled here as king. That is why he is worshipped in Dwarka as Dwarkadhish, the Lord of Dwarka, rather than as the playful child of Vrindavan.
  3. The sea takes the cityThe Mahabharata and the Puranas say that after Krishna left the world, the sea swallowed Dwarka. Marine finds off the coast are still debated by archaeologists.
  4. A shrine over the palaceIt is believed that Vajranabha, Krishna's great-grandson, built the first shrine over Krishna's palace, the Hari-griha. The temple we see today rose on that sacred spot.

Two more traditions are told in Dwarka. One says a devotee carried the old image to Dakor, and a new image was later installed here. Another explains why Rukmini's temple stands about 2 km outside town: Sage Durvasa, it is said, cursed Krishna and Rukmini to live apart.

History, as far as we know it: archaeologists date the site's earliest temple to around the 2nd century BCE. The temple was destroyed in 1473 by Mahmud Begada, and the present building is largely of the 15th–16th century; the present image is recorded as installed in 1559.

How to reach

How to reach Dwarkadhish temple

By train

Dwarka railway station is about 2 km from the temple, on the line from Ahmedabad, Rajkot and Jamnagar to Okha.

By air

Porbandar airport is about 107 km away (around 1.5 hours) and Jamnagar about 125–135 km (around 2 hours). Rajkot, about 225 km away, has more flights.

By road

NH-947 runs into Dwarka. Rajkot is about 225 km (around 3 hours), Ahmedabad about 440 km (around 6 hours) and Somnath about 237 km along the coast.

At the temple

Enter by the Swarga Dwar on the south side and leave by the Moksha Dwar facing the market. The 56 steps go down to the Gomti ghats; the market side is fairly level.

Sample itinerary

A simple Dwarka plan (2–3 days)

  1. Day 1
    Arrive in Dwarka

    Arrive by train at Dwarka, or by road from Porbandar or Jamnagar airport. Evening darshan at Dwarkadhish and the aarti, then a walk on the Gomti ghats.

  2. Day 2
    Rukmini, Nageshwar, Bet Dwarka

    Early darshan, then Rukmini Devi temple, Nageshwar Jyotirlinga and Bet Dwarka across Sudarshan Setu. Back to Dwarka for the evening.

  3. Day 3
    On to Somnath (optional)

    Drive about 237 km along the Saurashtra coast to Somnath, the first Jyotirlinga, for the evening aarti.

Before you go

Tips for a calm darshan

  1. Go earlyArrive before 6:30 am or in the first evening hour. Janmashtami, the Diwali–New Year week and the December–January holidays bring very long queues.
  2. With eldersUse the level market-side approach rather than the 56 steps from the ghats, and keep the full-day circuit gentle — the midday closure is a good rest.
  3. Travel lightKeep phones and valuables to a minimum and follow the gate notices. Wear modest clothes; footwear stays outside.
  4. SeasonThe temple is open all year. October to March is the most comfortable; summer on the coast is hot and humid.
Dhwaja

The 52-yard flag of Dwarkadhish

Above the tower flies the temple's dhwaja, made from 52 yards of cloth and visible from about 10 km away (Devbhumi Dwarka district). It bears the sun and the moon; devotees believe this says Krishna will remain in Dwarka as long as the sun and moon shine.

The flag is changed several times every day — sources differ on whether it is four or five times. Each new flag is offered by a devotee family and carried up with music and prayers, and the slots are booked far in advance. To offer one, ask at the temple office in Dwarka; we do not know of an official online booking site, so be wary of websites selling flag offerings.

Across the water

Bet Dwarka and Sudarshan Setu

Bet Dwarka (Beyt Dwarka) is an island off Okha, about 31 km from the Dwarkadhish temple. Tradition remembers it as Krishna's residence, and its Keshavraiji (Dwarkadhish) temple recalls the story of Sudama bringing his friend a gift of rice — so pilgrims still offer rice here. The Hanuman Dandi shrine, with Hanuman and his son Makardhwaja, is on the island too.

Since 25 February 2024 the island has been linked to Okha by Sudarshan Setu, a 2,320 m cable-stayed bridge with footpaths on both sides, so you can now drive across. Boats from Okha jetty (about 15–20 minutes) still run. The island temple's hours are reported as roughly 9 am–1 pm and 5–7 pm — confirm locally before you plan the afternoon.

FAQ

Questions pilgrims ask

What are Dwarkadhish temple timings?

Per Incredible India, 6:30 am to 1 pm and 5 pm to 9:30 pm. Aarti times vary and the schedule changes on Janmashtami and other festivals, so confirm locally.

Why is the flag on Dwarkadhish temple changed so often?

The 52-yard dhwaja is offered by devotee families and is changed several times every day. It bears the sun and moon — believed to mean Krishna will stay in Dwarka as long as they shine.

Is Dwarka one of the Char Dham?

Yes — of the all-India Char Dham linked with Adi Shankaracharya, with Badrinath, Puri and Rameswaram. The Uttarakhand Char Dham yatra is a different, Himalayan circuit.

How far is Bet Dwarka from Dwarkadhish temple?

About 31 km by road to Okha, then across Sudarshan Setu, the 2.3 km bridge opened in February 2024. Boats from Okha jetty also run.

How far is Nageshwar Jyotirlinga from Dwarka?

About 16–18 km on the Okha road, roughly 25–35 minutes by car, so it fits easily into the same day.

Why is Rukmini's temple outside Dwarka?

Tradition says Sage Durvasa's curse made Krishna and Rukmini live apart, so her temple stands about 2 km outside town.

Which is the nearest airport and railway station?

Dwarka railway station is about 2 km from the temple. The nearest airports are Porbandar (about 107 km) and Jamnagar (about 125–135 km).

How many days do I need for Dwarka?

One full day covers Dwarkadhish, Rukmini, Nageshwar and Bet Dwarka. Add a day to arrive, and another if you continue to Somnath, about 237 km away.
