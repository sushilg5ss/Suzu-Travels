Where is Hidimba Devi Temple and what are its timings?

The Hidimba Devi Temple stands in the Dhungri deodar forest, about 2 km from central Manali, in Kullu district, Himachal Pradesh. Built in 1553 by Raja Bahadur Singh of Kullu, it honours Hidimba, Bhima's wife in the Mahabharata, worshipped here as the valley's goddess. It is open from about 8 am to 6 pm — confirm locally, as winter hours can be shorter.

Hidimba Devi Temple — हिडिम्बा देवी मंदिर — is the best-known shrine of the Kullu valley. Locals call it the Hadimba temple or simply the Dhungri temple, after the forest of Dhungiri Van Vihar in which it stands. For the people of the valley Hidimba is no distant legend: she is their kuldevi and gram devi, the family and village goddess.

The temple is built in the Himachal pagoda style, about 24 metres high: three tiers of square wooden roofs crowned by a brass cone, rising from a whitewashed stone base. The low doorway is framed by rich wood carving — Durga, animals and foliage, dancers, scenes from Krishna's life and the nine planets (Navagrahas) — and the outer walls are hung with old antlers and ibex horns. Hidimba is not counted among the Shakti Peeths; she is revered here in her own right.

The legend

The story of Hidimba Devi

The Mahabharata tells the first half of this story. Kullu tradition tells the rest — and the temple stands where the two meet.

  1. A forest of rakshasasAccording to the Mahabharata, the Pandavas, escaping the burning house of lac, rested in a forest ruled by the rakshasa Hidimb. He sent his sister Hidimbi to lure them.
  2. Hidimbi and BhimaInstead she fell in love with Bhima. Bhima defeated Hidimb in a duel, and with Kunti's blessing Hidimbi married him.
  3. GhatotkachaTheir son was Ghatotkacha, the mighty warrior who later gave his life for the Pandavas at Kurukshetra.
  4. Penance on a rockKullu tradition says that when the Pandavas moved on, Hidimbi stayed in these mountains, gave the kingdom to her son and meditated on a rock in the forest. Through her penance — blessed by Durga, it is believed — she came to be worshipped as a goddess.

The temple was raised over that very rock in 1553 by Raja Bahadur Singh of Kullu, who is still remembered at a fair in his name each Shravan. In 2008 the Archaeological Survey of India, the National Culture Fund and UCO Bank signed an agreement to improve visitor facilities here.

How to reach

How to reach Hidimba Devi temple

From Manali town

About 2 km from central Manali. Walk up through the deodar forest, or take a taxi or auto to the temple lane — then a short slope and a few steps.

By air

Kullu–Manali Airport at Bhuntar is about 51 km by road. Chandigarh is the larger airport, a long day's drive away.

By rail

Joginder Nagar, a narrow-gauge station, is about 160 km away. Most travellers come by road from Chandigarh or Delhi instead.

By road

Overnight Volvo buses and taxis reach Manali from Delhi and Chandigarh; the temple is on the edge of town, in Dhungri.

Before you go

Before you go

  1. Go earlyQueues at the small doorway grow by late morning in season. Early morning is quiet, and the forest light is lovely.
  2. Easy for eldersNo ropeway or pony needed — just a short slope and steps. The forest path is uneven and slippery after rain or snow.
  3. Respect the shrineDress modestly, remove footwear, don't climb the carved woodwork, and follow the priests' instructions on photography.
  4. Keep the forest cleanDhungiri Van Vihar is a protected deodar forest — carry your litter back.
What you will see

What is inside the Hidimba Devi temple?

Many visitors are surprised: there is no large idol in human form. The sanctum is built over a big natural rock — the rock of Hidimba's penance in local tradition. Here a footprint of the goddess carved on a stone block is worshipped, along with a small brass image, about 7.5 cm (3 inches) tall. Bow low as you enter: the carved doorway is small.

About 70 metres away, under the deodars, is the shrine of her son Ghatotkacha — a sacred tree that is worshipped in his name, not a separate temple building. Most pilgrims visit both.

FAQ

Questions pilgrims ask

What are Hidimba Devi temple timings?

About 8 am to 6 pm, as published by Incredible India. Aarti times are not published officially, and winter hours can be shorter — confirm locally.

What is inside the Hidimba Devi temple?

There is no large idol. The shrine is built over a big rock, where a footprint of the goddess carved in stone and a small brass image, about 7.5 cm tall, are worshipped.

Who built the Hidimba Devi temple?

Raja Bahadur Singh of Kullu built it in 1553, in the Himachal pagoda style: three wooden roofs crowned by a brass cone.

Who was Hidimba?

In the Mahabharata she was the sister of the rakshasa Hidimb; she married Bhima and was the mother of Ghatotkacha. Kullu tradition says she later meditated here and came to be worshipped as a goddess.

How far is Hidimba temple from Manali?

About 2 km from central Manali, in the Dhungri deodar forest. You can walk up or take a taxi to the temple lane.

Is there an entry fee at Hidimba Devi temple?

No ticket is known to be charged. Confirm locally, and beware of anyone selling 'darshan passes'.

When is the Hidimba Devi mela?

The Dhungri Mela, her birthday fair, is held around 14 May every year. She also travels to Kullu for the Dussehra festival in October.

Is Hidimba Devi a Shakti Peeth?

No. She is not in any classical Shakti Peeth list; she is a Mahabharata figure revered as the goddess of the Kullu valley. Himachal's Devi circuit of Naina Devi, Chintpurni, Jwala Ji, Kangra and Chamunda is a separate yatra.
