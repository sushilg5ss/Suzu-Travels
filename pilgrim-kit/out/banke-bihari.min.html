Shri Banke Bihari Ji Temple (बांके बिहारी मंदिर) stands in the narrow old-town lanes of Vrindavan, Mathura district, Uttar Pradesh, a short walk from Nidhivan. Its deity was first worshipped as Kunj Bihari. "Banke" means bent in three places — Krishna's graceful tribhanga pose — and "Bihari" is one who delights. The temple's tradition holds that the single dark image is Radha and Krishna united, and the seva is still carried out by the Goswami families, descendants of Swami Haridas's disciple.

For many families the Banke Bihari mandir is the heart of a Vrindavan visit: the jostling, singing crowd, the cry of "Radhe Radhe", and the curtain that falls and rises before the deity. Most pilgrims pair it with Nidhivan, ISKCON's Krishna-Balaram temple and the white marble Prem Mandir about 3 km away, or travel on to Ayodhya and Kashi. See all our pilgrimage guides.

The legend

The story of Banke Bihari Ji and Swami Haridas

Told in our own words, as the temple's tradition remembers it — a story of devotion, not a history lesson.

  1. A saint who sang in NidhivanAccording to the temple, Swami Haridas was born on Radha Ashtami in 1478 CE. A saint and musician, he spent his days singing to Radha and Krishna in the groves of Nidhivan.
  2. Two become oneIt is believed that, moved by his music and at his request, the divine couple appeared before him and merged into a single dark image. Haridas worshipped it as Kunj Bihari.
  3. The Goswamis' sevaHaridas gave the seva to his disciple Goswami Jagannath, and his descendants still serve the deity. Haridas is also remembered as the teacher of Tansen, and a well-loved story tells of Akbar coming in disguise to hear him sing.
  4. A new home in the lanesThe image was later moved from Nidhivan to the present temple, built by the Goswamis in 1862 according to the temple (other sources say the 1860s). The day of the appearance is celebrated every year as Bihar Panchami.

The customs you see today grow out of that tender devotion. A curtain (parda) is drawn every few minutes — tradition says Bihari Ji's gaze is so captivating that a devotee could lose themselves in it. No bells are rung, because the deity is cared for like a child. The Mangla aarti happens only once a year, after Janmashtami; the deity holds a flute only on Sharad Purnima; his feet (charan) are shown only on Akshaya Tritiya; and in Shravan he sits on a festive swing for the jhulan.

How to reach

How to reach Banke Bihari temple

By train

Mathura Junction is about 13 km away (about 20 minutes by road) and has far better connections than Vrindavan station.

By air

Noida International Airport at Jewar (flights since 15 Jun 2026) is about 90 km away; Agra airport about 70 km; Delhi's IGI airport about 150 km, around 2 hours by the Yamuna Expressway.

By road

Cars cannot reach the temple door. Park at one of Vrindavan's parkings and walk or take an e-rickshaw through the lanes. Buses stop at Chhatikara on NH-44. See our Delhi to Vrindavan taxi.

Nearby

Nidhivan is about 1 km (closer on foot), ISKCON's Krishna-Balaram temple about 2.5 km and Prem Mandir about 3 km. Radha Vallabh, Seva Kunj, Radha Raman and Keshi Ghat are within walking distance.

Sample itinerary

A simple Vrindavan plan around Banke Bihari (1–2 days)

  1. Day 1
    Banke Bihari and Vrindavan's temples

    Banke Bihari darshan at opening time, then walk to Nidhivan, Seva Kunj and the Radha Raman and Radha Vallabh temples while the temple is closed at midday. In the evening, ISKCON's Krishna-Balaram temple, Keshi Ghat on the Yamuna at sunset and Prem Mandir for its lit marble and fountain show.

  2. Day 2
    Mathura (optional)

    Drive about 13 km to Mathura for the Krishna Janmabhoomi temple and the ghats, then head home — or on to Agra.

Before you go

Tips for a calm darshan

  1. Go at opening timeWeekday mornings from October to March are the calmest. Arrive before the doors open and avoid weekends, Ekadashi, Purnima and Amavasya.
  2. With elders and childrenThe lanes are flat but very narrow and crowded, with no vehicles to the door. On festival days the temple advises elders and small children to stay away.
  3. Travel lightNo phones, leather, umbrellas or footwear inside. Carry little cash, keep your spectacles safe from monkeys and hold children's hands.
  4. Holi and JanmashtamiSpectacular, but extremely crowded. Book your stay early and plan darshan on a quieter day either side.
Before you enter

Banke Bihari darshan rules, VIP darshan and crowds

No VIP darshan, no online booking. VIP slips were stopped in 2025 and the temple says there is no dedicated VIP darshan — everyone joins the same queue. Ignore websites and WhatsApp offers selling "Banke Bihari VIP darshan" or online passes; none are official.

What to leave behind. The temple's own guidelines ask devotees not to bring mobile phones or other electronic gadgets, leather items, umbrellas or footwear inside. Keep your phone in your hotel or vehicle. Watch for pickpockets in the crush, and for the Vrindavan monkeys — they are fond of spectacles.

Crowds. The temple advises elders and small children to avoid festival days; Ekadashi, Purnima, Amavasya and weekends are also very crowded. Follow the marked entry and exit gates and the railing queues. A pilgrim facilitation corridor is planned, but for now the lanes remain narrow.

FAQ

Questions pilgrims ask

What are Banke Bihari temple timings?

Per the temple website's summer schedule (till 10 Nov), it opens 7:45 am–12 noon and 5:30–9:30 pm. Winter timings began on Bhai Dooj, 11 Nov 2026, and run till Holi. The court-appointed committee has also extended hours since 2025, so confirm locally on the day.

Why is the curtain drawn again and again at Banke Bihari?

Tradition says Bihari Ji's gaze is so captivating that devotees could lose themselves in it, so the curtain (parda) is drawn every few minutes during darshan.

Why are no bells rung in Banke Bihari temple?

The deity is cared for as a child, and bells are not rung so as not to disturb him.

When is the Mangla aarti at Banke Bihari?

Only once a year, in the early hours after Janmashtami, with a limited number of entry passes. On other days the first aarti is the Shringar aarti, soon after opening.

Is there VIP or online darshan at Banke Bihari?

No. VIP slips were stopped in 2025, everyone queues together, and there is no official online booking. Avoid websites or agents selling "VIP darshan".

Are mobile phones allowed inside Banke Bihari temple?

The temple's guidelines ask devotees not to bring phones or electronic gadgets, leather items, umbrellas or footwear. Leave your phone in your hotel or vehicle.

How far is Banke Bihari from Mathura Junction and Delhi?

About 13 km (around 20 minutes) from Mathura Junction, then a short walk or e-rickshaw through the lanes. Delhi's IGI airport is about 150 km away, around 2 hours by the Yamuna Expressway.

What is the best time to visit Banke Bihari?

Weekday mornings from October to March, at opening time. Avoid weekends, Ekadashi, Purnima, Amavasya and festival days if you are travelling with elders or small children.
