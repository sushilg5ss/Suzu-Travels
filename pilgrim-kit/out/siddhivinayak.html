Where is Siddhivinayak temple and why is it so loved?

Shree Siddhivinayak Ganapati Temple stands in Prabhadevi, Mumbai, about 2 km from Dadar station. Its black-stone Ganesha, with the trunk turned to the right, was first consecrated on 19 November 1801. Mumbaikars begin new work, exams and journeys with his darshan. Doors open with the 5:30 am Kakad Aarti (3:15 am on Tuesdays) and darshan is free.

The temple is run by the Shree Siddhivinayak Ganapati Temple Trust. The idol is a single black stone about 2.5 feet high and 2 feet wide. Its four hands hold a lotus, a small axe, a rosary and a bowl of modaks, and an eye is etched on the forehead, like Shiva's third eye. Riddhi and Siddhi, the goddesses of prosperity and fulfilment, stand on either side. A right-turned trunk is rare — most Ganesha idols turn it to the left — and many devotees believe such a Ganapati answers prayers quickly but asks for careful worship.

Note that this is not one of the Ashtavinayak temples. The Ashtavinayak Siddhivinayak is at Siddhatek in Ahilyanagar (Ahmednagar) district. Mumbai is also the gateway to the Maharashtra Jyotirlingas — Trimbakeshwar, Bhimashankar and Grishneshwar — and to Shirdi. See all our pilgrimage guides.

The legend

The story and history of Siddhivinayak temple

Told in our own words, as tradition tells it. The Puranas give several versions of Ganesha's birth; this is the one most families in Maharashtra know.

  1. A guardian of clayAccording to the Puranas, Parvati shaped a boy from clay and set him to guard her door while she bathed.
  2. The elephant headShiva, not knowing the boy, was stopped at the door and struck off his head. To console Parvati, he gave the child the head of an elephant and brought him back to life.
  3. Honoured firstShiva made him lord of the ganas, his hosts, and declared that he would be worshipped before every other god — Vighnaharta, the remover of obstacles.
  4. The Vinayaka of siddhiSiddhivinayak is the Vinayaka who grants siddhi — success and fulfilment. In Mumbai, people come to him before a new job, an exam, a journey or a wedding.

The temple trust's records say the shrine was first consecrated on Thursday, 19 November 1801, a date noted in government records. It was then a small structure holding the black-stone idol; Incredible India names Laxman Vithu Patil and his wife Deubai Patil as its builders. Over the years the sanctum was rebuilt again and again, and the temple has grown into the large, many-storeyed building that pilgrims see today.

How to reach

How to reach Siddhivinayak temple

By train

Dadar station (Central and Western lines) is about 2 km away — a short taxi ride or a walk. Mumbai Central is about 7 km and CSMT about 11 km.

By metro

The Siddhivinayak metro station on Mumbai Metro Line 3 (Aqua Line) is about 200 m from the temple.

By air

Mumbai airport (Terminal 2) is about 15 km away. Allow 45–90 minutes in Mumbai traffic.

Nearby temples

Mahalakshmi temple and Mumbadevi temple are a short drive away and fit easily into the same day.

Before you go

Before you go

  1. Pick your dayTuesdays, Angaraki Sankashti and New Year's Day are the most crowded. A weekday mid-morning or afternoon is calmer, especially with elders.
  2. Dress modestlyThe trust asks devotees to wear decent, traditional clothes; revealing or indecent clothing is not allowed inside.
  3. Phones and shoesSwitch your phone off before you enter, and deposit it outside if staff ask (they often do on crowded days). Leave footwear at the stands outside the gate.
  4. Only official sevasBook poojas only on siddhivinayak.org or the trust's app. Ignore agents selling "VIP darshan" over WhatsApp or UPI.

FAQ

Questions pilgrims ask

What are Siddhivinayak temple timings?

From Wednesday to Monday the day begins with the Kakad Aarti at 5:30 am and ends with the Shej Aarti at 9:50 pm, with short breaks for the noon naivedya and the evening aartis. On Tuesdays darshan opens at 3:15 am and the gates close at 11:30 pm.

What are the aarti timings at Siddhivinayak?

Kakad Aarti 5:30–6 am (5–5:30 am on Tuesday), dhoop aarti 7–7:10 pm, evening aarti 7:30–8 pm and Shej Aarti at 9:50 pm (11:45 pm on Tuesday), as per the trust's schedule.

Why is Tuesday special at Siddhivinayak?

Tuesday is Ganapati's day for many Mumbai devotees, so the temple opens at 3:15 am and stays open late. When a Sankashti Chaturthi falls on a Tuesday — an Angaraki Sankashti — the queues are the longest of the year.

Is there an entry fee or online booking?

General darshan is free. The trust offers live darshan, online pooja booking and personalised online darshan on its own website and app. Use only those, never agents selling VIP darshan.

Can I take my mobile phone inside?

The trust asks devotees to switch phones off before entering, and on crowded days phones and bags are deposited at counters outside. Follow the staff's instructions on the day.

Is there a dress code at Siddhivinayak temple?

Yes. Wear decent, traditional clothes that cover the body; revealing or indecent clothing is not allowed inside.

Is Mumbai's Siddhivinayak one of the Ashtavinayak temples?

No. The Ashtavinayak Siddhivinayak is at Siddhatek in Ahilyanagar (Ahmednagar) district. The Prabhadevi temple in Mumbai is a separate, much-loved shrine.

Which is the nearest station to Siddhivinayak temple?

Dadar station, about 2 km away on both the Central and Western lines. The Siddhivinayak metro station on Metro Line 3 is about 200 m from the temple.
