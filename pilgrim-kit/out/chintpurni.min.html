Where is Chintpurni temple and why is it sacred?

Mata Shri Chintpurni stands in Chintpurni village (once called Chhaproh) in Una district, Himachal Pradesh, about 40 km north of Una town. The goddess is Chhinnamastika, one of the ten Mahavidyas, and she is believed to end her devotees' worries (chinta). Many lists count it among the Shakti Peeths of Himachal. The 2026 Sharad Navratri mela was held 11–20 October; Chaitra Navratri 2027 begins on 7 April.

The goddess of Chintpurni is Chhinnamastika (Chhinnamasta), the Mahavidya who is shown holding her own severed head — a form that speaks of the Mother giving even herself for those who depend on her. The temple trust describes the dham as guarded by Shiva, as Rudra Mahadev, on all four sides: Kaleshwar Mahadev to the east, Narahna to the west, Muchhkund to the north and Shiv Bari to the south.

Chintpurni is the second stop on the five Shakti Peeths of Himachal circuit, after Naina Devi and before Jwala Ji, and one of the nine shrines of the Nau Devi Yatra. Please note that this is the Himachal temple — the Chhinnamasta temple at Rajrappa in Jharkhand is a different shrine of the same goddess.

The legend

The story of Chintpurni

The temple trust tells the Chhinnamasta story, and a later tale of one devotee whose worries she lifted. This is how they are told.

  1. Hunger after the battleAfter a fierce battle, the Mother's two attendants, Jaya and Vijaya, were still burning with hunger and thirst, and nothing could satisfy them.
  2. The Mother gives herselfOut of compassion she struck off her own head, and streams of her blood nourished them. Devotees call this form Chhinnamastika — the goddess who holds nothing back.
  3. A hill ringed by ShivaTradition held that her seat would be a place guarded by Shiva on every side. This hill in Una, with a Shiva shrine at each of the four points, is believed to be that place.
  4. Mai Das at ChhaprohMuch later, a devotee named Pandit Mai Das from the old Patiala state is said to have received her darshan at Chhaproh village. She ended his worries, so she came to be called Chintpurni.

The temple's own account says Mai Das founded the shrine about twelve generations ago, and his descendants still serve as its priests; there is no dated record of the building, so we don't give a year. Where Chintpurni sits among the Shakti Peeths is debated: compiled 51-peetha tables say Sati's foot fell here, popular Himachal tradition says her forehead, and some accounts speak of her consciousness. The temple itself tells the Chhinnamasta story rather than naming a body part, and so do we.

Darshan & aarti

Chintpurni darshan timings and booking

Temple hoursNot published officially — confirm locally or on the trust's site. Hours are longer during Navratri.
AartiThree daily aartis — morning (Pratahkal), midday and evening (Sandhya). Clock times are not published officially.
QueueTake a darshan slip (parchi) from the temple guard and join the official queue. The trust asks pilgrims not to use back-door or middleman darshan.
Sugam DarshanPaid Sugam Darshan can be booked online only on the official site, matashrichintpurni.com — check the current fee there.
Fake sitesBook only on the temple trust's own site. Look-alike booking pages and agents selling "VIP darshan" are not the trust.

How to reach

How to reach Chintpurni

By road

About 40 km north of Una town. From the other Shakti Peeths: about 31 km from Jwala Ji and about 112 km from Naina Devi. Ghumarwin is about 147 km via Una (a shorter hill road may exist).

By rail

The nearest stations are in Una district — Una Himachal and Amb Andaura. Confirm the road distance from your train's station locally.

By air

Gaggal (Kangra) airport is the usual choice, with Chandigarh as the larger airport for more flights. Plan the last stretch by road.

The last stretch

From the bus stand and parking it is a short walk up the bazaar lane to the temple. There is no ropeway, pony or palki service published for Chintpurni.

Sample itinerary

A two-day Chintpurni and Jwala Ji trip

  1. Day 1
    Naina Devi → Chintpurni

    Morning darshan at Naina Devi, then about 112 km down to Una and up into the hills to Chintpurni. Evening darshan with a parchi; overnight at Chintpurni or Bharwain.

  2. Day 2
    Chintpurni → Jwala Ji

    Early darshan at Chintpurni, then a short 31 km to Jwala Ji for the flames. Continue to Kangra and Chamunda to complete the five Shakti Peeths, or head home.

Before you go

Before you go

  1. Parchi firstCollect your darshan slip from the temple guard before joining the queue, and use only the official line.
  2. Book Sugam Darshan only on the trust's sitePaid Sugam Darshan is sold on matashrichintpurni.com. Beware of look-alike websites and touts offering quick darshan.
  3. Respect the temple's rulesThe trust asks pilgrims not to feed the monkeys, not to smoke or drink, and not to drop prasad on the floor. Follow the notices about photography inside.
  4. Plan around the melasIn Navratri and the Sawan mela, go early in the morning, keep bags small, carry water and keep children close in the crowd.
  5. Elders and wheelchairsWheelchair facilities are not published — confirm locally before you travel. A weekday visit outside the melas is much easier for elders.
  6. Where to staySimple sarais and guest houses are in Chintpurni town, and HPTDC's Chintpurni Heights is at Bharwain nearby.

FAQ

Questions pilgrims ask

What are Chintpurni temple timings?

The temple trust does not publish clock times. There are three daily aartis — morning, midday and evening — and hours are longer during Navratri. Check matashrichintpurni.com or confirm locally before you go.

How do I book Sugam Darshan at Chintpurni?

Paid Sugam Darshan is booked online only on the temple trust's official site, matashrichintpurni.com, where the current fee is shown. Avoid look-alike sites and touts offering VIP darshan.

When is the next Chintpurni Navratri mela?

The next is Chaitra Navratri, from 7 April 2027. The 2026 Sharad (Asuj) Navratra mela was held from 11 to 20 October, and the Sawan Ashtami mela is held in July–August (2027 dates not announced yet).

Why is the goddess called Chintpurni?

Because she is believed to free her devotees from worry (chinta). Tradition says she lifted the worries of her devotee Pandit Mai Das at Chhaproh village, which later took the name Chintpurni.

Who is Chhinnamastika?

One of the ten Mahavidyas — the goddess who, in the temple's legend, cut off her own head so that her blood could feed her hungry attendants Jaya and Vijaya.

Which part of Sati fell at Chintpurni?

Traditions differ. Compiled 51-peetha tables say her foot, popular Himachal tradition says her forehead, and some accounts say her consciousness. The temple itself tells the Chhinnamasta story.

How far is Chintpurni from Jwala Ji and Una?

About 31 km by road from Jwala Ji and about 40 km from Una town. From Naina Devi it is about 112 km.

Do I need a darshan slip at Chintpurni?

Yes. The trust asks every pilgrim to take a darshan parchi from the temple guard and join the official queue, and not to use back-door or middleman darshan.
