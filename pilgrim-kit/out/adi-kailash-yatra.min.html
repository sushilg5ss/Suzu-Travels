What is the Adi Kailash Yatra and how do you go?

Adi Kailash (5,945 m), also called Chhota Kailash, is a sacred peak in the Vyas valley of Dharchula, Pithoragarh district, Uttarakhand. Most pilgrims also see Om Parvat (5,590 m) from Nabhidhang. You need an Inner Line Permit, verified with a medical check in Dharchula. Since 2020 the road reaches Jolingkong; a yatra takes about 6–9 days from Kathgodam or Tanakpur.

Adi Kailash — the first Kailash — is also known as Chhota Kailash, Shiva Kailash, Baba Kailash and Jolingkong peak. It stands in the Vyas (Kuthi Yankti) valley of Dharchula tehsil, Pithoragarh district, in the far east of Kumaon. Below it lies a still sacred lake (Parvati Sarovar / Gauri Kund) with a small Shiva–Parvati temple on its shore at Jolingkong, about 4,500 m up. It is counted as the second most sacred of the Panch Kailash after Mount Kailash; the others are Shrikhand, Kinner Kailash and Manimahesh Kailash in Himachal.

Om Parvat is a different peak, in a different direction: it is seen from the Nabhidhang camp near Lipulekh, where snow on its face forms a shape like the sacred syllable ॐ. Many websites mix up Adi Kailash, Om Parvat and Mount Kailash in Tibet — they are three different mountains, and most Adi Kailash yatras visit the first two. Since the roads of 2020 the journey is mostly a drive, not a long trek, but it remains a high-altitude, permit-only trip close to the border. The legend

The legend of Adi Kailash and Om Parvat

These are beliefs of devotees and the lore of the Kumaon valleys, retold in our own words — tradition held with love, not history.

  1. The first KailashDevotees believe Adi Kailash was a Himalayan home of Shiva and Parvati — a peak whose form recalls Mount Kailash across the border, which is why it is also called Chhota Kailash.
  2. A lake that mirrors the peakThe still lake at its foot is said to hold the mountain's reflection, and pilgrims pray at the small Shiva–Parvati temple on its shore.
  3. Om written in snowA short drive away, snow settles on Om Parvat in the shape of ॐ. The Shiva Purana speaks of Om as existing before creation, and local lore counts this peak among the natural Om forms of the world.
  4. The valley of VyasThe valley carries the name of the sage Ved Vyas, and the old pilgrim and trade path towards Mansarovar runs through it to the Lipulekh pass.

The yatra has been organised since 1981, alongside the Kailash Mansarovar Yatra. When roads reached Gunji and Lipulekh in May 2020 and Jolingkong in July 2020, the long walk shrank to a short one, and after the Prime Minister's visit on 12 Oct 2023 pilgrim numbers rose sharply — about 29,352 permits in 2024, 36,526 in 2025, and a record of more than 52,000 visitors in the first two months of the 2026 season.

Route

The Adi Kailash route: Kathgodam to Jolingkong

Road distances are approximate; these are slow mountain roads with landslide-prone stretches. Most pilgrims start from Kathgodam or Tanakpur.

  1. ~270 km
    Kathgodam to Dharchulaधारचूला

    Usually over two days via Pithoragarh (Pithoragarh to Dharchula about 90 km). From Tanakpur it is about 240 km.

  2. Permit stop
    Dharchulaधारचूला

    In-person permit verification and medical check at the SDM office. The last ATMs are here — carry cash.

  3. ~70–80 km
    Dharchula to Gunjiगुंजी

    About 5–6 hours up the Vyas valley. Spend a night here to acclimatise; homestays at Gunji, Nabhi and Kuti.

  4. ~36 km
    Gunji to Jolingkongज्योलिंगकांग

    Via Kuti village to the road head, then a short high-altitude walk to the lake and the Shiva–Parvati temple facing Adi Kailash.

  5. ~26 km
    Gunji to Nabhidhangनाभीढांग

    The viewpoint for Om Parvat, near Lipulekh.

Delhi to Dharchula is about 570 km — two days with halts. Expect little or no mobile network beyond Gunji, and follow ITBP and Army instructions at checkpoints, including on photography.

How to reach

How to reach Adi Kailash

By train

Kathgodam (about 270 km to Dharchula) or Tanakpur (about 240 km). Trips usually start early in the morning from either station.

By air

Pantnagar airport is about 330 km by road. Pithoragarh's Naini Saini airstrip is about 90 km from Dharchula — check current flights before you plan around it.

By road

Drive to Pithoragarh and Dharchula, then by taxi or 4×4 to Gunji, Jolingkong and Nabhidhang. KMVN, the state tourism corporation, also runs packages.

Helicopter

A state helicopter darshan has run in some seasons; we could not confirm 2026 operations, so don't plan around it until it is announced.

Before you go

Before you go

  1. AltitudeJolingkong is about 4,500 m. Acclimatise at Gunji, walk slowly and drink water. Fit elders can go with a doctor's clearance; it is not advisable for people with heart or lung problems — ask your doctor.
  2. Weather and roadsAvoid the monsoon (July to mid-September), when landslides and bridge washouts close the road. Keep a spare day and follow district advisories.
  3. Cash and networkNo ATMs beyond Dharchula — carry enough cash. Expect little or no mobile network beyond Gunji.
  4. Warm layersEven in May–June and October it is cold at Jolingkong and Nabhidhang. Carry a down jacket, gloves, cap and sunglasses.
  5. Respect the border zoneCarry your permit and ID at all times and follow ITBP and Army instructions on photography and movement.
  6. Keep it cleanCarry your plastic back, don't disturb the lake, and leave nothing at the shrine but prayers.
Inner Line Permit

Adi Kailash permit (ILP): how it works

Adi Kailash and Om Parvat lie in a protected border area, so every Indian visitor needs an Inner Line Permit.

  1. Apply online through the official Uttarakhand channels (KMVN / e-District) once the season opens.
  2. Go in person to the SDM office in Dharchula for verification and a compulsory medical check.
  3. Carry the permit, photo ID (Aadhaar) and your medical certificate through every checkpoint.

The reported age range is 10 to 70 years — confirm it when you apply. There is a small permit fee; see the official notice. The administration has warned about fake permits sold by agents: apply only through official channels such as the Pithoragarh district site and KMVN. Foreign nationals fall under separate protected-area rules — check with the Pithoragarh administration.

FAQ

Questions pilgrims ask

Where is Adi Kailash?

In the Vyas valley of Dharchula, Pithoragarh district, Uttarakhand, close to the Tibet border. The pilgrim point, Jolingkong, is about 36 km beyond Gunji.

Is Adi Kailash the same as Om Parvat?

No. They are two different peaks in the same region. Adi Kailash (5,945 m) is seen from Jolingkong; Om Parvat (5,590 m) from Nabhidhang. Most yatras visit both.

Do I need a permit for Adi Kailash?

Yes, an Inner Line Permit. Apply online through official channels, then complete in-person verification and a medical check at the SDM office in Dharchula. Beware of fake permits.

When does the Adi Kailash Yatra open?

In 2026 it opened on 1 May; permits paused for the monsoon from 2 July and resumed from about 20 September. Dates for 2027 are not announced yet.

What is the best time to visit Adi Kailash?

May–June and late September–October, outside the monsoon. Check the permit status and road conditions with the district before you travel.

Is Adi Kailash a trek?

Not any more for most pilgrims. Since 2020 the road reaches Jolingkong and Nabhidhang; there are only short walks, but at very high altitude.

How many days does the Adi Kailash Yatra take?

About 6–9 days from Kathgodam or Tanakpur, including a night at Gunji to acclimatise and a spare day for weather.

Can senior citizens go to Adi Kailash?

The reported upper age limit is 70. With an altitude of about 4,500 m and a compulsory medical check, it suits only fit elders with a doctor's clearance.
