Which are the Shakti Peeths in Himachal Pradesh?

Five Devi temples are grouped as the Shakti Peeths of Himachal Pradesh: Naina Devi (Bilaspur), Chintpurni (Una), Jwala Ji, Brajeshwari and Chamunda (all in Kangra). Tradition holds that parts of Sati's body fell at the first four; Chamunda joins the group by local tradition. All five fit in one road loop of about 378 km — four to five days at an easy pace.

The legend

The story behind the Shakti Peeths

One grief, carried across the worlds, left the Goddess present in many places. This is how tradition tells it.

  1. Daksha's yagnaSati, daughter of Prajapati Daksha, had married Shiva against her father's wishes. Daksha held a great yagna and invited every god except Shiva.
  2. Sati gives up her bodySati went anyway and heard her husband mocked before the guests. Unable to bear it, she gave up her body — in the fire, say some texts; through yogic power, say others.
  3. Shiva's griefVirabhadra, born of Shiva's anger, destroyed the yagna. Shiva lifted Sati's body and wandered the worlds, his dance of mourning threatening creation itself.
  4. The peethas are bornVishnu's Sudarshan Chakra divided the body. Wherever a part fell, the Goddess is believed to dwell — a peetha, a seat, each guarded by a form of Shiva called Bhairava.

How many peethas are there? The texts disagree — 4, 18, 51, 52, 64 or 108 depending on the tradition — and compiled lists differ on names and body parts. We retell the legend as tradition and note where the sources part ways.

Route

The 5 Shakti Peeth route, with distances

The natural order runs from the plains up into Kangra: Naina Devi first, Chamunda last. Distances are by road.

The five temples lie within a small corner of Himachal — Bilaspur, Una and Kangra. From our base near Ghumarwin the full loop is about 378 km; from Chandigarh, Naina Devi is the first stop, about 100 km away.

    Chandigarh or Ghumarwin

  4. ~36 km

How to reach

How to reach the Shakti Peeths of Himachal

By air

Chandigarh airport is closest to Naina Devi (about 100 km). Gaggal (Kangra) airport is about 8 km from Brajeshwari and about 25 km from Chamunda.

By rail

Anandpur Sahib and Kiratpur Sahib stations serve Naina Devi. Pathankot is the broad-gauge railhead for Kangra; the Kangra Valley narrow-gauge line stops at Jawalamukhi Road and Kangra.

By road

Delhi to Naina Devi is about 350–370 km. Within the circuit every leg is under 115 km — a private car or taxi is the easiest way to link all five.

Temple by temple

The five Shakti Peeths of Himachal, one by one

1 · Naina Devi, Bilaspur
नैना देवी

On a Shivalik hilltop above the Gobind Sagar lake. Tradition says Sati's eyes fell here; a later legend tells of a herd boy named Naina who found the Devi's pindi, and the temple carries his name. A short cable car climbs from the base. Not to be confused with the Naina Devi temple in Nainital.

2 · Chintpurni, Una
चिंतपूर्णी

Seat of Chhinnamastika, the Goddess shown holding her own head. The temple trust tells how she appeared to the devotee Pandit Mai Das and freed him of his worries (chinta) — hence the name. Which part of Sati fell here is disputed (foot, forehead or her consciousness, depending on the source).

3 · Jwala Ji, Kangra
ज्वाला जी

No idol: small flames rising from the rock are worshipped as the Devi, where tradition places Sati's tongue. Devotees see the Goddess in the flames; geologists describe natural gas seeping from the ground. Legend adds that Emperor Akbar tried and failed to put the flames out.

4 · Brajeshwari (Kangra Devi)
ब्रजेश्वरी

In Kangra town, old Nagarkot. Tradition places Sati's left breast here and credits the Pandavas with the first shrine. Raided more than once in medieval times and flattened by the 1905 Kangra earthquake, it was rebuilt each time; every Makar Sankranti the pindi is covered in butter (ghrit).

5 · Chamunda, near Dharamshala
चामुंडा

On the Baner river below the Dhauladhar. Chamunda is the fierce Devi who, in the Devi Mahatmya, slew the demons Chanda and Munda. It is not in the classical Shakti Peeth lists, but local tradition counts it among Himachal's five great Devi shrines.

FAQ

Questions pilgrims ask

How many Shakti Peeths are there in Himachal Pradesh?

Five are commonly grouped: Naina Devi, Chintpurni, Jwala Ji, Brajeshwari (Kangra Devi) and Chamunda. Strictly, Jwala Ji, Brajeshwari and Naina Devi appear in the classical 51-peetha lists, Chintpurni appears in some, and Chamunda is included by local tradition.

Which part of Sati fell at each temple?

Tradition places her eyes at Naina Devi, her tongue at Jwala Ji and her left breast at Brajeshwari. For Chintpurni sources differ — foot, forehead or her consciousness. Chamunda is not linked to a body part.

How many days do I need for all five Shakti Peeths?

Four to five days is comfortable. The road loop is about 378 km from Ghumarwin, and no single leg is longer than about 115 km.

What is the best order to visit them?

Naina Devi → Chintpurni → Jwala Ji → Brajeshwari (Kangra) → Chamunda. This follows the road from the plains up into Kangra and ends near Dharamshala and Gaggal airport.

Is Chamunda Devi a Shakti Peeth?

Not in the classical lists. It is one of Himachal's five great Devi shrines by local tradition, which is why it is part of the circuit.

When is the next Navratri, and how crowded do the temples get?

The next is Chaitra Navratri, from 7 April 2027; Sharad Navratri 2026 was held 11–19 October, with Dussehra on 20 October. All five temples see their biggest crowds in both Navratris — start early each day.

Why does the flame at Jwala Ji never go out?

Devotees worship the flames as the Goddess herself. Geologically, they come from natural gas seeping through the rock — both views live side by side at Jwala Ji.

Which is the nearest airport for the Shakti Peeth circuit?

Chandigarh for Naina Devi (about 100 km) and Gaggal (Kangra) for the three Kangra temples. Many pilgrims arrive at Chandigarh and leave from Gaggal.
