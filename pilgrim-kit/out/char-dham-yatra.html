What is the Char Dham Yatra and how does it work?

The Char Dham Yatra is a pilgrimage to four Himalayan shrines in Uttarakhand — Yamunotri, Gangotri, Kedarnath and Badrinath — traditionally visited west to east. Every pilgrim must register free on the state portal or app. By road it takes about 10–12 days from Haridwar, with two treks. The shrines open in late April or early May and close for winter around November.

The Char Dham (4 Dham) Yatra of Uttarakhand — also called the Chhota Char Dham — joins four shrines high in the Garhwal Himalaya: Yamunotri, source of the Yamuna; Gangotri, where the Ganga is worshipped; Kedarnath, Shiva's Himalayan Jyotirlinga; and Badrinath, Vishnu as Badri Narayan. It is different from the all-India Char Dham of Badrinath, Dwarka, Puri and Rameswaram.

Badrinath and Kedarnath are managed by the Shri Badarinath-Kedarnath Temple Committee (BKTC); Gangotri and Yamunotri by their own temple committees. Gangotri and Badrinath can be reached by road, while Yamunotri (about 6 km) and Kedarnath (about 16 km) need a walk, a pony, a palki — or, for Kedarnath, a helicopter booked only on IRCTC.

The legend

Why these four shrines

Tradition credits Adi Shankaracharya with reviving these four Himalayan shrines as one circuit in the 8th century. Each dham carries its own story — told here as tradition, not history.

  1. Yamunotri — the sister of YamaPilgrims honour the river goddess Yamuna, sister of Yama, the lord of death. A dip here is believed to free one from the fear of an untimely death.
  2. Gangotri — Bhagiratha's penanceGangotri remembers King Bhagiratha, whose long penance is said to have brought the Ganga down from heaven to free his ancestors.
  3. Kedarnath — the hidden bullShiva is believed to have hidden from the Pandavas as a bull. As it sank into the earth, its hump remained in the rock at Kedarnath.
  4. Badrinath — the sheltering treeVishnu is said to have meditated here in the bitter cold, while Lakshmi stood over him as a badri (jujube) tree to shelter him.

The order of the yatra follows the land as much as the legends: from the Yamuna valley in the west, across to the Bhagirathi, up the Mandakini to Kedarnath and finally up the Alaknanda to Badrinath. Many families also add Kedarnath and Badrinath alone as a shorter Do Dham yatra.

Route

The Char Dham route, west to east

The traditional order is Yamunotri → Gangotri → Kedarnath → Badrinath. Distances and drive times are approximate and depend on roads and weather.

The four dhams light up in yatra order, from Haridwar to Badrinath.

  1. ~210 km
    Haridwar / Rishikesh to Barkotबड़कोट

    About 7–8 hours by road; registration counters and railheads at the start.

  2. 45 km + ~6 km trek
    Yamunotriयमुनोत्री

    Drive to Janki Chatti, then walk about 6 km (or take a pony or palki). A day's round trip from Barkot.

  3. ~200 km
    Gangotriगंगोत्री

    Via Uttarkashi and Harsil; the road reaches the temple. Allow one to two days.

  4. ~220 km
    Guptkashi / Sitapurगुप्तकाशी

    From Uttarkashi via Tehri and Rudraprayag, about 8–9 hours. Kedarnath helipads lie along this stretch.

  5. ~5 km + ~16 km trek
    Kedarnathकेदारनाथ

    Shuttle from Sonprayag to Gaurikund, then about 6–8 hours uphill — or pony, palki, pithoo or helicopter.

  6. ~200 km
    Badrinathबद्रीनाथ

    From Guptkashi via Ukhimath, Chamoli and Joshimath, about 7–8 hours; drive-up to the temple.

  7. ~300 km
    Back to Rishikesh / Haridwarऋषिकेश

    About 9–10 hours down the Alaknanda valley.

Landslides and weather can halt traffic for a few hours even in October — keep a buffer day.

How to reach

How to reach the start of the yatra

By air

Jolly Grant Airport, Dehradun, is the nearest airport to Haridwar and Rishikesh.

By rail

Haridwar, Rishikesh and Dehradun are the railheads; most yatras start from Haridwar.

By road

Cabs, tempo travellers and buses run from Delhi and Haridwar into the hills.

By helicopter

Kedarnath shuttles fly from Guptkashi, Phata and Sersi — book only on heliyatra.irctc.co.in after registration (2 tickets per user ID, 6 pilgrims per ticket). Full "Char Dham by helicopter" trips from Dehradun are private charters.

Sample itinerary

A sample 11-day Char Dham Yatra from Haridwar

  1. Day 1
    Haridwar to Barkot

    A drive of about 210 km (7–8 hours) into the Yamuna valley.

  2. Day 2
    Yamunotri darshan

    Drive to Janki Chatti, walk about 6 km to Yamunotri, darshan, and return to Barkot.

  3. Day 3
    Barkot to Uttarkashi

    Drive to Uttarkashi and rest for the next day's climb to Gangotri.

  4. Day 4
    Gangotri darshan

    Drive via Harsil to Gangotri for darshan, then back to Uttarkashi or a stay near Harsil.

  5. Day 5
    Uttarkashi to Guptkashi

    A long drive via Tehri and Rudraprayag, about 8–9 hours.

  6. Day 6
    Trek to Kedarnath

    Shuttle from Sonprayag to Gaurikund and walk about 16 km (or fly from your helipad). Evening aarti and a night near the temple.

  7. Day 7
    Kedarnath to Guptkashi

    Early darshan, then walk down to Gaurikund and drive back.

  8. Day 8
    Guptkashi to Badrinath

    Drive via Ukhimath, Chamoli and Joshimath, about 7–8 hours. Evening darshan.

  9. Day 9
    Badrinath darshan

    Early morning darshan at Badrinath, then drive down towards Joshimath or Rudraprayag.

  10. Day 10
    Towards Rishikesh

    Drive down the Alaknanda valley towards Rishikesh.

  11. Day 11
    Rishikesh to Haridwar

    Reach Haridwar for your onward train, flight or drive home.

Before you go

Before you go

  1. Best timeMay–June and mid-September to October. July–August is peak monsoon, with landslides and road closures.
  2. Health check for eldersUttarakhand advises a check-up for anyone over 50 or with heart, lung or blood-pressure conditions. Acclimatise on the way, use the screening kiosks on the Kedarnath and Yamunotri routes, and come down if you feel unwell.
  3. Senior citizensNo official age limit has been announced for Char Dham. Gangotri and Badrinath are drive-up; for Yamunotri and Kedarnath use a pony or palki, or the Kedarnath helicopter.
  4. Helicopter: only IRCTCBook Kedarnath helicopter tickets only on heliyatra.irctc.co.in. Ignore WhatsApp sellers and cloned websites offering "confirmed seats".
  5. What to carryThe QR registration letter and photo ID, warm layers, a rain jacket, trekking shoes, medicines and ORS, a torch, a power bank and some cash — ATMs and mobile network are patchy.
  6. Winter Char DhamFrom November to April the deities are worshipped at their winter seats — Kharsali (Yamuna), Mukhba near Harsil (Ganga), Omkareshwar temple at Ukhimath (Kedarnath) and Pandukeshwar / Jyotirmath (Badrinath) — all by road. 2026–27 winter dates are not announced yet.
Free and compulsory

Char Dham Yatra registration, step by step

Every pilgrim — and every vehicle — must be registered. It is free. Use only the official channels: the portal registrationandtouristcare.uk.gov.in, the Tourist Care Uttarakhand app, the state's WhatsApp service, or offline counters such as those at Haridwar and Rishikesh. Helpline 1364 or 0135-2559898.

  1. Sign up with your mobile number and complete the Aadhaar-based e-KYC (in use since 2025).
  2. Create a tour and choose your dates for each dham.
  3. Add every pilgrim in your group.
  4. Download the QR Yatra Registration Letter — it is checked at each dham.

Keep the registration number handy: you need it to book a Kedarnath helicopter. Any website or agent charging a "registration fee" is fake.

FAQ

Questions pilgrims ask

When does the Char Dham Yatra 2027 start?

Not announced yet. Gangotri and Yamunotri open on Akshaya Tritiya; Kedarnath's date is fixed on Mahashivratri (6 March 2027) and Badrinath's on Basant Panchami (11 February 2027). The yatra usually begins in late April or early May.

How do I register for the Char Dham Yatra?

Register free on registrationandtouristcare.uk.gov.in, the Tourist Care Uttarakhand app, the state WhatsApp service or an offline counter, then download the QR registration letter. Helpline 1364.

Is there a fee for Char Dham registration?

No. Official registration is free — any site or agent charging a registration fee is fake.

What is the correct order of the Char Dham Yatra?

Traditionally west to east: Yamunotri, then Gangotri, then Kedarnath and finally Badrinath.

How many days are needed for the Char Dham Yatra?

About 10–12 days by road from Haridwar or Delhi. A Do Dham yatra to Kedarnath and Badrinath takes about 5–6 days.

Can senior citizens do the Char Dham Yatra?

Yes, with a medical check-up and careful planning. Gangotri and Badrinath are drive-up; for Yamunotri and Kedarnath use a pony or palki, or the Kedarnath helicopter booked on IRCTC.

When do the dhams close in 2026?

Not announced yet. By custom Gangotri closes on Annakut, Yamunotri and Kedarnath on Bhai Dooj (around 10–11 November), and Badrinath's date is fixed on Vijayadashami, 20 October 2026. See our Char Dham closing dates post.

Can I do the Char Dham Yatra in winter?

Not at the main shrines. In winter the deities are worshipped at Kharsali, Mukhba, Ukhimath and Pandukeshwar / Jyotirmath, which are reachable by road.
