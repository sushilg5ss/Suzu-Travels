# Mountains of India / Mountaineering in India: SEO Plan for suzutravels.com

Prepared 2026-10-01. Scope: a new informational section with pillar pages (highest peaks by altitude band from 3,000m to 8,586m, Himachal peaks, unclimbed peaks, records, IMF permits, courses, famous peaks), plus enquiry capture for guided expeditions run with authorised Himachal operators. Audience: global (US, UK, EU, Australia, Israel) plus India.

## 0. Data sources and caveats

- **Volumes come from Google Ads Keyword Planner** (API, Suzu Travels account 1172710099). They are 12-month averages from Sep 2025 to Aug 2026, English, Google Search only. The pulls were:
  - worldwide
  - US
  - UK
  - India
  - a combined "Intl-6" set: US + GB + AU + IL + DE + FR
- **The figures are banded.** The account has little recent spend, so Google returns range buckets (10, 20, 30 ... 1,300, 1,600, 1,900, 2,400, 2,900 ... 110,000) instead of exact counts. Treat them as orders of magnitude.
- **Some keywords are grouped as close variants**, so they share one number:
  - "mountains in india" = "mountaineering in india" = 14,800
  - "highest peaks in india" = "highest mountain in india" = 110,000

  Split the combined figure by intent. Most of the "mountains in india" demand is informational.
- **Some keywords are inflated by other meanings:**
  - "indian mountaineering foundation" (301K) almost certainly includes "IMF" searches for the International Monetary Fund. Treat the real figure as about 5-15K a month (estimate).
  - "trishul" (74K) is mostly about the religious symbol. "kamet" (27K) includes Kamet whisky. "kinner kailash" (74K) is pilgrimage (yatra) demand, not climbing.
- **India dominates generic volume.** "highest peaks in india" gets 90.5K in India versus 1.6K in the US and 720 in the UK. International demand is concentrated in named peaks and expedition terms:
  - kangchenjunga: 8.1K Intl-6
  - nanda devi: 4.4K Intl-6
  - kamet: 3.6K Intl-6
  - changabang: 590 of its 1.0K worldwide volume comes from the Intl-6 markets
  - "mountains in india": 2.4K in the US
  - "unclimbed mountains": 720 Intl-6
- **Nepal and generic expedition terms are much larger:**
  - everest expedition: 8.1K
  - mera peak: 18.1K
  - ama dablam: about 2K

  India-specific expedition terms are tiny. For example, "himalayan expedition india" and "7000m peaks india" show about 10 a month. The international opportunity is therefore **informational first**. Win the peak and entity queries, build authority, and convert with enquiry blocks. Don't chase head expedition terms.
- **Check facts before publishing.** Heights differ between sources; for example, Hanuman Tibba is quoted as both 5,982m and 5,932m. Fee figures below come from the IMF's 2018 foreign-team schedule as reported by third parties (verified July 2026 by theverticaltribe.com). Verify against indmount.org or the IMF PDF before publishing. Pick one height source (Survey of India, IMF, or Wikipedia/Peakvisor) and cite it on every table.

**Takeaways**
1. Friendship Peak, Hanuman Tibba, Deo Tibba, Yunam and Kanamo are Suzu's home turf. They carry real mid-tail volume (1-9K worldwide each), low competition and direct enquiry intent. They are the fastest route to leads.
2. For global traffic, the big entity pages matter most: Kangchenjunga, Nanda Devi, Kamet, Changabang/Shivling, and "mountains in india" in the US. These are H/M difficulty and build topical authority over 6-12 months. Win them with angles that Wikipedia and Nepal operators don't cover: India-side status, closure history, viewpoints and records.
3. The large Indian volume on "highest peaks in india" is exam-style (UPSC/GK) demand. It is dominated by Wikipedia and edtech sites. It's still worth one excellent data page: it anchors the dataset, earns links and feeds AI answers. Don't expect leads from it.

**Formats that win:** sortable tables with units, top-10 summaries above the fold, status boxes ("Open / Closed as of <date>"), FAQ sections, itinerary day-by-day, and calculators. Maps and altitude profiles are rare, which is a differentiator.

**Content gaps a new site can own:**
1. One master dataset (about 300 peaks at 5,000m and above) with height (m/ft), state, district, range, prominence, first ascent (year, team), climbed/unclimbed, IMF open/closed/restricted, grade, nearest road-head, and source. Reuse it across the band, state and peak pages, and offer it as a downloadable CSV (Dataset schema).
2. Altitude-band pages (3,000-4,999, 5,000-5,999, 6,000-6,999, 7,000m and above, plus 8,000m: Kangchenjunga and the K2 note).
3. Per-state pages (HP, Uttarakhand, Ladakh, Sikkim, J&K, Arunachal) plus the "highest point of every state" page.
4. Permit fees explained in USD with worked examples and a calculator.
5. A records and first-ascents timeline.
6. The K2 question, answered neutrally: India's official maps and textbooks list K2 (8,611m, in Gilgit-Baltistan, administered by Pakistan) as India's highest, while Kangchenjunga is the highest peak on Indian-administered territory. This is a top People-Also-Ask/AI query; answer it in the first paragraph.
7. "Is it open?" status boxes with dates (Stok Kangri, Nanda Devi, Kangchenjunga from Sikkim).

## (b) Recommended URL structure and site architecture

**Folder decision.** Make `/mountains-of-india/` a new top-level folder for the informational cluster. It keeps topical signals clean and gives short, keyword-rich URLs. Put the one commercial enquiry page under the existing `/adventure/` hub as `/adventure/himalayan-peak-expeditions/`. The existing `/adventure/` page already has `/adventure/snow-activities-manali/` as a child. This splits intent cleanly: information in one folder, sales in the other. Every informational page still carries an enquiry block.

**Page templates**
- **Peak page:**
  - Answer box: height in m/ft, state/district, range, first ascent, status (open/closed and IMF band), grade, best months, days needed.
  - Then: location map, how to reach the road-head, route and camps table with altitudes (altitude profile chart), day-by-day itinerary, difficulty and prerequisites, permits and fees (USD/INR), cost breakdown, gear, history and notable ascents, viewpoints, FAQs.
  - "Nearby peaks" (by height, up and down).
  - Enquiry form (Himachal and Ladakh peaks only; for closed peaks, a "suggested alternatives" block).
- **Band and state pages:**
  - Intro answer paragraph, then a sortable table from the dataset (filters: state, band, status, beginner/technical).
  - A map with pins, "Top 10 notable" cards linking to peak pages, methodology and sources, CSV download, FAQs.
- **Commercial page:**
  - Peaks offered (cards with grade, days, season and "from" price in USD/INR).
  - How it works: enquiry → itinerary → IMF permit handled by the authorised operator → pre-climb acclimatisation in Manali.
  - Trust: HP Tourism registration, safety and insurance, guide ratios.
  - FAQs, and the form (fields: peak, dates, group size, experience level, country, WhatsApp).
  - **Don't name partner operators.** Say "authorised, IMF-affiliated Himachal operators and certified guides".

**Title and currency conventions**
- Show heights as "m (ft)" everywhere, because the US audience reads feet.
- Show prices as USD first, then INR, with a "prices from" date.
- Add a "Last verified: <date>" line under each H1.

## (d) Schema recommendations (JSON-LD via Rank Math custom schema or a code snippet)

| Page type | Schema |
|---|---|
| All pages | `BreadcrumbList`. `Article` (or `WebPage`) with `author` (a named person with a bio and credentials: mountaineer/guide reviewer), `reviewedBy`, `dateModified`, and `about` → the Mountain entities. `Organization` (TravelAgency) with `identifier`/`hasCredential` for the HP Tourism registration, linked via `publisher`. |
| Hub | `CollectionPage` + `ItemList` of child pages. |
| Highest-peaks, band and state pages | `ItemList` (itemListOrder: Descending) whose `ListItem`s point to `Mountain` items (name, url, geo, containedInPlace → the State as `AdministrativeArea`, `sameAs` → Wikipedia/Wikidata). `Dataset` for the master table: name, description, `license` (e.g. CC BY 4.0, attribution to Suzu Travels, which earns links), `distribution` → `DataDownload` (CSV), `temporalCoverage`, `spatialCoverage`, `creator`, `dateModified`. Dataset makes the page eligible for Google Dataset Search, and AI models like it too. Add `Table` on the table section (optional). |
| Peak pages | `Mountain` (schema.org Landform > Place): `name`, `alternateName` (local names, e.g. "CCKN", "Chau Chau Kang Nilda"), `geo` (GeoCoordinates with elevation "6001 m"), `containedInPlace`, `sameAs` (Wikipedia, Wikidata QID, Peakvisor), `description`, `image`. `additionalProperty` (PropertyValue) for first ascent year, prominence, IMF band and status. |
| Peak pages that sell | Add `TouristTrip`: itinerary as an `ItemList` of `Place`s per day, `touristType` "mountaineers", `provider` → Suzu Travels, and `offers` (price from, priceCurrency USD and INR, availability, validFrom). Don't add a review/AggregateRating unless the reviews are first-party and genuine. |
| IMF fees page | `HowTo` (the application steps; Google no longer shows HowTo rich results, but it helps AI parsing) plus `FAQPage`. A `WebApplication` for the calculator (optional). |
| Courses page | An `ItemList` of `Course` items with `provider` as the institute (`EducationalOrganization`, e.g. ABVIMAS Manali), plus `hasCourseInstance` (courseMode "onsite", duration, location). Only use values that are published on official sites. |
| Records page | `ItemList` of `Event`-like items is risky. Prefer an `Article` with a timeline table and `mentions` → Person/Mountain entities. |
| FAQs | Add `FAQPage` on every page with 4-8 real PAA questions. Google shows FAQ rich results only for government and health sites since 2023, but the markup still feeds Bing/Copilot and AI answer extraction. |
| Commercial page | No `Product` markup (to avoid merchant-listing errors). Use `TouristTrip` list + `Organization` + `ContactPoint` (WhatsApp/phone, `availableLanguage` en/hi) + `FAQPage`. |

## (e) AEO / AI-answer (ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, Google AI Overviews, Copilot) tips

1. **Answer-first paragraph.** Put a 40-60 word direct answer under every H1 and H2 question, using the entity, the number and a date. For example: "Kangchenjunga (8,586 m) in Sikkim is the highest mountain in India, and the third-highest in the world. India's official maps also show K2 (8,611 m) in the Pakistan-administered Gilgit-Baltistan region as Indian territory, which is why some textbooks list K2." Handling the K2 nuance clearly is exactly what AI answers quote.
2. **Question-shaped H2s**, matching the People Also Ask questions. Examples:
   - "What is the highest peak in Himachal Pradesh?"
   - "Is Stok Kangri open in 2026?"
   - "How much does an IMF permit cost for foreigners?"
   - "Which is the easiest 6000m peak in India?"
   - "Can foreigners climb in India without a liaison officer?"
3. **Clean, citable facts.** Use tables with units in the headers (m, ft, USD, INR), one fact per cell, and consistent numbers across all pages. They come from one dataset, so an AI never sees conflicting heights on the same site. State the source per column ("Height: Survey of India / IMF").
4. **Freshness signals.** Put "Last verified: 1 Oct 2026" and "Status as of <date>" boxes on peaks that can close or reopen. Keep `dateModified` updated in the schema, and do quarterly status sweeps (IMF open-peaks list, Leh district orders, state fee changes).
5. **Entity alignment.** Link each peak to its Wikipedia/Wikidata entry via `sameAs`. Use the canonical name in the first sentence and alternates once (e.g. "Chau Chau Kang Nilda (CCKN, 'Blue Moon in the Sky')"; "Leo Pargial / Reo Purgyil"; "Kanchenjunga / Kangchenjunga").
6. **Original data AIs can't find elsewhere:** the downloadable CSV (CC BY), worked USD cost examples, and first-hand trip notes from operator guides (dates, snow conditions, summit rates). This is information gain, and it earns citations in AI answers.
7. **Crawlability for AI bots.** Check robots.txt and any CDN/WAF bot rules: allow GPTBot, OAI-SearchBot, PerplexityBot, ClaudeBot, Google-Extended and Bingbot. Submit to Bing Webmaster Tools (ChatGPT search and Copilot use Bing) and enable IndexNow in Rank Math. Serve the tables as HTML, not only as JS-rendered widgets: render the sortable table server-side and add JS sorting on top.
8. **Add an `llms.txt`** (optional, low cost) listing the hub, dataset page, IMF fees and Himachal peaks with one-line descriptions.
9. **E-E-A-T for a DMC writing about mountaineering:**
   - A named author or reviewer with climbing credentials (e.g. a BMC/AMC-certified guide or IMF-empanelled instructor; they don't need to be tied to a named partner operator).
   - An "About our data" methodology page.
   - Outbound citations to IMF, ABVIMAS, the Himalayan Club and the Himalayan Journal.
   - The HP Tourism registration on the commercial page.
10. **International audience touches.**
    - Heights in feet, prices in USD, seasons stated as months (not "post-monsoon" alone), and flight/visa notes (Delhi → Manali/Bhuntar; the e-Visa vs mountaineering visa question on the guide page).
    - Israeli travellers are a large Himachal segment. Consider a short Hebrew summary page for Friendship Peak and Hanuman Tibba in phase 3, with `hreflang="he"`. Otherwise keep a single English version (x-default) with no en-US/en-GB split.
11. **Media for multi-modal answers:** original photos with descriptive filenames and alt text ("deo-tibba-summit-ice-cap-6001m.jpg"), an altitude-profile SVG per peak, and one embedded map. Short vertical videos (HyperFrames reels) of each peak embedded with `VideoObject` schema help Google Discover and video panels.
