## Season, access status and permits (as of 28 Oct 2026)
- No commercial climbing scene: all recorded recent ascents are Army, NIMAS or club expeditions. Peaks sit in border districts (Tawang, West/East Kameng, Bichom) near the McMahon Line.
- Indians: eILP (14-day tourist). Foreigners: PAP via an approved operator + US$30 royalty; peaks need IMF permission.
- Window: post-monsoon, late Sep–Nov (recorded climbs).

## Questions people ask
1. **Which is the highest peak in Arunachal Pradesh?** Kangto, 7,090 m (some sources give 7,042–7,060 m), on the India–Tibet border in Bichom district.
2. **Has Kangto been climbed?** Yes — an 18-member Indian Army Eastern Command team made the first recorded ascent on 26 November 2025 by the southern route.
3. **Which is the second-highest peak in Arunachal?** Nyegyi Kansang, 7,047 m; a 1995 Indian team claimed it, but they stopped about 600 m below the summit, so it is still counted as unclimbed.
4. **What is Gorichen peak?** A 6,488 m peak in the Tawang–West Kameng area used for Army training; a West Bengal club made the first civilian ascent on 21 October 2024.
5. **Which Arunachal peak is named after the 6th Dalai Lama?** Tsangyang Gyatso (6,383 m), first climbed by NIMAS Dirang on 25 September 2024.
6. **Do I need a permit to visit Arunachal Pradesh?** Indians need an eILP (online, up to 14 days); foreigners need a Protected Area Permit through an approved tour operator, with a US$30 royalty.
7. **Can foreigners climb in Arunachal?** Only with a PAP plus IMF permission; all the state's big peaks are in restricted border areas, and recent climbs have been Army or institute expeditions.
8. **When is the best time to climb in Arunachal?** Recent ascents were made from late September to November, after the monsoon.
