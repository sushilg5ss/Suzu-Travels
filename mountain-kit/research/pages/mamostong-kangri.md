## Season, access status and permits   (as of Nov 2026)
- Season: ascents recorded in May (1988), July (2025), August (1992, 2007, 2010) and September (1984, 2007, 2019 announced 30 Sep). Page: "summer to early autumn; Army teams have also climbed it in May". Dataset "Jun–Aug" is too narrow.
- Climbed recently: Indian Army, July 2025 (launched from Leh 10 Jul 2025, summit "in 18 days", Brighter Kashmir, 19 Aug 2025). Ladakh Scouts, 2019 (reported 30 Sep 2019). No closure order found; no civilian or foreign ascent found after 2010.
- Foreigners: IMF permits only joint expeditions with Indian counterparts in the East Karakoram (IMF peak-fee page, per rules-records §1.2; indmount.org not fetched today). The last foreign ascent found was the 2007 Indo-French joint team. Restricted area → X (Mountaineering) visa per the 2018 IMF guidelines (rules-records §1.10, "confirm with the Indian mission"). Sasoma/Nubra needs a Ladakh PAP for foreigners (rules-records §1.7). IMF base fee above 7,000 m: US$1,000 (team of two) + US$500 LO equipment hire; per-member charges apply (IMF page checked 1 Oct 2026).
- Indian civilians: possible in principle via IMF clearance (Himalayan Club Kolkata team 2010, which showed permits at a checkpoint on the Leh–Sasoma road, HJ 67). No ILP for Indians since Aug 2021 (rules-records §1.7). Army logistics (Sasoma post, bulldozer, helipad) feature in every account.
- Siachen: the Oct 2019 tourism opening covers Siachen Base Camp to Kumar Post only; it says nothing about climbing Mamostong. Don't link the two.

## History and notable ascents
- 1907: Neve and Oliver explore (Wikipedia). Legend of Yarkandi traders lost using the Mamostong glacier/east col to bypass Saser La (HJ 41).
- 1984 first ascent: Indo-Japanese expedition, leader Col Balwant Sandhu (Nehru Institute of Mountaineering), deputy Yoshio Ogata (Himalayan Association of Japan); 5 Japanese + 8 Indians. NE ridge from the col east of the peak. 13 Sep 1984: N. Yamada, K. Yoshida, R. Sharma, P. M. Das, H. Chauhan (Wikipedia; AAJ 1985 gives 13 Sep, five climbers ~11 am). Five more on 15 Sep and two plus Sandhu on 16 Sep (AAJ 1985). AJ 1985: all members summited.
- 1988: Ladakh Scouts (Maj A. M. Sethi), second ascent, new eastern approach via Saser La and Thangman glacier; 6 summited 13 May, 9 on 15 May (HJ 46).
- 1989: Corps of Engineers (Maj M. P. Yadav); HJ 64 says first-ascent route, AAJ 1989 says "may have" taken a new route from the Kichik Kumdan glacier. Record as "1989, Army Corps of Engineers".
- 1990: Border Security Force (S. C. Negi), east ridge (HJ 64).
- 15 Aug 1992: Bachendri Pal's pre-Everest all-women team, 15 summiters in three groups via Mamostong Col and east ridge (AAJ 1993; HJ 64 gives 1992). 1992 also: Indo-Austrian first ascent of Mamostong Kangri II, 7,025 m (HJ 64).
- Aug 2007: Indo-French joint expedition (Chewang Motup, Paulo Grobel), normal route; date 19 Aug (Kapadia AJ notes) vs 20–21 Aug (HJ 64). Page: "August 2007".
- 20 Sep 2007: Indian Army (Col Ashok Abbey) via Saser La/Thangman, avoiding Hope Col (HJ 64).
- 7 Aug 2010: Himalayan Club Kolkata Silver Jubilee expedition (leader P. C. Sahoo); Debraj Dutta with Phurba, Pemba, Dawa Wanchuk (HJ 67).
- 2019: Ladakh Scouts battalion, 22 members, leader Maj Lotos Spalges, "within 18 days" (Republic, 30 Sep 2019).
- Jul 2025: Indian Army team flagged off from Leh 10 Jul 2025 by the Fire & Fury Corps; summit in 18 days (Brighter Kashmir, 19 Aug 2025). Summiters not named.

## Questions people ask
1. **How high is Mamostong Kangri?** 7,516 m (24,659 ft).
2. **Where is it?** In the Rimo Muztagh, eastern Karakoram, Ladakh, about 30 km ESE of the Siachen Glacier snout, above the Nubra and Shyok valleys.
3. **Why is it called the highest peak of the Rimo Muztagh?** At 7,516 m it is higher than Rimo I (7,385 m) and every other peak of that subrange.
4. **What is K35?** Its old survey label; it's also spelt Mamostang Kangri.
5. **Who first climbed it?** An Indo-Japanese expedition led by Balwant Sandhu and Yoshio Ogata; the first five reached the top on 13 Sep 1984 by the NE ridge.
6. **Has anyone climbed it recently?** Yes: Ladakh Scouts in 2019 and an Indian Army team in July 2025.
7. **Can foreigners climb it?** Only as a joint expedition with Indian partners through the IMF, plus a PAP and a mountaineering visa; no foreign ascent found since 2007.
8. **Is it part of the Siachen tourism opening?** No. The 2019 opening covers Siachen Base Camp to Kumar Post only.
9. **Where does the climb start?** Sasoma in the Nubra valley, then up the Tulum Puti valley to the Mamostong glacier.
10. **Is it the same as Rimo peak?** No. Rimo I (7,385 m) is a different peak in the same range.
