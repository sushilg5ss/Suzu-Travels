## Route and camps
Normal approach: from Korzok across to the east shore, to the 1995 base camp at Kurchyu, then an advance base on the Rungyado phu plateau. From there Chamser goes up its south/southwest ridge and Lungser via the col and the north ridge. Some itineraries trek in from Rumtse (via Tso Kar) or from Lato first.

- Typical length: operator plans run 14–15 days from Leh with a trek-in from Rumtse (Dream Ladakh) and 21 days via Lato (Project Himalaya). Outlook 2014 cites a 10-day Chamser trip from Leh. The 1995 team took 13 days from Karzok to both summits (4–16 Jul). **Page:** "about 10–15 days from Leh, longer with a trek-in".

## Season, access status and permits   (as of Nov 2026)
- **Status verdict: climbed recently, but civilian and foreign access is NOT confirmed.**
  - Recent ascents found:
    - JIM&WS (government institute; the 25/27 Jul 2024 climbs above)
    - Indian Army Corps of Signals (Chamser, Aug 2026)
    - NIMAS (MoD) first ascents in the surrounding Chamser–Lungser area (Sep 2026)
  - No 2023–2026 report of a civilian, commercial or foreign team summiting either peak was found.
  - Older evidence: operators reported army blocks in 2014–2016, and Project Himalaya (undated page, "No departures planned") still says the block continues.
  - Neither peak is named in the 2021 list of peaks open to foreigners.
  - No 2025–26 official order either opening or closing them was found.
  - **Page wording:** "Lungser and Chamser Kangri were climbed by Indian institute and Army teams in 2024 and 2026, but treks to them were blocked for civilian teams in 2014–2016 and we found no recent official confirmation that they are open, so confirm the current status with the IMF and the Leh district administration before planning (as of Nov 2026)."
- Border context: the peaks face Chumar and Hanle on the Tibet side (HJ 52). A Kiato–Korzok road through the Takling La tunnel is under construction and is reported as giving "the army easy access to the China border" (Amar Ujala, Apr 2025, via Wikipedia). Treat this as context only; it is not a stated reason for any closure.
- Season: dataset Jun–Sep. The 1995 climbs were 14–16 Jul. Other dated summits: 22 Jul 2013, 25–27 Jul 2024, Aug 2026. Operators: summer to early autumn. **Page:** "July–August is when most recorded ascents happened; June–September is the window".
- Foreigners: a Protected Area Permit is needed for Tso Moriri. The Leh permit portal lists "Upshi, TsomoRiRi/Korzok in Nyoma Sub-Division" as a permit circuit (lahdclehpermit.in/circuits, fetched 2026-11-16). The east-shore climbing area is not listed separately. Rules: minimum 2 foreigners, up to 15 days (validity raised from 7 days in Aug 2021); certain nationalities need MHA/MEA clearance (portal notice) (rules-records §1.7). Foreign teams also need IMF peak permission; fees in the IMF 6,501–7,000 m band (US$700 base for 2 + US$500 LO equipment hire; "per-member charges apply").
- Indians: no Inner Line Permit since the 6 Aug 2021 Ladakh order. An environment ("green") fee is collected via the Leh portal; don't give amounts. Indian teams need IMF clearance for a 6,000 m+ peak (see imf-permit-fees.md).
- Wildlife: Tso Moriri is a Ramsar wetland conservation reserve with a wildlife checkpoint at Mahe bridge. As in the Mentok pack, no official source places the route inside the Changthang Cold Desert Wildlife Sanctuary, so don't state it, and give no fee amounts.

## History and notable ascents
- Before 1995: a Survey of India khalasi (surveyor's assistant) built cairns on Chamser's southwest ridge and summit, date unknown (HJ 52). Ramesh Bhattacharjee explored Rupshu in 1984 and 1993 and climbed Mentok II (HJ 41, 50; cited in HJ 52). Tso Moriri opened to tourists in 1994 (HJ 52).
- 4–17 Jul 1995: Kapadia's South East Ladakh Expedition.
  - 10 Jul: Lapgo Peak 6,405 m, first ascent (Munis)
  - 14 Jul: Chamser, SW ridge, second ascent
  - 15 Jul: Chamser, NE ridge (Munis)
  - 16 Jul: **Lungser first ascent**, north ridge
  - Source: HJ 52 (1996). Kapadia's book *Meeting the Mountains* (1998, p. 260) also covers it (cited by Wikipedia).
- 22 Jul 2013: Jagged Globe team led by Tore Rasmussen, six to the summit of Lungser ("6,662m") (operator report, research only).
- 2014–2016: army blocks reported (see above).
- 25 and 27 Jul 2024: JIM&WS team on Chamser ("6,630 m"), the first snowboard descent claimed on Chamser, and Lungser.
- Aug 2026: Indian Army Corps of Signals on Chamser Kangri (6,622 m).
- Sep 2026: NIMAS, 20 first ascents in the Chamser–Lungser, Gyama Nala and Chokula areas (unnamed peaks).

## Questions people ask   (Google PAA could not be pulled, because the search tool is US-only with no PAA. These are drawn from related searches and forum questions)
1. **How high is Lungser Kangri?** 6,666 m (21,870 ft). Some sources give 6,645–6,667 m.
2. **Who first climbed Lungser Kangri?** Harish Kapadia, Kaivan Mistry, Harsinh Sr. and Harsinh Jr., on 16 July 1995, by the north ridge (Himalayan Journal 52).
3. **Is Lungser Kangri open for climbing?** Not confirmed. Institute and Army teams climbed it or its neighbour in 2024 and 2026, but civilian expeditions were blocked in 2014–2016 and we found no recent official opening, so check with the IMF and DC Leh (as of Nov 2026).
4. **How difficult is Lungser Kangri?** Non-technical by Himalayan standards but glaciated. The first-ascent party met crevasses and a ~150 m ice slope, so crampons, an ice axe and a rope are expected. Altitude is the main challenge.
5. **Where is Lungser Kangri?** Above the east shore of Tso Moriri in the Rupshu area of southeast Ladakh, reached from Korzok village (about 4,527 m).
6. **How high is Chamser Kangri, and who climbed it first?** 6,622 m. Kapadia's team found a Survey of India cairn on top in July 1995, so its first ascent is unrecorded.
7. **What is the difference between Lungser and Chamser Kangri?** They are neighbouring summits on the same massif: Lungser is 44 m higher and was first climbed in 1995, and Chamser is lower. Climbers often attempt both from the same advance camp.
8. **How many days does it take?** About 10–15 days from Leh including acclimatisation; longer if you trek in.
9. **What permits are needed?** IMF peak permission (6,501–7,000 m fee band for foreign teams), a Protected Area Permit for Tso Moriri for foreigners, and a Ladakh environment fee. Indians need no Inner Line Permit since 2021.
10. **Best time to climb?** July–August, within a June–September window.

## Do not say
- That Lungser or Chamser Kangri are "open", "bookable" or have "2026 departures". No 2025–26 source shows civilian or foreign access.
- That Suzu runs or sells these climbs. No Suzu or package prices of any kind, no operator names.
- "No crampons or ice axe needed" (Wikipedia, citing an operator page). The first ascent needed rope, ice work and rappels.
- "Accessible from Markha Valley" (Wikipedia). It is unsupported, and the approach is from Korzok/Tso Moriri.
- "Chamser Kangri first climbed in 1995", or "both first climbed in 1995" (the line in mentok-kangri.md). HJ 52 calls 1995 the **second** ascent.
- That Lungser/Chamser are IMF "trekking peaks". The dataset grade label is our difficulty class, not an IMF list. Don't claim they are on any IMF/MHA open list (they are absent from the 2021 list).
- Names or heights of the NIMAS 2026 peaks, or that NIMAS climbed Lungser or Chamser.
- JIM&WS's "first in 14 years" as fact (Jagged Globe summited in 2013).
- Prominence 1,468 m (uncited). Any Changthang sanctuary or LAHDC fee amounts. A reason for the army blocks beyond "border security concerns reported by operators".
- "Highest peak in Ladakh" (it isn't; that's the /ladakh/ page's keyword).
