## Questions people ask (answer as FAQ)
1. **What is a col in mountaineering?** A high pass or saddle between two summits — climbers often camp or cross there (e.g. Duhangan Col on Deo Tibba).
2. **What is a bergschrund?** The gap at the top of a glacier where moving ice tears away from the ice or rock above; often the first obstacle before a face.
3. **What is a serac?** A tower of glacier ice formed in icefalls; seracs can collapse at any time of day.
4. **What is the difference between alpine style and expedition style?** Alpine style carries everything in one push with no fixed ropes; expedition style uses stocked camps, fixed ropes and support staff.
5. **What does "summit push" mean?** The final climb from the top camp to the summit and back, usually starting before dawn (alpine start). (Definition not in a fetched glossary — phrase as plain description, no citation needed beyond [1] "alpine start".)
6. **What do the grades F, PD, AD, D, TD, ED mean?** French alpine grades from easy (F) to extremely difficult (ED) — e.g. Kang Yatse II is PD and Menthosa's normal route about AD.
7. **What is a cornice?** An overhanging lip of snow on a ridge — never walk near its edge.
8. **What is a fixed rope?** A rope anchored to the slope that climbers clip into for safety or climb with ascenders.
9. **What is an LO on an Indian expedition?** The IMF liaison officer every foreign team must take.
