## Questions people ask
1. What is an ice axe used for? Balance on snow, stopping a fall (self-arrest), cutting steps and as an anchor.
2. What are the parts of an ice axe? Pick, adze, head, shaft, spike, and often a leash.
3. What size ice axe do I need? Most climbers use 45–60 cm for general mountaineering; your guide sizes it for the peak.
4. What is self-arrest? Stopping your own slide on a snow slope by driving the pick in and pressing your body weight onto it; it must be practised with an instructor.
5. Basic vs technical ice axe? Basic (B) for walking and general mountaineering; technical (T) for steep ice — beginners on trekking peaks use a basic axe.
6. Do I need my own ice axe for Friendship Peak? Usually not — outfitters commonly lend one; confirm in the inclusions.
7. Which hand do you hold an ice axe in? The uphill hand (concept; guides teach it on the slope) — keep as one line.
8. Do I need an ice axe for a winter trek? Only where steep, hard snow is expected; your trek leader decides.
