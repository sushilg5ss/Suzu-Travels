## History and notable ascents
- 1912: first ascent by Bruce's guide Führer and party (HJ 25, 1964).
- 1964 HJ note (Robert Pettigrew): it was then the only major summit climbed in the group west of Manali bounded by the Solang stream, the Beas and the Manalsu stream.
- A winter ascent with Dorjee Lhatoo is mentioned in Outlook Traveller (2016) as "the first winter ascent … many years earlier" — **no date, do not use on the page**.

## Questions people ask
1. How high is Hanuman Tibba? 5,982 m (19,626 ft) in our dataset; other surveys give about 5,930 m.
2. Is Hanuman Tibba a trek? No — the walk to Beas Kund is a trek, but the summit is a technical snow-and-ice climb.
3. How difficult is it? Much harder than Friendship Peak: a steep couloir to Tentu Pass, glacier with crevasses, and 45–50° snow/ice near the top.
4. Do I need a mountaineering course? Expect to need a Basic Mountaineering Course (or equivalent) and prior high-altitude treks.
5. How many days? About 12–13 days from Manali including a buffer day.
6. Best time? May–June and September–October.
7. Where does it start? The Dhundi road-head in the Solang valley near Manali, via Bakarthach and Beas Kund.
8. Who first climbed it? The Himalayan Journal records a first ascent in 1912 by Bruce's guide Führer and party.
9. Do foreigners need a permit? Yes — IMF permission, apply at least 90 days ahead; base fee US$500 for two (plus per-member and LO charges).
