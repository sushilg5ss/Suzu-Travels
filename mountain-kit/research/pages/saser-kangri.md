## Season, access status and permits (as of Nov 2026)
- Season depends on side: eastern (Shyok) approaches went in Apr–May to cross the Shyok before snowmelt (1973 BC 7 May; 1986 summit 15 May); the Shyok approach is "impassable in summer runoff" (Alpinist, 2011), so summer climbs (Jun–Aug) use the Nubra side (1987 June; 2011 Aug).
- Permits: IMF permission required; the 2011 permit took nine months via IMF and the team had an Indian co-leader (Chewang Motup) and Army liaison officer (HJ 67). Alpinist reports Indian rules required an Indian climber on the team. Treat "joint expedition with Indian partners" as the norm, not a guarantee.
- IMF base fees for foreign teams (two members, as of 1 Oct 2026, per brief; indmount.org could not be fetched today — re-confirm): US$1,000 above 7,000 m + US$500 LO equipment hire; per-member charges apply.
- Foreigners need a Ladakh Protected Area Permit for Nubra; Ladakh admin was reviewing a single UT-wide PAP on 24 Aug 2026 (https://ladakh.gov.in/?p=61453). Indians: ILP removed 6 Aug 2021 (https://www.voiceofladakh.in/?p=3509).
- Restricted/border peak: X (mountaineering) visa — "confirm with the Indian mission". Near the Siachen sector; Richey was refused a Siachen permit three years running (HJ 67).
- Current status: no 2025–26 Saser Kangri ascents found in AAJ, HJ or news searches (2026-11-11). Last widely reported event: SK-IV 2018, Pemba Sherpa lost in a crevasse 13 Jul 2018 below Camp 1 (5,750 m).

## History and notable ascents
- 1899 Neve et al. climb a 20,580 ft peak east of Panamik and photograph the group (HJ 14).
- 1909 Longstaff, Neve, Slingsby explore via Phukpoche; ~18,000 ft (HJ 14; Wikipedia).
- 1922–1970 repeated failed western-side attempts (Wikipedia). Visser surveyed the area 1929 (HJ 14).
- 1946 J.O.M. Roberts (with Lorimer) — reached ~21,500 ft on Cloud Peak's NW ridge (HJ 14).
- 1956 Nandu Jayal / M.S. Kohli — west face abandoned (HJ 25).
- 1970 IMF expedition under Maj Harsh Bahuguna reached ~23,000 ft (HJ 33).
- 1973 ITBP first ascent from the east (5 Jun). 1979 second ascent, Indian Army.
- 1984 Mamostong Kangri first ascent (Indo-Japanese). 1985 SK-II West, Indo-Japanese.
- 1986 SK-III, ITBP. 1987 SK-IV and SK-I west ridge, Indo-British Army.
- 2009 Richey/Swenson/Wilford/Lowther with Indian partners stopped on SK-II's south face (6,500 m per Alpinist; 6,700 m per MEF).
- 2011 SK-II East first ascent, alpine style; 2012 Piolet d'Or. 2018 SK-IV tragedy.

## Questions people ask
1. How high is Saser Kangri? — SK-I is 7,672 m (25,171 ft); II E 7,518 m, III 7,495 m, IV 7,416 m.
2. Where is it? — Saser Muztagh, eastern Karakoram, Leh district, Ladakh, between the Nubra and Shyok rivers.
3. Who first climbed Saser Kangri? — An ITBP team; Dawa Norbu, Da Tenzing, Nima Tenzing and Thondup summited on 5 Jun 1973.
4. Who first climbed Saser Kangri II? — Richey, Swenson and Wilkinson (USA), 24 Aug 2011, SW face, alpine style.
5. Why was SK-II famous? — It was the world's 2nd-highest unclimbed peak until 2011.
6. What does the name mean? — "Yellow Snow Mountain" in Ladakhi.
7. Can foreigners climb it? — Only with IMF permission, a Ladakh PAP and usually Indian partners; border-area rules apply.
8. Best time? — Apr–May from the Shyok side; Jun–Aug from the Nubra side.
9. Is it the same as Saser La? — No. Saser La is the pass at the range's north boundary.
