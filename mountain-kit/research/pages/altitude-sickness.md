## Questions people ask (short verified answers)
1. At what height does altitude sickness start? It can start from about 2,450–2,500 m (8,000 ft), and is common above 3,000 m.
2. What are the first symptoms? Headache plus nausea, tiredness, dizziness or poor appetite — often after the first night.
3. How do you prevent it? Go up slowly: above 3,000 m sleep no more than 500 m higher each night and rest an extra night per 1,000 m; drink enough; no alcohol.
4. Can fit people get it? Yes — fitness does not protect you.
5. What should you do if you get it? Stop climbing, rest, tell your team; if it gets worse or does not improve in a day, go down 300–1,000 m.
6. What are HAPE and HACE? HAPE = fluid in the lungs (breathless at rest, cough); HACE = brain swelling (confusion, stumbling). Both are emergencies: descend and get help.
7. Is 48-hour acclimatisation compulsory in Leh? Ladakh's official advisory tells visitors to allow at least 48 hours of rest on arrival in Leh; the rule was made mandatory in 2022.
8. Should I take medicine to prevent altitude sickness? That is a decision for your doctor; descent is the treatment that always works.
