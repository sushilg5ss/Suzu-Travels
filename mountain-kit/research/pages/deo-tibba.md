## History and notable ascents
- 1952: first ascent, J. de V. Graaff's party, via the couloir to the Duhangan col (Wikipedia; HJ 67; AAJ). Party members reported differently — see Verified facts.
- 1956: second ascent, Eileen Gregory with two Ladakhi porters (HJ 21).
- 1958: British party (Bob Pettigrew and others) turned back in bad weather on 11 June and climbed a smaller peak instead (HJ 21).
- 1961: Derbyshire Himalayan Expedition made what it called the fifth ascent, and climbed it twice (AAJ).
- 13 Oct 1962: Japanese party (Y. Omori, J. Tanaka, T. Iwase and Gunding) reached the summit at noon from Camp III at 5,500 m, approaching from the Parbati / Malana glacier side (HJ 24).
- June 2026: All-India NCC expedition (Tribune, 21 May 2026; flag-off 1 Jun 2026).

## Questions people ask
1. How high is Deo Tibba? 6,001 m (19,688 ft).
2. Where is Deo Tibba? In the Pir Panjal range of Kullu district, above Jagatsukh village, about 6 km from Manali.
3. Is the Deo Tibba base camp trek the same as the expedition? No — the base-camp trek ends around 4,100–4,500 m; the expedition adds the Duhangan Col, glacier camps and the summit.
4. How difficult is Deo Tibba? The summit is a gentle snow dome, but the climb to the Duhangan Col is steep (45–65° reported) on fixed ropes, over a crevassed glacier.
5. How many days? About 13 days from Manali including acclimatisation and a buffer day.
6. Best time? May–June and September–October.
7. Who first climbed Deo Tibba? A party led by J. de V. Graaff in 1952.
8. Do foreigners need a permit? Yes — IMF permission (apply ≥ 90 days ahead); base fee US$500 for two, plus per-member and LO charges (as of Oct 2026).
9. Deo Tibba or Hanuman Tibba first? Deo Tibba is higher (6,001 m vs 5,982 m) but its summit is less steep; both need snow-and-ice skills. (Comparison only — keep it factual.)
