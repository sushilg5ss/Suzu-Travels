## Season, access status and permits   (Nov 2026)
- Restricted border peak; no civilian ascent reported since 1989 [2]. Sikkim permit question: IMF asked Sikkim (Nov 2025) to be single-window permit authority, Sikkim bodies opposed (Jan 2026), no decision (see LOG 23 Nov sweep) — page says "climbing here needs state and defence clearances; confirm with the IMF and Sikkim Tourism".
- Historical ascents in June (1911, 1949), September (1945) and October–November (1934 attempt, 1989).

## History and notable ascents
1911 Kellas (record, unknown at the time) → 1934 Gourlay/Auden attempt → 1945 Noyce → 1949 Walter → Army c.1986–88 → 1 Nov 1989 SGMI. About five ascents by ~10 people [2].

## Questions people ask
1. How high is Pauhunri? — 7,128 m in most sources (our dataset); a newer survey gives 7,107 m.
2. Who first climbed Pauhunri? — Alexander Kellas with two Sherpas, 14 June 1911.
3. Why is Pauhunri famous? — For 17 years it was the highest summit ever climbed, though nobody knew it then.
4. Is the Teesta river from Pauhunri? — Yes, the Teesta rises from the glaciers below it (Teesta Khangtse, Cholamo lake).
5. Where is Pauhunri? — North Sikkim on the India–Tibet border, ~75 km north-east of Kangchenjunga.
6. Can you climb Pauhunri today? — It is in a restricted border area and no ascent is recorded after 1989.
7. How many times has Pauhunri been climbed? — About five recorded ascents (1911, 1945, 1949, an Army team c.1986–88, 1989).
8. Which peak broke the Trisul record? — Pauhunri (1911) beat Trisul (1907).
