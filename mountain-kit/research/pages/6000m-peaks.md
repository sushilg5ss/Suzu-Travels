## Season, access status and permits (as of Oct 2026)
- Stok Kangri: closed since 2020 (Ladakh admin ban 21 Dec 2023), no reopening. Kanamo is 5,964 m (not in this band) and on hold.
- Sikkim 6,000ers: restricted (Pandim: no foreigners; sacred-peak rules — rules-records §1.8).
- Siachen/Saltoro-belt 6,000ers (Shahi Kangri, Argan Kangri): restricted.
- Arunachal: PAP, fixed routes; Chiumo unclimbed.
- Every foreign team: IMF permission ≥ 90 days ahead, LO, insurance (rules-records §1.2).

## Questions people ask
1. **How many 6,000 m peaks are there in India?** Our dataset lists 74 notable peaks of 6,000–6,999 m; the true number of minor summits is far higher — no official total.
2. **Which is the highest 6,000 m peak in India?** Kedarnath and Saraswati Parbat I, both 6,940 m (Uttarakhand), in our dataset.
3. **Which is the easiest 6,000 m peak in India?** Non-technical options include Yunam (6,111 m, Himachal) and, in Ladakh, Mentok Kangri and Dzo Jongo — link `first-6000m-peak`.
4. **How many 6,000 m peaks are in Himachal Pradesh?** 14 in our dataset, from Deo Tibba (6,001 m) to Reo Purgyil (6,816 m).
5. **Can foreigners climb 6,000 m peaks in India?** Yes, most (63 of 74 in our dataset) through the IMF; Sikkim, Siachen-belt and some border peaks are restricted.
6. **What is the IMF fee for a 6,000 m peak?** US$500 (up to 6,500 m) or US$700 (6,501–7,000 m) base for two members + US$500 LO equipment + per-member charges (Oct 2026).
7. **When is the best time to climb a 6,000 m peak in India?** May–June and Sep–Oct in Kullu/Uttarakhand; June–September in Lahaul, Spiti, Kinnaur and Ladakh.
8. **Is Stok Kangri still open?** No — closed since 2020.
9. **Which 6,000 m peaks in India are unclimbed?** Chiumo (Arunachal), Parvati Parbat (Kullu) and Chombu (Sikkim) have no documented ascent in our dataset.
10. **Do I need a mountaineering course for a 6,000 m peak?** Not for guided non-technical climbs (outfitter prerequisites apply); IMF rules require AMC/BMC for Indian expeditions — link `mountaineering-courses`.
