|---|---|---|---|
| Height | **5,425 m (17,799 ft)**. Same figure in HJ 8 / HJ 16 (17,799 ft), HJ 44 and AJ 1990 (5,425 m), Wikipedia, TERI and PeakVisor. No credible alternate metric height found. Outlook (2018) prints "17,770 ft" and Outlook Traveller (2024) "17,798 ft"; treat both as typos. HJ 44 also has "(17,900 ft)" next to 5425 m, another typo. **Dataset 5,425 is correct; alternates stay empty** | [1] ; [2] ; [3] ; [5] ; [6] ; [12] | 2026-11-16 |
| Prominence / coordinates | Prominence **~1,570 m** (an "ultra"). Wikipedia infobox gives 1,570 m; PeakVisor 5,151 ft. Coordinates **34.165 N, 75.327 E** (Wikipedia 34°9′52″N 75°19′37″E) | [1] ; [12] | 2026-11-16 |
| Location | Head of the **West Lidder** (Lidder valley), **Anantnag district**, Pahalgam tehsil, J&K, in the Great Himalaya. Overa-Aru Wildlife Sanctuary (511 km²) names Kolahoi (5,425 m) as its highest point. PeakVisor instead files it under Thajwas–Baltal WS, so it is a boundary peak. Page wording: "on the edge of the Overa-Aru Wildlife Sanctuary" | [1] ; [13] ; [12] | 2026-11-16 |
| Kashmir Valley high point | Wikipedia: the highest mountain of the **Kashmir Division**. **Nun (7,135 m) is higher in J&K overall** (Jammu Division, J&K–Ladakh border) | [1] ; research/pages/jammu-kashmir.md | 2026-11-16 |
| Names | Local name **Gashibrar**, glossed "the Goddess of Light" (HJ 16, 1951). Wikipedia spells it "Gashe-braed" and glosses it "Illuminated Cat"; state the gloss as "said to mean". TERI says locals call the glacier the "Goddess of Light". Also spelled **Kolhoi** (Wikipedia redirects, mountain-forecast). Dataset spelling "Gashe-braer" is not found in any source and should be replaced (see fixes) | [2] ; [1] ; [6] | 2026-11-16 |
| "Matterhorn of Kashmir" | Ernest Neve's own phrase: "Mt. Kolahoi, the Matterhorn of Kashmir" (Alpine Journal 1910). HJ 44 (1988) repeats it | [7] ; [5] | 2026-11-16 |
| Rock / shape | A pyramid of "very stable trap" rock that "affords good rock practice" (Mason, HJ 8; Leeson, HJ 16). The climb "presents no outstanding difficulties … the difficulty depends on the amount and condition of the snow" (HJ 16). Later parties warn of **loose rock and rockfall on the south face** when warm (AJ 1990; 2018 accident) | [3] ; [2] ; [4] ; [9] | 2026-11-16 |
| First ascent | **28 June 1912: Dr Ernest F. Neve and Kenneth Mason** (Survey of India). They approached up the Armiun Nar and over the Har Nag pass to the ice-field south of the peak. They camped at ~16,200 ft, climbed a snow couloir (now **"Neve's couloir" / "Neve-Mason couloir"**) to the **east ridge** and reached the summit at 2 p.m. (HJ 8 editor's note by Mason; HJ 16). AJ 1990 says "1911", wrongly: on 21 Jun 1911 Neve and Mason made the first ascent of the lower **south peak** (AJ 1911). HJ 44 says the 1912 approach was "by the north glacier", which contradicts Mason's own note. **Use Mason's account** | [3] ; [2] ; [8] ; [4] ; [5] | 2026-11-16 |
| 1911 near-miss | **1911, Capt. Corry and Lt Squires** reached "within 40 feet of the summit"; Neve's party found their cairn in 1912 | [2] | 2026-11-16 |
| Later ascents (selection) | Cooke & Battye: 2nd ascent, east ridge, on or about **10 Jul 1926**. **John Hunt & Reggie Brotherhood**: first **south face** route, by a rib on the face (camp at Armiun, ~7,500 ft up and down in a day), **12 Jul 1935**. **Miss M. V. Sanderson** with shikari Aziza: first ascent by a woman, Aug **1936**, by Hunt's great couloir route. Sanderson & Lt James Waller, Jul 1937. Leeson & shikari Ahdoo, SW ridge, Sep **1941**. R. D. Leakey solo, east ridge, 30 Jun 1945. Punjab Mountaineering Club party led by **H. A. Hamid Khan**, 1945. Jackson & Bryson 1952 (Neve-Mason couloir). **1987**: Canadian party (summit by Couture, 15 Jul) and a Scottish schools party (Glenalmond, south face, late Jul). **6 Sep 2023**: JIM&WS + All J&K Mountaineering Foundation team led by Col Hem Chandra Singh, by the east ridge | [2] ; [3] ; [5] ; [4] ; [10] | 2026-11-16 |
| 2018 accident | Two climbers of a 9-member local team were killed on **7 Sep 2018** while descending after the summit: **Naveed Jeelani** (a J&K tax officer) and **Adil Shah**. They were hit by falling rock and one fell into a crevasse; a third member was injured and airlifted out. Officials called the warm-afternoon descent a "lapse of judgement" because rockfall rises when the sun warms the face | [9] ; [14] ; [1] | 2026-11-16 |
| Kolahoi Glacier, basics | Average elevation **~4,700 m** (Wikipedia, from Ahmed & Hashimi 1974). Main source of the **Lidder** (73 km) → Jhelum. About 5 km long in 1974. Reached by a **~35 km trail from Pahalgam via Aru** (Wikipedia Pahalgam). Wikipedia Kolahoi gives ~21 km of trail from Aru to the glacier, and TERI "about 35 km upstream from Pahalgam" | [1] ; [6] ; [15] | 2026-11-16 |
| Glacier retreat: area | **14.46 km² (1962) → 11.28 km² (2014): −22.99 % ± 2.3 %**, with 11.1 % lost in 2000–2014 alone (Shukla, Ali, Hasan & Romshoo, *Environmental Monitoring and Assessment* 189, 2016/17). Older study: 13.57 km² (1963) → 10.69 km² (2005) (Kanth et al. 2011, via Wikipedia) | [11] ; [1] | 2026-11-16 |
| Glacier retreat: snout | Snout retreated **~3,423 m in 1857–2014 (~21.8 m a year)**, faster at **~27.8 m a year in 2000–2014** (Shukla et al.) | [11] | 2026-11-16 |
| Glacier retreat: TERI | TERI (with the University of Kashmir) monitored it from 2008, with a weather station at **3,925 m** installed in 2009. For **1980–2015** it found **length −10 %, area −13.5 %, volume −18 %** (Dr Shresth Tayal) | [6] | 2026-11-16 |
| Road-head | **Aru** (Adav), **2,414 m**, ~12 km above Pahalgam (2,200 m). It is the base for the Kolahoi Glacier, Tarsar–Marsar and Lidderwat treks (Wikipedia, from M.S. Kohli 2004). Neve (1910): "Aru, the last village" | [13] ; [15] ; [7] | 2026-11-16 |
| Grade | **Technical** alpine climb: crevassed glacier and icefalls on the approach, a snow couloir and a corniced ridge to the summit, and rockfall in warm conditions. Not a trekking peak. The *trek* to the glacier viewpoint is a different, non-technical outing | [2] ; [3] ; [10] ; [14] | 2026-11-16 |
| Season | Sources favour **June to early September**. HJ 16: "Without doubt June is the best month". Summits on record: 28 Jun 1912, 12 Jul 1935, 15 Jul 1987, 6 Sep 2023; the 2018 accident was on 7 Sep. JKTDC gives the J&K climbing season as mid-May to mid-Oct. **Dataset Jun–Sep is OK** | [2] ; [3] ; [5] ; [10] ; [16] | 2026-11-16 |

## Route and camps   (normal modern route via Lidderwat; altitudes approximate)
| Point | Altitude (m) | Typical day | Note | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Pahalgam | ~2,200 | Day 0 | Lidder valley town, ~95 km from Srinagar airport | [15] |
| Aru (road-head) | 2,414 | Day 1 start | Village 12 km above Pahalgam; road ends | [13] |
| Lidderwat | ~2,780 | Day 1 | Riverside meadow ~10 km from Aru (altitude from operators only, **not citable**: 2,780 m / 9,131 ft) | operator (not citable) |
| Satlanjan (intermediate camp) | ~3,200–3,400 | Day 1–2 | 2023 JIM&WS team camped here after leaving Aru the same day (operators disagree on the height: 3,185 vs 3,400 m) | [10] ; operator (not citable) for altitude |
| Dudhsar (base camp) | 3,750 | Day 2–3 | 2023 team's base camp | [10] |
| Advanced base, Kolahoi glacier | 4,700 | Day 3–4 | Reached by roping up through crevasses and icefalls | [10] |
| Summit | 5,425 | Day 4–5 | East ridge (Neve-Mason couloir), south face or SW ridge | [10] ; [3] ; [2] |
- Timing: the 2023 team took **4 days from Aru to the summit** (3–6 Sep) and summited at 10:15 a.m. Page wording: "about a week from Aru and back, plus spare days for weather". This is our estimate from that timeline; operators sell the glacier **trek** (not the climb) as 5–6 days.
- Historic alternative: from Pahalgam via the **Armiun Nar** / Har Nag to the south ice-field (1912, 1935). Hunt called it "short and very attractive" but advised a bivouac at the col [3].
- Advice for the page (sourced): start descents early, while the face is cold. In 2018, rockfall followed a warm-afternoon descent [14].

## Season, access status and permits (as of 16 Nov 2026)
- **Status: closed to trekkers since the April 2025 Pahalgam attack; no reopening found as of 16 Nov 2026.**
  - After the 22 Apr 2025 Baisaran (Pahalgam) attack, the J&K administration shut nearly 50 tourist spots and closed trekking routes [17] ; [18].
  - **Aru Valley** reopened on **29 Sep 2025** by the Lieutenant Governor's order after a UHQ security review on 26 Sep 2025 [17] ; [18].
  - A further 14 spots reopened on 16–17 Feb 2026, bringing the total to 42. That list does not include Aru-area treks [19].
  - On **4 Jun 2026** Kashmir Life and The Himalayan Post reported trek operators still appealing to reopen routes. The closed routes they named include the **"Kolahoi Glacier Trek"** and Tarsar–Marsar in Pahalgam [20] ; [21].
  - **No official reopening order for the Kolahoi/Lidderwat trails was found** (searched 16 Nov 2026).
  - Page wording: "As of November 2026, the trails beyond Aru to the Kolahoi glacier had been closed to trekkers since the 2025 Pahalgam attack, while Aru itself reopened in September 2025. Check the J&K administration's current advisory before planning."
- **Permits:**
  - JKTDC: mountaineering in Kashmir needs "permission from the Government of India through the Indian Mountaineering Foundation (IMF)". Foreign expeditions need IMF permission "for climbing all listed peaks" [16].
  - IMF fee band for foreign teams on peaks up to 6,500 m: **US$500** base for two, plus per-member charges and **US$500** LO equipment hire (IMF page, checked 1 Oct 2026; see `imf-permit-fees`). Foreign teams need an LO; the 1987 parties both had one [4] ; [5].
  - **[VERIFY]** We could not confirm Kolahoi on the 2021 list of peaks open to foreigners: the tourism.gov.in PDF does not load from our container. Dataset "foreigners: yes" rests on JKTDC listing Kolahoi among its mountaineering peaks, plus foreign ascents with LOs (1987). Page: "foreign teams apply through the IMF".
  - A 2018 Outlook report says "as per the new IMF guidelines, trekkers do not need permission to climb" Kolahoi. This is a single, unclear, outdated source. **Do not repeat it.**
  - Sanctuary: the route lies in or along the **Overa-Aru Wildlife Sanctuary** [13]. No separate published entry-permit rule for climbers was found, so don't state one.

## History and notable ascents
- 1909–10: Neve explores the northern glacier and calls Kolahoi "the Matterhorn of Kashmir" [7].
- 1911: Corry & Squires reach ~40 ft below the top [2]. 21 Jun 1911: Neve & Mason climb the lower south peak [8].
- **28 Jun 1912: first ascent, Ernest Neve & Kenneth Mason**, by the couloir to the east ridge [3] ; [2].
- ~10 Jul 1926: second ascent (Cooke & Battye) [2].
- 12 Jul 1935: John Hunt & Reggie Brotherhood open the south face route [3]. Hunt later led the 1953 Everest expedition; don't add that unless it is sourced on the page.
- Aug 1936: first woman, M. V. Sanderson [2].
- 1941: SW ridge (Leeson) [2]. 1945: Leakey solo; H. A. Hamid Khan's Punjab Mountaineering Club party [2].
- 1987: Canadian expedition (Neve-Mason couloir, 15 Jul) and Glenalmond schools party (south face) [5] ; [4].
- 7 Sep 2018: two climbers die in rockfall on the descent [9] ; [14].
- 6 Sep 2023: JIM&WS and AJKMF joint team summits by the east ridge [10].

## Questions people ask   (built from Google related searches and the questions the SERP answers; Google's PAA box could not be read directly)
1. **How high is Mount Kolahoi?** 5,425 m (17,799 ft).
2. **Where is Kolahoi?** At the head of the West Lidder valley above Aru and Pahalgam, in Anantnag district, Jammu & Kashmir.
3. **Is Kolahoi the highest peak in Jammu and Kashmir?** It is the highest peak of the Kashmir Valley. Nun (7,135 m), on the J&K–Ladakh border, is higher.
4. **Why is it called the Matterhorn of Kashmir?** Ernest Neve, who later made the first ascent, used the phrase in 1910 for its steep pyramid shape.
5. **Who first climbed Kolahoi?** Ernest Neve and Kenneth Mason, on 28 June 1912, by a snow couloir to the east ridge.
6. **Can you trek to Kolahoi glacier?** The usual trail runs from Aru via Lidderwat. It has been closed to trekkers since the April 2025 Pahalgam attack, and no reopening was found as of November 2026. Check the current advisory.
7. **Is the Kolahoi glacier melting?** Yes. One study measured its area shrinking about 23 % between 1962 and 2014, and its snout has retreated about 3.4 km since 1857.
8. **How difficult is climbing Kolahoi?** It is a technical climb: crevassed glacier, a snow couloir and a corniced ridge, with rockfall when the face warms up. Two climbers died in a rockfall in 2018.
9. **When is the best time to climb Kolahoi?** June to early September. Early-season snow makes the rock steadier.
10. **Do you need a permit?** Climbing in Kashmir needs IMF permission. Foreign teams pay the IMF fee and take a liaison officer.

## Do not say
- "First climbed in 1911" (that was the south peak). Don't name anyone besides Neve and Mason as first ascensionists.
- "Kolahoi is the highest peak in J&K." Also avoid "highest peak of the Great Himalayan range" (PeakVisor copy) and "16 km south of Kashmir" (Wikipedia error).
- The **"first Kolahoi Greater traverse, 11–13 Sep 2023"** (Wikipedia, marked "citation needed"; no news source found). **The `jammu-kashmir` page and its pack state it, so flag it for removal.**

- "Trekkers don't need permission" (Outlook 2018).
- 17,770 / 17,798 / 17,900 ft as the height.
- "Gashe-braer"; "Gashe-braed means Illuminated Cat" as fact. Say "a local name said to mean…".
