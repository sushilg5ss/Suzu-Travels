## Route and camps
Shitidhar, classic line (operator altitudes vary, so the page uses "about" values):
| Point | Altitude (m) | Typical day | Note | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Manali | 2,050 | Day 1 | Arrival, gear check | brozaadventures.com (operator) |
| Dhundi (road-head) | ~2,700–2,850 | Day 2 | Drive up the Solang valley, then walk | ghumakkar.com; aquaterra (operator) |
| Bakarthach (Bakkarthach / Lohali variant) | ~3,300 (3,280–3,328) | Day 2 | Meadow camp. The mountaineering institute uses it as a training camp | brozaadventures.com; aquaterra (operators) |
| Beas Kund area | 3,586 | Day 2–3 | Lake area; source of the Beas | aquaterra (operator); ghumakkar.com |
| Lady Leg base camp | ~3,840 | Day 3 | Base camp and acclimatisation | brozaadventures.com (operator); Friendship fact pack 3,840–3,900 |
| Summit camp | ~4,650 (4,655; a 2011 blog says ~4,800 for "Camp One", where the snow begins) | Day 4–5 | Load ferry, then occupy | brozaadventures.com (operator); ghumakkar.com |
| Summit | 5,294 (dataset) | Day 6 | Pre-dawn start. Operators quote ~6 h up and 13–14 h back to Lady Leg | dataset; operators |
Days 7–8: down to Dhundi, drive to Manali, buffer day.

Ladakhi (H2): Dhundi → Bakarthach → Beas Kund base camp → high camp → col on the ridge → summit ridge. In 1963 the first-ascent party left a camp at about 16,500 ft (~5,030 m) and summited about three hours later (HJ 26). Outlook (2014): about 3 h from the valley near Beas Kund to the col, and 5 h to gain 3,500 ft to the next camp. Operators quote 8–12 days.

## Season, access status and permits (as of Nov 2026)
- Best months are May–June (snow cover, pre-monsoon) and September–October (post-monsoon, clearer and drier) (Outlook; dataset). Avoid July–August. In July 2021 the Kullu District Tourism Development Officer banned adventure activities for the monsoon, then exempted trekking a day later, so monsoon orders do happen (https://indiahikes.com/blog/himachal-govt-reverses-prohibitive-order-on-trekking-in-kullu-manali). Winter is not a standard season.
- Access: no closure of the Solang–Dhundi road or the Beas Kund trail was found for 2026. Beas Kund batches were running on 21 Jun 2026 (https://trekthehimalayas.com/trek-update-21-june-2026, operator). A new Nehru Kund–Solang road was under construction in Aug 2026, with completion targeted before 2027 (https://www.amarujala.com/himachal-pradesh/kullu/construction-of-the-nehru-kund-solang-road-is-in-full-swing-kullu-news-c-89-1-ssml1014-183896-2026-08-11). Page copy: "check the latest Kullu district advisories before you go."
- Indians: no IMF permission is shown as required for these peaks in our sources. Climb with an HP Tourism-registered outfitter and guide (2021 Rules). Kangra's 2026 trekker registration does not apply here (Kullu district).
- Foreigners: IMF permission plus the fee above. Apply at least 90 days ahead (IMF expeapp). Whether Shitidhar or Ladakhi is on the IMF trekking-peak list is not confirmed by IMF (see Do not say).

## History and notable ascents
- Ladakhi Peak: first ascent in October 1963 by Robert Pettigrew, Bob Menzies (Alpine Club) and the Ladakhi Wangyal, from a camp at ~16,500 ft. The HJ gives the height as "ca. 17,525 feet" and places the peak due south of "Claim Peak" on the ridge toward Mukar Beh. Reported in "Kulu Notes", Himalayan Journal vol. 26 (1965) (https://www.himalayanclub.org/hj/26/18/expeditions-and-notes-26/). The peak name likely honours the Ladakhi climbers Pettigrew worked with (inference; don't state it as fact).
- Shitidhar: Himalayan Journal vol. 67 (2011) says a 1977 Irish party (Mick Curran, John Forsythe, Foster Kelly, J. Kerr, Terry Mooney, P. Lamont, T. Chand) climbed Karcha Parvat (6,270 m) and "Shiti Dhar (5290 m), both first ascents" (https://www.himalayanclub.org/hj/67/19/expeditions-and-notes/). This appears in a Spiti/Lahaul history paragraph. The height matches, but the sources don't confirm it is the Manali/Solang summit. Page wording: "The Himalayan Journal records an ascent of 'Shiti Dhar' by an Irish team in 1977."
- Training use: in Sept 2013 an NCC girl cadets expedition (18 cadets, led by Lt Col Vishal Dubey) was flagged off for Mt Shitidhar, "over 17,000 feet", near Manali (https://www.business-standard.com/article/news-ians/ncc-girl-cadets-to-scale-mount-shitidhar-113090900800_1.html). Summit outcome not found.
- Bakarthach hosts a mountaineering-institute training camp (aquaterra, operator; a 2011 blog also saw cadets training there). Don't name ABVIMAS for Shitidhar courses without a source.

## Questions people ask
(PAA-style, built from SERP/keyword themes; WebSearch returns no PAA box.)
1. How high is Shitidhar Peak? 5,294 m in our dataset. Published figures run 5,250–5,294 m, and Wikipedia and the Himalayan Journal give 5,290 m.
2. How high is Ladakhi Peak? 5,342 m, matching the Himalayan Journal's "ca. 17,525 ft". Other figures run 5,257–5,345 m.
3. Where is Shitidhar? Above the Solang valley near Manali, Kullu district, Himachal Pradesh, east of Ladakhi on the ridge north of Beas Kund.
4. Where does the climb start? At the Dhundi road-head in the Solang valley, then via Bakarthach and Beas Kund to Lady Leg base camp.
5. How many days? About 8 days from Manali for Shitidhar including a buffer. Outlook suggests about 10 days for beginners.
6. Is it good for beginners? It is among the easier snow summits near Manali, but you still need crampons, an ice axe and ropes, and a professional guide. Ladakhi is harder, with a corniced ridge.
7. Best time? May–June and September–October.
8. Do I need a permit? Indians climb with an HP Tourism-registered outfitter. Foreigners need IMF permission and pay IMF fees.
9. Who first climbed Ladakhi? Pettigrew, Menzies and Wangyal, October 1963 (HJ).
10. Shitidhar vs Friendship Peak? Similar height and the same Solang approach. Link to /mountains-of-india/friendship-peak/.
