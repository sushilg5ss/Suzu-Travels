## Season, access status and permits (as of 26 Oct 2026)
- Open, climbed every summer: Kang Yatse II/I, Dzo Jongo (Markha), Lungser/Chamser/Mentok Kangri (Rupshu), Nun/Kun (Kargil side). Foreigners: PAP for Rupshu/Tso Moriri + IMF permit for the peak.
- Closed: **Stok Kangri** (2020 / 21 Dec 2023 order; no reopening). Restricted: Siachen and East Karakoram peaks (Saltoro, Teram, Ghent, Apsarasas, Singhi, Padmanabh, K12…) — joint expeditions with Indian counterparts only.
- Season: Jul–Sep (Jun–Aug for the Karakoram).

## Questions people ask
1. **Which is the highest peak in Ladakh?** Saltoro Kangri, 7,742 m, on the Saltoro Ridge in the Siachen area — the highest on ground India administers. India's official map also places K2 (8,611 m) in Ladakh, but K2 is in Pakistan-administered Gilgit-Baltistan.
2. **Which is the second-highest peak in Ladakh?** Saser Kangri I, 7,672 m, in the Saser Muztagh above the Nubra valley — first climbed by an ITBP team on 5 June 1973.
3. **How many mountains in Ladakh are above 7,000 m?** 24 in our 35-peak Ladakh dataset — almost all in the Karakoram.
4. **Is Stok Kangri open?** No. Climbs stopped in 2020 and the Ladakh administration banned expeditions on 21 December 2023; no reopening as of October 2026.
5. **Which peaks can I climb in Ladakh?** Kang Yatse II (6,250 m) in the Markha valley, and Rupshu peaks like Lungser Kangri (6,666 m) near Tso Moriri, are climbed every summer; Nun and Kun are full expeditions from the Kargil side.
6. **Is Nun Kun in Ladakh?** The massif sits on the J&K–Ladakh border: Kun (7,077 m) is in Ladakh, Nun (7,135 m) is counted in Jammu & Kashmir; both are approached from Kargil's Suru valley.
7. **Do Indians need a permit for Ladakh?** No Inner Line Permit since August 2021; foreigners still need a Protected Area Permit for areas like Nubra, Pangong and Tso Moriri, and every peak needs IMF permission.
8. **When is the mountaineering season in Ladakh?** July to September, because Ladakh lies in the Himalayan rain shadow; June brings high snow-melt rivers.
9. **Can you climb on the Siachen Glacier?** Siachen peaks are restricted; the 2019 opening was for tourist visits from Siachen Base Camp to Kumar Post, arranged by the Army, not for climbing.
10. **What record was set in Ladakh in 2026?** A 20-member NIMAS Dirang team climbed 20 previously unclimbed 6,000 m peaks in the Ladakh Range in 23 days (PIB, 25 Sep 2026).
