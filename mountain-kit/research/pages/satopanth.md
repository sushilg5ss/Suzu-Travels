## History and notable ascents
- 1938 Austrian attempt (Schwarzgruber).
- 1947 first ascent 1 Aug, Swiss (Roch, Dittert, Graven, Sutter).
- 1981 second ascent; 1982 first Japanese ascent; 1986 Polish south-face route; 1997 NIM ascent.
- 1933 "Satopanth" ascent by Kirkus & Warren = Bhagirathi III.

## Questions people ask
1. **How high is Satopanth?** 7,075 m (23,212 ft); Wikipedia gives 7,084 m.
2. **Where is Satopanth peak?** In the Gangotri group of Garhwal, Uttarakhand, above the Vasuki Tal lake; second-highest in Gangotri National Park.
3. **Who first climbed Satopanth?** A Swiss team — André Roch, René Dittert, Alexander Graven and Alfred Sutter — on 1 Aug 1947.
4. **Is Satopanth the easiest 7,000 m peak in India?** It is often called one of the more accessible 7,000ers (AD snow/ice), but the corniced ridge and avalanche slopes make it a serious climb. (Link 7000m-peaks; no "easiest" claim.)
5. **Is Satopanth Tal the same as Satopanth peak?** No — Satopanth Tal is a lake on the Badrinath side reached by a trek; the peak's base camp is Vasuki Tal on the Gangotri side. *(Verify the Tal's Badrinath location in one neutral source before the page states it; otherwise just say "a different place".)*
6. **Is Satopanth open in 2026?** Yes — it is on Uttarakhand's Feb 2026 list of 83 open peaks.
7. **What does Satopanth mean?** From Sanskrit, roughly "the true path/figure of devotion".
8. **Best time to climb Satopanth?** May–June and September–October.
9. **What is the IMF fee for Satopanth?** Above-7,000 m band: US$1,000 base for two plus US$500 LO hire (Oct 2026); Indian teams pay no state fees in Uttarakhand in 2026.
