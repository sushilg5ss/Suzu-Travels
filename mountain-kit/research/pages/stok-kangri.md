## Status box (the page's core; date it)
**Closed. As of 1 October 2026 no reopening order has been found.**
- Dec 2019: the All Ladakh Tour Operators Association (ALTOA) announced a halt to climbs for 2020–2022, citing pollution of the Stok village water source, glacier retreat and over-tourism. — Outdoor Journal, 23 Dec 2019
- 21 Dec 2023: the Ladakh administration ordered that "all kinds of expeditions to the region have been temporarily closed and restricted with immediate effect", citing "depletion of Stok mountain glacier and environmental issues"; the order covers the Army, Air Force, paramilitary forces and travel agencies. — The Statesman, 21 Dec 2023
- 2026: no reopening found in news or official sources; operator pages say "currently cannot be accessed" (research only).
- Wording to use: "closed since 2020; a Ladakh administration order of December 2023 keeps all expeditions closed, with no reopening date". Never "temporarily closed until …".

## Questions people ask
1. Is Stok Kangri open in 2026? No. Climbs stopped in 2020, and a Ladakh administration order of 21 Dec 2023 keeps all expeditions closed; no reopening found as of Oct 2026.
2. Why was Stok Kangri closed? Glacier depletion, pressure on Stok village's water source and over-tourism.
3. When will Stok Kangri reopen? No date has been announced.
4. How high is Stok Kangri? 6,153 m (20,187 ft).
5. Can I still trek to Stok Kangri base camp? The 2023 order closes "all kinds of expeditions to the region"; treat the route as closed.
6. What is the best alternative to Stok Kangri? Yunam (6,111 m) in Himachal is the closest match; Friendship Peak (5,289 m) for a first snow summit; Mentok Kangri and Dzo Jongo in Ladakh.
7. How difficult was Stok Kangri? Non-technical but very high, with an 8–14 hour summit day.
8. When was it first climbed? 1951.
