## Season, access status and permits
- As of Oct 2026: Nanda Devi main summit closed since 1983 (only Nanda Devi East on the 2026 list). Siachen/Saltoro peaks: military zone. Sikkim high peaks: restricted, Kangchenjunga banned from India. Uttarakhand's open 7,000ers go through UKMPS + IMF.
- Every foreign expedition: IMF permission ≥ 90 days ahead, LO, insurance (rules-records §1.2). Nationals of China, Pakistan, Bangladesh, Afghanistan need central-government permission even for opened peaks (Foreigners (Amendment) Order 2019) — https://www.scconline.com/blog/post/2019/12/21/foreigners-amendment-order-2019-amendment-to-restrictions-on-mountaineering-expeditions/
- Indian teams: IMF rules (leader AMC + above 6,400 m, 50% AMC) — rules-records §1.11.

## History and notable ascents (for a short timeline block)
- 1907 Trisul I — first 7,000 m summit anywhere.
- 1911 Pauhunri (7,128 m) — Kellas; highest summit climbed 1911–1928.
- 1930 Jongsong — highest summit climbed until 1931.
- 1931 Kamet — first above 25,000 ft.
- 1936 Nanda Devi — Tilman & Odell; highest summit climbed until 1950.
- 1953 Nun — Vittoz & Kogan.
- 2025 Kangto — first recorded ascent, Indian Army (26 Nov 2025).
Sources: rules-records §4.1; dataset; Arunachal Governor release.

## Questions people ask
1. **How many 7,000 m peaks are in India?** 48 between 7,000 and 7,999 m in our dataset (plus Kangchenjunga above 8,000 m); exact counts vary with how subsidiary summits are counted — say "about 48 (our dataset)".
2. **Which is the highest 7,000 m peak in India?** Nanda Devi, 7,816 m — closed since 1983.
3. **Which 7,000 m peaks can foreigners climb?** 13 marked open in the dataset (mostly Uttarakhand: Kamet, Nanda Devi East, Satopanth, Trisul I, Chaukhamba I, Mana Peak, Mukut Parbat, Abi Gamin, Hardeol, Tirsuli, Dunagiri; plus Nun (J&K–Ladakh boundary) and Kun (Ladakh)) — always via IMF.
4. **What is the easiest 7,000 m peak in India?** No official "easiest". The dataset grades Satopanth, Trisul I and Abi Gamin "moderate" (the rest technical or extreme). Even these need prior 6,000 m experience, a full expedition (about 3–4 weeks) and IMF permission.
5. **Is there a 7,000 m peak in Himachal Pradesh?** No. Reo Purgyil (6,816 m) is the highest; Himachal's 6,000ers (Deo Tibba 6,001, Yunam 6,111) are the usual stepping stones.
6. **What was the first 7,000 m peak climbed?** Trisul I, 1907.
7. **How much does the IMF charge for a 7,000 m peak?** US$1,000 base for two members above 7,000 m (US$700 for 6,501–7,000 m) + US$500 LO equipment + per-member charges (as of Oct 2026).
8. **Can you climb Kangchenjunga or Nanda Devi from India?** No — Kangchenjunga banned from Sikkim; Nanda Devi closed.
9. **Which 7,000 m peaks are still unclimbed in India?** Zemu Gap Peak, Apsarasas Kangri II, Shudu Tsenpa; Nyegyi Kansang disputed.
