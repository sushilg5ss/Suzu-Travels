## Route and camps
1976 first-ascent route (AAJ 1977, Misawa; feet converted, rounded). These are the **only** documented camp heights for Apsarasas; the 1980 Army account gives no summit-camp heights (its "about 5180 m (12,000 ft)" for ABC is internally inconsistent — do not use).
| Point | Altitude (m) | Typical day | Note | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Khapalu (start of march) | not given | 24 May 1976 | Then Bilafond Glacier, Bilafond La, Lolofond, Siachen, Teram Shehr glaciers. Historic only — not a route anyone can use today | [5] |
| Base camp, Teram Shehr side | ~5,200 (17,060 ft) | 26 Jun | Porters left 8 Jul | [5] |
| Camp 1 | ~5,700 (18,700 ft) | 3 Jul | Foot of the west ridge | [5] |
| Camp 2 | ~6,200 (20,350 ft) | 20 Jul | In a crevasse on the hanging glacier; 500 m (1,650 ft) of fixed rope C1–C2 | [5] |
| Camp 3 | ~6,700 (22,000 ft) | 26 Jul | Foot of the south peak | [5] |
| South peak | ~7,120 (23,350 ft) | after 26 Jul | Bad weather held all 8 climbers at C2/C3 | [5] |
| Camp 4 | ~7,010 (23,000 ft) | 4 Aug | Beyond the south peak; 4 stayed | [5] |
| Summit (Apsarasas I, west summit) | 7,245 | 7 Aug 1976, 9 a.m. | Inagaki, Yabuta, Miyomoto | [5] |
- Length: 24 May (Khapalu) → 7 Aug summit, about 11 weeks; base camp to summit 6 weeks.
- 1980 (Indian side, HJ 38): Leh 25 Jul → Khardung La → Nubra → Siachen snout (base) → ABC at the Siachen/Teram Shehr/Lolofond junction → summit camps 1–3 (from 7 Sep) → summit 18 Sep; 4 camps in the Nubra valley + 3 on the glacier + summit camps = 12 camps. No heights given.

## Season, access status and permits   (as of Nov 2026)
- Season: the three recorded ascents were in July (1988), August (1976) and September (1980). Page: "July to September". Dataset "Jun–Aug" → propose "Jul–Sep".
- Status: restricted. Inside the Siachen military zone on the line of control with China-held territory; no civilian or foreign ascent after 1976 and no ascent of any kind found after 1988 (sources checked 18 Nov 2026). No IMF or Army notice opening the peak found.
- Foreigners: IMF permits only joint expeditions with Indian counterparts in the East Karakoram (rules-records §1.2, IMF peak-fee page; indmount.org not fetched today). Restricted area → X (Mountaineering) visa, "confirm with the Indian mission" (§1.10). Foreigners need a PAP for Nubra (§1.7). In practice, Siachen peaks are reached only with Army logistics (all post-1976 ascents were Army).
- IMF base fee band above 7,000 m: US$1,000 (team of two) + US$500 LO equipment hire; per-member charges apply (IMF page checked 1 Oct 2026, rules-records §1.3). Information only — it does not mean this peak is open.
- Siachen tourism (21 Oct 2019): Siachen Base Camp (~12,000 ft) → Kumar Post (~15,000 ft) only, Army-run. Do not link it to climbing Apsarasas.
- CTA: information only. Suggest Himachal climbs: `reo-purgyil` (Himachal's highest — information page), `cb-13`, `deo-tibba`; wording "we pair you with a registered Himachal outfitter and certified guides". No prices.

## History and notable ascents
- 1909: Longstaff, Neve and Slingsby name the Teram Shehr glacier ("destroyed city") (Kapadia [9]).
- 1911–12: Workman expedition; surveyor Grant Peterkin names Apsarasas and Teram Kangri (Kapadia [9]; Wikipedia names the person but gives 1908 — do not repeat the year).
- 1975: Shizuoka University (leader Hajime Katayama), via Bilafond La; Teram Kangri II climbed by all members, main summit (Teram Kangri I, 24,490 ft) by Kazuo Kodaka and Yasunori Kobayashi on 12 Aug 1975 (AAJ 1976 [10]).
- 7 Aug 1976: first ascent of Apsarasas I, Osaka University (above) [5].
- 1978: Indian Army (Col Narendra Kumar) approaches from the Nubra and climbs Teram Kangri II; Wikipedia: India's first move to assert its Siachen claim [9][11].
- 1979: Japanese (S. Hanada) first ascent of Teram Kangri III via Bilafond La [9][11].
- 18 Sep 1980: second ascent of Apsarasas I, Indian Army (Brig K. N. Thadani), 16 summiters [6][8].
- 20 & 22 Jul 1988: third ascent, Indian Army, six members [AAJ 1989 note; 9].
- 1992: Indian Army (Col M. S. Gill) climbs Teram Kangri I (Kapadia [9]; Wikipedia "climbed once more in 1992").
- 2002: Kapadia–Sakai expedition explores the Teram Shehr Ice Plateau to its head [9].
- 2023: ExplorersWeb ranks "Apsarasas Kangri I" (east summit) 3rd-highest unclimbed mountain [7]; 13 Aug 2024 Karjiang climbed, leaving Apsarasas among the top three on Jurgalski's list [12].

### H2: Teram Kangri, the neighbour (dataset)
Teram Kangri I 7,462 m (highest of the Siachen Muztagh; prominence 1,702–1,704 m; Wikipedia rank 56th), II 7,407 m, III 7,382 m. Apsarasas's parent peak, 7.8 km WNW. Name from a Yarkandi legend of a large town at the Teram Shehr glacier (Wikipedia [11]). Ascents as above.

## Questions people ask   (built from related searches/"unclimbed" coverage; Google PAA not directly accessible)
1. **How high is Apsarasas Kangri?** 7,245 m (23,770 ft) for the main (western) summit in most sources; its three summits are all about 7,230–7,245 m.
2. **Is Apsarasas Kangri unclimbed?** The western summit was climbed in 1976, 1980 and 1988. The other two summits (II and III) have no recorded ascent; one survey analysis says the unclimbed eastern summit is actually a few metres higher.
3. **Where is Apsarasas Kangri?** In the Siachen Muztagh, East Karakoram, north of the Teram Shehr glacier, on the line between the India-held Siachen area (Ladakh) and China-held territory.
4. **What does Apsarasas mean?** "Place of the fairies" (apsara + sas), a name given by surveyor Grant Peterkin of the 1911–12 Workman expedition.
5. **Who first climbed Apsarasas Kangri?** Yoshio Inagaki, Katsuhisa Yabuta and Takamasa Miyomoto of Osaka University, on 7 August 1976, by the west ridge.
6. **Can you climb Apsarasas Kangri?** Not as a normal expedition. It sits in the Siachen military zone; every ascent since 1976 was by the Indian Army, and none is recorded after 1988.
7. **Can tourists go to Siachen?** Since October 2019 the Army has opened Siachen Base Camp to Kumar Post for tourism; the peaks beyond, including Apsarasas, are not part of it.
8. **How is Apsarasas related to Teram Kangri?** Teram Kangri I (7,462 m), about 8 km west-north-west, is its parent peak and the highest of the Siachen Muztagh.
9. **Is it among the highest unclimbed mountains?** On Eberhard Jurgalski's list, its eastern summit (7,243 m) is one of only three unclimbed main summits higher than Karjiang (climbed 2024).
