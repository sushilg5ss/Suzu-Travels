### CB-13 vs CB-13A (the confusion)
- **CB-13A = 6,240 m**, a separate summit with its own ridges: first ascent **1973 by a Polish team (Czarnecki, Kucyper, Kubit) via the rocky east ridge**, PD+ with pitches of III-, approached from the **Batal glacier** (HJ 66). A Bengal team summited it on **17 Aug 2017** from the South Dhaka glacier; Camp 2 (5,300 m) sits in a cwm shared with CB-14 [2] ; [9].
- Pages that quote "CB-13, 6,240 m" are mixing the two. Our page: CB-13 = 6,264 m; mention CB-13A once as "a separate, slightly lower summit (6,240 m)".

## Season, access status and permits (as of 16 Nov 2026)
- Climbed regularly by Indian club and commercial teams (latest documented ascent with a neutral report: 28 Jul 2017 [8]; Army expedition in the area 2024 [13]). No closure order, ban or 2025–26 accident found (search 16 Nov 2026).
- Season: July to September (documented summits); Batal road access depends on the Manali–Kaza road opening (Kunzum/Rohtang side), typically early summer — not sourced with a date → don't state an opening month.
- Indians: IMF permission (via a registered HP operator), AMC/BMC team rules. Foreigners: IMF ≥ 90 days ahead, LO, US$500 base fee band — open-list status to be confirmed (see facts).

## History and notable ascents
- 1960 — Hamberger and Weber (Austria) climb **CB 12** (14 Sep) next to CB-13; later sources mix this with CB-13 / CB-13A [6] ; [1] ; [2].
- 1968 — IAF An-12 lost (7 Feb); wreck later found on the Dhaka glacier near CB-13 [11].
- 1973 — Polish first ascent of **CB-13A** (east ridge); two Polish climbers killed in an avalanche on the Batal glacier icefall on 17 Aug 1973 [2].
- 1981 — **First ascent of CB-13**, Japanese Tampopo party, north ridge from the South Dakka glacier [2] ; [1].
- 26 Jul 1982 — Institute of Exploration, Bengal (S. Paul, A. Dey / Arati De); same season a Japanese team led by Osamu Sakiguchi [3] ; [4].
- 4–5 Aug 1989 — Katuyama Alpine Club (Isao Minami), 16-year-old Yosuke Minami summits [1].
- 2003 / 2019 / 2024 — An-12 remains and wreckage recovered from the Dhaka glacier area [11] ; [12] ; [13].
- 28 Jul 2017 — Giripremi (Vivek Shivade) [7] ; [8]. 17 Aug 2017 — Bengal team on CB-13A [9].

## Questions people ask
1. **How high is CB-13?** 6,264 m (20,551 ft).
2. **What does CB stand for?** Chandra Bhaga — the peaks of the Chandra Bhaga group in Lahaul carry survey numbers (CB-12, CB-13, CB-14 …).
3. **Where is CB-13?** In Lahaul, Himachal Pradesh, above the Chandra valley near Batal; climbers approach over the South Dakka (Dhaka) glacier.
4. **Who first climbed CB-13?** A Japanese party in 1981, by the north ridge from the South Dakka glacier.
5. **How difficult is CB-13?** A technical 6,000 m climb: crevassed glacier, fixed ropes to the north-ridge col and loose rock and scree near the summit — not a trekking peak.
6. **How many days does CB-13 take?** Roughly two to three weeks from Manali; the 1989 team summited ten days after reaching base camp.
7. **What is the base camp altitude?** About 4,300 m at the end of the South Dakka glacier, a day's walk from Batal.
8. **Best time to climb CB-13?** July to September; most recorded summits are late July and early August.
9. **Is CB-13 the same as CB-13A?** No — CB-13A (6,240 m) is a separate, slightly lower summit, first climbed by Poles in 1973.
10. **Do foreigners need a permit?** Yes — IMF permission with a liaison officer; the base fee for a 6,000–6,500 m peak is US$500 for a team of two (as of Oct 2026).
