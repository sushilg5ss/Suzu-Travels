## Season, access status and permits   (as of Nov 2026)
- Season: dataset May–Jun & Sep–Oct fits the dated ascents: II 30 May 2023, 11 Sep 2021, 30 Sep 2023, 29 Sep 1989, 9 Sep 1938, 20 Oct 1966; III May 2016, 7 Sep 1990, 8 Oct 2024; I 21 Aug 1983. Operator: "June and September". UKMPS: Apr–Oct.
- Status: no closure found (search 18 Nov 2026). Bhagirathi peaks are **not named** in the Feb 2026 83-peak press reports → page says "check the UKMPS list", not "on the 2026 open list".
- Indians: UKMPS (via IMF portal → Forest Dept e-Permit); no state expedition fee in 2026.
- Foreigners: IMF permission (≥90 days ahead, LO, insurance). I & II US$700 band, III US$500 band, + US$500 LO hire, per-member charges apply.
- Gangotri NP permit (Pathik portal / Uttarkashi), park 1 Apr–30 Nov 2026, 150/day cap on Gaumukh–Tapovan.
- Accidents: 1966 II descent (3 dead) [8]; 1983 I, Charlie Heard died abseiling after the west-ridge summit [3]; Sep 2018 a Bengaluru climber (Bharat Ashok) fell ill at Nandanvan en route to II and died at Bhojbasa [15]. No 2024–26 incident found.

## History and notable ascents
- **18 Jun 1933**: Kirkus & Warren climb "Central Satopanth" = Bhagirathi III, from the Gangotri Glacier side [17] ; [9].
- **9 Sep 1938**: Ellmauthaler & Messner (Schwarzgruber's expedition) climb "Bhagirathi North Peak" 21,364 ft = Bhagirathi II [6].
- **20 Oct 1966**: first Indian ascent of II (Govinda Raj, Amar Roy, Karma, Gyalboo); three die on the descent [8].
- **1980**: Japanese first ascent of I, SE ridge, ~2,000 m fixed rope [3].
- **Autumn 1982**: Allen Fyffe & Bob Barton (Scottish), **SW ("Scottish") Pillar of III**, summit 8 Oct; base at Tapovan [10b] HJ 40 ; [11]. (HJ photo captions say 1983; the text and PlanetMountain say 1982.)
- **21 Aug 1983**: Martin Moran & Charlie Heard, **west ridge of I**, alpine style, second ascent of the peak; Heard killed abseiling on 22 Aug [3].
- **1984**: Catalans Aldeguer, Martínez, Moreno, Tomàs: "**Estrella Impossible**" on the III west face (capsule style, pre-monsoon) [11]; later repeated alpine-style by Canadians Scott Flavelle and David Lane [32]. The name is "Estrella Impossible" (Impossible Star), **not** "Impossible Dream".
- **Late 1980s (AAJ 1990)**: Japanese Aizuwakamatsu Alpine Club, new **north ridge** route on I, summit 20 Sep [21].
- **2–7 Sep 1990**: Silvo Karo & Janez Jeglič (Slovenia), central **west face of III**, 1,300 m, alpine style, five bivouacs [10].
- **7 Sep 1990**: Korean Alpine Club pair repeat the Fyffe route on III [19].
- 2004 German "Stairway to Heaven" (west face, partly finished on the Scottish route); 2016 Italian/French repeats of Estrella Impossible and the Scottish Pillar (incl. an all-women pair); Oct 2023 Spanish 1,400 m west-face line joining the Scottish route [11] ; [12].
- **8 Oct 2024**: Siegrist, Schild, Grossniklaus, Béguin: probable first ascent of the **SW ridge of III** (UIAA IV–V, loose rock), after aborting Shivling [13] ; [33].
- Recent Indian service teams on II: Army 2021, NDRF May 2023, Navy Sep 2023 [7] ; [23] ; [5].

## Questions people ask   (related searches + SERP; no PAA box tool available)
1. **How high are the Bhagirathi peaks?** Bhagirathi I is 6,856 m, II 6,512 m and III 6,454 m; a fourth summit (IV) is about 6,193 m.
2. **Where are the Bhagirathi peaks?** In Gangotri National Park, Uttarkashi district, on the east side of the Gangotri Glacier above Gaumukh and Nandanvan, facing Shivling.
3. **Which Bhagirathi peak is easiest to climb?** Bhagirathi II, by its east-face snow route from Nandanvan, though it still has ~60° pitches and a rock band.
4. **Who first climbed Bhagirathi II?** Edi Ellmauthaler and Toni Messner, on 9 Sep 1938.
5. **Who first climbed Bhagirathi I?** A Japanese team in 1980, by the south-east ridge.
6. **Why is Bhagirathi III famous?** For its 1,300 m granite west face, climbed by Scottish (1982), Catalan (1984) and Slovenian (1990) teams.
7. **How many days does a Bhagirathi II expedition take?** Allow about two to three weeks from Gangotri (a 2023 navy team took 15 days from Gangotri to the summit).
8. **Where is Bhagirathi base camp?** At Nandanvan, a meadow about 4,400 m high (sources give 4,000–4,800 m) across the Gangotri Glacier from Tapovan.
9. **What permits do you need?** A Gangotri National Park permit plus an expedition permit: UKMPS for Indians (no state fee in 2026), IMF for foreigners (US$700 band for I and II, US$500 for III, as of Oct 2026).
10. **Best time to climb?** May–June and September–October.
