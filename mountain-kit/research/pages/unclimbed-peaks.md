## Season, access status and permits
- Virgin peaks need IMF permission like any other (foreign teams: ≥ 90 days ahead, LO); restricted-area peaks need MHA/defence clearance and are generally not available (rules-records §1.2, §1.8).
- Sacred peaks: Sikkim 2001 notification; Bhutan (context) bans all climbing.

## Questions people ask
1. **Which is the highest unclimbed mountain in India?** Zemu Gap Peak (7,780 m) in Sikkim — a subsidiary of the Kangchenjunga massif with no known attempts.
2. **What is the highest unclimbed mountain in the world?** Gangkhar Puensum (about 7,570 m) in Bhutan, where climbing has been banned since 2003.
3. **How many unclimbed peaks are there in India?** No official total; our dataset of 156 notable peaks has 8, and the IMF keeps a longer list of unnamed "virgin peaks".
4. **Is Kangchenjunga unclimbed?** No — first climbed in 1955 from Nepal; it is closed from the Indian side (sacred, 2001).
5. **Is Nanda Devi unclimbed?** No — first climbed 1936; closed since 1983.
6. **Are there unclimbed peaks in Himachal Pradesh?** Parvati Parbat (6,633 m, Kullu) has no documented summit; Manimahesh Kailash (5,653 m) is sacred and unclimbed.
7. **Can I climb an unclimbed peak in India?** Only with IMF permission; many virgin peaks lie in restricted border zones.
8. **Which Indian peak was climbed for the first time recently?** Kangto (7,090 m), Arunachal, 26 Nov 2025.
9. **Why are some Indian peaks never climbed?** Sacred status (Sikkim, Manimahesh), military zones (Siachen), border controls (Chombu, Arunachal), and remoteness.
