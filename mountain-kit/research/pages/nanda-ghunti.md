## Season, access status and permits   (Nov 2026)
- Historic climbs: May–June (2001) and September (1945, 1947). Pre-monsoon couloirs may be snow-filled; monsoon snow stopped the 1945 party [1][2] → season "May–June and September–October".
- Permits: Uttarakhand runs mountaineering permits through UKMPS (Indians: state fees waived, Feb 2026). Nanda Ghunti is **not named** in the Feb 2026 reports of 83 opened peaks — those reports print only examples (Trishul is named), not the full list [6][7] → write "confirm with UKMPS / the IMF whether Nanda Ghunti is on the current list". Foreigners: unknown.

## History and notable ascents
1936 Shipton survey → 1944 Goodfellow/Buzzard (S face) → 1945 Wood/Sams (N side) → **Sep 1947 Roch, Dittert, Ang Tenzing (E ridge, first ascent)** → 1960, 1977, 1980 Indian N-ridge ascents → 1989 British E ridge → early Jun 2001 S face new route + N ridge same day [1][2][3][4].

## Questions people ask
1. How high is Nanda Ghunti? — 6,309 m (20,699 ft).
2. Where is Nanda Ghunti? — Chamoli, Garhwal, on the western side of the Nanda Devi–Trisul group, 2.4 km south of Ronti.
3. Who first climbed Nanda Ghunti? — André Roch, René Dittert and Ang Tenzing (Swiss Garhwal Expedition), September 1947.
4. Can you see Nanda Ghunti from Roopkund / Ranikhet? — From Ranikhet, just left of Trisul (HJ 14); from Bedni its ridge is visible (HJ 58). Don't claim Roopkund view unless sourced.
5. How hard is Nanda Ghunti? — North ridge PD+, south face AD+ — glaciated, needs ice axe, crampons and rope.
6. What is the Ronti saddle? — The 5,322 m col between Nanda Ghunti and Ronti, crossed on the north-ridge route.
7. What is the link with the Nanda Devi Raj Jat? — Hom Kund (4,650 m) below the peak is the goal of the 12-yearly Raj Jat.
8. Is Nanda Ghunti open for climbing? — Uttarakhand permits go through UKMPS; it is not named in the Feb 2026 reports — confirm before planning.
