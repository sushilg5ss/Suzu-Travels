## Season, access status and permits   (checked 2026-11-23)
- North Sikkim restricted area; dataset status "restricted", foreigners restricted. Recent climbs of North Sikkim big peaks are Army/HMI/ITBP teams only (sikkim.md).
- Sikkim keeps a short list of **five alpine peaks open** for small expeditions (Sikkim Express, 10–11 Jan 2026); Siniolchu is not among the named open peaks (Frey, Tinchenkhang, Jopuno, Lama Wangden, Brumkhangse — sikkim.md). Say: "not on Sikkim's open-peak list".
- IMF asked (15 Nov 2025) to be the single-window authority for Sikkim expeditions; Sikkim tourism bodies opposed it (Jan 2026). No decision found (23 Nov 2026).
- **Green Lake trek reopened 31 Oct 2026** after four years (Lachen Dzumsa + a local operator; first group finished 8 Nov 2026); Green Lake ~5,050 m, base ~4,500 m; views of Kangchenjunga and Siniolchu; permit via the Sikkim Tourism Adventure Cell through a registered agency (Forest, Home, Army clearances); foreigners need PAP (Outlook Traveller, 14 Nov 2026). Trek = Adventure-lane intent: one H2 "See Siniolchu from the Green Lake trek" + no booking claim.
- IMF band if ever permitted: 6,501–7,000 m US$700 + US$500 LO hire, per-member charges apply.

## Questions people ask
1. **How high is Siniolchu?** 6,888 m (22,598 ft); older surveys give 6,887–6,891 m.
2. **Why is Siniolchu called the most beautiful mountain?** Douglas Freshfield praised it as perhaps the most beautiful snow peak in the world, as quoted by Kenneth Mason.
3. **Who first climbed Siniolchu?** Karl Wien and Adolf Göttner of Paul Bauer's German expedition, on 23 Sep 1936, by the west ridge.
4. **Where is Siniolchu?** North Sikkim, above the Zemu glacier, about 17 km east of Kangchenjunga.
5. **Can you climb Siniolchu today?** It lies in a restricted area and is not on Sikkim's open-peak list; recent big-peak climbs there are by Army or institute teams.
6. **Where can you see Siniolchu?** From the Green Lake trek in North Sikkim (reopened 31 Oct 2026).
7. **Is Hotel Siniolchu related?** No — that is a hotel in Gangtok named after the peak.
8. **How many times has it been climbed?** At least five ascents by 1995 (1936, 1937, 1979, 1994, 1995).
