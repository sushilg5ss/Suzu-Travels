## Season, access status and permits   (checked 2026-11-23)
- Arunachal: Indians need an **eILP** (Inner Line Permit); foreigners need a **PAP** through an approved operator + US$30 royalty (state tourism page, see arunachal-pradesh.md). Kangto lies in a border area on the McMahon Line; dataset foreigners = restricted.
- Any foreign peak expedition also needs **IMF** permission; >7,000 m base fee US$1,000 + US$500 LO hire, per-member charges apply (IMF peak-fee page, checked 1 Oct 2026). No record found of a civilian or foreign permit for Kangto from India. Wording: "No civilian expedition from the Indian side is on record; check with the IMF and Arunachal Home Department."
- The Governor (8 Dec 2025) said the State "could now open its mountaineering routes to international climbers" — an aspiration, not a policy. No opening order found (23 Nov 2026).

## History and notable ascents
- 1913 British–Indian exploration of the region; 1930/1939 British attempt (JAN gives 1930, HJ 71 gives 1939 — say "a British party in the 1930s") [2] [3]; Tilman 1939 [4].
- 24 & 26 Mar 1988: Doshisha University, first ascent from Tibet, NW ridge [2] [3] [7].
- 1994 HMI; 2010 Kapadia; 21 Oct 2017 ITBP–IMF base at the Pachuk [4] [5].
- 26 Nov 2025: Indian Army 18-member team, southern route, first ascent from India [1] [8].

## Questions people ask
1. **What is the height of Kangto?** 7,090 m in our dataset; surveys give 7,042–7,060 m (Survey of India 7,042 m; Chinese maps 7,060 m).
2. **Which is the highest peak of Arunachal Pradesh?** Kangto, on the India–Tibet border in East Kameng.
3. **Who first climbed Kangto?** A Japanese Doshisha University team (Miyazaki and Takano) on 24 Mar 1988, from the Tibet side by the NW ridge.
4. **Did the Indian Army make the first ascent in 2025?** It made the first recorded ascent from the Indian side, by the southern route, on 26 Nov 2025; the Army called it the first recorded successful ascent.
5. **Where is Kangto?** East Kameng district, Arunachal Pradesh, on the McMahon Line, near Gorichen and Nyegyi Kansang.
6. **Why is Kangto so hard to reach from India?** Thick jungle valleys, a river crossing on the Pachuk and almost no trails; teams in 1994 and 2010 never reached its base.
7. **Can foreigners climb Kangto?** It is a restricted border area; foreigners need a PAP and IMF permission, and no civilian permit is on record.
8. **Can I see Kangto as a tourist?** Not confirmed from a road viewpoint in our sources — do not claim a viewpoint.
