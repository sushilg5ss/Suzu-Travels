## Season, access status and permits (dated)
- Stok Kangri: closed (no reopening found, Oct 2026). Kanamo: access status conflicting — on hold, do not sell. Nanda Devi: closed since 1983 (Nanda Devi East open in Uttarakhand's 2026 list). Kangchenjunga: cannot be climbed from India.
- Kangra registration window ends 15 Oct 2026; no extension found as of 12 Oct 2026.

## History (one short block)
- 1965: first Indians on Everest, Avtar Singh Cheema and Nawang Gombu (20 May). 1984: Bachendri Pal, first Indian woman on Everest. 1907 Trisul: first 7,000 m summit. (rules-records §4)

## Questions people ask
1. **Do foreigners need a permit to climb in India?** Yes — central permission through the IMF for every peak; apply at least 90 days ahead.
2. **How much does a climbing permit cost in India?** IMF base fees for a team of two: US$500 (to 6,500 m), US$700 (6,501–7,000 m), US$1,000 (above 7,000 m), US$200 (IMF-listed trekking peaks), + US$500 LO equipment and per-member charges (Oct 2026). Link `imf-permit-fees`.
3. **Which visa do I need?** Ask your Indian mission; missions issue a mountaineering visa after IMF approval, and 2018 IMF guidelines say a tourist visa covers open-area peaks.
4. **Do I need a liaison officer?** Yes, every foreign expedition gets an IMF liaison officer.
5. **Can I climb without a guide or agency in India?** Foreign teams can apply to the IMF directly; in Himachal, commercial trips must be run by HP Tourism-registered operators with registered guides.
6. **Is Stok Kangri open in 2026?** No.
7. **When is the best season for mountaineering in India?** May–June and Sep–Oct south of the main range; Jul–Sep in the rain-shadow regions.
8. **Do I need a mountaineering course?** Not for foreign teams' IMF permits; Indian teams need BMC/AMC; guided trekking peaks expect fitness and high-altitude trekking experience.
9. **Where should a first-timer start?** A Himachal trekking peak such as Friendship Peak (5,289 m), then Yunam (6,111 m) or Deo Tibba (6,001 m).
