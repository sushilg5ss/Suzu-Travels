## History and notable ascents
- 1953 Gibson's party ~100 ft short [2] [5].
- **7 Jun 1955 first ascent**, Gibson + two Sherpas, NW flank from the Osla/Tons side [2] [1] [5].
- **7–8 Jun 1960** Indian Army Regiment of Artillery, Capt Jagjit Singh [3].
- **Oct 1986** British east-face reconnaissance (Powell) from the Son Gad — face judged "sheer", north-ridge attempt to ~5,790 m [1].
- Since then: a regular objective for Indian clubs and institute graduates (operators; no neutral count — say "often climbed by Indian teams" only with "operators report" or leave out).

## Questions people ask
1. **How high is Kalanag (Black Peak)?** 6,387 m (20,955 ft) — the highest summit of the Bandarpunch massif.
2. **Why is it called Black Peak?** Kalanag means "black serpent" (HJ 44); "Black Peak" is its English name. No sourced reason for the name — don't invent one.
3. **Is Black Peak the same as Bandarpunch?** No; older Survey maps called it Bandarpunch I, but today Bandarpunch I is the 6,316 m summit ~4 km SW.
4. **Who first climbed Kalanag?** Jack Gibson with two Sherpas on 7 June 1955, after his 1953 party stopped about 100 ft short.
5. **Where does the Black Peak expedition start?** Sankri/Taluka in Uttarkashi, then Osla, Ruinsara Tal and a base camp in the upper Ruinsara valley.
6. **How difficult is Black Peak?** A glaciated 6,000 m climb with crevasses and a steep, narrow summit ridge: basic mountaineering training and rope skills needed.
7. **How many days?** Typically about two weeks or more from Sankri.
8. **Best time?** May–June and September–October.
