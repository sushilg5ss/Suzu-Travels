## Questions people ask (short verified answers)
1. What is the easiest 6,000 m peak in India? Among peaks Suzu can arrange, Yunam (6,111 m) in Lahaul: non-technical, but steep snow near the top and a long, high summit day. In Ladakh, Mentok Kangri and Dzo Jongo are also non-technical.
2. Can a beginner climb a 6,000 m peak? A fit trekker can, after a 5,000 m peak or high trek first; altitude, not technique, is the main test.
3. Do I need a mountaineering course? Not for a guided trekking peak like Yunam; outfitters teach crampons, ice axe and rope basics at base camp. A BMC is the next step before technical peaks.
4. How long does it take? About 8 days from Manali for Yunam; 13–15 days for Deo Tibba.
5. How should I train? Build endurance for long days carrying a pack — NIM's benchmark is 10 km with 10 kg; add hill/stair work. Fitness won't stop altitude sickness, so the itinerary must include acclimatisation.
6. Is Stok Kangri still the best first 6,000er? No — Stok Kangri has been closed since 2020.
7. Is Kanamo a 6,000 m peak? No — it is 5,964 m in our dataset, and its access status is unclear in 2026.
8. When is the best time? Jul–Sep for Lahaul (Yunam); May–Jun or Sep–Oct for Kullu peaks (Deo Tibba).
