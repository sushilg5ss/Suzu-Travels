## History and notable ascents
- **1937**: Jack Gibson and John Martyn (Doon School masters) reconnoitred; judged the SE ridge "the only feasible route"; camped ~17,000 ft [5] [6]. (Wikipedia/Bharatpedia add Tenzing and say "reached the summit ridge"/"climbed it" — see Do not say.)
- **1943, 1944**: A.R. Leyden attempts; high point estimated 19,800 ft [6].
- **July 1946**: Gibson-organised party (R.L. Holdsworth, Maj John Munro, Lt Nandu Jayal, M.P. Chengappa, Sherpas Tenzing, Dawa Thondup, with Dhian Singh) from the Upper Hanuman Ganga up the ridge; Holdsworth, Jayal and Tenzing passed Leyden's high point; monsoon turned them back [5] [6] [8].
- **20 June 1950: first ascent** — Tenzing Norgay, Sgt Roy Greenwood, Kin Chok Tshering; party organised by Gibson with Maj Gen Harold Williams, Gurdial Singh, Jagjit Singh, Sherpa Kusang [3] [4] [5]. Three years later Tenzing summited Everest.
- **7 June 1955**: Gibson and two Sherpas made the **first ascent of Kalanag/Black Peak** (20,956 ft) from Osla/Tons side with Doon School and Mayo College boys; 1953 Gibson party had turned back ~100 ft short [10] [11] [7].
- **7–8 June 1960**: Indian Army Regiment of Artillery (Capt Jagjit Singh) climbed Black Peak [11].
- **15–16 May 1975**: NIM advance course, leader Col L.P. Sharma — new SE approach from Suki/Chhaiyan Gad; **19 June 1975** women's course summit incl. Gurdial Singh (52) and 16-year-old Rekha Sharma [8] [5].
- **5 June 1978**: St Stephen's College Hiking Club (P.M. Das, a Doon School old boy, R.K. Bhuyan, guide Prem Singh) — alpine style, no fixed rope; also climbed Hanuman (5,548 m) on 12 June [9] [5].

## Questions people ask
1. **What is the height of Bandarpunch?** 6,316 m (20,722 ft); older surveys say 20,720 ft.
2. **Where is Bandarpunch?** In Uttarkashi district, Uttarakhand, on the Garhwal Himalaya's western edge, between Yamunotri and the Gangotri road; the massif lies in Govind Pashu Vihar.
3. **What does Bandarpunch mean?** "Monkey's tail" — the legend says Hanuman doused his burning tail on its summit.
4. **Who first climbed Bandarpunch?** Tenzing Norgay, Roy Greenwood and Kin Chok Tshering, on 20 June 1950, in a Doon School party organised by Jack Gibson with Maj Gen Harold Williams.
5. **Which river starts from Bandarpunch?** The Yamuna rises above Yamunotri at the massif's west end; its glaciers also feed the Hanuman Ganga (south) and the Ruinsar Gad/Tons system (north).
6. **What is the difference between Bandarpunch and Kalanag (Black Peak)?** Kalanag (6,387 m) is the massif's highest summit, ~4 km NE; Bandarpunch I is 6,316 m; White Peak (6,102 m) is above Yamunotri. Kalanag was first climbed in 1955.
7. **Can you trek to Bandarpunch?** Not to the summit — it's a glaciated expedition peak. Trekkers see it from Dodital/Darwa Pass, Ruinsara Tal and (per HJ 34) Mussoorie; the base camp in the Chhaiyan Gad is reached from Suki over Khagi Pass (3,420 m).
8. **How difficult is Bandarpunch?** A moderate-to-serious 6,000 m climb: icefalls, crevasses, a steep blue-ice spur often fixed with rope, and a bergschrund below the top.
9. **Is Bandarpunch open for climbing in 2026?** It was reported among the 83 peaks Uttarakhand opened in Feb 2026; Indians apply on UKMPS, foreigners via the IMF.
10. **Best time to climb Bandarpunch?** May–June and September–October.

## Do not say
- "Williams and Tenzing made the first ascent" / "Williams summited" (tourmyindia) — Williams was a member; summiteers were Tenzing, Greenwood, Kin Chok Tshering. Don't state "led by Williams" as plain fact (HJ 16/AAJ credit Gibson as organiser).
- "Gibson climbed Bandarpunch in 1937" (Bharatpedia) or "Tenzing was on the 1937 trip / reached the summit ridge" (Wikipedia) — HJ 62 and AJ 1948 describe a reconnaissance by Gibson and Martyn only.
- "Bandarpunch is the highest peak of the massif" — Kalanag (6,387 m) is higher.
- Wikipedia's coordinates 78°55′33″E; thrillophilia's "6,136 m"; "6,320 m" (unsourced).
- That the Taluka–Osla–Ruinsara trek is the route to Bandarpunch's summit (thrillophilia, and our own BACKLOG note) — that side is the Kalanag/north approach.
- "Ruinsar Gad joins the Yamuna at Seema" as stated by Wikipedia (Seema is on the Supin/Tons).
- "Confirmed on the official 83-peak list" (one report only); "officially open to foreigners" (not verified this run).
- Govind/forest fee amounts, operator prices, per-member IMF amounts.
- An altitude for the forest camp/Dinara; that the Suki approach lies inside Govind NP (unverified).
