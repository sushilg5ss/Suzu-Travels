## Season, access status and permits (as of 28 Oct 2026)
- Sacred / not permitted: Kangchenjunga (2001 ban), Kabru, Pandim and other sacred summits; IMF has not issued permits for Sikkim's sacred peaks since 2019.
- Restricted (border/army areas): most North Sikkim 7,000ers — Jongsong, Pauhunri, Kangchenjau, Gurudongmar, Siniolchu… — climbed recently only by Army/HMI/ITBP/NDRF teams.
- Officially listed for small expeditions (2005–07 orders): Frey Peak, Tinchenkhang, Jopuno, Lama Wangden, Brumkhangse.
- Season: May–Oct (state), Apr–May & Oct–Nov in practice.

## Questions people ask
1. **Which is the highest peak in Sikkim?** Kangchenjunga, 8,586 m, on the Sikkim–Nepal border — the highest peak in India and the third-highest in the world.
2. **Can you climb Kangchenjunga from Sikkim?** No. Sikkim banned climbing it in 2001 because it is sacred; modern ascents, including the Indian Army's in 2025, go from Nepal.
3. **How many peaks in Sikkim are above 7,000 m?** Nine besides Kangchenjunga in our dataset, including Jongsong (7,462 m), Kabru North (7,412 m) and Pauhunri (7,128 m).
4. **Which peaks are open for mountaineering in Sikkim?** Sikkim's official list for small expeditions (2005–07 orders) names Frey Peak, Tinchenkhang, Jopuno, Lama Wangden and Brumkhangse; confirm the current list with the state.
5. **What is the second-highest mountain in Sikkim?** Zemu Gap Peak (7,780 m) is higher but is a subsidiary summit of Kangchenjunga; the second-highest independent peak is Jongsong, 7,462 m.
6. **Which mountain is the source of the Teesta?** Pauhunri (7,128 m) on the Sikkim–Tibet border, first climbed by Alexander Kellas in 1911.
7. **Why is Siniolchu famous?** Douglas Freshfield called it the most beautiful snow mountain in the world; it was first climbed in 1936.
8. **When is the mountaineering season in Sikkim?** The state names May to October; in practice April–May and October–November avoid the monsoon.
9. **Where can I see Sikkim's big peaks?** From Goecha La (4,940 m) on the Yuksom trek and Sandakphu (3,636 m) in West Bengal; both look across to Kangchenjunga.
10. **Is Khangchendzonga National Park a World Heritage Site?** Yes — UNESCO listed it in July 2016 as India's first mixed (natural and cultural) site.
