## Season, access status and permits
- Season: dataset May–Jun & Sep–Oct; Forest Dept "April–October", "post monsoon" best [5]; AAJ 2010 team recommends pre-monsoon (Apr–May) [4].
- 2026: **not named** in the Feb 2026 "83 peaks" reports [7] [8]; Forest Dept lists it as "Open Peak" (page undated, fetched 2026-11-20) [5]; on the 2019 list of 137 peaks opened to foreigners (dataset). Page: "listed as an open peak by the Uttarakhand Forest Department; confirm on UKMPS".
- Permits: IMF above 7,000 m **US$1,000 + US$500 LO**, per-member charges apply (as of 1 Oct 2026); Indians: UKMPS, state fees waived 2026. Milam valley is a border valley — "border-area permits are arranged with the expedition; confirm with the Pithoragarh district administration" (no ILP rule stated).

## History and notable ascents
- 1939 Polish party: HJ 1978 calls it the first attempt with two deaths; HJ 45/12 assigns those deaths to **Tirsuli**. Page: put the 1939 Polish tragedy under Tirsuli only.
- **1964** M.S. Kohli's team, east side, to C3, gear lost in an avalanche; **1965** K.P. Sharma, weather [2].
- **1974** Indo-New Zealand women's expedition: four climbers (two Indian, two NZ) killed by an avalanche on 30 May (HJ 45/11, 45/12); AAJ editor says the team gave up at 5,450 m because of avalanche danger [2] [6] [4]. Page: "an avalanche killed four members of a 1974 Indo-New Zealand women's expedition" (two HJ sources).
- **1975** ITBP (S.P. Mulasi, 28 climbers) from the Bagini side to C3 6,550 m; blizzard [2].
- **31 May 1978 first ascent** — ITBP, leader S.P. Mulasi; summiters **Nima Tenzing, Nima Dorjee (Sr), Thondup (Thandup), Kanhaiya Lal, Dawa Rinzing, Prahlad** [3] [2].
- **24 Sep 1991** second ascent, Border Security Force, five summiters, likely the same route [1] [4].
- **Autumn 2010** Slovenian team (Matija Jošt, Urban Golob, Boris Lorenčič, Karel Završnik) tried the **south ridge**, bivouac 5,800 m, retreated (rock- and icefall); made the probable first ascent of Nital Thaur 6,236 m [4].

## Questions people ask
1. **How high is Hardeol?** 7,151 m (23,461 ft).
2. **What does Hardeol mean?** "Temple of God"; Johar valley people worship it as Hardol.
3. **Where is Hardeol?** Pithoragarh district, Kumaon, at the head of the Milam valley — the highest point of the Nanda Devi Sanctuary's northern rim.
4. **Who first climbed Hardeol?** An ITBP team led by S.P. Mulasi; six men summited on 31 May 1978.
5. **How many times has Hardeol been climbed?** Two recorded ascents: 1978 (ITBP) and 1991 (BSF).
6. **How do you reach Hardeol?** Road to Munsiyari, then on foot up the Gori Ganga via Martoli to Milam and the Nitwal Thaur base; the 1978 line went up the Ikualari glacier.
7. **Is Hardeol open for climbing?** Listed as an "Open Peak" by the Uttarakhand Forest Department; confirm on UKMPS.
8. **Hardeol vs Tirsuli?** Tirsuli (7,074 m) stands just north; Hardeol was long called Tirsuli's south peak.
