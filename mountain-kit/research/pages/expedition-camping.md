## Questions people ask (short verified answers)
1. What does base camp mean? The main camp at the foot of a climb — the staging area where the team lives, cooks and acclimatises before moving higher.
2. What is the difference between base camp and summit camp? Base camp is the big, comfortable camp lower down; summit (high) camp is a small tent camp as close to the top as is safe, used for one or two nights before the summit push.
3. What are camp 1, camp 2? Intermediate camps between base camp and the summit on bigger peaks, stocked by carrying loads up and coming back down ("climb high, sleep low").
4. How cold is it at high camp? Well below freezing at night above 5,000 m, even in autumn — bring a bag rated for it.
5. What sleeping-bag rating do I need? Look at the ISO "comfort" and "lower limit" numbers; the outfitter tells you the camp altitude and expected night temperature, and you choose a bag whose comfort rating covers it (no fixed number on the page).
6. Where do you go to the toilet on an expedition? In toilet pits dug at base camp; higher up, waste is packed out or buried following the rules; non-biodegradable rubbish is carried down (IMF rule).
7. Is drinking water safe at camp? Water is boiled or treated; don't drink untreated stream water.
8. Do I need my own tent? Not on a guided climb — tents and the kitchen come with the expedition; confirm in writing.
