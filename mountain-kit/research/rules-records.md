# Mountaineering in India: Rules, Training, Seasons, Records, Costs, Safety

*Research notes for the Suzu Travels "Mountaineering in India" section. Compiled 1 Oct 2026 using web sources listed inline. Everything here is informational. Official bodies (IMF, state governments, institutes) are the final word, and fees and rules change often.*

Legend: **[CHANGED 2025–26]** = new or recently changed. **[VERIFY]** = single source, conflicting sources or possibly outdated.

---

## 1. Permits and rules

### 1.1 Indian Mountaineering Foundation (IMF): the national regulator
- IMF is India's apex mountaineering body. It "regulates as well as authenticates climbing for foreigners and Indian climbers all along the Indian Himalayas". It began in 1957 as the Sponsoring Committee of the Cho Oyu Expedition and was registered in 1961. It is headquartered in New Delhi and is a UIAA member. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Mountaineering_Foundation
- Online expedition applications have gone through the IMF portal since it was "commissioned on 28th July 2017" (profile creation, team management, application forms). Source: https://www.indmount.org/IMF/expeapp
- **I could not verify any new IMF portal or national fee waiver in 2024–2026.** The IMF site still shows the 2017 portal and the standard fee table. The new online system in 2026 is Uttarakhand's own portal (see 1.5).

### 1.2 How foreign expeditions get permission (IMF)
- **Timeline:** apply at least **90 days** before the expedition. Peaks are booked provisionally on a **first-come, first-served** basis. Bookings are accepted **up to three years ahead** with a 25% advance on handling charges. Source: https://www.indmount.org/IMF/expeapp
- **Validity:** a peak booking runs **30–45 days, base camp to base camp**. The limit is **30 climbing members** per permit on the IMF page, though the fee table caps team size at 12 (see below). "NO deviation is permissible" from the approved route, except in emergencies with the LO's written approval. Source: https://www.indmount.org/IMF/expeapp
- **Processing:** the 2018 IMF guidelines say the permit is "issued within 7 days" for peaks in open areas. Restricted areas take **two to six months**. Late, incomplete or changed applications add about four weeks. Source: https://indianembassyberlin.gov.in/pdf/IMFForeignexp(4_4_2018).pdf
- **Liaison Officer (LO):** every foreign expedition must take an IMF-appointed LO. The team pays **US$500 for LO equipment hire** on top of handling charges and covers the LO's travel to and from New Delhi. Sources: https://www.indmount.org/IMF/peakfee ; https://indianembassyberlin.gov.in/pdf/IMFForeignexp(4_4_2018).pdf
- **East Karakoram:** only joint expeditions with Indian counterparts are permitted. Source: https://www.indmount.org/IMF/peakfee
- **Insurance:** the team must show "copy of the insurance policy" at the IMF briefing. It must cover "ground/helicopter search and rescue" and "altitude and exposure related physiological conditions". Support staff (cooks, HAPs, etc.) must be insured for at least **INR 7 lakh**. Sources: https://www.indmount.org/IMF/expeapp ; https://indianembassyberlin.gov.in/pdf/IMFForeignexp(4_4_2018).pdf
- **Briefing and report:** the team gets a pre-expedition briefing and post-expedition debriefing at the IMF and must submit an expedition report. Source: https://www.bikatadventures.com/home/blog/procedure-for-getting-a-peak-climbing-permit-in-india

- **[VERIFY]** Other IMF documents give simpler rates of US$225 / 325 / 450 per extra member (IMF expedition-application page and the 2018 guidelines PDF). The 2018 PDF lists trekking peaks at US$100, while the application page lists US$200. Sources: https://www.indmount.org/IMF/expeapp ; https://indianembassyberlin.gov.in/pdf/IMFForeignexp(4_4_2018).pdf. **On the website, show only the base fees (US$500 / 700 / 1,000 + US$500 LO) and say "plus a per-member charge".**
- Discounts are old news: the 2017 reduced rates (US$300 / 500 / 800) applied only to listed rarely climbed peaks for that one season (https://www.outlooktraveller.com/News/imf-lowers-peak-fees ; https://www.indmount.org/IMF/peakfee). A 50% discount applied "for 2020 season only" (https://www.bikatadventures.com/home/blog/procedure-for-getting-a-peak-climbing-permit-in-india).

### 1.4 The 2019 opening of 137 peaks (MHA)
- On **21 Aug 2019** the Ministry of Home Affairs, with the Ministry of Tourism as lead, opened **137 peaks** to foreigners for mountaineering and trekking: **Himachal Pradesh 47, Uttarakhand 51, Sikkim 24, J&K 15**. Source: https://www.pib.gov.in/newsite/PrintRelease.aspx?relid=192750&reg=48&lang=2 ; also https://www.theuiaa.org/imf-confirms-opening-of-137-himalayan-peaks/
- Sikkim objected because the list included Kangchenjunga and other peaks regarded as sacred (see 1.8). Source: https://m.thewire.in/article/government/sikkim-centre-open-sacred-peaks-kanchenjunga-trekking

### 1.5 Uttarakhand **[CHANGED 2025–26]**
- **4 Feb 2026:** CM Pushkar Singh Dhami announced **83 peaks** open for mountaineering, ranging from **5,700 m to 7,756 m**. Named peaks include Kamet, Nanda Devi **East**, the Chaukhamba and Trishul groups, Shivling, Satopanth, Changabang, Panchachuli and Neelkanth. Sources: https://thenewsmill.com/2026/02/uttarakhand-opens-83-himalayan-peaks-for-mountaineering-expeditions/ ; https://www.etvbharat.com/en/state/uttarakhand-opens-83-himalayan-peaks-for-montaineering-waives-fees-for-indian-climbers-enn26020304743
- **Indian climbers:** all government expedition fees are waived (peak, camping, trail-management and environmental fees). The state covers the costs previously charged by the IMF and Forest Department. **Foreign climbers:** state/Forest fees are abolished and only IMF fees apply. Sources: same as above.
- Applications go through the new online **Uttarakhand Mountaineering Permission System (UKMPS)**. "Leave No Trace" is mandatory. Source: https://thenewsmill.com/2026/02/uttarakhand-opens-83-himalayan-peaks-for-mountaineering-expeditions/
- Background: in **July 2022** Uttarakhand ended the "dual fee", under which foreign teams had paid both the IMF and the state Forest Department since 2004. It also opened 30 unclimbed peaks and 10 trekking peaks. Source: https://publications.americanalpineclub.org/articles/13201216469
- **Nanda Devi main peak (7,816 m) is still closed.** It has been closed since 1983, and the core zone of the sanctuary is off-limits. In 2025 the IMF and the Uttarakhand Tourism Secretary discussed reopening it, but "Nanda Devi Peak and Inner Sanctuary remain closed to general trekkers and climbers as of July 2025". Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanda_Devi ; https://www.justwravel.com/blog/nanda-devi-peak-reopens-guide-2025/ . **Say "Nanda Devi East", never "Nanda Devi", when listing open peaks.**

### 1.6 Himachal Pradesh
- **ABVIMAS, Manali** (Atal Bihari Vajpayee Institute of Mountaineering and Allied Sports) was founded in **1961** as the Western Himalayan Mountaineering Institute and renamed in 2008. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atal_Bihari_Vajpayee_Institute_of_Mountaineering_and_Allied_Sports
- **HP adventure rules:** under the HP Miscellaneous Adventure Activities (Amendment) Rules 2021, trekking and mountaineering operators and guides must **register with the HP Tourism Department** (at least 2 years' experience; registration valid statewide). Guides must be 21 or older, medically fit and certified in First Aid and CPR. **Difficult treks may be led only by holders of a Method of Instruction (MOI) course** from ABVIMAS or an equivalent institute. Other requirements:
  - Insurance of **Rs 2 lakh life plus Rs 2 lakh rescue** per participant.
  - GPS geo-tracking monitored by the district.
  - Pre-trek police registration.
  - Operating hours of 7 am–7 pm.

  Source: https://www.legitquest.com/act/himachal-pradesh-miscellaneous-adventure-activities-amendment-rules-2021/A36F
- **[CHANGED 2026] Kangra district trek registration:** from **8 Jul to 15 Oct 2026**, trekkers must register at check posts (5 am–5 pm) on 10 Dhauladhar routes, including Indrahar and Jalsu Pass, and give route, contacts, photos and expected return time. The order is made under Section 30 of the Disaster Management Act 2005. Unregistered trekkers or those who leave their declared route can be charged the **full rescue cost** and face action under Section 51 of the Act. Guides are limited to groups of at most 10 and GPS tracking is required. Sources: https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/himachal/kangra-administration-makes-registration-compulsory-for-trekkers/ ; https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/himachal/himachal-mandatory-check-posts-on-trekking-routes-to-boost-monsoon-safety/ ; https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/himachal/adventure-meets-accountability
- **Kinnaur and Spiti border belt:** foreigners need a Protected Area Permit (often called an ILP) beyond **Jangi** in Kinnaur and for villages near the Tibet border in Spiti. It costs about Rs 250 at Reckong Peo, is valid 15 days and is issued in person at the DM/SDM/ADC offices (Reckong Peo, Shimla, Kaza and others). Officially 2 or more foreigners are required. Indians need no permit. Sources: https://discoverwithdheeraj.com/inner-line-permits-for-kinnaur-lahaul-spiti-valley/ ; https://himalayatrekker.com/trek-permits-regulations/permits-rules-regulations-indian-himalayas-guide/
- **[VERIFY]** I could not find a current official page for a separate HP state mountaineering registration fee or rescue deposit for foreign expeditions. Don't state one.

### 1.7 Ladakh
- **Stok Kangri (6,153 m):** the All Ladakh Tour Operators Association closed it from 2020 because of over-tourism and glacier and water concerns. There is still no official reopening, and 2025–26 guides describe it as "temporarily closed". Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stok_Kangri ; https://wanderon.in/blogs/stok-kangri-trek-ladakh-ultimate-guide . **Do not sell or feature Stok Kangri as open.**
- **Foreigners' PAP:** required for Nubra/Khardung La, Pangong/Chang La, Tso Moriri and Hanle/Chushul. It is not required for Leh town, the highways or **Zanskar**. Minimum 2 foreigners; valid up to 15 days; issued via the DC Leh office or online at lahdclehpermit.in. Approximate costs: Rs 400 environment fee plus agent charges. Source: https://discoverwithdheeraj.com/protected-area-permit-in-ladakh-for-foreigners/
- Operator-quoted local fees for Kang Yatse II: expedition permit **Rs 1,000 (Indians) / Rs 5,000 (foreigners)** and ILP Rs 700. **[VERIFY]** This is operator data. Source: https://www.bikatadventures.com/Home/Itinerary/Kang-Yatse-II-Trek

### 1.8 Sikkim
- **Kangchenjunga cannot be climbed from India.** Sikkim banned climbing it on religious grounds (**Notification No. 70/HOME/2001**) and the ban is backed by the Places of Worship (Special Provisions) Act 1991. The Sikkim route has been closed since 2000. Sources: https://theprint.in/india/sikkim-bhutia-lepcha-apex-committee-urges-govt-to-uphold-ban-on-climbing-mt-kanchenjunga/2638144/ ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kangchenjunga
- **[CHANGED 2025]** A joint Indo-Nepal Army team summited Kangchenjunga **from Nepal** on **19 May 2025**. Sikkim's Bhutia-Lepcha apex body (SIBLAC) protested and a sanctification ceremony followed. Sources: https://www.aninews.in/news/world/asia/joint-team-of-indian-and-nepali-army-summits-mt-kanchenjunga20250519224123/ ; https://theprint.in/india/sikkim-bhutia-lepcha-apex-committee-urges-govt-to-uphold-ban-on-climbing-mt-kanchenjunga/2638144/ ; https://arunachaltimes.in/index.php/2025/06/16/sanctification-ceremony-begins-in-sikkim-to-restore-sacredness-of-mt-kanchenjunga/
- **Sikkim state rules (official page, dated 2005 notification):**
  - **Peaks open for alpine expeditions:** Frey's Peak 5,830 m, Lama Wangden 5,868 m and Brumkhangse 5,635 m. Permits are issued by the Home Department.
  - **Royalty for foreign teams:** US$350 (up to 4 members), US$500 (up to 8), plus US$75 per extra member.
  - **Insurance:** Rs 3 lakh per member.
  - **Limits:** at most 10 expeditions a year.
  - **Season:** May–October.

  Source: https://www.sikkim.gov.in/department/departmentmenudetails?url=Menu%3Dtourism-civil-aviation-department%2Fadventure-tourism **[VERIFY]** This may be outdated. The page cites a 17 Sep 2005 order.
- Many Sikkim PAPs require a group of two or more foreigners arranged through a registered Sikkim agent. Source: https://himalayatrekker.com/trek-permits-regulations/permits-rules-regulations-indian-himalayas-guide/

### 1.9 Arunachal Pradesh
- Indians need an e-ILP. Foreigners need a PAP and must keep to fixed routes. Source: https://himalayatrekker.com/trek-permits-regulations/permits-rules-regulations-indian-himalayas-guide/

### 1.10 Visa
- The IMF's 2018 foreign-team guidelines say a **regular tourist visa** is enough for open-area peaks. Peaks in restricted areas need an **"X Mountaineering Visa"** from the nearest Indian mission. Source: https://indianembassyberlin.gov.in/pdf/IMFForeignexp(4_4_2018).pdf . **[VERIFY]** This comes from a 2018 document. Tell clients to confirm the visa category with the Indian mission and the IMF when they apply.
- OCI cardholders are generally treated as foreign nationals for protected and restricted areas. Source: https://himalayatrekker.com/trek-permits-regulations/permits-rules-regulations-indian-himalayas-guide/

### 1.11 Rules for Indian expeditions (IMF)
- Leader must be over 25, hold advanced-course certification and have summited above 6,400 m. **50% of members must have passed the Advance Mountaineering Course (AMC)**; the rest need a BMC, with at most two experienced high-altitude trekkers allowed instead. Team size is 2–12 climbing members. The report is due **within two months** of the first summit attempt. Sources: https://www.indmount.org/IMF/expeapp ; https://www.bikatadventures.com/home/blog/qualifying-for-a-mountaineering-expedition
- Historically there was a flat **INR 3,000** booking charge for Indian teams (2020 operator blog). **[VERIFY]** The IMF does not publish an INR rate card online. Sources: https://www.bikatadventures.com/home/blog/procedure-for-getting-a-peak-climbing-permit-in-india ; https://theverticaltribe.com/imf-permit-cost-estimator/
- Commercial guided trekking-peak trips (Friendship, Yunam and similar) are open to non-BMC clients with high-altitude trekking experience. Operators set these prerequisites. Source: https://www.bikatadventures.com/Home/Itinerary/friendship-peak-expedition

### 1.12 Environmental rules (IMF)
- Biodegradable waste may be disposed of only below the snow line, and base camps must dig deep toilet pits. **Non-biodegradable waste must be carried down in sacks** to the road head. **All ropes and technical gear must be retrieved.** **Photo evidence** of compliance is mandatory in the report. Sources: https://www.indmount.org/IMF/expeapp ; https://indianembassyberlin.gov.in/pdf/IMFForeignexp(4_4_2018).pdf
- **[VERIFY]** The IMF documents I found mention **no refundable garbage deposit**. Don't claim one at national level.

## 3. Seasons by region
- **Himachal (Kullu, Pir Panjal, Dhauladhar) and Uttarakhand (Garhwal, Kumaon):** **pre-monsoon May–June** and **post-monsoon September–October**. Operator itineraries for Deo Tibba and Satopanth confirm this. Sources: https://trekthehimalayas.com/deo-tibba-expedition ; https://www.bikatadventures.com/Home/Itinerary/Mt-Satopanth-Peak-Expedition ; https://www.shikhar.com/mt-kamet-expedition-7756-m-sti326
  - Monsoon (July–August) brings landslides, flash floods and poor visibility on the southern slopes. This is why Kangra runs its registration order from July to 15 October: https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/himachal/himachal-mandatory-check-posts-on-trekking-routes-to-boost-monsoon-safety/
- **Ladakh, Zanskar, Lahaul and Spiti (rain shadow):** **July–September**. Ladakh sits in the rain shadow of the Great Himalaya, and Leh gets about 100 mm of precipitation a year. September has the clearest, quietest weather, and June brings dangerous snowmelt river crossings. Source: https://jtreks.co/ladakh/articles/ladakh-when-to-go
  - Operator windows: Mt Nun July–September (https://www.shikhar.com/mount-nun-expedition-7135-m-sti114); Yunam July–September (https://www.bikatadventures.com/Home/Itinerary/yunam-peak-expedition); Kang Yatse II June–September (https://www.bikatadventures.com/Home/Itinerary/Kang-Yatse-II-Trek).
- **Sikkim:** the state says "May to October is the ideal season for mountaineering in Sikkim". Source: Sikkim govt page in 1.8. **[VERIFY]** Operators more often recommend April–May and October–November because of the eastern Himalayan monsoon.
- **Arunachal:** no authoritative source found. Leave it out or keep it generic.

### 4.2 1965 Indian Everest expedition
- Led by **Capt. M.S. Kohli**. **Lt Col Avtar Singh Cheema and Nawang Gombu** made the first Indian summit on **20 May 1965**. Gombu became the **first person to climb Everest twice** (he had summited with the Americans in 1963). **Nine summiteers**, a world record that stood for 13 years. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1965_Indian_Everest_Expedition

### 4.3 The Nanda Devi plutonium device (brief, factual)
- In **October 1965** the CIA and India's Intelligence Bureau tried to place a **plutonium-238-powered (SNAP-19C)** sensor on Nanda Devi to monitor Chinese missile tests. The team was led by M.S. Kohli. A blizzard forced the climbers to leave the device at Camp IV on 16 Oct 1965, and the 1966 recovery attempt found nothing. A second device was placed on **Nanda Kot in 1967**. The story became public in **April 1978** (*Outside* magazine), and PM Morarji Desai addressed Parliament. Local concerns about contaminating the Ganga headwaters continue, but none has been scientifically confirmed. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanda_Devi_Plutonium_Mission ; see also https://www.dawn.com/news/1961177

### 4.4 Indian mountaineers and records
- **Bachendri Pal** (b. Nakuri, Uttarkashi): **first Indian woman on Everest, 23 May 1984**. Padma Shri 1984, Arjuna 1986, Padma Bhushan 2019. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachendri_Pal
- **Santosh Yadav:** **first woman in the world to climb Everest twice** (May 1992 and 1993). Padma Shri 2000. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santosh_Yadav
- **Arunima Sinha:** **first female amputee to summit Everest**, 21 May 2013. Completed the Seven Summits with Vinson on 4 Jan 2019. Padma Shri 2015. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arunima_Sinha
- **Malavath Purna (Poorna):** **youngest female Everest summiter** at **13 years 11 months** (25 May 2014). Completed the Seven Summits on 5 Jun 2022 (Denali). Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malavath_Purna
- **Anshu Jamsenpa** (Bomdila, Arunachal): **first woman to summit Everest twice in one season** (2017), within 5 days. Five Everest summits. Padma Shri 2021. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anshu_Jamsenpa
- **Baljeet Kaur** (Solan, **Himachal Pradesh**):
  - First Indian woman on Pumori (2021) and Dhaulagiri (1 Oct 2021).
  - In 2022, **first Indian woman to summit Manaslu without supplemental oxygen** and the **first Indian to climb six 8,000 m peaks**, in 5 months 2 days.
  - Now described as the first Indian woman with **seven 8,000 m peaks**.
  - Survived an ordeal on Annapurna in April 2023.

  Sources: https://english.nepalnews.com/s/travel-tourism/baljeet-kaur-indian-mountaineer-breaks-record/ ; https://himachalstory.com/famous-himachali/baljeet-kaur-indian-mountaineer/ ; https://www.globalindian.com/story/cover-story/baljeet-kaur-meet-the-first-indian-mountaineer-to-summit-seven-8000m-peaks/ . **[VERIFY]** I found no specific 2024 or 2025 record for her.
- **Arjun Vajpai:**
  - Everest in 2010 at 16 years, 11 months and 18 days, then the **youngest Indian** to do so.
  - In 2018, the world's youngest to climb six 8,000 m peaks.

## 6. Safety
- **AMS / HACE / HAPE (CDC Yellow Book):**
  - **AMS:** headache plus nausea, fatigue or dizziness.
  - **HACE:** confusion and ataxia; it is "end-stage" AMS.
  - **HAPE:** breathlessness at rest and cough; it "can be more rapidly fatal than HACE".
  - **Rule of thumb above 3,000 m:** raise your sleeping altitude by **no more than 500 m per night**, and add an **extra night for every 1,000 m** gained.
  - **Descent is the best treatment**; dropping 300 m or more often relieves AMS. Acetazolamide and dexamethasone are medical options, used under a doctor's guidance.

  Source: https://www.cdc.gov/yellow-book/hcp/environmental-hazards-risks/high-altitude-travel-and-altitude-illness.html
- **Rescue:**
  - Serious evacuations in the Indian Himalaya often rely on **Indian Air Force helicopters**. Example: in Aug 2023 an IAF helicopter from 114 HU, Leh, rescued an injured climber from the Mt Nun base camp area. Source: https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/j-k/body-of-missing-italian-trekker-found-in-ladakh-iaf-rescues-injured-mountaineer-534766/amp
  - District administrations run disaster-management check posts. In Kangra the **full rescue cost can be recovered** from unregistered trekkers. Source: https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/himachal/kangra-administration-makes-registration-compulsory-for-trekkers/
- **Insurance:**
  - **IMF:** policy must cover ground and helicopter search and rescue plus altitude illness, and support staff need at least INR 7 lakh cover (see 1.2).
  - **HP operators:** must insure clients for Rs 2 lakh life plus Rs 2 lakh rescue (see 1.6).
  - **Operators:** advise buying cover "for the heights you are likely to attain, and for helicopter rescue". Source: https://www.shikhar.com/mount-nun-expedition-7135-m-sti114
- **Context of real risk:**
  - The **Draupadi ka Danda II avalanche (4 Oct 2022)** killed **29** people on an NIM course, described as India's worst mountaineering tragedy. Source: https://publications.americanalpineclub.org/articles/13201216469
  - A 2019 avalanche killed eight climbers in the Nanda Devi area. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanda_Devi

## 8. Facts to avoid or treat as uncertain
1. **"Kamet was the first 7,000 m summit": wrong.** **Trisul (1907)** was the first peak over 7,000 m. Kamet (1931) was the first over **25,000 ft / 7,620 m**.
2. **"Nanda Devi is open to climb" / "Uttarakhand opened Nanda Devi": wrong.** The 2026 list includes **Nanda Devi East**. The main peak has been closed since 1983, and reopening was only "discussed" in 2025.
3. **"You can climb Kangchenjunga from Sikkim / India": wrong.** It is banned under Sikkim Notification 70/HOME/2001 and the Places of Worship Act. All modern ascents, including the Indian Army's in 2025, go via Nepal. The 1955 team deliberately did not stand on the summit.
4. **Stok Kangri:** do not advertise it. It has been closed since 2020 with no official reopening.
5. **Kanamo:** status is conflicting (banned versus reopened). Confirm with Kibber/Kaza authorities before listing.
6. **IMF per-member fees:** the official pages give different figures (US$200/250 vs US$225, etc.). Publish base fees plus "per-member charges apply".
7. **"Mountaineering visa is required for all expeditions": wrong.** Per the 2018 IMF guidelines, a tourist visa is enough for open areas, and the X Mountaineering visa is needed for restricted areas. Ask clients to verify, because the guidelines are dated.
8. **"IMF launched a new online portal in 2025/26" or "national IMF fee waiver":** not found. The only 2026 change is Uttarakhand's UKMPS portal and state-fee waiver. Indians pay no government fees in Uttarakhand, and foreigners still pay IMF fees.
9. **Garbage deposit:** there is no national refundable deposit in the IMF documents. The rule is carry-down plus photo proof.
10. **Heights that vary by source:**
    - Hanuman Tibba: 5,860 / 5,932 / 5,982 m.
    - Ladakhi: 5,257 / 5,342 / 5,345 m.
    - Shitidhar: 5,250 / 5,290 / 5,294 m.
    - Nanda Devi: 7,816 / 7,817 m.

    Use "about" or a single consistent source.
11. **Santosh Yadav's "Kangshung Face 1999"** claim (on Wikipedia) is poorly sourced. Omit it.
12. **Youngest Indian male Everest summiter:** sources disagree. Avoid naming anyone except "Arjun Vajpai was the youngest Indian at the time (2010)".
13. **"First Indian on Everest was Sonam Gyatso"** (a garbled reading of the Wikipedia list): the first Indians were **Avtar Singh Cheema and Nawang Gombu, 20 May 1965**.
14. **Sikkim peak list and royalty** (Frey's, Lama Wangden, Brumkhangse; US$350/500) come from a 2005-based state page and may be outdated.
15. **Chadar trek "cancelled in 2026"** appears in a single source (jtreks.co). Don't use it.
16. **Kangra registration rules** apply to specific Kangra routes for 8 Jul–15 Oct 2026. They are not a statewide HP mountaineering rule.
17. **Baljeet Kaur "2024/2025 records":** I could not confirm any specific 2024–25 record. Use the verified 2021–22 records and "seven 8,000 m peaks".
18. **Army "49 people on 27 May 2025" Everest record:** Wikipedia list only. Verify before use.
