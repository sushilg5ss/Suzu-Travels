Which is the highest peak in Sikkim?

Kangchenjunga (8,586 m / 28,169 ft) is the highest peak in Sikkim and India, but it is sacred and cannot be climbed from Sikkim. Nine more top 7,000 m, led by Jongsong (7,462 m) and Kabru North (7,412 m). Only a few smaller peaks, such as Frey Peak (5,830 m), are open to small expeditions. All 19 are in our list of India's highest peaks.

Sikkim is a small state with some of the biggest mountains on Earth. Its northern and western borders run along the Kangchenjunga Himal and the Janak Himal, where India meets Nepal and Tibet, and the Singalila ridge runs south along the Nepal border. From Gangtok and Pelling you look straight at peaks above 7,000 m.

The mountains in Sikkim are also among the most protected in the country. Kangchenjunga and several other summits are sacred to the people of Sikkim, most of the North Sikkim giants sit in restricted border areas, and recent ascents have been made by the Army, the Himalayan Mountaineering Institute (HMI) and other government teams. Only a handful of lower peaks are on the state's official list for small expeditions.

Our table holds the 19 Sikkim peaks in the dataset behind our Indian Himalaya guide: one eight-thousander, nine more above 7,000 m, six in the 6,000s and three in the 5,000s. Twelve are restricted or sacred, three have never been climbed, and four are open.

Permits

Permits for Sikkim's mountains: Indians and foreigners

Foreign visitors need a Protected Area Permit or Restricted Area Permit for Sikkim's high areas. These are usually issued for groups of two or more through a registered Sikkim travel agent, and a 2026 trade report says Sikkim now handles foreigners' permits online. Rules change, so confirm with Sikkim Tourism before you plan. A tourist visa covers open areas; confirm visa rules with the Indian mission.

Indian visitors do not need an Inner Line Permit to enter Sikkim, but North Sikkim and the high lakes need area permits, arranged locally through registered operators.

Any peak expedition needs permission from the Indian Mountaineering Foundation (IMF) plus the state's royalty. The IMF base fee for a foreign team of two is US$500 for peaks up to 6,500 m, US$700 for 6,501–7,000 m and US$1,000 above 7,000 m, plus US$500 for liaison-officer equipment; per-member charges apply (IMF, checked 1 Oct 2026). Since 2019 the IMF has not issued permits for Sikkim's sacred peaks. See our IMF permit and fees guide and how to climb in India.

Why most are closed

Sacred peaks and the 2001 ban

Kangchenjunga is sacred to the people of Sikkim. In 2001 the state banned climbing it under Notification 70/HOME/2001, and the route from Sikkim has been closed since about 2000. Kangchenjunga was first climbed in 1955 by Joe Brown and George Band, from Nepal; the Indian Army climbed it from Sikkim by the north-east spur in 1977. Modern ascents, including the Indian Army's in 2025, go from Nepal. The full story is on our Kangchenjunga page.

The 2019 row. On 21 August 2019 the Home Ministry's list of 137 peaks opened to foreigners included 24 in Sikkim, Kangchenjunga among them. After protests in Sikkim, the IMF's governing council decided on 28 August 2019 not to issue permits for Sikkim's sacred peaks. Kabru, Pandim and other summits are also regarded as sacred.

See them without climbing

Where to see Sikkim's big peaks

Goecha La (4,940 m / 16,207 ft), on the trek from Yuksom, looks up at Pandim and the south-east face of Kangchenjunga. Sandakphu (3,636 m / 11,929 ft), on the Singalila ridge just across the border in West Bengal, looks across the whole range. Khangchendzonga National Park (1,784 km², in Mangan and Gyalshing districts) was listed by UNESCO in July 2016 as India's first mixed natural and cultural World Heritage Site. FAQ

Sikkim's mountains: your questions

Which is the highest peak in Sikkim?

Kangchenjunga, 8,586 m (28,169 ft), on the Sikkim–Nepal border. It is the highest peak in India and the third-highest in the world.

Is Kangchenjunga open to climbers in Sikkim?

No. Sikkim banned climbing it in 2001 because it is sacred. Modern ascents, including the Indian Army's in 2025, go from Nepal.

How many peaks in Sikkim are above 7,000 m?

Ten in our dataset, Kangchenjunga included: nine more besides it, such as Jongsong (7,462 m), Kabru North (7,412 m) and Pauhunri (7,128 m).

Which peaks are open for mountaineering in Sikkim?

Sikkim's official list for small expeditions (2005–07 orders) names Frey Peak, Tinchenkhang, Jopuno, Lama Wangden and Brumkhangse. Confirm the current list with the state.

What is the second-highest mountain in Sikkim?

Zemu Gap Peak (7,780 m) is higher but is a subsidiary summit of Kangchenjunga. The second-highest independent peak is Jongsong, 7,462 m.

Which mountain is the source of the Teesta?

Pauhunri (7,128 m) on the Sikkim–Tibet border, first climbed by Alexander Kellas in 1911.

When is the mountaineering season in Sikkim?

The state names May to October. In practice April–May and October–November avoid the monsoon.

Where can I see Sikkim's big peaks?

From Goecha La (4,940 m) on the trek from Yuksom, and from Sandakphu (3,636 m) in West Bengal. Both look across to Kangchenjunga.
