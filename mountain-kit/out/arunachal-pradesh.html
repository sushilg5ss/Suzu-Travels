Which is the highest peak in Arunachal Pradesh?

Kangto (7,090 m / 23,261 ft; also given as 7,042–7,060 m) is the highest peak in Arunachal Pradesh, on the India–Tibet border in Bichom district. It stayed unclimbed until 26 November 2025, when an 18-member Indian Army team reached the top by the southern route. Second is Nyegyi Kansang (7,047 m), still unclimbed. All five are in our list of India's highest peaks.

Arunachal Pradesh is India's north-eastern corner, and its high peaks stand on the border with Tibet in the Eastern (Assam) Himalaya. They are some of the least-visited big mountains in the country: remote, deep in border districts such as Tawang, West and East Kameng and Bichom, and reached through forested valleys rather than the high, dry approaches of Ladakh or Spiti.

The highest is Kangto (7,090 m), one of three major peaks of the Kangto massif and the source of the Pachuk river, a tributary of the Kameng. Its height is published as 7,090, 7,060 and 7,042 m, the last being the Indian Army's figure. Its neighbour Nyegyi Kansang (7,047 m) is the state's only other 7,000er, and both appear in our 7,000 m peaks list.

Our table holds the five Arunachal peaks in the dataset behind our Indian Himalaya guide: two above 7,000 m and three in the 6,000s. Three have been climbed and two have no accepted ascent. All five are technical climbs in restricted border areas, and every recent ascent was made by the Army, the national mountaineering institute or a club expedition, not a commercial team.

Permits

Permits for Arunachal Pradesh: Indians and foreigners

Indian citizens need an Inner Line Permit, applied for online as an eILP on the state's portal. A tourist eILP covers stays of up to 14 days; longer stays need a provisional ILP. Children under 14 need no individual eILP but must be accompanied.

Foreign visitors need a Protected Area Permit (PAP), applied for through an approved local tour operator only, plus a royalty of US$30 per person to the Government of Arunachal Pradesh (as listed on the state tourism site, Oct 2026). A tourist visa covers open areas; confirm visa rules with the Indian mission.

Any peak expedition by foreigners also needs permission from the Indian Mountaineering Foundation (IMF). The IMF base fee for a foreign team of two is US$500 for peaks up to 6,500 m, US$700 for 6,501–7,000 m and US$1,000 above 7,000 m, plus US$500 for liaison-officer equipment; per-member charges apply (IMF, checked 1 Oct 2026). Arunachal was not on the Home Ministry's 2019 list of 137 peaks opened to foreigners. See our IMF permit and fees guide and how to climb in India.

The highest

Kangto peak: Arunachal's highest, first climbed in 2025

For decades Kangto was out of reach. Earlier attempts could not even reach its base; on 21 October 2017 a joint ITBP–IMF team set up a base camp at about 9,920 ft (3,024 m) on the Pachuk river.

  • 3 November 2025An 18-member Indian Army Eastern Command team (4 Corps) is flagged off from a forward base.
  • 26 November 2025The team reaches the summit by the "extremely challenging" southern route: the first recorded ascent of Kangto.
  • 5 and 8 December 2025The Army calls the peak "hitherto unconquered", and the Governor of Arunachal Pradesh congratulates the team.

Why "recorded"? No earlier ascent of Kangto is documented, so the 2025 climb is the first recorded one. Summit details such as camp heights and day counts have not been published.

Where they are

Arunachal's peaks, area by area

The five peaks sit in two groups along the border.

The Kangto massif (Bichom, East Kameng)

  • Kangto (7,090 m): the state's highest, first recorded ascent 2025
  • Nyegyi Kansang (7,047 m, also 7,050 m): a 1995 Indian team led by M.P. Yadav claimed it by the north-east ridge on 23 October, but was later found to have stopped about 600 m below the main summit, so it is still counted as unclimbed
  • Chiumo (6,890 m): no documented ascent

Tawang and West Kameng

  • Gorichen (6,488 m): used by the Army for training; on 21 October 2024 the Mountaineers' Association of Krishnanagar (West Bengal) made what was reported as the first civilian ascent
  • Tsangyang Gyatso (6,383 m): first climbed on 25 September 2024 by NIMAS Dirang, and named after the 6th Dalai Lama

NIMAS, the National Institute of Mountaineering and Adventure Sports, is based at Dirang in West Kameng; see our mountaineering courses guide.

When to go

When are Arunachal's peaks climbed?

There is no official climbing season for Arunachal Pradesh. The recent ascents give the pattern: late September (Tsangyang Gyatso), October (Gorichen) and November (Kangto), all after the monsoon. Our dataset lists October–November for all five peaks.

FAQ

Arunachal Pradesh's peaks: your questions

Which is the highest peak in Arunachal Pradesh?

Kangto, 7,090 m (23,261 ft); some sources give 7,042–7,060 m. It stands on the India–Tibet border in Bichom district.

Has Kangto been climbed?

Yes. An 18-member Indian Army Eastern Command team made the first recorded ascent on 26 November 2025, by the southern route.

Which is the second-highest peak in Arunachal Pradesh?

Nyegyi Kansang, 7,047 m. An Indian team claimed it in 1995, but it stopped about 600 m below the main summit, so the peak is still counted as unclimbed.

What is Gorichen peak?

A 6,488 m peak in the Tawang–West Kameng area, used by the Army for training. A West Bengal club made the first civilian ascent on 21 October 2024.

Which Arunachal peak is named after the 6th Dalai Lama?

Tsangyang Gyatso (6,383 m) in Tawang, first climbed by NIMAS Dirang on 25 September 2024.

Do I need a permit to visit Arunachal Pradesh?

Yes. Indians need an eILP (online, up to 14 days for tourists). Foreigners need a Protected Area Permit through an approved tour operator, with a US$30 royalty.

Can foreigners climb in Arunachal Pradesh?

Only with a Protected Area Permit plus IMF permission. All the state's big peaks are in restricted border areas, and recent ascents have been Army or institute expeditions.

When is the best time to climb in Arunachal Pradesh?

There is no official season, but recent ascents were made from late September to November, after the monsoon.
