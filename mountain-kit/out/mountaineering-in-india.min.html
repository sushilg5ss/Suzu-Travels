How does mountaineering in India work for foreign climbers?

Foreign climbers need central-government permission for every Indian peak, obtained through the Indian Mountaineering Foundation (IMF): apply online at least 90 days ahead, travel with an IMF liaison officer, carry rescue insurance and pay the peak fee in US dollars. Choose an open peak first, then add the state rules. Compare heights on our list of the highest peaks in India.

India holds a long stretch of the Himalaya, from Ladakh and Himachal Pradesh through Uttarakhand to Sikkim and Arunachal Pradesh. Climbing there is open to visitors, but it is regulated more closely than in many countries: every foreign expedition is approved in advance and accompanied by a government liaison officer. This guide walks through that chain in the order you will meet it. For ranges, records and the wider picture, see our Indian Himalaya guide.

Rules change. Each step below is dated, and the official bodies (the IMF, the Ministry of Home Affairs, the state governments and India's missions abroad) always have the final word.

Six steps

How to climb in the Himalayas in India: the six steps

  1. 1 · Choose an open peak.Foreigners may climb only peaks the government has opened to them; many border peaks are restricted, and a few are closed outright. Start from the open lists, not from a photo.
  2. 2 · Apply to the IMF.Applications go through the IMF's online portal (live since July 2017) at least 90 days before the expedition, first come, first served. Peaks can be booked up to three years ahead with a 25% advance.
  3. 3 · Pay the peak fee.The IMF charges a base fee in US dollars by peak height, plus liaison-officer costs and per-member charges.
  4. 4 · Get the right visa.Indian missions issue a mountaineering visa once the expedition is approved; ask your mission which category your peak needs.
  5. 5 · Add state permits.Some valleys need a Protected Area Permit for foreigners, and each state has its own rules for operators and routes.
  6. 6 · Brief, climb, report.The team attends an IMF briefing in New Delhi, climbs with its liaison officer, carries its waste down and files a report after a debriefing.
Open, restricted, closed

Which peaks can foreigners climb in India?

On 21 August 2019 the Ministry of Home Affairs opened 137 peaks to foreign climbers: 47 in Himachal Pradesh, 51 in Uttarakhand, 24 in Sikkim and 15 in what was then Jammu and Kashmir. The amended Foreigners Order of December 2019 still requires the prior written permission of the Central Government for any peak, given through the IMF, which also fixes the route and the conditions. Nationals and people of origin of China, Pakistan, Bangladesh and Afghanistan need central permission even for opened peaks.

The IMF permit

The IMF permit: fees, liaison officer and insurance

Fees (team of two)

  • IMF-listed trekking peaks: US$200
  • Peaks up to 6,500 m: US$500
  • 6,501–7,000 m: US$700
  • Above 7,000 m: US$1,000
  • Plus US$500 liaison-officer equipment hire; per-member charges apply

IMF peak-fee page, as of October 2026.

What the permit asks of you

  • A mandatory IMF liaison officer; the team pays their travel to and from New Delhi
  • A team insurance policy covering ground and helicopter search and rescue and altitude illness, shown at the briefing; support staff insured too
  • Validity of 30–45 days, base camp to base camp, on the approved route only
  • Non-biodegradable waste carried down and all ropes retrieved, with photo proof

Under the IMF's 2018 guidelines, a complete application for an open peak was to be cleared within 7 days, while restricted areas take months: plan with time to spare. The full fee table and the permit categories are in our IMF permit and peak fees guide.

Visa

Which visa do you need to climb in India?

Indian missions abroad issue a mountaineering visa once the IMF or the relevant authority has approved the expedition. The Consulate General in Istanbul, for example, asks climbers to apply at least four months ahead and issues it for six months or a year. The IMF's 2018 guidelines for foreign expeditions said a tourist visa was enough for peaks in open areas, and the mountaineering (X) visa was needed for restricted areas.

Confirm the visa category with your Indian mission when you apply. Rules differ between missions and change over time.

State by state

State rules on top of the IMF permit

Himachal Pradesh

  • Trekking and mountaineering operators and guides must be registered with HP Tourism; guides are 21 or older and first-aid certified, and difficult routes are led by qualified leaders (2021 adventure rules)
  • Every participant insured; GPS tracking and registration before the trek
  • Foreigners need a Protected Area Permit beyond Jangi in Kinnaur and near the Tibet border in Spiti
  • Kangra made check-post registration compulsory on ten Dhauladhar routes from 8 July to 15 October 2026; that window has ended, so check for a new order before a trek there

Uttarakhand

  • 83 peaks opened on 4 February 2026
  • Applications on the state's UKMPS portal
  • Indians pay no state expedition fees; foreigners pay the IMF fees
  • Leave No Trace is mandatory

Ladakh

  • Stok Kangri closed (2023 administration order)
  • Foreigners need a Protected Area Permit for Nubra, Pangong, Tso Moriri and Hanle; not for Leh, the highways or Zanskar

Sikkim

  • Kangchenjunga is banned as a climbing objective
  • Few peaks are open; Protected Area Permits through registered Sikkim agents
When to go

When is the mountaineering season in India?

RegionBest monthsWhy
Himachal (Kullu, Pir Panjal) and UttarakhandMay–June, September–OctoberBefore and after the monsoon
Lahaul, Spiti, Kinnaur and LadakhJuly–SeptemberRain-shadow valleys stay dry through the monsoon

In July and August the monsoon brings rain and landslides to the southern slopes, so roads to Kullu and Garhwal base camps can close for days. Rain-shadow peaks such as Yunam are climbed in exactly that window.

Skills

Do you need a mountaineering course?

Foreign teams are not asked for a course certificate by the IMF. Indian teams are: the leader must be 25 or older with an advanced course and a summit above 6,400 m, and half the members need an advanced course. On a guided trekking peak, the outfitter teaches crampons, ice axe and rope work at base camp.

India's institutes run the full ladder of courses, from basic and advanced mountaineering to method of instruction and search and rescue: NIM in Uttarkashi, HMI in Darjeeling, ABVIMAS in Manali, JIM&WS in Pahalgam and NIMAS in Dirang. Compare them in our guide to mountaineering courses in India, and read about altitude sickness before any high climb.

FAQ

Mountaineering in India: questions from foreign climbers

Do foreigners need a permit to climb in India?

Yes. Every peak needs central-government permission, given through the Indian Mountaineering Foundation (IMF). Apply on the IMF portal at least 90 days before the expedition.

How much does a climbing permit cost in India?

The IMF base fee for a team of two is US$200 for IMF-listed trekking peaks, US$500 up to 6,500 m, US$700 for 6,501–7,000 m and US$1,000 above 7,000 m, plus US$500 liaison-officer equipment hire and per-member charges (as of October 2026).

Which visa do I need for mountaineering in India?

Indian missions issue a mountaineering visa after the expedition is approved. The IMF's 2018 guidelines said a tourist visa covered open-area peaks and the X visa restricted areas. Confirm the category with your Indian mission when you apply.

Do I need a liaison officer?

Yes. Every foreign expedition is accompanied by an IMF liaison officer. The team pays US$500 for their equipment and their travel to and from New Delhi.

Can I climb in India without a guide or an agency?

A foreign team can apply to the IMF directly. In Himachal Pradesh, commercial climbs must be run by operators registered with HP Tourism, with registered guides.

Is Stok Kangri open in 2026?

No. Stok Kangri in Ladakh has been closed since 2020, and no official reopening has been announced as of October 2026.

When is the best season for mountaineering in India?

May–June and September–October for Himachal's Kullu peaks and Uttarakhand; July–September for the rain-shadow regions of Lahaul, Spiti, Kinnaur and Ladakh.

Where should a first-time Himalayan climber start?

A Himachal trekking peak such as Friendship Peak (5,289 m), then Yunam (6,111 m) or Deo Tibba (6,001 m).
