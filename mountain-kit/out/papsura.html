What is Papsura, and can you climb it?

Papsura (6,451 m / 21,165 ft) is a technical peak in Kullu, Himachal Pradesh, at the head of the Tos glacier above Tosh in the Parvati valley. Locals call it the 'Peak of Evil', twin of Dharamsura, the 'Peak of Good'. First climbed on 3 June 1967, it is open to foreign teams with IMF permission. Compare it with India's other 6,000 m peaks.

Papsura stands on the Kullu–Lahaul watershed, at the head of the upper Tos glacier, the ice stream that feeds the Tos nala and joins the Parvati valley near Tosh. Its summit is 6,451 m (21,165 ft), the classic survey figure used by the Himalayan Journal and the American Alpine Journal. A 2021 government list of open peaks gives 6,440 m, and a few accounts quote 6,456 or 6,461 m.

This page brings together the legend, every documented route from 1967 to 2017, the walk-in from Barshaini with approximate altitudes, the season and the permits. Papsura is a hard mountain and we say so plainly. For the wider picture, see our Indian Himalaya guide, the full list of India's highest peaks and our guide to the peaks of Himachal Pradesh.

Route and camps

Route up the Tos glacier

From Bhuntar the road follows the Parvati river past Manikaran to the road-head at Barshaini. The walk begins at Tosh and follows the Tos nala past Budhaban and the Sharam (Shamsi) meadows to the Kuta Thach base camp, about 4,260 m, which the 2017 Korean team reached in about four days from Tosh. In 1971 the Indian Air Force team took seven days from Manikaran to its advanced base.

Above base, an advanced camp sits near the Papsura icefall, and the 1971 team placed two higher camps on the crevassed Papsura glacier before the summit push. The altitudes below come from the 1971 and 2017 accounts and are approximate; camps move with conditions and with the route chosen on the upper mountain.

Day by day

How a Papsura expedition is staged (outline, not a fixed schedule)

  1. Stage 1 · Kullu to BarshainiDrive from Bhuntar up the Parvati valley past Manikaran, with permits, police registration and gear checks.
  2. Stage 2 · Tosh to Kuta ThachWalk up the Tos nala through Budhaban and the Sharam meadows to base camp at about 4,260 m, about four days.
  3. Stage 3 · Acclimatise and stock the mountainCarry loads to advanced base by the icefall and rest at base camp.
  4. Stage 4 · Camps on the glacierClimb through the icefall and set Camp 1 (~5,180 m) and Camp 2 (~5,490 m), with fixed lines where needed.
  5. Stage 5 · Summit pushA long, early day on the chosen line; turn back if the couloirs are loaded with snow.
  6. Stage 6 · Spare days and walk-outKeep days for weather, then return down the Tos glacier to Tosh. Plan a long expedition of several weeks.

Itineraries vary with the outfitter, group and weather; rest days are added when needed.

What it takes

How hard is Papsura?

Papsura is a technical peak, not a trekking peak. The south-face couloirs are prone to avalanches, the Papsura glacier is crevassed, the approach to the upper camps goes through an icefall, and steep rock guards the top. In 1967 three members of the first-ascent team fell about 1,500 ft (450 m) in what they named 'Avalanche Couloir', and in 2005 a member of an IMF women's expedition died on the descent.

Come with several earlier summits, including steep ice and mixed ground, and a full mountaineering course. In Kullu, Deo Tibba and then Indrasan are natural stepping stones; our list of the hardest peaks in India shows where Papsura's kind of climbing sits. When to go

Best time to climb Papsura

Mid-May to June. The 1967 first ascent (3 June), the 1971 Air Force ascent (4 June) and the 2017 Korean ascent (3 June) all reached the top in early June. A West Bengal team summited in late July 2012, and teams have also tried in September.

The Parvati valley gets monsoon rain in July and August, and the couloirs are avalanche-prone in any season. Keep spare days and start summit days well before dawn. Our guide to the best time for mountaineering in India compares the regions.

Permits

Permits for Papsura

Indian climbers need permission from the Indian Mountaineering Foundation (IMF). In Himachal, climbs go through an HP Tourism-registered outfitter with registered guides; under the state's 2021 adventure rules difficult routes need a certified leader, and every member needs police registration and insurance.

Foreign nationals can climb Papsura: it is on the government list of peaks open to foreign expeditions (Rajya Sabha reply, March 2021), together with Dharamsura. Apply to the IMF at least 90 days ahead; a liaison officer goes with the team. At 6,451 m Papsura falls in the up-to-6,500 m band: a base fee of US$500 for a team of two, plus US$500 for liaison-officer equipment, and per-member charges apply (IMF peak-fee page, checked Oct 2026). Our IMF permit and fees guide and our guide to mountaineering in India for foreigners explain the system.

A tourist visa covers open areas, but visa rules can change: confirm with the Indian mission before you travel.

History

Climbing history of Papsura

Dharamsura, the twin, was climbed first, in 1941 by J.O.M. Roberts' party. Robert Pettigrew's British party explored the area in the mid-1960s, crossing the Sara Umga La, and returned in 1967. On 3 June 1967 Geoffrey Hill and Colin Pritchard reached the top of Papsura by the south-face couloir and a corniced ridge, days after three of the team survived a long fall in 'Avalanche Couloir'.

The Indian Air Force made the second ascent on 4 June 1971 by the south-east ridge. Needle and Perriment climbed the south-west ridge in 1977, a New Zealand team the west-face couloir in 1991, and a West Bengal team the north-north-west ridge in July 2012. In 2017 Kim Chang-ho's Korean team climbed the South Face Direct, and the same year Hilaree O'Neill, Jim Morrison and Chris Figenshau made the first ski descent of the west couloir.

Not every attempt succeeds. In 2005 a Himalayan Club Kolkata team turned back about 40 m below the top, and a 2025 British attempt on the north face was abandoned because of illness. Papsura rewards patience more than speed.

Kullu's hard peaks

Papsura and Dharamsura: the peaks of evil and good

Drawn at their true heights, the two Tos glacier peaks are almost level. Survey figures give Papsura 6,451 m and Dharamsura 6,446 m; the 1967 Himalayan Journal account put Papsura 'about 17 ft' higher. Dharamsura is also called White Sail, and a government list gives it 6,420 m.

Local people say the relative heights of the two peaks shift with whether good or evil prevails in the world: when Papsura looks higher, evil has the upper hand. That is a belief, not a survey result, but it gave the mountain its name. A 1971 account called Papsura 'one of the devil's thrones', a peak the valley people avoided.

For scale, Deo Tibba (6,001 m) and Indrasan (6,220 m) stand further west in Kullu, and Mulkila (6,517 m) is the highest peak of Lahaul.

FAQ

Papsura: questions people ask

How high is Papsura?

6,451 m (21,165 ft) by the classic survey figure. A 2021 government list gives 6,440 m.

Why is Papsura called the Peak of Evil?

Local belief pairs it with Dharamsura, the 'Peak of Good': whichever looks higher shows whether good or evil prevails. It is a legend, not a survey fact.

Is Papsura higher than Dharamsura?

Yes, by survey: 6,451 m against 6,446 m, only a few metres.

Who first climbed Papsura?

Geoffrey Hill and Colin Pritchard of Robert Pettigrew's British expedition, on 3 June 1967.

Where is Papsura?

At the head of the Tos glacier above Tosh in the Parvati valley, Kullu district, on the Kullu–Lahaul divide.

How do you reach Papsura base camp?

From the Barshaini road-head and Tosh up the Tos nala, about four days to the Kuta Thach base camp at roughly 4,260 m.

Can foreigners climb Papsura?

Yes. It is on the government list of peaks open to foreigners. IMF permission, a liaison officer and the US$500 base fee for a team of two apply (Oct 2026).

Has anyone skied Papsura?

Yes. Hilaree O'Neill, Jim Morrison and Chris Figenshau made the first ski descent of the west couloir in 2017.
