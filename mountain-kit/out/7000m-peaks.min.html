How many 7000m peaks are there in India?

Our dataset counts 48 peaks between 7,000 and 7,999 m in India, with Kangchenjunga (8,586 m) above them. The highest is Nanda Devi (7,816 m / 25,643 ft), closed since 1983. Ladakh holds 24, Uttarakhand 12 and Sikkim 9. Most are restricted: only 13 are open to IMF-permitted foreign teams. All are in our list of India's highest peaks.

Seven-thousanders are the great expedition peaks of the Indian Himalaya: three to four weeks on the mountain, big glaciers, long summit days and, for foreign teams, permission from the Indian Mountaineering Foundation (IMF). Exact national counts differ with how subsidiary summits are treated, so we say "48 in our dataset" rather than claim a single official number.

Half of them stand in Ladakh's East Karakoram, around the Siachen glacier and the Nubra valley. Uttarakhand's Garhwal and Kumaon hold the classic, historic peaks such as Trisul, Kamet and Nanda Devi; Sikkim's line the Kangchenjunga massif; and Arunachal Pradesh has two, including Kangto. Himachal Pradesh has none: its highest summit, Reo Purgyil, stops at 6,816 m (22,362 ft).

Why most 7,000 m peaks in India are off-limits

Of the 48 peaks, 33 are restricted for foreign climbers in our dataset and two are not open to them at all. The reasons differ by region.

Ladakh: a military frontier

Saltoro Kangri, K12, the Teram Kangri group, Ghent, Singhi, the Apsarasas group and Padmanabh stand on or near the Siachen–Saltoro line, a military zone. The Saser, Mamostong and Rimo peaks lie in the inner-line Nubra area: the IMF permits them only with joint or army clearance, and East Karakoram climbs run as joint expeditions.

Sikkim: sacred and restricted

Sikkim restricts climbing on most of its high peaks, some of them sacred, and bans Kangchenjunga outright (Notification 70/HOME/2001). Peaks such as Jongsong, Kabru North and Pauhunri are restricted, and Zemu Gap Peak has never been climbed.

Uttarakhand: closed sanctuary, open list

Nanda Devi, India's highest seven-thousander, has been closed since 1983 with its sanctuary. Only Nanda Devi East is on Uttarakhand's 2026 open list. Most other Garhwal and Kumaon 7,000ers are open through the IMF.

Arunachal Pradesh: remote and new

Kangto (7,090 m), the state's highest, had its first recorded ascent on 26 November 2025 by an 18-member Indian Army team. Nyegyi Kansang (7,047 m) has no confirmed ascent; a 1995 Army claim is disputed.

An honest answer

What is the easiest 7000m peak in India?

No official body ranks India's 7,000ers by difficulty, so no peak is "the easiest" as a fact. Our dataset grades three of the open peaks moderate; every other seven-thousander is graded technical or extreme.

  • Satopanth (7,075 m / 23,212 ft)Garhwal, Uttarakhand; first climbed in 1947. On the 2026 open list.
  • Trisul I (7,120 m / 23,360 ft)Kumaon, Uttarakhand; the first 7,000 m summit ever climbed, in 1907.
  • Abi Gamin (7,355 m / 24,131 ft)Garhwal, next to Kamet; first climbed in 1950.

"Moderate" here still means a full expedition of about three to four weeks, IMF permission and earlier experience on a 6,000 m peak. The usual stepping stones are in Himachal: Yunam (6,111 m) and Deo Tibba (6,001 m). Our guide to climbing your first 6,000 m peak sets out that path step by step.

Permits and fees

Permits and IMF fees for a 7,000 m expedition

Foreign expeditions need IMF permission, applied for at least 90 days ahead, a liaison officer and insurance. For a team of two the IMF base peak fee is US$1,000 above 7,000 m (US$700 for 6,501–7,000 m), plus US$500 for liaison-officer equipment; per-member charges apply (IMF, checked 1 October 2026). Our IMF permit and peak fee guide explains each charge.

Nationals of China, Pakistan, Bangladesh and Afghanistan need central-government permission even for opened peaks (Foreigners (Amendment) Order 2019). Indian teams follow IMF rules too: the leader must hold an advanced-course certificate and have summited above 6,400 m, and at least half the members must have passed the Advanced Mountaineering Course.

Restricted peaks need extra clearances on top of the IMF permit, and several are not permitted at all. A tourist visa covers open areas; the X (mountaineering) visa is for restricted areas, so confirm with the Indian mission before you apply.

Firsts

Seven-thousander firsts in the Indian Himalaya

  1. 1907 · Trisul ITom Longstaff, Alexis and Henri Brocherel and Karbir reached the top on 12 June: the first 7,000 m summit climbed anywhere.
  2. 1911 · PauhunriAlexander Kellas and two Sherpas made it the highest summit climbed until 1928.
  3. 1930 · JongsongHermann Hoerlin and Erwin Schneider set a new height record, held until the next year.
  4. 1931 · KametFrank Smythe, Eric Shipton and party made the first ascent of a summit above 25,000 ft (not the first 7,000 m summit).
  5. 1936 · Nanda DeviBill Tilman and Noel Odell: the highest summit climbed until 1950.
  6. 1953 · NunPierre Vittoz and Claude Kogan, on 28 August.
  7. 2025 · KangtoFirst recorded ascent of Arunachal's highest peak, by an Indian Army team on 26 November.

Four of India's 7,000 m peaks still have no recorded ascent in our dataset: Zemu Gap Peak (7,780 m), Apsarasas Kangri II (7,239 m), Nyegyi Kansang (7,047 m, a disputed 1995 claim) and Shudu Tsenpa (7,024 m).

FAQ

7,000 m peaks: your questions

How many 7,000 m peaks are there in India?

About 48 between 7,000 and 7,999 m in our dataset, plus Kangchenjunga (8,586 m) above 8,000 m. Counts vary with how subsidiary summits are treated.

Which is the highest 7,000 m peak in India?

Nanda Devi, 7,816 m (25,643 ft), the highest peak wholly within India. It has been closed to climbers since 1983.

Which 7,000 m peaks can foreigners climb in India?

Thirteen are marked open in our dataset, always with IMF permission: Kamet, Nanda Devi East, Abi Gamin, Mana Peak, Mukut Parbat, Hardeol, Chaukhamba I, Trisul I, Satopanth, Tirsuli and Dunagiri in Uttarakhand, plus Nun and Kun.

What is the easiest 7,000 m peak in India?

There is no official ranking. Our dataset grades Satopanth, Trisul I and Abi Gamin moderate; the rest are technical or extreme. Even these need 6,000 m experience, about three to four weeks and IMF permission.

Is there a 7,000 m peak in Himachal Pradesh?

No. Himachal's highest peak is Reo Purgyil at 6,816 m. Its 6,000 m peaks, such as Yunam (6,111 m) and Deo Tibba (6,001 m), are common stepping stones.

What was the first 7,000 m peak ever climbed?

Trisul I (7,120 m) in Uttarakhand, on 12 June 1907, by Tom Longstaff, Alexis and Henri Brocherel and Karbir. Kamet (1931) was the first summit above 25,000 ft.

How much does the IMF charge for a 7,000 m peak?

For a foreign team of two, a base fee of US$1,000 above 7,000 m (US$700 for 6,501–7,000 m), plus US$500 for liaison-officer equipment and per-member charges (IMF, as of October 2026).

Can you climb Nanda Devi or Kangchenjunga from India?

No. Nanda Devi has been closed since 1983, and Sikkim bans climbing Kangchenjunga (Notification 70/HOME/2001). Nanda Devi East is on Uttarakhand's 2026 open list.
