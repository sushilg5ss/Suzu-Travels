How high is Chaukhamba, and where is it?

Chaukhamba is a massif of four summits in the Garhwal Himalaya of Uttarakhand, west of Badrinath. The highest, Chaukhamba I, is 7,138 m (23,419 ft), the top of the Gangotri group, and the Gangotri glacier rises on its north-western slopes. The name means "four pillars". It was first climbed on 13 June 1952. See all 7,000 m peaks in India.

Four pillars on one ridge. Chaukhamba means "four pillars", for the four summits that stand in a line on a north-east to south-west ridge. The main summit, Chaukhamba I, is at the north-east end; the heights of all four are in the four pillars table below. The massif rises 1,594 m above its key col at Mana Pass, which makes it an ultra-prominent peak.

Where the Ganga's glacier begins. Chaukhamba stands above three glaciers: the Gangotri, the Bhagirathi Kharak and the Satopanth. The Gangotri glacier, the source of the Bhagirathi, rises on its north-western slopes, and the Satopanth glacier links it to Satopanth (7,075 m). Route and camps

The route to Chaukhamba I, camp by camp

From Badrinath, up the Bhagirathi Kharak glacier. The first ascent in 1952 and the 2022 solo climb both came from the north-east side. The road ends at Badrinath, at about 3,300 m. Base camp sits at the fork of the Bhagirathi Kharak glacier at 4,627 m, about five days from the road. In 1952 the summit team climbed the north-east face from a high camp at about 5,950 m.

The 1952 line on the north face is described today as no longer practicable, and the north flank as very dangerous. In 2001 an Indian team climbing from Badrinath had its Camp 3 at 6,350 m; an avalanche hit that camp. Later climbers have used the north-east ridge.

The west side is reached from Gangotri and Gaumukh: a 2004 team put base camp at about 4,900 m where the Swachand glacier meets the Gangotri glacier. Camp heights change from year to year. No official itinerary exists; about four weeks from the road-head is a fair estimate.

What it takes

How hard is it to climb Chaukhamba?

Our dataset grades Chaukhamba I technical. It is a full expedition above 7,000 m, and the record shows real danger: avalanches and rockfall on the north side, an avalanche that killed two climbers at Camp 3 in 2001, and a three-day ordeal on Chaukhamba III in October 2024 that ended with an Indian Air Force rescue.

Arrive with at least one earlier summit above 6,000 m and solid fixed-rope and glacier skills. In Himachal, good steps towards a peak like this are Deo Tibba (6,001 m), Mulkila (6,517 m) and Reo Purgyil (6,816 m). Read our altitude sickness guide before you go high.

  • ExperienceAt least one summit above 6,000 m and days on crevassed glaciers.
  • TechniqueCrampons and ice axe on steep snow and ice, fixed ropes, jumaring, abseiling and crevasse rescue.
  • FitnessRepeated load carries between camps and a long summit day near 7,000 m.
  • JudgementReading avalanche slopes and fresh snow, and the discipline to turn back.
  • TrainingA Basic and an Advanced Mountaineering Course; Indian teams also follow IMF rules on team qualifications.
When to go

Best time to climb Chaukhamba

May to June and September to October. The recorded climbs fit those windows: the first ascent on 13 June 1952 and the Korean ascent of Chaukhamba II on 14 May 1996. The October 2024 rescue shows that autumn weather can still turn fast at 6,000 m.

Permits

Permits for Chaukhamba in 2026

Indian climbers apply on Uttarakhand's UKMPS portal. The Chaukhamba group is on the state's February 2026 list of 83 open peaks, and all state fees are waived for Indian climbers in 2026. IMF rules for Indian teams still apply.

Foreign nationals need IMF permission, with a liaison officer and insurance. Chaukhamba I and II are above 7,000 m, where the IMF base fee is US$1,000 for a team of two, plus US$500 for liaison-officer equipment, and per-member charges apply; Chaukhamba III and IV fall in the 6,501–7,000 m band (US$700) (IMF peak-fee page, as of 1 Oct 2026). More in our IMF permit and peak fees guide and mountaineering in India for foreign climbers.

The west approach from Gaumukh lies inside Gangotri National Park; park entry is arranged with the expedition permit.

History

Climbing history of Chaukhamba

1938 and 1939: the first attempts on Chaukhamba failed.

13 June 1952: Lucien George and Victor Russenberger, Swiss members of a French expedition led by E. Frendo, made the first ascent of Chaukhamba I by the north-east face from the Bhagirathi Kharak glacier, starting from a high camp at about 5,950 m.

1995: a team of instructors from the Nehru Institute of Mountaineering, led by Col M.P. Yadav, climbed Chaukhamba I by the west face and made the first ascent of Chaukhamba II. 1996: the Garhwal Rifles climbed Chaukhamba I by the south-west face, and on 14 May a Korean team made the second ascent of Chaukhamba II by the north-west ridge.

2001: an Indian expedition from Badrinath lost two members when an avalanche hit Camp 3 at 6,350 m. 2004: a Nehru Institute team tried Chaukhamba III and IV from the Gangotri side and reached about 6,300 m.

2022: Boris Textor climbed Chaukhamba I solo by the north-east ridge, reaching it by the left side of the north face; his GPS read 7,136 m on top.

October 2024: two foreign women climbers, one American and one British, were stranded at about 6,015 m on Chaukhamba III after losing their food and gear. A French team found them on 5 October, and Indian Air Force helicopters lifted them to Joshimath on 6 October. February 2026: the Chaukhamba group was named in Uttarakhand's list of 83 opened peaks.

Geography

Chaukhamba and the Gangotri glacier

Chaukhamba is the highest point of the Gangotri group. The Gangotri glacier, the source of the Bhagirathi, rises on its north-western slopes. The Bhagirathi Kharak and Satopanth glaciers lie on its other flanks, and the Satopanth glacier links it to Satopanth peak.

That is why the peak has two ways in: from Gangotri and Gaumukh on the west, inside Gangotri National Park, and from Badrinath on the east. For other Garhwal giants, see Shivling (6,543 m) and Kamet (7,756 m).

FAQ

Chaukhamba: questions people ask

What is the height of Chaukhamba?

Chaukhamba I, the highest of its four summits, is 7,138 m (23,419 ft). The other three are Chaukhamba II (7,070 m), III (about 6,990 m) and IV (6,854 m).

Why is it called Chaukhamba?

Chaukhamba means "four pillars", for the four summits that stand in a line on one ridge.

Where is Chaukhamba?

In the Garhwal Himalaya of Uttarakhand, west of Badrinath, at the head of the Gangotri glacier. It is the highest peak of the Gangotri group.

Which glacier starts from Chaukhamba?

The Gangotri glacier, the source of the Bhagirathi, rises on Chaukhamba's north-western slopes.

Who first climbed Chaukhamba?

Lucien George and Victor Russenberger, Swiss members of a French expedition, reached the summit of Chaukhamba I on 13 June 1952 by the north-east face.

Is Chaukhamba open for climbing in 2026?

Yes. The Chaukhamba group is on Uttarakhand's February 2026 list of 83 opened peaks. Indian climbers apply on the UKMPS portal with no state fees; foreign teams apply through the IMF.

How difficult is Chaukhamba?

It is a technical expedition above 7,000 m with avalanche and rockfall danger; the north flank is described as very dangerous. Climbers need an earlier 6,000 m summit and mountaineering training.

What happened on Chaukhamba III in 2024?

Two foreign women climbers were stranded at about 6,015 m for three days after losing their food and gear. Indian Air Force helicopters rescued them on 6 October 2024.
