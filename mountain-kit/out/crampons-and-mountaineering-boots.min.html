What are crampons, and which boots do they need?

Crampons are steel or aluminium frames with sharp points, usually 10 to 12 and up to 14, that fix under your boots so you can walk and climb on hard snow and ice. The two front points let you kick into steep slopes. Match them to your boots: crampons no stiffer than the boot. See our mountaineering gear list for the full kit.

A crampon is a traction device that you attach to your footwear so you can move safely on snow and ice: across glaciers and snowfields, up steep snow and over ice-covered rock. On almost every snow peak in the Indian Himalaya, summit day is done in crampons, usually with an ice axe and a rope.

How a crampon grips

Crampon parts and points explained

The points

  • Most crampons have 10 or 12 points; some modern models have up to 14.
  • The downward points bite into the snow under your whole foot as you walk flat-footed.
  • The two forward-slanting front points let you face into a steep slope and kick in: this is called front-pointing.

The frame

  • Made of steel alloy, lightweight aluminium, or both.
  • Steel is the usual choice for hard Himalayan snow, ice and rock.
  • Aluminium is lighter and suits soft snow and light ski touring.

Anti-balling plates

  • Rubber or plastic plates under the frame.
  • They stop wet, sticky snow from building up into a ball under your foot, which can make you slip.
  • Look for them on any crampon you rent or buy.

A little history. Oscar Eckenstein designed the first 10-point crampon in 1908, which spared climbers from cutting steps in the ice. Henry Grivel made it commercially, and in 1932 Laurent Grivel added the two front points, creating the 12-point crampon and the front-pointing technique climbers still use.

The welt is the narrow ledge around the sole of a mountaineering boot. If your boot has no welt, only strap crampons will fit it.

Boot and crampon ratings

B1, B2 and B3 boots: matching boots and crampons

Boots are rated by how stiff their soles are, from B0 to B3. Crampons are rated C1 to C3 in the same way. The rule is simple: a crampon should be no stiffer than the boot it goes on, or the frame flexes against a stiff sole, or a soft boot bends out of a rigid crampon.

BootSoleTypical useC1 (flexible)C2 (semi-rigid)C3 (rigid)
B0BendyWalking and trekking shoesNoNoNo
B1Flexible but stiffenedTrekking and hill walking in snowYesNoNo
B2Semi-rigidMountaineering and easier snow climbsYesYesNo
B3Fully rigidMountaineering, ice and mixed climbing at all gradesYesYesYes

Can I use trekking shoes with crampons? Only a flexible strap (C1) crampon on a stiffened trekking boot, and only on easy, gentle snow. For a snow peak, plan on a B2-type mountaineering boot.

Snow spikes or crampons?

Microspikes vs crampons: which do you need?

Microspikes (snow spikes)

  • Chains with short spikes on a rubber harness that slips over ordinary shoes.
  • Made for packed snow and icy paths and trails.
  • No front points and no rigid frame.
  • Useful for winter visitors walking on frozen paths around Manali and Solang.

Crampons

  • A rigid or semi-rigid frame with long points, including front points.
  • Made for steep snow, ice and glaciers.
  • Need a boot that matches their stiffness.
  • The standard for summit day on Himalayan snow peaks.

Microspikes are a walking aid, not climbing gear. On a snow peak, use crampons unless your guide tells you otherwise for that day's conditions.

Rent · borrow · buy

Rent, borrow or buy crampons and boots for a Himachal climb

  1. Ask what is on loan.On guided climbs the outfitter often lends crampons, an ice axe, a helmet and a harness. Get the list of what is provided in writing before you pack.
  2. Rent in Manali or Solang.Renting snow boots and crampons is common in Manali and Solang before Himachal climbs. Try the crampons on the exact boots and socks you will climb in, and check every point, strap and lever.
  3. Buy if you will climb often.Boots that fit your own feet are the item most worth owning; crampons can follow once you know which boots you have.
  4. Check the safety marks.The UIAA sets safety standards for crampons among more than 25 types of climbing equipment. Look for the UIAA Safety Label or CE/EN marking, whether the gear is yours or rented.

On a course? At the Nehru Institute of Mountaineering the institute provides mountaineering clothing and technical equipment to trainees, who bring rubber-soled trekking shoes (not PVC-soled or canvas shoes). Compare the institutes in our mountaineering courses guide.

Before and after the climb

How to fit crampons and look after them

  • Fit them at home or in town, on your climbing boots, never for the first time at high camp
  • Adjust the length so the frame sits tight under the sole with no gap
  • Toe and heel bails seat firmly; the heel lever snaps shut with effort
  • Tuck in loose strap ends so they cannot catch the other foot
  • Walk with feet a little wider apart than usual to avoid snagging trousers and gaiters
  • Carry them in a bag or with point covers so they do not tear your pack
  • Dry them after every use and check points, rivets and straps

Reading is a start, but the British Mountaineering Council puts it plainly: there is no substitute for skills taught face to face by an experienced instructor. On a guided climb, ask your guide for a crampon and ice-axe practice session on easy snow before summit day.

FAQ

Crampons and mountaineering boots: questions beginners ask

What are crampons?

Metal frames with sharp points that strap or clip onto your boots so you can walk and climb on hard snow and ice, on glaciers, snowfields and steep slopes.

How many points do crampons have?

Usually 10 or 12, and some modern models up to 14. The two front points let you face into a steep slope and kick in.

Do crampons fit any boot?

Strap crampons fit almost any boot. Hybrid crampons need a heel welt, and step-in crampons need welts at the front and back. Stiffness must match too: the crampon should be no stiffer than the boot.

What do B1, B2 and B3 mean on mountaineering boots?

They rate sole stiffness. B1 is flexible (trekking in snow), B2 is semi-rigid (most mountaineering) and B3 is fully rigid (technical ice and mixed climbing).

Microspikes or crampons: which do I need?

Microspikes for icy paths and packed snow on trails. Crampons for snow peaks, glaciers and steep slopes.

Do I need crampons for Friendship Peak?

Yes. The top of Friendship Peak is steep snow, climbed with crampons and an ice axe, usually roped.

Can I rent crampons in Manali?

Yes, renting snow boots and crampons is common in Manali and Solang, and outfitters often lend crampons on guided climbs. Try them on your own boots before you leave town.

Can I use trekking shoes with crampons?

Only flexible strap crampons on stiffened trekking boots, and only on easy terrain. Snow peaks need a B2-type mountaineering boot.
