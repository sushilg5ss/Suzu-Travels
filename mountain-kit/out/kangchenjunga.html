How high is Kanchenjunga, and can you climb it from India?

Kangchenjunga (Kanchenjunga) is 8,586 m (28,169 ft) high, the third-highest mountain in the world, on the border of Sikkim and Nepal. It is the highest peak on ground India administers. You cannot climb it from India: Sikkim treats it as sacred and has banned climbing since 2001, and expeditions go via Nepal. See where it sits in the list of India's highest peaks.

Status, checked 21 October 2026: closed from India. The Indian-side route has been closed since 2000, and Sikkim's Notification 70/HOME/2001 bans climbing Kangchenjunga and other sacred peaks under the Places of Worship (Special Provisions) Act, 1991. In August 2019 the Home Ministry's list of 137 opened peaks included Kangchenjunga, but after Sikkim protested, the Indian Mountaineering Foundation (IMF) decided not to issue permits for Sikkim's sacred peaks. No change has been found since.

The name. Kangchenjunga is usually explained as "the five treasures of the high snow", for its five summits. Indian maps and signs often use Kanchenjunga or Khangchendzonga. Until 1852 it was thought to be the highest mountain on Earth; in 1856 it was announced as third, after Everest and K2.

Is it India's highest? India's official map also shows K2 (8,611 m / 28,251 ft) inside Ladakh, but K2 stands in Gilgit-Baltistan, which Pakistan administers, and is climbed only from Pakistan. So Kangchenjunga is the highest peak on ground India controls, and Nanda Devi (7,816 m) is the highest wholly inside India. Both sit at the top of our mountains of India guide.

What it takes

How hard is Kangchenjunga?

Our dataset grades Kangchenjunga extreme: an 8,000 m expedition with serious objective hazards, and every normal route today starts in Nepal. Wikipedia notes that since the 1990s more than 20% of people attempting the main summit have died.

The one Indian-side line is the north-east spur from Sikkim, the ridge that turned back German teams in 1929 and 1931 and that the Indian Army climbed in 1977. That route has been closed since 2000, so it is history, not an option.

Permits

Permits: why India issues none for Kangchenjunga

No permit is issued from India. Sikkim's 2001 notification bans climbing Kangchenjunga and other peaks it holds sacred, and since 2019 the IMF has said it will not issue permits for Sikkim's sacred peaks. Any "Kangchenjunga expedition" sold from the Indian side has no legal basis.

Expeditions on the Nepal side follow Nepal's own permit system, which this page does not cover. In May 2025 Sikkim's Chief Minister asked the Union Home Minister to press Nepal to stop expeditions; that was a request, not a ban on the Nepal route. For how permits work on India's open peaks, read our IMF permit and peak fees guide and mountaineering in India for foreign climbers.

History

History of Kangchenjunga: 1852 to today

1852–1856: long thought the world's highest mountain, Kangchenjunga is announced as third, after Everest and K2.

1929 and 1931: German expeditions try the north-east spur from Sikkim and fail.

25 May 1955: Joe Brown and George Band of a British expedition reach the top from Nepal, stopping just short of the summit as promised to the Chogyal of Sikkim. Norman Hardie and Tony Streather follow on 26 May.

31 May 1977: an Indian Army team led by Col. Narendra Kumar makes the second ascent, by the north-east spur from Sikkim. Maj. Prem Chand becomes the first Indian on top.

11 January 1986: Jerzy Kukuczka and Krzysztof Wielicki make the first winter ascent.

2000–2001: the Indian-side route closes, and Sikkim's Notification 70/HOME/2001 bans climbing its sacred peaks. 2019: after Kangchenjunga appears on a list of opened peaks, the IMF says it will not issue permits for Sikkim's sacred peaks.

May 2025: Indian Army teams, including a joint Indo-Nepal Army team, summit from the Nepal side. The Sikkim Bhutia Lepcha Apex Committee protests, the Chief Minister writes to the Union Home Minister, and a sanctification ceremony follows in June 2025.

Five treasures

The five summits of Kangchenjunga

Kangchenjunga is a massif, not a single point. Three of its five summits, Main, Central and South, stand on the Sikkim–Nepal border. The West summit (Yalung Kang) and Kangbachen are in Nepal.

Why climbers stop below the top. The 1955 British team promised the Chogyal of Sikkim to leave the summit itself untouched. Joe Brown and George Band stopped just short of it, and the custom continues on the Nepal side.

Viewpoints

Where to see Kanchenjunga in India

You cannot climb it from India, but you can stand face to face with it. Two treks are the classic Indian viewpoints:

Goecha La, Sikkim

A 4,940 m (16,207 ft) pass inside Khangchendzonga National Park. The trek starts at Yuksom and climbs through Sachen, Tshoka, Dzongri and Thansing. From the top you look straight at the south-east face of Kangchenjunga.

Sandakphu, West Bengal

At 3,636 m (11,929 ft), the highest point of West Bengal, on the Singalila Ridge. The trek starts at Manebhanjan. Singalila National Park is known for views of both Kangchenjunga and Everest.

Permits. Goecha La needs Sikkim permits, and foreigners also need a Protected Area Permit, usually issued for groups of two or more through a registered Sikkim agent. Check the current rules with Sikkim Tourism. Base-camp treks to Kangchenjunga itself are in Nepal.

Khangchendzonga National Park, which protects the Indian side of the mountain, became a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2016.

FAQ

Kanchenjunga: questions people ask

What is the height of Kangchenjunga?

8,586 m (28,169 ft). It is the third-highest mountain in the world, after Everest and K2. Older surveys gave 8,598 m.

Is Kangchenjunga the highest peak in India?

It is the highest peak on ground India administers. India's official map also shows K2 (8,611 m) in Ladakh, but K2 stands in Pakistan-administered Gilgit-Baltistan and is climbed from Pakistan. The highest peak wholly inside India is Nanda Devi (7,816 m).

Is Kangchenjunga open to climbers from India?

No. Sikkim treats it as a sacred peak and has banned climbing it (route closed since 2000, Notification 70/HOME/2001), and since 2019 the IMF has said it will not issue permits for Sikkim's sacred peaks. Expeditions go via Nepal.

Who first climbed Kangchenjunga?

Joe Brown and George Band of a British expedition, on 25 May 1955, from Nepal. They stopped just short of the summit.

Why do climbers stop below the summit of Kangchenjunga?

In 1955 the British team promised the Chogyal of Sikkim to leave the summit untouched, and climbers have kept the custom since.

Which Indian first climbed Kangchenjunga?

Maj. Prem Chand, on an Indian Army expedition led by Col. Narendra Kumar, on 31 May 1977, by the north-east spur from Sikkim.

Where can I see Kanchenjunga in India?

Goecha La (4,940 m) in Sikkim, a trek from Yuksom, and Sandakphu (3,636 m) in West Bengal, a trek from Manebhanjan, are the classic viewpoints.

Is Kanchenjunga in Nepal or India?

Both: the main summit is on the border between Sikkim (Mangan district) and Nepal (Taplejung district). Two of its five summits lie wholly in Nepal.
