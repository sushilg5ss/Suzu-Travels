How high is Nun Kun, and where is it?

Nun Kun is a massif of the Great Himalaya on the Jammu & Kashmir–Ladakh boundary, above the Suru valley in Kargil district, about 250 km east of Srinagar. Its highest top, Nun, is 7,135 m (23,409 ft); Kun is 7,077 m (23,219 ft). Nun was first climbed on 28 August 1953 by the west ridge. See all 7,000 m peaks in India.

One massif, two territories. Nun Kun straddles the boundary between the union territories of Jammu & Kashmir and Ladakh. The Suru valley lies to the north and the Pensi La (4,400 m), the pass into Zanskar, to the east. Nun, the higher top, is counted as the highest point of Jammu & Kashmir; Kun lies in Ladakh. Our highest peaks in Jammu & Kashmir and peaks of Ladakh pages show both in context.

Four tops. Besides Nun and Kun, the massif carries Pinnacle Peak (6,930 m) and White Needle (about 6,600 m); the heights are in the four tops table below. Kun's height varies by source, from 7,035 m to 7,086 m; we use 7,077 m. For the wider picture, read our Indian Himalaya guide and the full list of India's highest peaks.

Route and camps

The west ridge of Nun, camp by camp

From Kargil up the Suru valley. The road runs from Kargil through Sankoo and Panikhar to Tangol, about two hours' drive. From there teams walk to base camp at about 4,600 m in the upper Sentik valley.

The normal route is the west ridge, the line of the 1953 first ascent: glacier, snow and ice, with the snow wall of the west face leading up to the ridge. The 1953 team put base camp a little higher, at about 4,730 m (15,500 ft), and three camps above it, at 18,000, 19,200 and 21,000 ft. The table converts those heights to metres; camp heights change from year to year.

The 1953 expedition reached base camp on 30 July and the summit on 28 August, so about four weeks from base camp is a fair estimate. No official itinerary exists.

Day by day

How a Nun expedition unfolds

  1. Stage 1 · Kargil and the Suru valleyThe drive from Kargil through Sankoo and Panikhar to the road-head at Tangol, with time to acclimatise.
  2. Stage 2 · Walk to base campUp to base camp at about 4,600 m in the upper Sentik valley.
  3. Stage 3 · Stock the campsLoad carries up the snow wall of the west face to camps near 5,500, 5,850 and 6,400 m, sleeping high and coming back down.
  4. Stage 4 · Summit pushFrom the top camp along the west ridge to the 7,135 m summit, then down the same line.
  5. Stage 5 · Weather days and the walk outSpare days for snow and wind, then clearing the camps and the walk back to Tangol.

Itineraries vary with the outfitter, group and weather; rest days are added when needed.

What it takes

How hard is it to climb Nun?

Our dataset grades Nun technical: a glacier, snow and ice route with serious avalanche terrain. On 23 August 1953, five days before the summit, an avalanche carried one rope of the first-ascent team about 300 m. Rescues still happen: the Indian Air Force flew out an Italian climber with acute mountain sickness on 20 August 2022, and an injured climber from the Nun base camp area in August 2023.

Arrive with at least one earlier summit above 6,000 m and solid glacier and fixed-rope skills. In Himachal, good steps towards a peak like this are Menthosa (6,443 m), Mulkila (6,517 m) and Reo Purgyil (6,816 m). Read our altitude sickness guide before you go high.

  • ExperienceAt least one summit above 6,000 m and days on crevassed glaciers.
  • TechniqueCrampons and ice axe on steep snow and ice, fixed ropes, jumaring, abseiling and crevasse rescue.
  • FitnessRepeated load carries between camps and a long summit day above 7,000 m.
  • JudgementReading avalanche slopes and fresh snow, and the discipline to turn back.
  • TrainingA Basic and an Advanced Mountaineering Course; Indian teams also follow IMF rules on team qualifications.
When to go

Best time to climb Nun Kun

June to September. Nun Kun stands on the Ladakh side of the Great Himalaya, and climbs happen in the summer months. The 1953 first ascent fits that window: base camp on 30 July and the summit on 28 August.

Permits

Permits for Nun Kun

IMF permission. Nun (7,135 m) and Kun (7,077 m) are both above 7,000 m. For foreign teams the IMF base fee in that band is US$1,000 for a team of two, plus US$500 for liaison-officer equipment, and per-member charges apply (IMF peak-fee page, as of 1 Oct 2026). More in our IMF permit and peak fees guide and mountaineering in India for foreign climbers.

Area permits. The Suru valley is not on Ladakh's list of protected areas, which covers Nubra, Pangong, Tso Moriri and Hanle–Chushul, and Indian citizens have needed no inner line permit in Ladakh since August 2021. No protected-area permit is listed for the Suru valley: confirm with the Kargil district administration before you travel.

History

Climbing history of Nun Kun

1906: Fanny Bullock Workman, with the guide Cyprien Savoye, claimed an ascent of Pinnacle Peak (6,930 m). Workman's claims were disputed.

1913: the Italian Mario Piacenza, with Lorenzo Borelli, made the first ascent of Kun by the north-east ridge.

28 August 1953: Pierre Vittoz and Claude Kogan, of a French-Swiss-Indian expedition led by Bernard Pierre, made the first ascent of Nun by the west ridge, reaching the top at 2:50 p.m. It gave Claude Kogan the record for the highest summit reached by a woman at that time.

27–28 October 1976: a Czech team led by F. Čejka climbed the north-west face of Nun. 1981: the first British ascent, by the east ridge, with Steve Berry.

July 2021: an Indian Army team from 26 Punjab, with NIMAS, climbed Kun for Kargil Vijay Diwas.

20 August 2022 and August 2023: two separate Indian Air Force rescues, first of an Italian climber with acute mountain sickness, then of an injured climber from the Nun base camp area (114 Helicopter Unit, Leh).

Geography

Is Nun Kun in Ladakh or Kashmir?

Both. The massif straddles the boundary between the two union territories. Nun is listed as the highest point of Jammu & Kashmir, and Kun lies in Ladakh. Yet the way in is from the Ladakh side: from Kargil, the road climbs the Suru valley through Sankoo and Panikhar to Tangol, about two hours' drive, and base camp sits in the upper Sentik valley.

Where is Nun Kun?

On the Jammu & Kashmir–Ladakh boundary, above the Suru valley in Kargil district, about 250 km east of Srinagar.

Is Nun Kun in Ladakh or Kashmir?

It straddles the boundary. Nun is counted as the highest point of Jammu & Kashmir, and Kun lies in Ladakh. Both are approached from Kargil.
