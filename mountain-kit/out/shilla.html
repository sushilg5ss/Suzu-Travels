What is Shilla peak, and can you climb it?

Shilla (6,132 m / 20,118 ft) is a glaciated peak in Spiti, Himachal Pradesh, between the Lingti valley and the Shilla nala above Langza. Old survey sheets showed it at 23,064 ft, so it was long listed as a 7,000er. Climbers approach from Langza over the 5,670 m Shilla jot, from June to September. Shilla stands in Spiti, the high, dry valley of Himachal Pradesh, on the ridge that divides the Lingti valley from the Shilla nala above the village of Langza. Its summit is 6,132 m (20,118 ft). That number surprises people who have read older books, because for decades Shilla was carried on maps as a peak of 23,064 ft, more than 7,000 m, and even as an early altitude record.

This page sets out what is actually known: how the height mistake happened, why the reported 1860 ascent is doubted, the approach from Langza over Shilla jot, the season and the permits. Shilla is thinly documented, and we say so where the record is sparse. For the wider picture, see our Indian Himalaya guide, the full list of India's highest peaks and our guide to the peaks of Himachal Pradesh.

Route and camps

Route from Langza over Shilla jot

From Manali the road crosses the Atal Tunnel and the Kunzum Pass to Kaza (or comes up from Shimla through Kinnaur). From Kaza a short road climbs to Langza, at about 4,420 m, the same base village as Chau Chau Kang Nilda. Spend nights here to acclimatise.

The route goes up the Shilla nala to a camp about 150 m below Shilla jot (5,670 m), crosses the pass into the Syarma nala on the Lingti side, then makes a long descent and traverse to the foot of the peak, which Harish Kapadia says may take two days. From there, he wrote, Shilla was 'a gentle climb'. There is no easy approach directly from the west up the Shilla nala; the peak can also be reached from Lashitanga in the Lingti valley. Only a few altitudes are published, so the heights below are approximate and there are no fixed camp numbers.

Day by day

How a Shilla climb is staged (outline, not a fixed schedule)

  1. Stage 1 · Manali or Shimla to KazaThe long drive over the Kunzum Pass or through Kinnaur, with permits, police registration and gear checks on the way.
  2. Stage 2 · Kaza and LangzaSeveral nights at 3,650–4,420 m to acclimatise, with short walks above Langza.
  3. Stage 3 · Up the Shilla nalaClimb to a camp about 150 m below Shilla jot, around 5,520 m.
  4. Stage 4 · Over Shilla jotCross the 5,670 m pass into the Syarma nala and make the long descent and traverse to the foot of the peak, about two days.
  5. Stage 5 · Summit dayA pre-dawn start up the glaciated slopes to 6,132 m, then back to camp.
  6. Stage 6 · Spare days and walk-outKeep days for weather, then return over the jot to Langza and Kaza.

What it takes

How hard is Shilla?

Shilla is graded moderate in our dataset: a glaciated 6,000 m peak that Kapadia describes as a gentle climb from its base on the Lingti side. No alpine technical grade has been published, and few route descriptions exist, so treat it as a real expedition rather than a walk-up. The hard part is the approach: a 5,670 m pass, a long traverse and nights well above 5,000 m in thin, dry air.

Come with an earlier 5,000–6,000 m summit and sound crampon and rope skills. If you are building up to it, start with Friendship Peak or Yunam, read our guide to climbing your first 6,000 m peak, and plan the acclimatisation with our altitude sickness guide.

  • ExperienceAn earlier high summit (5,000–6,000 m) or a glaciated trekking peak.
  • TechniqueCrampons and ice axe, moving roped on a glacier, self-arrest.
  • FitnessA high pass crossing, a long traverse with loads and a summit day above 6,000 m.
  • AcclimatisationNights in Kaza and Langza before going higher, and rest days on the way.
  • TrainingA Basic Mountaineering Course helps; Indian teams follow IMF rules on team qualifications.
When to go

Best time to climb Shilla

June to September. Spiti lies in the Himalayan rain shadow, so the summer months that bring the monsoon to Manali and Kullu are the climbing season here, the same window as Manirang and Chau Chau Kang Nilda.

Permits

Permits for Shilla

Indian climbers need permission from the Indian Mountaineering Foundation (IMF) for an expedition. In Himachal, climbs go through an HP Tourism-registered outfitter with registered guides, with police registration and insurance for every member under the state's adventure rules.

Foreign nationals can climb Shilla with IMF permission, applied for at least 90 days ahead, and a liaison officer. At 6,132 m it falls in the up-to-6,500 m band: a base fee of US$500 for a team of two, plus US$500 for liaison-officer equipment, and per-member charges apply (IMF peak-fee page, checked Oct 2026). Our IMF permit and fees guide explains the system.

Protected Area Permit: Kaza and Langza are not on Himachal's protected-area list, so the Langza approach needs no PAP. If you approach through the lower Lingti valley, check the PAP list first. Shilla lies inside the Kibber Wildlife Sanctuary, so also check local forest rules in Kaza. Visa rules can change: confirm with the Indian mission before you travel.

History

Climbing history of Shilla

The story begins with the reported 1860 ascent by a Survey of India khalasi, published in 1910 and still unproven, and the 1874 survey sheet that gave the peak 23,064 ft. In 1939 J.O.M. Roberts looked across from Chau Chau Kang Nilda and doubted that height, and in 1952 J. de V. Graaff and his party, who also made the first ascent of Manirang that year, camped at Langza to identify the peak and found no 23,000 ft summit.

The first documented ascent came in 1966, by an Indian expedition; the same year Indian teams also climbed Chau Chau Kang Nilda and Kanikma. They found no survey pole on top. In 1983 Harish Kapadia's Lingti expedition crossed Shilla jot and described the easier approach from the Lingti side. Indian teams have climbed it since, but detailed accounts are rare, which is why this page sticks to what the record shows.

The lost 7,000er

Why Shilla was once thought to be over 7,000 m

Shilla's fame comes from paperwork, not from its height. On survey sheet 64 SW of 1874, a peak named 'Parang La No. 2 S' was shown at 23,064 ft, about 7,030 m. Then, in 1910, a volume of the Trigonometrical Survey of India reported that an unnamed Survey of India khalasi (a survey hand) had climbed it in 1860 and put up a pole. Taken together, that made Shilla a 7,000 m summit climbed in 1860. Harish Kapadia notes that it 'remained a dubious altitude record for 47 years' until Tom Longstaff climbed Trisul in 1907.

  1. 1860 · The reported climbA Survey of India khalasi is said to have reached the top and left a pole. The story was published only in 1910.
  2. 1874 · The survey sheetSheet 64 SW shows 'Parang La No. 2 S' at 23,064 ft.
  3. 1939 · Doubt from a neighbourJ.O.M. Roberts saw Shilla from Chau Chau Kang Nilda and felt its height was suspect.
  4. 1952 · No 23,000 ft peakA South African party camped at Langza to identify it and concluded there was no peak of 23,000 ft there.
  5. 1966 · First documented ascentAn Indian expedition reached the top and found no survey pole.

So, was it climbed in 1860? Nobody knows. The 1966 team found no pole, earlier visitors doubted that a survey party ever came that way, and Kapadia himself asks whether the khalasi really climbed it or whether it is a legend. A later Himalayan Journal letter put the peak near 20,050 ft, and modern surveys give 6,132 m.

Shilla in context

Shilla beside its high neighbours

Drawn at their true heights, the Spiti peaks put Shilla in its place. At 6,132 m it sits below Chau Chau Kang Nilda (6,303 m), which shares the same Langza base, and Manirang (6,593 m). Gya (6,794 m) is the highest peak in Spiti, and Reo Purgyil (6,816 m) is the highest in Himachal Pradesh.

So Shilla is not Himachal's highest peak, whatever some travel blogs say. Its fame comes from an old mapping error, not from its height.

FAQ

Shilla peak: questions people ask

What is the height of Shilla peak?

6,132 m (about 20,118 ft).

Was Shilla once thought to be over 7,000 m?

Yes. An 1874 survey sheet showed it at 23,064 ft (about 7,030 m). Later surveys fixed it at 6,132 m.

Who first climbed Shilla?

An 1860 climb by a Survey of India khalasi was reported in 1910 but is doubted. The first documented ascent was by an Indian expedition in 1966.

Is Shilla the highest peak in Himachal Pradesh?

No. Reo Purgyil (6,816 m) is the highest in Himachal, and in Spiti, Gya (6,794 m) is higher too.

Where is Shilla peak?

In Spiti, Himachal Pradesh, between the Lingti valley and the Shilla nala above Langza, inside the Kibber Wildlife Sanctuary.

How do you approach Shilla?

From Langza up the Shilla nala and over Shilla jot (5,670 m) into the Lingti side, then a long traverse to the base. The direct western side from the Shilla nala is not easy.

When can you climb Shilla?

June to September, Spiti's dry summer season.

Do foreigners need a permit for Shilla?

Yes: IMF permission and a liaison officer (US$500 base fee for a team of two, Oct 2026). No Protected Area Permit is needed for the Kaza–Langza side.
