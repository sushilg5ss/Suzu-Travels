How high is Changabang, and why is it so hard to climb?

Changabang is 6,864 m (22,520 ft) high, in the Garhwal Himalaya, Chamoli district, Uttarakhand, on the north-east rim of the Nanda Devi Sanctuary next to Kalanka. Its name means roughly "shining mountain", for its pale granite. Every route is steep rock, ice or mixed ground: there is no walk-up. It is open in 2026. Status, checked 3 November 2026: open. Changabang is on the list of 83 peaks that Uttarakhand opened in February 2026. Indian climbers apply on the state's UKMPS portal and pay no state fees in 2026; foreign teams pay only the IMF charges. In 1997 the IMF still classed it as restricted, but that is history: today it needs the same permission as any open Garhwal peak.

Who climbs it. Changabang is a goal for experienced alpinists, not a first expedition. Its West Wall was climbed in 1976 and then turned back some 20 expeditions before its first repeat in 2022, and its north face is a landmark of modern alpinism. If you are building towards peaks like this, start with technical Himachal climbs such as Indrasan or Mulkila.

The peak stands on the wall of the Nanda Devi Sanctuary, which has been closed since 1983, so modern teams come in from the north. For the wider picture, see our guide to the mountains of India and the full list of India's highest peaks.

Route and camps

How climbers reach Changabang today

The 1974 team walked in through the Rishi Gorge, the 1935 Tilman–Shipton line, and needed over ten days to cover twenty miles. That way leads into the Nanda Devi Sanctuary, closed since 1983, so it is no longer used.

Modern teams take the northern approach: by road from Joshimath to the road-head at Jumma (2,500 m, about 3 hours), a walk of about 5 hours to Dunagiri village (3,615 m), then 6–7 hours to base camp on the grassy meadows of the Bagini glacier, about 4,550 m. Advance base, about 5,150 m, faces the north face. These heights come from the 1997 Mount Everest Foundation report and are approximate. There is no normal route with fixed camps: from advance base, each line is a multi-day alpine or big-wall climb, so this page gives no day-by-day itinerary.

What it takes

How hard is Changabang?

Our dataset grades Changabang extreme: steep granite, and every route is serious. Even the least difficult line, the 1974 route up the south-east face and east ridge, is a serious snow-and-ice climb: the first-ascent team made a night climb to the Changabang–Kalanka col and then followed a knife-edge ridge to the top.

The walls are a different game. The 1976 West Wall took Peter Boardman and Joe Tasker more than 25 days in big-wall style; the 1997 north face was graded ED over about 1,300–1,600 m. In 2022 ExplorersWeb noted that no team had climbed Changabang and returned to base camp for more than 24 years. Arrive with several technical Himalayan summits and solid rock, ice and mixed skills; read our altitude sickness guide too.

  • ExperienceSeveral technical Himalayan summits and alpine-style climbs on rock, ice and mixed ground.
  • TechniqueSteep ice, granite crack climbing, aid on the big walls, rope work on a knife-edge ridge.
  • Self-relianceMulti-day climbs from advance base with bivouacs or portaledges and no fixed camps.

When to go

Best time to climb Changabang

May to June and September to October. The record fits that window: the first ascent was on 4 June 1974, the West Wall in autumn 1976, the south face on 27 September 1978, the north face on 1 June 1997, the French north-face line on 11–13 May 2018, and the 2022 West Wall repeat from 25 April to 2 May.

July and August are monsoon months and not climbing season here. Every route is a multi-day climb, so build spare days for weather into any plan.

Permits

Permits for Changabang in 2026

Indian climbers apply on Uttarakhand's UKMPS portal. Changabang is on the state's February 2026 list of 83 open peaks, and all state fees are waived for Indian climbers in 2026. IMF rules for Indian teams still apply.

Foreign nationals need IMF permission, with a liaison officer and insurance. For peaks of 6,501–7,000 m the IMF base fee is US$700 for a team of two, plus US$500 for liaison-officer equipment, and per-member charges apply (IMF peak-fee page, as of 1 Oct 2026). More in our IMF permit and peak fees guide and mountaineering in India for foreign climbers.

The Dunagiri and Bagini glacier area was closed for military reasons until the rules were relaxed in 1994. Check the current access conditions with the Chamoli district administration when you apply.

History

Climbing history of Changabang

4 June 1974: first ascent. An Indo-British team co-led by Lt Col Balwant Sandhu and Chris Bonington, with Martin Boysen, Dougal Haston, Doug Scott and Tashi Chewang, crossed Shipton's Col (about 5,950 m) to the upper Changabang glacier, climbed through the night to the col with Kalanka and finished up the knife-edge east ridge.

1976: Peter Boardman and Joe Tasker climbed the West Wall in big-wall style over more than 25 days; Boardman's book about it, The Shining Mountain, won the 1979 John Llewellyn Rhys Prize. The same year a Japanese Alpine Club team climbed the south-west ridge in 33 days.

1978: Wojciech Kurtyka, Krzysztof Żurek, Alex MacIntyre and John Porter climbed the direct South Face, summiting on 27 September. 1981: Ugo Manera and Lino Castiglia climbed the South Ridge.

1997: Andy Cave and Brendan Murphy made the first ascent of the North Face, reaching the top on 1 June; Murphy was killed by an avalanche on the descent. 1998: Carlos Buhler and a Russian team climbed the "Lightning Route" on the north face. 2006: two Mexican climbers summited by a new route and were lost in a storm on the way down.

2018: the French GMHM team of Léo Billon, Sébastien Moatti and Sébastien Ratel climbed the north face in alpine style in three days. 2022: Matthew Scholes, Kim Ladiges and Daniel Joll made the first repeat of the West Wall, 46 years on, in alpine style with portaledges. FAQ

Changabang: questions people ask

How high is Changabang?

6,864 m (22,520 ft). It is a satellite of Kalanka (6,931 m), with about 300 m of prominence.

Where is Changabang?

In the Garhwal Himalaya, Chamoli district, Uttarakhand, on the north-east rim of the Nanda Devi Sanctuary, beside Kalanka.

What does Changabang mean?

Roughly "shining mountain", for its pale granite. Peter Boardman used the name for his book about the West Wall, The Shining Mountain.

Who first climbed Changabang?

An Indo-British team co-led by Lt Col Balwant Sandhu and Chris Bonington, with Martin Boysen, Dougal Haston, Doug Scott and Tashi Chewang, on 4 June 1974.

What is the Changabang West Wall?

A huge granite face first climbed by Peter Boardman and Joe Tasker in 1976 over more than 25 days. About 20 expeditions failed on it before its first repeat in 2022.

Why is Changabang so hard to climb?

Every route is steep granite, ice or mixed ground at high altitude. Even the least difficult line, the 1974 route, needed a night climb and a knife-edge ridge.

Can you climb Changabang in 2026?

Yes, with permission. It is on Uttarakhand's February 2026 list of 83 open peaks: Indian climbers apply on the UKMPS portal, foreign teams through the IMF. Only very experienced alpinists attempt it.

How do climbers reach Changabang now?

From Joshimath by road to Jumma, then on foot via Dunagiri village to a base camp near 4,550 m on the Bagini glacier. The old Rishi Gorge route enters the Nanda Devi Sanctuary, closed since 1983.
