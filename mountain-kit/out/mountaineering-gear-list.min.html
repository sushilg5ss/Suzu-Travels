What gear do you need to climb a peak in India?

For a snow peak in India you need boots that take crampons, crampons, an ice axe, a harness and helmet, a layered clothing system, a sleeping bag for high camp, a 50–60 L pack, a headlamp and glacier glasses. On a guided climb the outfitter normally brings ropes, tents and group gear. Pick your peak from our trekking peaks guide.

Most gear lists online are written for the Alps, the Rockies or New Zealand. Climbs in the Indian Himalaya are different: summit days start before dawn from camps at 5,000 to 6,000 m, the gap between noon sun and night cold is large, and rental shops in Manali and Solang make it easy to borrow the bulky items. So this list splits your kit into three piles: what the outfitter usually provides, what you bring yourself, and what you can rent.

Gear also depends on the peak and the month. Kullu peaks such as Friendship Peak (5,289 m / 17,352 ft) are climbed in May–June and September–October, with snow early in the season and rock and scree later. Lahaul and Ladakh peaks are climbed from June to September in the rain shadow: dry, with strong sun and cold nights. For where each range sits, start with our guide to the mountains of India.

Provided · you bring · rent

Mountaineering gear checklist: who brings what

Usually provided by the outfitter

  • Tents for base camp and high camp
  • Kitchen and mess tent, and meals
  • Fixed and climbing ropes
  • Snow anchors and pickets
  • Group first aid; oxygen and radios on bigger trips
  • Often on loan: ice axe, crampons, helmet and harness

You bring

  • Your clothing layers (see below)
  • Boots that take crampons, and gaiters
  • A sleeping bag rated for high camp
  • A 50–60 L pack and a small summit pack
  • Headlamp and spare batteries
  • Glacier sunglasses (category 3–4) and ski goggles
  • Sunscreen and lip balm
  • Two water bottles, one insulated
  • Personal first aid and medicines
  • Trekking poles and toiletries
  • ID or passport, permits and insurance papers

Can be rented in Manali or Solang

  • Sleeping bags
  • Down jackets
  • Snow boots and gaiters
  • Trekking poles
  • Check every rented technical item for fit, condition and safety marking before you leave town.

Every outfitter's list is a little different. Ask for the exact list of what is provided in writing before you pack, so you neither double up nor arrive without something you need.

Clothing system

The layering system for a Himalayan climb

LayerWhat it isWhy it matters
BaseWicking top and leggings; no cottonMoves sweat off your skin on the climb up
MidFleece jacketWarmth you can vent while moving
InsulationDown or synthetic jacketFor camp evenings, rests and the summit
ShellWaterproof-breathable jacket and trousersKeeps out wind, snow and rain
HandsLiner gloves, insulated gloves and mittsMitts are warmest on a cold summit push
HeadSun hat, warm hat, buff or balaclavaSun by day, cold before dawn
FeetLiner socks and warm socksDry feet stay warm

Add or remove layers as you move: the aim is to stay dry, not to wear everything at once. On summit day you will usually wear all of them.

Check the safety marks. The UIAA sets standards for more than 25 types of safety equipment, including ropes, harnesses, helmets, carabiners, crampons and ice tools, and in some cases its tests are stricter than the European (CEN) standard. Look for the UIAA Safety Label or CE/EN marking on anything that holds a fall, whether you own it or rent it.

The small pack

What goes in your summit-day pack

Summit day on an Indian peak usually starts in the dark from high camp and ends back at camp by afternoon. Keep the pack light and carry only this:

  • Shell jacket and down jacket
  • Spare gloves or mitts
  • Ski goggles and glacier glasses
  • Headlamp with fresh batteries
  • 1 to 1.5 litres of warm water
  • Snacks you can eat in the cold
  • Sunscreen and lip balm
  • Personal medicines, as advised by your doctor
  • A small first-aid kit

No piece of gear treats altitude sickness: descent is the best treatment. Climb slowly, follow your guide's acclimatisation plan and carry only the medicines your doctor advises.

Trekking gear list or climbing list?

Trekking gear vs climbing gear: what changes on a snow peak

Same as a trek

  • The layering system
  • A 50–60 L pack and trekking poles
  • Headlamp, sunglasses, sunscreen, water bottles
  • Personal first aid

Added for a climb

  • Boots that take crampons: ordinary trekking shoes are not enough above the snow line
  • Crampons, ice axe, harness and helmet
  • Rope work with carabiners and slings
  • Ski goggles and mitts for the summit push
  • A warmer sleeping bag for a high camp

On a course

  • At the Nehru Institute of Mountaineering, the institute provides mountaineering clothing and technical equipment to trainees.
  • Trainees bring rubber-soled trekking shoes, not PVC-soled or canvas shoes.

Rules that touch your kit

Gear rules in the Indian Himalaya: ropes, waste and insurance

  1. Bring every rope back.The Indian Mountaineering Foundation (IMF) requires expeditions to retrieve all ropes and technical gear from the mountain.
  2. Carry your waste down.Non-biodegradable waste goes down in sacks to the road-head, with photo evidence in the expedition report.
  3. Insure for rescue.For IMF expeditions, the policy must cover ground and helicopter search and rescue and altitude-related conditions. In Himachal, registered operators must insure their participants under the state's 2021 adventure rules; personal high-altitude rescue cover is still wise.

Permit fee bands, the liaison officer and the paperwork are in our IMF permit and peak fees guide.

FAQ

Mountaineering gear list: questions climbers ask

What gear do I need for mountaineering in India?

Boots that take crampons, crampons, an ice axe, a harness, a helmet, a layered clothing system, a sleeping bag for high camp, a 50–60 L pack, a headlamp and glacier glasses. Group gear such as ropes and tents normally comes from the outfitter.

What does the outfitter provide on a guided climb?

Usually tents, meals, ropes and group technical gear, and often an ice axe, crampons and a helmet on loan. Always confirm the exact list in writing.

Can I rent mountaineering gear in Manali?

Yes. Renting jackets, sleeping bags and snow boots is common in Manali and Solang. Check technical items for fit, condition and safety marking.

Do I need special boots for a snow peak?

Yes: boots that take crampons. Ordinary trekking shoes are not enough above the snow line.

How warm should my sleeping bag be?

Rated for well below freezing at high camp. Ask your outfitter for the camp altitude: camps at 5,000 m and above are cold even in September.

Do I need my own ice axe and crampons?

Not on most guided trekking-peak climbs, where they can be provided on loan. Bring your own if you already own gear that fits you.

What should I wear on summit day?

All your layers: base layer, fleece, down jacket and shell, with mitts, goggles and a warm hat.

Is there a rule about gear in the mountains?

Yes. The Indian Mountaineering Foundation requires expeditions to retrieve all ropes and technical gear and to carry non-biodegradable waste down to the road-head.
