How many 6000m peaks are there in India?

Our dataset lists 75 notable peaks between 6,000 and 6,999 m in India, the largest height band in our list of India's highest peaks. The highest are Kedarnath and Saraswati Parbat I (6,940 m / 22,769 ft), the lowest is Deo Tibba (6,001 m). Uttarakhand holds 40, Himachal Pradesh 14 and Ladakh 10. Most are open to IMF-permitted climbers.

Six-thousanders are where many climbers in the Indian Himalaya first go properly high: usually with a high camp, a pre-dawn start and, on most peaks, rope and crampons. India has far more minor summits above 6,000 m than any list can hold, so we count only notable peaks and say "75 in our dataset" rather than claim an official total.

Uttarakhand's Garhwal and Kumaon hold more than half of them, including famous hard lines such as Shivling, Changabang and Thalay Sagar. Himachal Pradesh has 14, from Deo Tibba above Manali to Reo Purgyil on the Tibet border. Ladakh's six-thousanders include the non-technical Rupshu and Markha peaks; Sikkim's and Arunachal's are mostly restricted.

Where they are

6,000 m peaks by state: count, access and season

Uttarakhand: 40 peaks, the 2026 open list

Garhwal and Kumaon hold the band's highest summits, Kedarnath and Saraswati Parbat I (both 6,940 m), and its most famous technical walls. On 4 February 2026 Uttarakhand opened 83 peaks of 5,700–7,756 m: Indian climbers pay no state or government fees, foreign climbers pay the IMF fees only, and applications go through the UKMPS portal.

Himachal Pradesh: 14 peaks

From Deo Tibba (6,001 m) and Yunam (6,111 m) up to Reo Purgyil (6,816 m). Parvati Parbat (6,633 m) has no documented ascent, and foreign climbers need a protected-area permit for the Kinnaur and Spiti border peaks. See all of the state's summits in our Himachal Pradesh peaks guide.

Ladakh: 10 peaks

Home to most of the band's non-technical peaks: Mentok Kangri, Dzo Jongo, Kang Yatse II, Chamser Kangri and Lungser Kangri. Stok Kangri has been closed since 2020, and Shahi Kangri and Argan Kangri are restricted military-area peaks.

Sikkim and Arunachal: mostly restricted

Sikkim restricts climbing on most of its high peaks, some of them sacred: Kangchenjau, Siniolchu and Gurudongmar are restricted and Pandim is not open to foreigners. Arunachal's three, including the unclimbed Chiumo, are restricted border peaks.

Grades

Which 6,000 m peaks in India are non-technical?

No official body grades Indian peaks, so these are the grades in our dataset. Seven six-thousanders are graded trekking peak: high and tiring, but climbed on scree and snow without technical rock or ice. One of them, Stok Kangri, is closed.

  • Yunam (6,111 m / 20,049 ft) · HimachalClimbed from the Bharatpur camp on the Manali–Leh highway; steep scree and snow near the top, and altitude is the main challenge.
  • Mentok Kangri (6,250 m / 20,505 ft) · LadakhRupshu, above Tso Moriri. Season June–September.
  • Kang Yatse II (6,250 m / 20,505 ft) · LadakhThe lower western summit of the Kang Yatse massif, inside Hemis National Park; a steep snow slope on summit day. Published heights range from 6,175 to 6,250 m.
  • Dzo Jongo (6,280 m / 20,604 ft) · LadakhIn Hemis National Park, approached from Shang Sumdo or the Markha valley. Season June–September.
  • Chamser Kangri (6,622 m) and Lungser Kangri (6,666 m) · LadakhThe Tso Moriri pair; Lungser is the highest non-technical peak in the band.

Thirteen more are graded moderate, including Deo Tibba, Shilla, Chau Chau Kang Nilda, Menthosa, Manirang and Leo Pargial in Himachal, and Bandarpunch, Jogin I and Kedar Dome in Uttarakhand. "Moderate" still means glacier travel, fixed rope and a full expedition. The other 55 are technical or extreme. Permits and fees

Permits and IMF fees for a 6,000 m peak

Foreign expeditions need Indian Mountaineering Foundation (IMF) permission, applied for at least 90 days ahead, a liaison officer and insurance. For a team of two the IMF base peak fee is US$500 up to 6,500 m and US$700 for 6,501–7,000 m, plus US$500 for liaison-officer equipment; per-member charges apply (IMF, checked 1 October 2026). In our dataset 30 six-thousanders fall in the lower fee band and 45 in the upper one. Our IMF permit and peak fee guide explains each charge.

Indian teams follow IMF rules too: the leader needs an advanced-course certificate and a summit above 6,400 m, and at least half the members must have passed the Advanced Mountaineering Course. Our guide to mountaineering courses in India covers the institutes.

In Himachal Pradesh, adventure operators and guides must be registered with HP Tourism, and climbers register with the police before setting out. Foreigners need a protected-area permit beyond Jangi in Kinnaur and near the border in Spiti, which covers the approaches to Reo Purgyil, Leo Pargial and Manirang. A tourist visa covers open areas; the X (mountaineering) visa is for restricted areas, so confirm with the Indian mission before you apply.

Firsts

Six-thousander firsts, from 1912 to 2024

  1. 1912 · KangchenjauAlexander Kellas and his Sherpa companions made the earliest first ascent of any peak in this band in our dataset.
  2. 1933 · Leo Pargial and the Bhagirathi peaksEarly climbs in Kinnaur and Garhwal: Leo Pargial, Bhagirathi Parbat II and III.
  3. 1952 · Deo Tibba and ManirangHimachal's first six-thousanders above Manali and on the Spiti–Kinnaur divide.
  4. 1971 · Reo PurgyilFirst ascent of Himachal's highest peak. On 16 September 2026 an ITBP team summited it again.
  5. 1974 · Shivling and ChangabangTwo of the band's most famous technical summits, first climbed the same year.
  6. 2018 to 2024 · the latest firstsJanhukut (6,805 m) in 2018, Shahi Kangri (6,934 m) in 2022 and Tsangyang Gyatso (6,383 m) in 2024.

Three six-thousanders have no documented ascent in our dataset: Chiumo (6,890 m) in Arunachal, Parvati Parbat (6,633 m) in Kullu and Chombu (6,362 m) in Sikkim. India's higher band is covered in our list of 7,000 m peaks in India.

FAQ

6,000 m peaks: your questions

How many 6,000 m peaks are there in India?

Our dataset lists 75 notable peaks of 6,000–6,999 m. India has many more minor summits above 6,000 m, and there is no official total.

Which is the highest 6,000 m peak in India?

Kedarnath and Saraswati Parbat I, both 6,940 m (22,769 ft), in Uttarakhand. Next is Shahi Kangri (6,934 m) in Ladakh.

Which 6,000 m peaks in India are non-technical?

In our dataset: Yunam (6,111 m) in Himachal, and Mentok Kangri, Kang Yatse II, Dzo Jongo, Chamser Kangri and Lungser Kangri in Ladakh. Stok Kangri is also non-technical but has been closed since 2020.

How many 6,000 m peaks are in Himachal Pradesh?

Fourteen in our dataset, from Deo Tibba (6,001 m) to Reo Purgyil (6,816 m), Himachal's highest peak.

Can foreigners climb 6,000 m peaks in India?

Yes, most of them, with IMF permission: 63 of the 75 in our dataset, not counting closed Stok Kangri. Sikkim, Arunachal and the military-area Ladakh peaks are restricted.

What is the IMF fee for a 6,000 m peak?

For a foreign team of two, a base fee of US$500 up to 6,500 m or US$700 for 6,501–7,000 m, plus US$500 for liaison-officer equipment and per-member charges (IMF, as of October 2026).

When is the best time to climb a 6,000 m peak in India?

May–June and September–October in Uttarakhand and Kullu; June–September in Lahaul, Spiti, Kinnaur and Ladakh; April–May and October–November in Sikkim.

Which 6,000 m peaks in India are unclimbed?

Chiumo (Arunachal), Parvati Parbat (Kullu) and Chombu (Sikkim) have no documented ascent in our dataset.
