What is the easiest 6000m peak in India?

For most first-timers the easiest 6,000 m peak in India is Yunam (6,111 m / 20,049 ft) in Himachal's Lahaul: non-technical, but with steep snow of about 50° near the top and a long, high summit day. It takes about 8 days from Manali. Climb a 5,000 m peak first, and compare heights on our list of the highest peaks in India.

A six-thousander is the summit many trekkers dream about once they have crossed a 5,000 m pass. India has dozens of them, but very few are open, non-technical and reachable in about a week. This guide narrows the choice using our peak dataset and the climbing status as of October 2026, and sets out a sensible path to the top. For the wider picture of Indian ranges, permits and records, see our Indian Himalaya guide.

Two names come up in older blog posts that no longer fit. Stok Kangri (6,153 m) in Ladakh, once India's most popular first 6,000er, has been closed since 2020 with no official reopening. Kanamo in Spiti is often sold as a 6,000 m peak, but our dataset puts it at 5,964 m, and its access status is unclear in 2026.

Four steps

How to climb your first 6,000 m peak: four steps

Most people who reach a first 6,000 m summit do not jump straight to it. They build up in steps, so that each new height and each new skill comes one at a time.

  1. Step 1: go high on a trek or a 5,000 m peak.A high trek above about 4,000 m (13,100 ft), or a beginner snow peak such as Friendship Peak (5,289 m) or Shitidhar (5,294 m; sources give 5,250–5,294 m) above Manali, shows you how your body handles altitude. Friendship Peak takes about 8 days from Manali.
  2. Step 2: learn the basics.Crampons, ice axe and walking roped together. On a guided trekking peak the outfitter teaches these at base camp; our guides to crampons and boots and the ice axe explain the kit before you go.
  3. Step 3: a non-technical 6,000er.Yunam (6,111 m) in Lahaul is the usual choice: scree, moraine and a steep snow slope near the top, about 8 days from Manali.
  4. Step 4: a 6,000er with a technical section.Deo Tibba (6,001 m) has a gentle snow-dome summit, but the approach crosses the steep Duhangan Col and a crevassed glacier on fixed ropes, over about 13–15 days. A basic mountaineering course is the natural step before harder peaks.

Lahaul and Ladakh lie in the rain shadow of the main Himalaya, so their peaks are climbed in July to September, while the Kullu peaks above Manali are best in May–June and September–October. More beginner summits are compared in our guide to trekking peaks in India.

Fitness and altitude

How to train for a 6,000 m climb

Build the engine

  • Train for long days on your feet carrying a pack: summit day on a 6,000er is long and starts in the dark.
  • A useful benchmark: India's National Institute of Mountaineering (NIM) tests its trainees on a 10 km walk carrying 10 kg.
  • Add hill or stair work with a loaded pack, and practise walking downhill: tired legs on the descent cause many slips.

Respect the altitude

  • The CDC says training and fitness do not affect your risk of altitude sickness. Fitness helps you move and recover; only time lets you acclimatise.
  • Above 3,000 m, sleep no more than 500 m (1,600 ft) higher each night, and add an extra night for every 1,000 m.
  • Choose an itinerary with rest days where the height jumps are big.

Before you book. Read our guide to altitude sickness and see a doctor before a high-altitude trip, especially with a heart or lung condition.

Course or no course

Do you need a mountaineering course for your first 6,000er?

Not for a guided trekking peak. Guided climbs of Friendship Peak, Yunam and similar peaks accept clients without a course if they have high-altitude trekking experience; the exact requirements are set by the outfitter. Crampon, ice-axe and rope basics are taught at base camp.

A course is the next step. A basic mountaineering course (BMC) takes about 24–28 days at an institute such as ABVIMAS in Manali, NIM, HMI, JIM&WS or NIMAS. It is the recognised step before technical peaks, and it is required if you want to climb without an outfitter: for Indian teams the Indian Mountaineering Foundation (IMF) expects the leader to hold an advanced course and a summit above 6,400 m, half the members an advanced course and the rest a basic course. Permits

Permits and rules for a first 6,000 m climb

Foreign climbers need an IMF permit. The IMF peak fee is US$200 for a team of two on IMF-listed trekking peaks, US$500 for other peaks up to 6,500 m and US$700 for peaks of 6,501–7,000 m, plus US$500 liaison-officer equipment hire; per-member charges apply (IMF peak-fee page, checked 1 Oct 2026). Check the IMF list for your peak's category: our IMF permit and fees guide explains it.

In Himachal Pradesh, adventure operators and guides must be registered with HP Tourism, and climbers must be insured for life and rescue. Book only with a registered outfitter.

FAQ

First 6,000 m peak questions

What is the easiest 6,000 m peak in India?

Among peaks you can climb on a guided trip from Himachal, Yunam (6,111 m) in Lahaul: non-technical, but with steep snow near the top and a long, high summit day. In Ladakh, Mentok Kangri and Dzo Jongo are also non-technical.

Can a beginner climb a 6,000 m peak?

A fit trekker can, after a high trek or a 5,000 m peak first. Altitude, not technique, is the main test, and guides teach the basics at base camp.

Do I need a mountaineering course to climb Yunam?

No. Guided trekking-peak climbs accept clients with high-altitude trekking experience; the outfitter sets the requirements. A basic mountaineering course is the next step before technical peaks.

How many days does a first 6,000 m climb take?

About 8 days from Manali for Yunam, and about 13–15 days for Deo Tibba.

How should I train for a 6,000 m peak?

Build endurance for long days carrying a pack; NIM's benchmark is 10 km with 10 kg. Add hill or stair work. Fitness does not prevent altitude sickness, so the plan must also include acclimatisation days.

Is Stok Kangri still the best first 6,000er?

No. Stok Kangri has been closed to climbers since 2020, with no official reopening as of October 2026.

Is Kanamo a 6,000 m peak?

No. Our dataset puts Kanamo at 5,964 m, and its access status is unclear in 2026.

When is the best time to climb a first 6,000er?

July to September for Yunam in Lahaul, and May–June or September–October for Kullu peaks such as Deo Tibba.
