Uttarakhand · Garhwal · Gangotri group · 6,831 m

The big snow dome of the Gangotri group, first crossed in 1947.

Kedar Dome is the north-west shoulder of the Kedarnath massif, a broad snow summit of 6,831 m (22,411 ft) above Tapovan and the Gangotri Glacier. Its north-west flank is the easiest big line in the Gangotri group, yet it is still a full glaciated expedition. Here is how it differs from Kedarnath main peak, the route from Gangotri camp by camp, the 2026 permit rules and the story from the Swiss in 1947 to the 1978 summit ski descent.

How high is Kedar Dome, and is it the same as Kedarnath peak?

Kedar Dome is a 6,831 m (22,411 ft) snow summit in the Gangotri group of Uttarakhand. It is not Kedarnath main peak: it is the north-west subpeak of the Kedarnath massif, about 2 km from the 6,940 m main summit. Climbers approach from Tapovan (about 4,400 m) by the north-west flank. Kedar Dome or Kedarnath? The two are often mixed up. Kedarnath main is the higher summit at 6,940 m (22,769 ft). Kedar Dome, 6,831 m (22,411 ft), is its north-west subpeak, about 2 km away on a spur that runs towards the Gangotri Glacier, with only about 100 m of prominence. Above Tapovan. Kedar Dome stands in Gangotri National Park, Uttarkashi district, about 15 km south of Gaumukh. Climbers share the Tapovan meadow with teams bound for Shivling and Meru. Route and camps

The route to Kedar Dome, from Gangotri to the summit

The walk-in is the Gaumukh–Tapovan trail. From the temple town of Gangotri (about 3,415 m / 11,204 ft) the trail follows the Bhagirathi past Bhojbasa to Gaumukh, the snout of the Gangotri Glacier, then climbs to the Tapovan meadow at about 4,400 m (14,400 ft). That is about three days on foot.

Above base camp the line is the north-west flank. Teams cross the Gangotri and Kirti Bamak glaciers to an advance base by two small lakes at about 4,740 m (15,550 ft), then put in a camp on the north-west slopes and a summit camp above the last big crevasse, near 6,000 m (19,700 ft). Camp heights in the table come from the 1978 and 1994 climbs and change from year to year.

Plan for weeks, not days. There is no official day count. Allow about two and a half to three weeks from Gangotri and back, including acclimatisation and spare days for weather; the exact length varies with the team and the season.

Day by day

How a Kedar Dome expedition unfolds

  1. Stage 1 · Road to GangotriDrive up the Bhagirathi valley to Gangotri, about 3,415 m, with time to acclimatise and the national park permit checked.
  2. Stage 2 · Walk to TapovanAbout three days via Bhojbasa and Gaumukh to the Tapovan meadow at about 4,400 m, with rest days to adjust.
  3. Stage 3 · Advance base and campsCross the Gangotri and Kirti Bamak glaciers to advance base, then ferry loads to Camp 1 and the summit camp, fixing rope on steep sections.
  4. Stage 4 · Summit dayA long push from the summit camp up the north-west flank to the broad 6,831 m top, and back down the same way.
  5. Stage 5 · Weather days and the walk outSpare days for storms and fresh snow, which have turned many teams back, then clearing the mountain and walking out to Gangotri.

Itineraries vary with the outfitter, group and weather; rest days are added when needed.

What it takes

How hard is it to climb Kedar Dome?

Our dataset grades Kedar Dome moderate. Its north-west flank is a glacier and snow climb, the easiest big line in the Gangotri group, but it is a full 6,800 m expedition. The Himalayan Journal accounts describe crevasses, cornices, bowls that avalanche after fresh snow, stonefall where the Gangotri and Kirti Bamak glaciers meet, whiteouts and lightning. In 1987 a Polish team's advance base was buried by avalanches twice, and the top section is steep.

It is not a trekking peak. You need earlier summits on glaciated peaks and solid rope skills. If Kedar Dome is your long-term goal, Himachal has the steps: start with Deo Tibba (6,001 m) or Yunam (6,111 m), then aim for Mulkila (6,517 m) or Reo Purgyil. Our first 6,000 m peak guide explains the path. For kit, see our mountaineering gear list.

  • ExperienceAt least one or two glaciated 6,000 m summits before an attempt.
  • TechniqueCramponing on steep snow, ice-axe arrest, moving roped on a glacier, crevasse rescue and using fixed rope.
  • FitnessLoad carries between camps and a long summit day above 6,000 m.
  • JudgementReading avalanche slopes after fresh snow and turning back in storms: a 1994 British team stopped at about 6,000 m.
  • TrainingA Basic Mountaineering Course as a minimum; an Advanced course is strongly advised.
When to go

Best time to climb Kedar Dome

May to June and September to October. The recorded climbs fit those windows: the Indian Air Force summit on 7 May 1978, the Polish ascent on 25 September 1987, the British ski attempt in April and May 1994 and the 2006 climb of the south-east pillar from 1 to 9 October.

July and August are monsoon months in Garhwal, and Gangotri National Park is open from 1 April to 30 November (2026). Our best time for mountaineering in India guide compares the seasons region by region.

Permits

Permits for Kedar Dome in 2026

Indian climbers apply on Uttarakhand's UKMPS portal. Since February 2026 Indian climbers pay no state or IMF expedition fee on the opened peaks. Kedar Dome is named in one report of the 83 peaks opened then, so confirm it on the portal before planning.

Foreign nationals need IMF permission at least 90 days ahead, with a liaison officer and insurance. At 6,831 m Kedar Dome is in the 6,501–7,000 m band: IMF base fee US$700 for a team of two, plus US$500 for liaison-officer equipment; per-member charges apply (IMF peak-fee page, as of 1 Oct 2026). More in our IMF permit and peak fees guide and mountaineering in India for foreign climbers.

Gangotri National Park. The approach needs a park permit, online or from the forest office in Uttarkashi. The Gaumukh–Tapovan trail is capped at 150 visitors a day, and the park is open from 1 April to 30 November (2026).

Not the Kedartal accident. In late October 2026 a trekking group was caught by heavy snow and avalanches on the way to Kedartal, the lake below Thalay Sagar, and seven people died (reports up to 6 November 2026). That was the Kedar Ganga valley, not Kedar Dome or the Gaumukh–Tapovan trail, but it is a reminder of how fast autumn snow can change these routes.

History

Climbing history of Kedar Dome

1947: André Roch's Swiss Garhwal expedition reached the "large snow dome" (22,410 ft) on 25 June and turned back after Wangdi Norbu was injured. On 11 July 1947 the team crossed the dome again on the way to the first ascent of Kedarnath main peak (Alfred Sutter, Alexander Graven, André Roch, René Dittert and Tenzing Norgay). The Himalayan Journal credits the Swiss with both summits in 1947.

1967–1976: Indian expeditions were, in the Himalayan Journal's words, "partly successful in climbing Kedar Dome"; no names or dates are recorded.

7 May 1978: an Indian Air Force team of four members and two porters reached the summit, then skied from the top to advanced base, about 2,100 m (7,000 ft) of descent, in about 90 minutes. The Himalayan Journal called it the first attempt to ski down the peak; the top 300 ft were steeper than 60°.

25 September 1987: a Polish team led by Marek Grochowski summited by the normal route, then retreated high on a buttress on the east face. April–May 1994: a British ski expedition was stopped at about 6,000 m by storms and avalanche risk.

1–9 October 2006: Ian Parnell and Tim Emmett climbed the south-east pillar of the east face in alpine style, on sight and without aid: about 2,000 m of climbing graded ED+ 6c M3. They descended the west face.

FAQ

Kedar Dome: questions people ask

How high is Kedar Dome?

Kedar Dome is 6,831 m (22,411 ft) high, in the Gangotri group of the Garhwal Himalaya, Uttarakhand.

Is Kedar Dome the same as Kedarnath peak?

No. Kedar Dome is the north-west subpeak of the Kedarnath massif. Kedarnath main peak is 6,940 m and stands about 2 km away.

Who first climbed Kedar Dome?

André Roch's Swiss expedition in 1947. The team crossed the dome on 11 July 1947 on the way to the first ascent of Kedarnath main peak.

Is Kedar Dome a trekking peak?

No. Its north-west flank is the easiest big snow route in the Gangotri group, but it is a glaciated 6,800 m expedition with crevasses, avalanche-prone slopes and a steep top section.

Where is the Kedar Dome base camp?

At Tapovan, about 4,400 m, a meadow at the foot of Shivling above Gaumukh, reached from Gangotri in about three days.

Has anyone skied Kedar Dome?

Yes. An Indian Air Force team reached the summit on 7 May 1978 and skied from the top to advanced base in about 90 minutes.

What is the best time to climb Kedar Dome?

May to June and September to October, before and after the monsoon.

Is Kedar Dome open in 2026, and what are the fees?

It is reported among the 83 peaks Uttarakhand opened in February 2026; confirm on the UKMPS portal. Indian climbers pay no government expedition fee; foreign teams pay the IMF fee of US$700 for two plus US$500 for liaison-officer equipment.
