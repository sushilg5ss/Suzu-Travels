What is the toughest mountain to climb in India?

There is no official ranking, but climbers' records point to Meru Central's Shark's Fin (6,310 m / 20,702 ft), first climbed in 2011, and Thalay Sagar (6,904 m / 22,651 ft), guarded by a band of rotten shale. Changabang's granite walls come close. In Himachal, Indrasan is called the hardest peak of the Pir Panjal. Difficulty follows the route, not the height.

No authority ranks Indian peaks by difficulty, and height is a poor guide: the hardest summits in the Indian Himalaya are mostly between 6,200 m and 7,000 m. What makes them hard is the terrain on the way up: steep granite, overhanging cliffs, serac barriers and loose rock high on the mountain, where every move costs more.

This page lists nine peaks that the climbing record treats as among the hardest in India. Seven carry our dataset's top grade, extreme, and two Himachal peaks are added as the hard end of what you can plan from Manali. Heights, first ascents and grades come from our list of the highest peaks in India; the reasons each one is hard come from the sources at the foot of the page.

Peak by peak

What makes each one so hard

Meru Central: the Shark's Fin

Meru Central (6,310 m / 20,702 ft) was first summited by Valery Babanov, solo, in 2001. Its Shark's Fin line beat attempts from 1986 to 2008. The hardest rock is near the top, so heavy gear has to be hauled almost all the way, with aid climbing up to A4. Conrad Anker, Jimmy Chin and Renan Ozturk finally climbed it in October 2011, the story of the 2015 film Meru.

Thalay Sagar: the shale band

Thalay Sagar (6,904 m / 22,651 ft) is steep on all sides, and a notorious band of rotten shale guards the summit. Climbers have called it perhaps the hardest summit to reach in the whole Gangotri group. It was first climbed on 24 June 1979 by the north-west couloir and ridge. Two climbs on it were honoured at the Piolets d'Or: the Australian route through the shale band (1997, awarded in 1999) and the Russian 'Moveable Feast' (2016, honoured in 2017).

Changabang: steep granite

Changabang (6,864 m / 22,520 ft), the 'shining mountain', is a tower of steep granite on the rim of the Nanda Devi Sanctuary. An Indo-British team made the first ascent on 4 June 1974. Every route is serious, and the 1976 Boardman–Tasker West Wall is a classic of Himalayan alpinism.

Shivling: long and exposed

Shivling (6,543 m / 21,467 ft) is a steep twin-summited rock and ice peak above Gaumukh. Even its easiest line, the west ridge of the 1974 first ascent by an ITBP team, is long, technical and threatened by a serac barrier.

Bhagirathi III: the big wall

Bhagirathi Parbat III (6,454 m / 21,175 ft) was first climbed in 1933 by Colin Kirkus and Charles Warren. Its huge, steep-to-overhanging west face is a big-wall test piece and some of the most extreme rock climbing in the Himalaya. The normal south-east ridge is easier.

Saser Kangri II East: the high one

At 7,518 m / 24,665 ft, Saser Kangri II East in Ladakh's Karakoram was the world's second-highest unclimbed peak until 24 August 2011, when Mark Richey, Steve Swenson and Freddie Wilkinson climbed its steep south-west face in alpine style. The climb won the 2012 Piolet d'Or.

Kalanka (6,931 m / 22,740 ft), first climbed by a Japanese team in 1975, is in the table for its steep north face; we make no further claims about it.

The hard end of Himachal

The hardest peaks in Himachal, and how to work up to them

Indrasan (6,220 m / 20,407 ft), above Manali, is considered the hardest peak of the Pir Panjal: an icefall, steep ice and a rock band on the final peak. A Kyoto University pair, Kojiro Tomita and Yasumasa Miyaki, first climbed it in 1962. Mulkila (6,517 m / 21,381 ft), the highest peak of Lahaul, asks for a crevassed glacier, a narrow icefall and loose rock on the summit ridge; it was first climbed on 7 September 1939.

India and the world

How do India's hardest peaks compare with K2 or Annapurna?

World lists of the hardest mountains to climb are led by 8,000 m peaks such as K2 and Annapurna. India's hard peaks are lower, about 6,200–7,500 m, so they bring less thin-air danger, but many of them are steeper and more technical: big walls, aid climbing and rotten rock rather than long snow slopes. The two kinds of difficulty are not measured on one scale, so we do not rank them against K2 by number.

Is India's highest peak the hardest? No. Kangchenjunga (8,586 m) cannot be climbed from India at all: Sikkim has banned it since 2001. On the Indian side, difficulty comes from the route, not the altitude.

Before you plan

Can you climb them? Access and objective hazards

Shivling and Changabang are named in Uttarakhand's February 2026 list of peaks opened for mountaineering. For Meru, Thalay Sagar, Bhagirathi III and Kalanka, check the current Uttarakhand list before planning; see our Uttarakhand peaks guide. Saser Kangri II East lies in a restricted area of Nubra in Ladakh. Every expedition above the trekking-peak level needs an IMF permit; our guide to mountaineering in India explains the process.

People also ask which is the most dangerous mountain in India. No source ranks Indian peaks by fatality rate, so we do not name one. The real dangers on these peaks are objective hazards: stonefall from loose rock bands, collapsing seracs and avalanches, plus altitude and storms. Choosing the season, the route and the team well matters more than the summit's height.

FAQ

Questions people ask about India's hardest peaks

What is the toughest mountain to climb in India?

There is no official ranking. By the climbing record, the Shark's Fin on Meru Central (6,310 m) and Thalay Sagar (6,904 m), with its rotten shale band below the summit, are among the hardest. Changabang's granite walls are close behind.

Has the Shark's Fin on Meru been climbed?

Yes. Conrad Anker, Jimmy Chin and Renan Ozturk climbed it in October 2011 after failed attempts from 1986 to 2008. Meru Central itself was first summited in 2001 by Valery Babanov, solo, on a different route.

Why is Thalay Sagar so hard to climb?

It is steep on all sides, and a band of rotten, dangerous shale guards the summit. Climbers have called it perhaps the most difficult summit to reach in the Gangotri group.

Which is the hardest peak to climb in Himachal Pradesh?

Indrasan (6,220 m) is considered the hardest peak of the Pir Panjal, with an icefall, steep ice and a rock band near the top. Mulkila (6,517 m), the highest peak of Lahaul, is another serious climb.

Is the highest mountain in India the hardest to climb?

No. Kangchenjunga (8,586 m) cannot be climbed from India because Sikkim has banned it since 2001. In India, difficulty comes from steep rock, ice and loose bands on the route, not from height.

What is the Piolet d'Or?

An annual mountaineering award for outstanding climbs. Climbs in India it has honoured include Thalay Sagar in 1999 and 2017 and Saser Kangri II East in 2012.

What is the most dangerous mountain in India?

No source ranks Indian peaks by fatality rate, so no peak can fairly be called the deadliest. The main dangers on hard peaks are stonefall, seracs, avalanches, altitude and storms.

Can a beginner climb any of these peaks?

No. Start with a trekking peak such as Friendship Peak or Yunam, take a basic mountaineering course, then move to a first 6,000 m peak before trying anything on this list.
