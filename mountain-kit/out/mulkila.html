What is Mulkila, and how do you climb it?

Mulkila (6,517 m / 21,381 ft), also called M4, is the highest peak in Lahaul, Himachal Pradesh, at the head of the Milang Glacier between the Chandra and Bhaga rivers. From the road-head at Darcha, teams walk about 25 km to base camp, then climb a crevassed glacier, an icefall and the loose south ridge. It is a technical climb, best from June to September. Mulkila stands near the centre of a cirque of peaks that early surveyors simply numbered: M1, M2, M3 and so on around the head of the Milang Glacier. Mulkila is M4, and at 6,517 m it is the highest peak in Lahaul, the only summit over 6,500 m in that corner of Himachal. Its name is usually translated as "Silver God". The waters it sheds run into the Chandra and the Bhaga, which join to become the Chenab.

This is not a trekking peak. The normal route crosses a crevassed glacier, threads a narrow icefall and finishes on a steep, loose rock ridge. This page sets out that route with camp altitudes, the honest difficulty, the season, the permits and the climbing history since 1939. Route and camps

Route from Darcha, camp by camp

The road-head is Darcha (3,360 m) on the Manali–Leh highway, reached from Manali through the Atal Tunnel or over the Rohtang Pass, via Keylong. From there the walk-in follows the Milang valley past Yotse village, about 25 km and roughly 1,100 m of height gain, to a base camp on the moraine on the north bank of the Milang Glacier.

Above base camp the route keeps to the east edge of the glacier, past crevasses, to Camp 1. A narrow icefall with hidden crevasses, between about 5,300 m and 5,600 m, leads to a high cwm near the glacier's source, where the 1939 party placed its top camp. The summit is reached by the south ridge (loose rock and gendarmes) or, in early season or heavy snow, the south-west face. Day by day

A typical plan (estimate, not a fixed schedule)

  1. Days 1–2 · Delhi to ManaliTravel to Manali and sort permits and gear.
  2. Day 3 · Manali to DarchaDrive through the Atal Tunnel and Keylong to the road-head at 3,360 m.
  3. Days 4–6 · Walk-in to base campUp the Milang valley past Yotse, with a night near the village, to base camp on the glacier moraine.
  4. Days 7–9 · Base camp and Camp 1Rest, training on the glacier and load ferries to Camp 1 at about 5,000 m.
  5. Days 10–12 · Through the icefallFix the icefall and move up to Camp 2 in the high cwm, about 5,800 m.
  6. Day 13 · Summit dayBy the south ridge to the 6,517 m summit, then back to Camp 2.
  7. Days 14–17 · Spare daysKept for weather, a second attempt and a careful descent.
  8. Days 18–21 · Back to DelhiWalk out to Darcha, drive to Manali and travel on.

Itineraries vary with the outfitter, group and weather; rest days are added when needed.

What it takes

How hard is Mulkila?

Mulkila is graded technical. The Milang Glacier is crevassed, the icefall between about 5,300 m and 5,600 m is narrow and hides crevasses, and the upper mountain has steep, hard ice. The summit ridge is loose rock with gendarmes. In 1939 the first party described nine hours of difficult climbing on rock and ice from its high camp. It is not a beginner peak or a first 6,000er.

You should arrive with at least one 6,000 m summit and solid glacier skills. If you are not there yet, start with Yunam or Deo Tibba, read our guide to choosing a first 6,000 m peak, and take altitude seriously with our altitude sickness guide.

  • ExperienceAt least one earlier 6,000 m summit and several high-altitude expeditions.
  • TechniqueCrampons and ice axe on steep hard ice, crevasse rescue, fixed ropes and moving on loose rock.
  • FitnessLong load carries between camps and a summit day of up to nine hours above 5,800 m.
  • AcclimatisationA slow walk-in from Darcha and rest days at base camp before the icefall.
  • TrainingA Basic and preferably an Advanced Mountaineering Course; Indian teams also follow IMF rules on team qualifications.
When to go

Best time to climb Mulkila

June to September, with early October possible. Lahaul lies in a rain shadow, so the monsoon rarely reaches the Milang valley in force, though stray monsoon weather does happen. The recorded ascents summited in late August, early September and October: 7 September 1939, 10 October 1977, 31 August 1979 and 22 August 1989.

Build in weather days: fresh snow makes the icefall and the summit ridge slower and more dangerous, and early-season snow may push teams onto the south-west face.

Permits

Permits for Mulkila

Indian climbers need permission from the Indian Mountaineering Foundation (IMF) for an expedition, and IMF rules set team qualifications, such as the leader's course and summit record. In Himachal, climbs go through an HP Tourism-registered outfitter with registered guides, with pre-trek police registration and life and rescue insurance for every participant under the state's 2021 adventure rules.

Foreign nationals can climb Mulkila with IMF permission, applied for at least 90 days ahead, and a liaison officer. At 6,517 m it falls in the 6,501–7,000 m band: a base fee of US$700 for a team of two, plus per-member charges and US$500 for liaison-officer equipment (IMF peak-fee page, checked Oct 2026). Darcha and the Milang valley are not in the Kinnaur–Spiti border belt. More in our IMF permit and peak fee guide and our guide to mountaineering in India for foreign climbers.

History

Climbing history of Mulkila

The first ascent was made on 7 September 1939 by Ludwig Krenek, Robey Johnson and Dr Fritz Kolb of an Anglo-Austrian expedition. They climbed the south ridge from a high camp at about 5,800 m. The trip was marked by tragedy: Hilda Richmond was killed by a falling stone near base camp.

A Japanese team led by Y. Watanabe climbed the peak on 10 October 1977. In 1979 a joint Royal Air Force and Indian Air Force expedition put Ted Rogers on the summit on 31 August, and the Himalayan Journal lists a Pune Mountaineers ladies' expedition led by Usha Bhide as having climbed it the same year. Wikipedia records the first Indian ascent as 22 August 1989, by Goutam Dutta and R.S. Negi. Because the records disagree, we do not number the ascents.

Lahaul's big peaks

Mulkila and the Lahaul giants, side by side

Mulkila is the top of the Lahaul skyline. Below it stand Menthosa (6,443 m) above the Miyar valley, Shikar Beh (6,200 m) on the Pir Panjal, and Yunam (6,111 m) near Baralacha La, the easiest 6,000er of the four.

FAQ

Mulkila: questions people ask

How high is Mulkila?

6,517 m (21,381 ft). Some sources give between 6,507 m and 6,518 m.

Where is Mulkila?

In Lahaul, Himachal Pradesh, at the head of the Milang Glacier between the Chandra and Bhaga rivers. The road-head is Darcha, on the Manali–Leh highway.

What is the highest peak in Lahaul?

Mulkila, also called M4, at 6,517 m. It is the only peak above 6,500 m in that part of Lahaul.

Who first climbed Mulkila?

Ludwig Krenek, Robey Johnson and Fritz Kolb, on 7 September 1939, by the south ridge.

How difficult is Mulkila?

It is a technical climb: a crevassed glacier, a narrow icefall, steep ice and loose rock on the summit ridge. It is not a first 6,000er.

What does Mulkila mean, and what is M4?

The name is translated as "Silver God". M4 (Mulkila IV) is its survey label among the "M" peaks around the Milang Glacier.

When is the best time to climb Mulkila?

June to September, into early October. Lahaul is in the rain shadow, and the recorded ascents summited between late August and October.

Can foreigners climb Mulkila?

Yes, with IMF permission applied for at least 90 days ahead and a liaison officer. The base fee band for 6,501–7,000 m peaks is US$700 for a team of two (Oct 2026), plus other charges.
