Which mountains in India are still unclimbed?

Our dataset of 157 notable Indian peaks has 8 with no documented summit. The highest is Zemu Gap Peak (7,780 m / 25,525 ft) in Sikkim, one of the world's highest unclimbed named peaks. Most sit in sacred or border zones that are closed to climbers. Two more are closed despite past ascents; see the full list.

India has far more unnamed and minor summits than anyone has counted, and many of them have never been climbed. This page covers the notable ones: the named peaks in our dataset with no documented ascent. That is why we always say "8 in our dataset" and never claim an official total.

In the Indian Himalaya, a peak usually stays unclimbed for one of four reasons: it is sacred (Sikkim's high peaks, Manimahesh Kailash), it sits in a military zone (Siachen), it lies on a controlled border (Chombu, the Arunachal peaks), or it is simply remote and hard. Only one of the eight, Parvati Parbat in Kullu, is open to foreign teams.

India and the world

The highest unclimbed mountain in India, and in the world

India: Zemu Gap Peak, 7,780 m

The highest unclimbed peak in our dataset is Zemu Gap Peak (7,780 m / 25,525 ft) in Sikkim. It is a low-prominence subsidiary summit of the Kangchenjunga massif, east of the south summit, rather than an independent mountain, and it has no known attempts. Wikipedia calls it one of the highest unclimbed named peaks of the world.

World: Gangkhar Puensum, about 7,570 m

The world's highest unclimbed mountain is usually given as Gangkhar Puensum (about 7,570 m) in Bhutan. Bhutan banned climbing on peaks above 6,000 m in 1994 and stopped all mountaineering in 2003, so it is likely to stay that way.

The IMF's virgin peaks

The Indian Mountaineering Foundation keeps its own list of unclimbed "virgin peaks", many of them above 7,000 m, and describes Peak 7160 in the Saltoro Muztagh as one of the highest unclimbed peaks of the world. Many of these are unnamed and do not appear in our dataset.

Off limits

Closed, sacred and restricted peaks: why climbers can't go

Some famous Indian peaks were climbed long ago but are now off limits. Our dataset marks 2 as closed: Nanda Devi (7,816 m), first climbed in 1936 and closed since 1983, and Stok Kangri (6,153 m), closed since 2020, with a Ladakh administration ban in December 2023. Another 25 are restricted: sacred, in a military zone or on a controlled border.

Kangchenjunga is not unclimbed. It was first climbed in 1955 from Nepal. From the Indian side it is closed: Sikkim treats it as sacred and banned climbing in 2001.

Recently climbed

Peaks climbed for the first time only recently

Unclimbed lists change. The most recent big first ascent in India is Kangto, Arunachal Pradesh's highest peak, whose first recorded ascent came on 26 November 2025. In September 2026 a 20-member team from NIMAS Dirang made what the Government of India calls the first-ever recorded summits of 20 unclimbed peaks above 6,000 m in the Ladakh Range (the Chamser–Lungser, Gyama Nala and Chokula areas), in 23 days. The peaks have not been named.

Permits

Can you climb an unclimbed peak in India?

  1. IMF permission comes first.Virgin peaks need Indian Mountaineering Foundation permission like any other peak. Foreign teams apply at least 90 days ahead and climb with a liaison officer; see our IMF permit and fees guide.
  2. Most are in restricted zones.Peaks near the Siachen, Tibet and Arunachal borders also need defence and Home Ministry clearance, and are generally not available.
  3. Sacred peaks stay sacred.Sikkim's 2001 notification and the pilgrim mountains such as Manimahesh Kailash are not climbing objectives.
  4. Parvati Parbat is the exception.It is open to foreign teams, but it is a serious technical objective with no documented summit, not a first expedition.

Want a Himalayan summit you can actually stand on? Climbed, open Himachal peaks such as Yunam Peak (6,111 m), Deo Tibba (6,001 m) and Chau Chau Kang Nilda (6,303 m) are where most climbers start.

FAQ

Unclimbed mountains: your questions

Which is the highest unclimbed mountain in India?

Zemu Gap Peak (7,780 m / 25,525 ft) in Sikkim is the highest unclimbed peak in our dataset. It is a subsidiary summit of the Kangchenjunga massif with no known attempts.

What is the highest unclimbed mountain in the world?

Gangkhar Puensum (about 7,570 m) in Bhutan. Bhutan has banned all mountaineering since 2003.

How many unclimbed peaks are there in India?

There is no official total. Our dataset of 157 notable peaks has 8 with no documented ascent, and the IMF keeps a longer list of virgin peaks, many of them unnamed.

Is Kangchenjunga unclimbed?

No. It was first climbed in 1955 from Nepal. It is closed from the Indian side, because Sikkim treats it as sacred and banned climbing in 2001.

Is Nanda Devi unclimbed?

No. Nanda Devi (7,816 m) was first climbed in 1936, but it has been closed to climbers since 1983.

Are there unclimbed peaks in Himachal Pradesh?

Yes, two in our dataset: Parvati Parbat (6,633 m) in Kullu has no documented summit, and Manimahesh Kailash (5,653 m) in Chamba is a sacred peak that has never been summited.

Can I climb an unclimbed peak in India?

Only with IMF permission, and many virgin peaks lie in restricted border or military zones that are generally closed. Parvati Parbat is the only one in our dataset open to foreign teams.

Which Indian peak was climbed for the first time most recently?

Kangto (7,090 m), Arunachal Pradesh's highest peak, is the most recent big one: its first recorded ascent was on 26 November 2025, by an Indian Army team. In September 2026 a NIMAS Dirang team also made the first recorded summits of 20 unnamed peaks above 6,000 m in the Ladakh Range.
