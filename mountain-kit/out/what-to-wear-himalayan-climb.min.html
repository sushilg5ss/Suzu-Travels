What is the layering system?

The layering system means wearing three layers you can add or remove as you heat up or cool down: a close-fitting base layer that moves sweat off your skin, an insulating mid-layer (fleece, then down or synthetic) that holds warmth, and a windproof, waterproof shell. Skip cotton. On summit day you wear them all. Pair it with our mountaineering gear list.

Base layerMerino wool or synthetic, close-fitting
Mid layerFleece; down or synthetic for warmth
ShellWindproof, waterproof, breathable, with a hood
AvoidCotton: it holds moisture
Hands up highMitts over gloves
Summit dayWear every layer

On a Himalayan climb the temperature can swing more in one day than in a season at home. You may walk up from a warm valley road-head in the afternoon, sleep in a camp well below freezing at night, and leave for the summit in the dark. On Hanuman Tibba, for example, summit pushes have started at 2:30 am. No single jacket covers all of that, so climbers dress in layers.

Most layering guides online are written by clothing brands. This one names no brands and no prices. It explains the system the British Mountaineering Council teaches, adds what the 2024 Wilderness Medical Society frostbite guideline says about keeping hands and feet safe, and fits it to climbs in the Indian Himalaya guide, from a first peak such as Friendship Peak to a 6,000 m expedition.

Base · mid · shell

The three-layer system, layer by layer

LayerIts jobGood materialsAvoid
Base layerMoves sweat away from your skin so you stay dryMerino wool or synthetic; close-fitting, moisture-wickingCotton, which holds moisture and leaves you damp, clammy and then cold
Mid layerTraps warm air; the layer you add or take off mostFleece, which keeps much of its warmth even when damp; a softshell adds wind resistanceA layer so thick you cannot vent it while moving
Warm jacketHolds heat at rests, in camp and on the summitDown (warmest for its weight) or synthetic (keeps working when wet)Letting down get wet
Outer shellKeeps out wind, rain and snowWindproof, waterproof yet breathable jacket with a hood, plus waterproof over-trousersA shell that does not breathe: you get wet from the inside

The rule behind it. You add a layer before you get cold and take one off before you start to sweat. The aim is to stay dry, not to wear everything at once: damp clothing loses its warmth as soon as you stop.

Your warm jacket

Down vs synthetic: which warm jacket?

Down

  • The best warmth for its weight, and packs small.
  • Loses its insulation when wet.
  • Best for stops, camp evenings and the summit.

Synthetic

  • Keeps working when damp or wet.
  • Bulkier for the same warmth.
  • Better while moving and in wet weather.

On the drier, rain-shadow peaks of Lahaul and Ladakh, down suits most climbers. On the wetter Kullu side, a synthetic layer, or a down jacket kept dry under the shell, is the safer choice. Many climbers carry a fleece for moving and a down jacket for stopping.

Extremities

Hands, head and feet: where the cold bites first

Hands

  • Thin liner gloves for dexterity.
  • Insulated gloves for most of the climb.
  • Mitts for the coldest hours: higher up, change from gloves to mitts.
  • A spare pair in your pack.

Head and eyes

  • A sun hat for the day.
  • A warm hat for camp and the summit push.
  • A buff or balaclava against wind.
  • Glacier glasses (category 3–4) and ski goggles.

Feet

  • Liner socks plus warm socks.
  • Boots that are not tight: room to wiggle your toes.
  • A dry pair kept for camp.

Frostbite: prevention first. The Wilderness Medical Society's 2024 guideline says to protect skin from moisture, wind and cold, to avoid sweating and wet hands or feet, to add insulation (for example by changing from gloves to mitts), and to avoid tight clothing or footwear that cuts blood flow. If you suspect frostbite or altitude illness, tell your guide at once: descent is the best treatment, and medicines are only for a doctor to advise.

Kullu-side peaks such as Hanuman Tibba and Deo Tibba are climbed in May–June and September–October. Rain-shadow peaks such as Yunam Peak (6,111 m / 20,049 ft) are climbed June–September: dry, with strong sun and cold nights.

Wear it all

The summit-day outfit

  1. Start with a dry base layer.Change into a dry one the night before if the one you climbed in is damp.
  2. Fleece, then the warm jacket.Your down or synthetic jacket goes on for the cold start. Your guide tells you when to vent.
  3. Shell on top.Jacket and over-trousers keep the wind out on exposed ridges.
  4. Head and hands.Warm hat, balaclava or buff, and mitts, with spare gloves in an inside pocket.
  5. Eyes and light.A headlamp with spare batteries for the dark, and goggles or glacier glasses for sunrise on the snow.

Everything else you need for that day, from water to crampons, is on our gear checklist and in the crampons and boots guide.

Two kinds of cold

Snow trek in winter Manali vs a 6,000 m climb

Winter snow trek or day out

  • The same idea on a smaller scale.
  • Thermal base layer, fleece, warm jacket and a waterproof outer.
  • Warm socks and waterproof shoes.
  • Still no cotton: jeans and canvas shoes stay wet in snow.

Climbing a snow peak

  • The full system, worn for days in a row.
  • A warm jacket rated for camps well below freezing.
  • Liner gloves, insulated gloves and mitts.
  • Mountaineering boots that take crampons, and glacier glasses.

Can you rent winter clothing in Manali?

  1. Rent the bulky items.Down jackets and other snow wear are commonly rented in Manali and Solang. Check the fit and condition before you leave town.
  2. Bring your own base layers and socks.They sit next to your skin and must fit well.

Camp by camp

Friendship Peak, camp by camp: what you wear

The animation follows one real climb, Friendship Peak (5,289 m / 17,352 ft) above Manali. You leave the road-head at about 2,050 m (6,700 ft) in a base layer and sun hat, walk in to Bakarthach at about 3,300 m (10,800 ft) with the shell still in the pack, and add the down jacket at Lady Leg base camp, about 3,850 m (12,600 ft).

From summit camp, about 4,200 m (13,800 ft), the route is on snow, so glacier glasses go on. The summit push starts before dawn, the coldest hours of the climb, and you wear every layer you carried. Camp heights are approximate.

FAQ

Layering questions climbers ask

What is the layering system?

Wearing a base layer to manage sweat, a mid-layer to hold warmth and a shell to block wind and rain, and adding or removing layers as you heat up or cool down.

Why should I avoid cotton on a climb?

Cotton holds moisture. Once you stop moving, the damp fabric makes you cold. Wear merino wool or synthetic base layers instead.

Down or synthetic jacket for the Himalaya?

Down is the warmest for its weight but stops insulating when wet; synthetic keeps you warm when damp. Many climbers move in fleece and keep a down jacket for camp and the summit.

What should I wear on summit day?

Everything: base layer, fleece, down or synthetic jacket, shell, warm hat, balaclava or buff, mitts and goggles, with a headlamp for the pre-dawn start.

Gloves or mittens at high altitude?

Carry both. Mitts are warmer, and changing from gloves to mitts is one of the ways the Wilderness Medical Society suggests to prevent frostbite.

What should I wear for a snow trek in Manali in winter?

A thermal base layer, a fleece, a warm jacket, a waterproof outer, warm socks and waterproof shoes. Leave jeans and cotton at home.

Can I rent winter clothing in Manali?

Yes, down jackets and other snow wear are commonly rented in Manali and Solang. Check the fit and condition before you go.

How cold is it at high camp?

Well below freezing at night on camps at 5,000–6,000 m, even in September. Plan your sleeping layers for that.
