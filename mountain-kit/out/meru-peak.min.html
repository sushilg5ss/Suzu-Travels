Three summits and the most famous fin of granite in the Himalaya.

Meru rises between Shivling and Thalay Sagar above the Gangotri Glacier. Its highest summit, Meru South, is 6,660 m; Meru Central, 6,310 m, carries the Shark's Fin, the granite prow that turned back more than 20 expeditions before 2011. How high is Meru Peak, and where is it?

Meru Peak is a mountain with three main summits in the Gangotri group of Uttarakhand, between Shivling and Thalay Sagar. Meru South is the highest at 6,660 m (21,850 ft), Meru North is 6,450 m and Meru Central, 6,310 m, carries the Shark's Fin, first climbed in 2011. Base camp is Tapovan, about 4,300 m. Which Meru is which? "Meru Peak" means a whole mountain, not one number. Meru South, 6,660 m (21,850 ft), is the highest summit. Meru North is 6,450 m (21,161 ft); published heights for it vary, from 6,400 m to 6,460 m. Meru Central, 6,310 m (20,702 ft), is the summit above the Shark's Fin, the line most people mean when they say Meru. A western top was first climbed in 1981. The three summits table below has them side by side.

Between Shivling and Thalay Sagar. Meru stands in Gangotri National Park, Uttarkashi district, above the Gangotri Glacier and the Kirti Bamak. Climbers base themselves at the Tapovan meadow, which it shares with Shivling. Route and camps

The route to Meru, from Gangotri to Tapovan and up

The approach is the Gaumukh–Tapovan trail. From the temple town of Gangotri (about 3,415 m / 11,204 ft) the trail climbs past Bhojbasa (about 3,775 m) to Gaumukh, the snout of the Gangotri Glacier at 4,023 m (13,199 ft), then up to the Tapovan meadow at about 4,300 m (14,100 ft), the base camp for Meru and Shivling. That is three to four days on foot.

Above base camp, the line depends on the summit. The table follows the 1980 Japanese route on Meru South, the only full camp list on record: an advance base on the Kirti Bamak and four camps on the south-east ridge. That team reached base camp on 23 August and the first pair stood on top on 10 October, about seven weeks later.

The Shark's Fin is a different game. From Tapovan it takes about two days to reach the foot of the fin: a 700 m (2,300 ft) snow slope and a rock ramp, then a granite pillar about 1,400 m (4,600 ft) high whose overhanging headwall starts near 5,900 m. In 2011 the team spent 12 days on the wall. A Meru expedition is measured in weeks, not days.

Day by day

How a Meru expedition unfolds

  1. Stage 1 · Road to GangotriDrive up the Bhagirathi valley to Gangotri, about 3,415 m, with time to acclimatise and the national park permit checked.
  2. Stage 2 · Walk to TapovanThree to four days via Bhojbasa and Gaumukh to the Tapovan meadow at about 4,300 m.
  3. Stage 3 · Advance base and campsFerry loads up the Kirti Bamak and fix ropes camp by camp; in 1980 that meant an advance base and four camps.
  4. Stage 4 · The climbA summit push from the top camp on Meru South or North, or a multi-day big-wall push with portaledges on the Shark's Fin.
  5. Stage 5 · Weather days and the walk outSpare days for storms, which have stopped many Meru teams, then clearing the mountain and the walk back to Gangotri.

Itineraries vary with the outfitter, group and weather; rest days are added when needed.

What it takes

How hard is it to climb Meru?

There is no easy way up Meru. Our dataset grades Meru Central and Meru South extreme and Meru North technical. The Shark's Fin was graded VII 5.10 A4 M6 WI5: hard aid climbing, mixed ground and steep ice on one route, more than 1,400 m long. It saw well over 20 serious attempts before 2011, and avalanches killed climbers on Meru North in 1986 and 1990.

This is a peak for climbers with years of alpine and big-wall experience. If you are building towards technical Himalayan objectives, Himachal has the steps: Deo Tibba (6,001 m) first, then Indrasan (6,220 m) or Mulkila (6,517 m). See also the hardest peaks in India.

  • ExperienceSeveral technical Himalayan or alpine summits, and multi-day climbs on steep rock and ice.
  • TechniqueAid climbing, mixed climbing, steep ice, hauling and living on a portaledge (for the Shark's Fin).
  • FitnessWeeks of load carries and long days above 5,500 m.
  • JudgementReading avalanche slopes and storms, and turning back: the 2008 team stopped two pitches below the top.
  • TrainingBasic and Advanced Mountaineering Courses as a minimum; Indian teams also follow IMF rules on team qualifications.
When to go

Best time to climb Meru

May to June and September to October. The recorded climbs fit those windows: the 2023 "Goldfish" route on 13 May, and the 1980, 1986, 2011 and September 2023 ascents between late August and October. The 1980 team reached base camp on 23 August.

July and August are monsoon months in Garhwal, and Gangotri National Park is open from 1 April to 30 November (2026). Our best time for mountaineering in India guide compares the seasons region by region.

Permits

Permits for Meru in 2026

Indian climbers apply on Uttarakhand's UKMPS portal, where state fees are waived in 2026. Meru is not named in the press reports of the February 2026 list of 83 opened peaks, so check the list on the portal first.

Foreign nationals need IMF permission at least 90 days ahead, with a liaison officer and insurance. Meru South (6,660 m) falls in the 6,501–7,000 m band (IMF base fee US$700 for a team of two); Meru North (6,450 m) and Meru Central (6,310 m) are in the up-to-6,500 m band (US$500). Add US$500 for liaison-officer equipment; per-member charges apply (IMF peak-fee page, as of 1 Oct 2026). More in our IMF permit and peak fees guide and mountaineering in India for foreign climbers.

Gangotri National Park. The approach needs a park permit, online or from the forest office in Uttarkashi. The Gaumukh–Tapovan trail is capped at 150 visitors a day, and the park is open from 1 April to 30 November (2026).

History

Climbing history of Meru

October 1980: two Japanese expeditions made the first ascents. On 4 October Osamu Shimada and Tamotsu Iwashima of the Hida Alpine Club reached Meru North from the north. On 10 October a team led by Kenshiro Ohtaki put the first pair, Takahashi and Ominato, on Meru South by the south-east ridge; the last pair summited on 29 October.

1981–1990: an Austrian team led by Sepp Friedhuber made the second ascent of Meru North in 1981 and the first ascent of a western top. A Polish team made the first women's ascent of Meru North in 1983, and on 7 September 1986 an Indo-Swedish team made the first Indian ascent of it. Avalanches on Meru North killed three Japanese climbers in 1986 and three Spanish climbers in 1990.

1993: Paul Pritchard's British team gave the east pillar of Meru Central its name: the Shark's Fin. September 2001: the Russian Valery Babanov climbed Meru Central solo in five days by a line right of the prow ("Shangri-La"), the first ascent of that summit, and won a Piolet d'Or for it.

2003 and 2008: Conrad Anker turned back halfway in 2003. In 2008 Anker, Jimmy Chin and Renan Ozturk spent 19 days on the fin and turned back within two pitches of the top. 2 October 2011: the same three made the first ascent of the Shark's Fin after a 12-day push in hybrid alpine and capsule style. The route was nominated for the 2012 Piolet d'Or.

2023: on 13 May Simon Gietl, Mathieu Maynadier and Roger Schäli climbed a new route, "Goldfish", on the south-east face of Meru South, ending on an unclimbed central top rather than the main summit. On 2 September a nine-member Indian team led by Umesh Zirpe climbed a new west-face route to make the first Indian ascent of Meru South. The east ridge of Meru South was still unclimbed in August 2025.

Meru Central

The Shark's Fin and the film Meru

The Shark's Fin is the north-east (east) pillar of Meru Central: a granite prow about 1,400 m (4,600 ft) high with an overhanging headwall from about 5,900 m. It saw well over 20 serious attempts from 1986 onwards. Conrad Anker, Jimmy Chin and Renan Ozturk finished it on 2 October 2011, three years after they had turned back two pitches from the top.

Their story is the documentary Meru (2015), co-directed by Chin and filmed by Chin and Ozturk, which follows both the 2008 attempt and the 2011 ascent. For other famous Indian lines, see the hardest peaks in India and the Indian mountaineering records.

True heights

Meru's three summits beside Shivling, Kedar Dome and Thalay Sagar

Meru is not the tallest peak around Tapovan. Shivling (6,543 m / 21,467 ft) stands just across the meadow, and Kedar Dome (6,831 m / 22,411 ft) and Thalay Sagar (6,904 m / 22,651 ft) are higher still.

What sets Meru apart is steepness, not height. The Shark's Fin on Meru Central (6,310 m / 20,702 ft) is the lowest of its three summits and by far the hardest to reach. All six peaks in the chart are in our list of 6,000 m peaks in India.

FAQ

Meru Peak: questions people ask

How high is Meru Peak?

Meru has three main summits: Meru South, the highest, at 6,660 m (21,850 ft), Meru North at 6,450 m and Meru Central at 6,310 m, the summit above the Shark's Fin.

Where is Meru Peak?

In the Gangotri group of the Garhwal Himalaya, Uttarakhand, between Shivling and Thalay Sagar, above Tapovan and the Gangotri Glacier.

What is the Shark's Fin?

The north-east (east) pillar of Meru Central, a granite prow about 1,400 m high. Conrad Anker, Jimmy Chin and Renan Ozturk made its first ascent on 2 October 2011.

Who first climbed Meru?

Japanese teams in 1980: Meru North on 4 October and Meru South on 10 October. Valery Babanov made the first ascent of Meru Central, solo, in September 2001.

Is the film Meru a true story?

Yes. The 2015 documentary follows the 2008 attempt and the 2011 ascent of the Shark's Fin, filmed by Jimmy Chin and Renan Ozturk.

Has an Indian team climbed Meru?

Yes. An Indo-Swedish team made the first Indian ascent of Meru North on 7 September 1986, and an Indian team led by Umesh Zirpe climbed Meru South by a new west-face route on 2 September 2023.

Can I trek to Meru base camp?

Yes. The Gaumukh–Tapovan trail reaches the base camp meadow at about 4,300 m. You need a Gangotri National Park permit; the trail is capped at 150 visitors a day and the park is open April to November.

Is Meru Peak the same as Mount Meru in Tanzania?

No. Mount Meru in Tanzania is a different mountain. India's Meru is in the Garhwal Himalaya of Uttarakhand.
