Which are the highest peaks in Himachal Pradesh?

Reo Purgyil (6,816 m / 22,362 ft), in Kinnaur on the Tibet border, is the highest peak in Himachal Pradesh, ahead of Gya (6,794 m) and Leo Pargial (6,791 m). The highest summits stand in the Zanskar range and the Great Himalaya; the most-climbed ones are in the Pir Panjal above Manali, like Friendship Peak (5,289 m). Below are 26 notable Himachal peaks from our list of India's highest peaks.

Himachal Pradesh rises in steps from south-west to north-east. The Shivalik foothills give way to the Lesser Himalaya, which holds the Dhauladhar above Dharamshala and the Pir Panjal above Manali. Behind them runs the Great Himalaya, with peaks of 5,000–6,000 m, and on the Tibet border the Zanskar range, which separates Kinnaur and Spiti from Tibet and carries the state's highest summits.

There is no single official count of Himachal's peaks. Our table covers 26 notable ones, each with its height, range, first recorded ascent and status, taken from the same dataset as our Indian Himalaya guide. Where sources give different heights, as for Hanuman Tibba (quoted from 5,860 m to 5,982 m), we use the most-cited figure.

Permits

Permits and rules for climbing in Himachal

Himachal Pradesh's adventure rules require trekking and mountaineering outfitters and their guides to be registered with HP Tourism. Difficult routes must be led by a qualified mountaineering guide, every participant must carry life and rescue insurance, and groups are tracked by GPS. Your outfitter handles the local permissions.

Foreign nationals need permission from the Indian Mountaineering Foundation (IMF), applied for at least 90 days ahead. The IMF base fee for a team of two is US$500 for peaks up to 6,500 m and US$700 for 6,501–7,000 m (no Himachal peak is higher), or US$200 on IMF-listed trekking peaks, plus US$500 for liaison-officer equipment; per-member charges apply (IMF, checked 1 Oct 2026). In 2019 the Ministry of Home Affairs opened 137 peaks to foreign climbers, 47 of them in Himachal, including Mulkila and Shigri Parbat.

Border belt. Foreigners need a Protected Area Permit beyond Jangi in Kinnaur and for the villages near the Tibet border in Spiti, which covers the approaches to Reo Purgyil and Leo Pargial; check with the Deputy Commissioner's office. Indian citizens need none.

Kangra district, 8 July–15 October 2026: trekkers on ten Dhauladhar routes, such as Indrahar and Jalsu Pass, must register. The rule is for those routes only, not the whole state. For training, the Atal Bihari Vajpayee Institute of Mountaineering in Manali (founded 1961) runs India's basic and advanced courses; see mountaineering courses and our permit guide.

Where they are

Himachal's peaks, region by region

The peaks fall into six areas, each with its own road-head and season.

Kullu–Manali (Pir Panjal)

  • Friendship Peak (5,289 m): the classic first summit
  • Deo Tibba (6,001 m)
  • Indrasan (6,220 m): a technical peak
  • Hanuman Tibba (5,982 m)
  • Shitidhar (5,294 m) and Ladakhi Peak (5,342 m)
  • Patalsu (4,470 m): a trekking summit above Solang

Base: Manali. Season May–June and September–October.

Lahaul

  • Mulkila (6,517 m): highest in Lahaul
  • Menthosa (6,443 m)
  • Yunam (6,111 m): a trekking-grade 6,000er near Baralacha La
  • Shikar Beh (6,200 m)

Rain-shadow valleys: roughly June–September.

Spiti

  • Gya (6,794 m): border peak, restricted for foreigners
  • Chau Chau Kang Nilda (6,303 m)
  • Shilla (6,132 m)
  • Kanamo (5,964 m): access status unclear, check before planning
  • Manirang (6,593 m) on the Kinnaur border

Base: Kaza. Roughly June–September.

Kinnaur

  • Reo Purgyil (6,816 m): the highest in Himachal
  • Leo Pargial (6,791 m): its twin, first climbed in 1933
  • Jorkanden (6,473 m) in the Kinnaur Kailash range

Border belt: foreigners need a Protected Area Permit.

Chamba, Kangra and the Parvati valley

  • Manimahesh Kailash (5,653 m): sacred and unclimbed
  • Parvati Parbat (6,633 m): no documented summit ascent
  • Shrikhand Mahadev (5,227 m): a pilgrim trek, not a climb

Sacred or unclimbed summits: admire them, do not plan a climb.

Shimla, Sirmaur and Mandi

  • Churdhar (3,647 m): highest of the outer Himalaya
  • Chanshal (4,520 m)
  • Hatu (3,400 m) near Narkanda
  • Shikari Devi (3,359 m): Mandi's temple summit

Day or weekend summits for walkers; no climbing gear.

When to go

Best time to climb in Himachal

Himachal has two climbing climates. The Kullu side of the Pir Panjal and the Dhauladhar get the monsoon, so climbers go before and after it. Lahaul, Spiti and Kinnaur lie in the rain shadow behind the Great Himalaya, so their climbing season sits in high summer.

Your first Himachal peak

Which Himachal peaks can you climb?

Most of Himachal's high peaks can be climbed with the right permission and an experienced team. The ones walkers and first-time climbers ask about most are in the Pir Panjal above Manali, a short drive from the road-head.

  • First summitFriendship Peak (5,289 m) — about 8 days from Manali, steep snow at the top; fit trekkers are taught the basics on the climb.
  • First 6,000 m peakYunam (6,111 m) in Lahaul, or Deo Tibba (6,001 m) above Manali for a glacier expedition.
  • Technical objectivesHanuman Tibba, Indrasan and Reo Purgyil — for climbers with a mountaineering course and earlier expeditions behind them.

FAQ

Himachal's peaks: your questions

What is the highest peak in Himachal Pradesh?

Reo Purgyil, 6,816 m (22,362 ft), in the Pooh area of Kinnaur on the India–Tibet border. It was first climbed in 1971.

Which range has the highest peaks in Himachal?

The Zanskar range in Kinnaur and Spiti. It holds Reo Purgyil (6,816 m), Gya (6,794 m) and Leo Pargial (6,791 m).

How many peaks are there in Himachal Pradesh?

There is no single official count. Our list covers 26 notable peaks, and the Ministry of Home Affairs opened 47 Himachal peaks to foreign climbers in 2019.

What is the highest peak of the Dhauladhar range?

Hanuman Tibba, 5,982 m in our dataset (published heights range from 5,860 m to 5,982 m). It stands where the Dhauladhar meets the Pir Panjal, above Manali.

Which Himachal peak is best for beginners?

Friendship Peak (5,289 m) above Manali is the classic first summit. For a first 6,000 m peak, Yunam (6,111 m) in Lahaul is a common choice.

Can foreigners climb peaks in Himachal Pradesh?

Yes. They need Indian Mountaineering Foundation permission, applied for at least 90 days ahead, and a Protected Area Permit in the Kinnaur and Spiti border belt.

When is the climbing season in Himachal?

May–June and September–October around Manali and the Pir Panjal; roughly June–September in Lahaul, Spiti and Kinnaur, which lie in the rain shadow.

Is there an easy summit near Shimla?

Yes. Churdhar (3,647 m) and Hatu Peak (3,400 m) are walking summits, and Chanshal (4,520 m) is a longer trek from Rohru.
