How high is Shivling peak, and how hard is it to climb?

Shivling peak (Mount Shivling) is 6,543 m (21,467 ft) high, in the Gangotri group of Uttarakhand, about 6 km south of Gaumukh above the Tapovan meadow. Its easiest line, the west ridge, was first climbed by an ITBP team on 3 June 1974 and is still technical, with a serac barrier and steep ice. It is open in 2026. Compare all 6,000 m peaks in India.

The mountain, not the shrine. The name comes from the Shiva Linga, the Hindu sacred symbol, and locals also call it Mahadev ka Linga. This page is about the 6,543 m mountain in the Gangotri group, not the ice lingam of Amarnath that is sometimes called the "snow Shivling".

For trekkers, Shivling is the great view of the Gaumukh–Tapovan walk: it rises straight above the Tapovan meadow, opposite the Bhagirathi massif. For climbers it is a serious alpine objective with no easy side. For the wider picture, see our Indian Himalaya guide and the full list of India's highest peaks.

Route and camps

The west ridge from Gangotri, camp by camp

The road-head is the temple town of Gangotri (about 3,415 m). The trail follows the Bhagirathi up the valley to Bhojbasa (about 3,775 m, 14 km from Gangotri) and on to Gaumukh (4,023 m), the snout of the Gangotri Glacier, about 18 km from Gangotri. Base camp is the meadow of Tapovan, at the foot of Shivling: the Himalayan Journal gives 4,280 m, other sources go a little higher, so call it about 4,300 m.

Above Tapovan, a 1995 Mount Everest Foundation team placed advance base at 5,060 m below the west ridge and Camp 1 at 5,700 m. The objective hazard is a serac barrier at about 6,100 m, where some of that team turned back. The ridge leads to the col between the twin summits, then steep snow and a short ice wall reach the main summit. The approach heights are approximate; the upper camp heights are from the 1995 expedition report.

What it takes

How hard is Shivling?

Our dataset grades Shivling extreme: there is no easy route. The west ridge is the lowest-angle line, but it still means serious mixed climbing, a serac barrier that can drop ice, and steep snow and ice above the col. The 1995 British team called it "surprisingly technical and long" and used fixed ropes. The Uttarakhand Forest Department puts it in its "High Risk Group".

This is a peak for experienced alpinists, not a first 6,000er. In Himachal, the natural steps are Deo Tibba (6,001 m), then technical peaks such as Indrasan (6,220 m) or Mulkila (6,517 m). Read our altitude sickness guide before any climb above 6,000 m.

  • ExperienceSeveral earlier 6,000 m summits, including technical snow and ice.
  • TechniqueMixed climbing on rock, snow and ice, leading and following on fixed ropes, and moving fast below seracs.
  • FitnessLoad carries above Tapovan and a long summit day from Camp 1.
  • AcclimatisationA slow walk-in from Gangotri and rest days at Tapovan before the upper camps.
  • TrainingA Basic and an Advanced Mountaineering Course at least; Indian teams also follow IMF rules on team qualifications.
When to go

Best time to climb Shivling

May to June and September to October. The Uttarakhand Forest Department gives the climbing window as April to October and says the post-monsoon months are the best time to attempt the peak.

Gangotri National Park reopened on 1 April 2026 and closes on 30 November, so autumn teams must be out before the park shuts. Keep spare days for weather: fresh snow makes the ridge slower and the serac zone more dangerous.

Permits

Permits for Shivling in 2026

Indian climbers apply on Uttarakhand's UKMPS portal. Shivling is on the state's February 2026 list of 83 open peaks, and state fees are waived for Indian climbers in 2026.

Foreign nationals need IMF permission, applied for at least 90 days ahead, with a liaison officer and insurance; they pay only the IMF fees. At 6,543 m Shivling falls in the 6,501–7,000 m band: US$700 for a team of two, plus US$500 for liaison-officer equipment, and per-member charges apply (IMF peak-fee page, as of 1 Oct 2026). More in our IMF permit and peak fees guide and mountaineering in India for foreign climbers.

Everyone also needs a Gangotri National Park permit, online through the Pathik portal or from the forest office in Uttarkashi. The Gaumukh–Tapovan route is capped at 150 visitors a day, and the Forest Department lists Kankhu as the check-post.

Kit list

What to pack

  • Insulated mountaineering boots with crampons suited to steep ice
  • Two technical ice tools or a technical axe and hammer
  • Climbing harness, helmet, jumar (ascender), belay device, carabiners and slings
  • Down jacket and trousers and a very warm sleeping bag
  • Windproof and waterproof shell jacket and trousers
  • Summit mitts plus liner gloves
  • Glacier sunglasses (UV400), goggles and sunscreen
  • Headlamp with spare batteries for the early summit start
  • Trekking poles for the walk-in and moraine
  • Two water bottles and a personal first-aid kit
History

Climbing history of Shivling

1933 and 1938: Marco Pallis considered the peak in 1933. In 1938 a German reconnaissance under R. Schwarzgruber found no feasible route because of the steep faces and falling seracs.

3 June 1974: the first ascent, by an Indo-Tibetan Border Police team led by Hukam Singh, on the west ridge, after two failed bids on 26 and 30 May. The west ridge is still the normal route.

1980–1986: a Japanese route on the north pillar (1980); the East Ridge by Bettembourg, Child, Scott and White (1981); the first ascent of the 6,501 m west summit by Bonington and Fotheringham (1983); and the north-east face by an Italian team (1986).

1993 and 2000: Hans Kammerlander and Christoph Hainz climbed the North Pillar in 1993. In 2000 Thomas Huber and Iwan Wolf added "Shiva's Line", a direct finish to the pillar.

2005–2017: Kazuya Hiraide and Kei Taniguchi climbed the north face to the north-west ridge (2005). On 25 May 2012 Valery Rozov made the first wingsuit BASE jump from the summit. In 2017 "Shiva's Ice" was added by Gietl and Messini.

2023–2024: new lines are still being tried. A 2023 team reached about 6,000 m on the east face, and a 2024 Indian-led attempt on the west face turned back from its Camp 2 at 5,800 m because of icefall and avalanches. ExplorersWeb counted about ten routes and 55 teams by 2024.

FAQ

Shivling: questions people ask

How high is Shivling peak?

6,543 m (21,467 ft) for the main north-east summit. The south-west summit is slightly lower at 6,501 m.

Where is Mount Shivling?

In the Gangotri group of the Garhwal Himalaya, in Uttarkashi district, Uttarakhand. It stands about 6 km south of Gaumukh, the snout of the Gangotri Glacier, above the Tapovan meadow.

Who first climbed Shivling?

An Indo-Tibetan Border Police team led by Hukam Singh, on 3 June 1974, by the west ridge.

Why is Shivling called the Matterhorn of the Himalaya?

Its steep, pointed pyramid reminded early European visitors of the Matterhorn in the Alps, and they called it the "Matterhorn Peak".

How hard is Shivling to climb?

Very hard. Every route is technical: even the west ridge, the easiest line, has a serac barrier at about 6,100 m and steep ice below the summit. It is for experienced alpinists only.

Can you see Shivling from Gaumukh and Tapovan?

Yes. It is the most striking peak on the Gaumukh trek, and the Tapovan meadow lies at its foot. You need a Gangotri National Park permit, and the route is capped at 150 visitors a day.

Is Shivling open for climbing in 2026?

Yes. It is on the list of 83 peaks Uttarakhand opened on 4 February 2026. Indian climbers apply on the UKMPS portal; foreign teams need IMF permission.

What is the best time to climb Shivling?

May to June and September to October. The Uttarakhand Forest Department says the post-monsoon months are best, and Gangotri National Park closes on 30 November.

What is the IMF fee for Shivling?

For foreign teams it falls in the 6,501–7,000 m band: US$700 for two climbers plus US$500 for liaison-officer equipment, and per-member charges apply (as of October 2026). Indian teams pay no state fees in Uttarakhand in 2026.
