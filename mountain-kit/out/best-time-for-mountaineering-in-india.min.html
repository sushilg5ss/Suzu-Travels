When is the best time for mountaineering in India?

It depends on which side of the main Himalayan range you climb. On the monsoon side (Kullu, Garhwal, Kumaon) the windows are May–June and September–October. In the rain shadow of Lahaul, Spiti and Ladakh, July to September is best, because the monsoon brings little rain there. Check each peak in our list of the highest peaks in India.

India's mountains are not one climate. The south-west monsoon reaches the outer ranges of the Indian Himalaya in June and stays until late September or October, bringing heavy rain, landslides and poor visibility to the approaches. Behind the main range, in Lahaul, Spiti and Ladakh, the same summer is dry. That is why one month can be the worst time for one peak and the best time for another a hundred kilometres away.

This guide splits India into five climbing regions, gives the official India Meteorological Department (IMD) monsoon dates for each, and lists the typical window for the peaks we cover. The windows are typical, not guaranteed: weather, snow and local orders change from year to year.

IMD normals

When the monsoon arrives and leaves the Himalaya

The IMD publishes normal dates for the onset and withdrawal of the south-west monsoon at each station (new normals, IMD research report of 2020). They explain the climbing calendar better than any rule of thumb: the monsoon side has its windows before the onset and after the withdrawal.

IMD stationNormal monsoon onsetNormal withdrawal
Hayuliang (Arunachal Pradesh)5 June14 October
Gangtok (Sikkim)8 June12 October
Dehradun (Uttarakhand)20 June27 September
Shimla (Himachal Pradesh)22 June24 September
Ladakh24 June22 September
Jammu28 June21 September

Across extreme north-west India the monsoon normally covers the whole region by 8 July. Real years differ from the normals. In 2026 the monsoon entered Himachal Pradesh on 30 June and left on 3 October, about a week later than normal, with season rainfall 11% below normal (IMD Shimla).

Climbing in July and August

Can you climb in the Himalaya during the monsoon?

Yes, behind the main range

Ladakh lies on the rain-shadow side of the Himalaya. Leh gets only about 100 mm (4 in) of precipitation a year, mostly as snow, and is called a cold desert (CGWB). Lahaul and Spiti share much of that dry summer. This is why the trekking peaks there, from Yunam to Kang Yatse, are climbed from June to September.

No, on the monsoon side

In July and August the approaches to Kullu, Garhwal and Kumaon peaks get the full monsoon: landslides, flash floods, swollen streams and cloud that hides the route. Most teams avoid expedition climbs there until the monsoon withdraws in late September.

The catch in June

Early in the rain-shadow season, snowmelt swells the rivers, so stream crossings on the walk-in are higher in June than in late summer. The IMD still lists normal monsoon dates for Ladakh: it gets very little rain, not none.

September to November

Is October too late to climb?

Early and mid-October often bring the clearest, most stable weather of the year on the monsoon side, which is why autumn is the main expedition season in Garhwal and Kullu. Days are shorter and nights colder, and the first heavy snow can come before the end of the month.

Plan a buffer. On 31 October 2026 an avalanche struck a trekking party beyond Bhojkharak on the Kedartal route above Gangotri, and the district administration launched an NDRF and SDRF rescue. Late-season snow is a reason to keep spare days and follow local advisories.

In Sikkim the autumn window runs later: the IMD normal withdrawal at Gangtok is 12 October, and our dataset gives Frey Peak April–May and October–November. The state's adventure tourism page says May to October is the ideal season for mountaineering in Sikkim; that page draws on an older order, so confirm the current rules. See our Sikkim peaks guide.

December to April

Can you climb in the Himalaya in winter?

Not the 5,000–6,000 m expedition peaks, in practice: high camps are buried, days are short and the road-heads may be snowed in. Winter is the season for lower snow summit treks, such as Kedarkantha (3,810 m / 12,500 ft) in Uttarakhand, whose dataset season is December to April, or Nag Tibba (3,022 m / 9,915 ft), walked year-round with snow from December to March.

Local orders matter too. In Kangra district of Himachal, the administration has banned trekking above 3,000 m in winter (an order of November 2024, repeated in January 2026). That order covers Kangra only, not the whole state, and no order for winter 2026–27 had been found when we checked in November 2026.

Peak by peak

Five peaks, three different seasons

The animation draws five popular Himalayan peaks at their true heights. The two Kullu peaks above Manali, Friendship Peak (5,289 m) and Deo Tibba (6,001 m), have two short windows on either side of the monsoon.

Cross the Rohtang or the Baralacha La and the season changes: Yunam (6,111 m), Chau Chau Kang Nilda (6,303 m) and Kang Yatse II (6,250 m) are summer peaks, climbed while it rains in Manali.

FAQ

Questions people ask about the best time to visit the Himalayas

When is the best time for mountaineering in India?

On the monsoon side of the Himalaya (Kullu, Garhwal, Kumaon) the windows are May–June and September–October. In the rain shadow of Lahaul, Spiti and Ladakh, the best months are July to September.

Can you climb in the Himalaya during the monsoon?

Yes, in Lahaul, Spiti and Ladakh, which get very little rain: Leh receives only about 100 mm of precipitation a year, mostly as snow (CGWB). Avoid Kullu and Garhwal approaches in July and August.

When does the monsoon reach and leave Himachal Pradesh?

The IMD normal onset at Shimla is 22 June and the normal withdrawal is 24 September. In 2026 the monsoon entered the state on 30 June and left on 3 October, about a week late.

Is October too late to climb in the Himalaya?

Early and mid-October are often clear and stable on the monsoon side. Late October can bring the first heavy snow; on 31 October 2026 an avalanche hit a trekking party on the Kedartal route. Keep buffer days.

Can I climb a Himalayan peak in winter?

Expedition peaks of 5,000–6,000 m are not climbed in winter in practice. Winter is for snow summit treks such as Kedarkantha (3,810 m), whose season is December to April. In Kangra, winter orders have banned trekking above 3,000 m.

What is the best month to climb Friendship Peak?

May–June or September–October, either side of the monsoon. Friendship Peak (5,289 m / 17,352 ft) is above Manali in Kullu, on the monsoon side.

What is the best month for a first 6,000 m peak?

For Yunam (6,111 m / 20,049 ft) in Lahaul, June to September, with July to September the driest. Deo Tibba (6,001 m) above Manali fits May–June or September–October.

When is the best time to climb in Sikkim?

The IMD normal monsoon onset at Gangtok is 8 June and the withdrawal is 12 October, so spring (April–May) and autumn (October–November) are the usual windows, as for Frey Peak.
