What is an ice axe, and what is it used for?

An ice axe is a mountaineer's tool for snow and ice: a steel head with a pointed pick and a flat adze, on a shaft with a spike at the base. Climbers use it for balance on slopes, to stop a slide (self-arrest), to cut steps and as an anchor. Most carry a 45–60 cm basic axe. See our mountaineering gear list for the rest of the kit.

On the snow peaks of the Indian Himalaya the ice axe goes everywhere the crampons go. On a guided climb you carry it from the snow line to the summit: as a third point of contact on steep snow, and as the tool that can stop a slide.

Most ice-axe guides online are written for Scotland, the Alps or the Cascades. This one is brand-free and written for climbs in India: what each part does, which type and length suit a first peak such as Friendship Peak, and why self-arrest is something you learn on the snow with a guide, not from a web page. Anatomy

Ice axe parts and what each one does

PartWhat it isWhat it does
HeadThe top of the axe, usually steel, with a hole for a leash or carabinerCarries the pick and the adze; you hold the axe by it when walking
PickThe toothed, pointed end of the head, typically slightly curvedBites into snow and ice: the part that does the work in a self-arrest
AdzeThe flat, wide end of the headChops steps in hard snow and ice
ShaftStraight or slightly angled; aluminium, titanium, steel alloys or compositesThe handle; on moderate slopes it works like a walking stick
Spike (ferrule)The pointed metal tip at the base of the shaftPushes into the snow when you use the axe for balance
Leash (optional)A strap clipped through the head or shaftKeeps the axe attached to you if you let go

A little history. Climbers first used the alpenstock, a wooden pole with an iron spike. Around 1840 the pole and a hatchet were merged into the first true ice axe. In 1966 Yvon Chouinard pioneered strongly curved picks, which led to the modern ice tools used on steep ice.

Basic or technical

Basic vs technical ice axes

Basic (B)

  • For general mountaineering: walking on snow, glaciers and moderate slopes.
  • The axe a beginner uses on a trekking peak.

Technical (T)

  • Built stronger for steep and vertical terrain.
  • For experienced climbers on steep snow and ice.

Ice tools

  • Short, strongly curved axes used in pairs.
  • Made for climbing vertical ice.
  • Not needed on beginner peaks.

The standard to look for. The UIAA safety standard for ice tools, UIAA 152 (2025), is based on the European standard EN 13089 and separates basic (B) from technical (T) axes. Certified tools carry the UIAA trademark or the letters “UIAA”.

Sizing

Ice axe length: what size do you need?

Modern mountaineers typically carry an axe of 45 to 60 cm (about 18 to 24 in) for general use. Anything over 60 cm is usually regarded as too large and unwieldy.

Older rules that size an axe from your body height are disputed, so we do not give one. Your guide sizes the axe for you and for the peak.

On a guided climb the axe is often lent to you. Ask for one in the 45–60 cm range and check it on the first snow day.

Four jobs

How climbers use an ice axe

  1. Balance on a slope.On moderate snow you hold the axe by the head and push the spike into the slope with each step, like a walking stick: a third point of contact.
  2. Stop a slide (self-arrest).If you slip on a snow slope, the pick is your brake. More on this below.
  3. Cut steps.The adze chops footholds in hard snow and ice where kicking steps is not enough.
  4. Anchor.Buried in the snow, an axe can serve as an anchor for a rope.

On a guided climb in India you will mostly use the first two. On steep sections, guides often fix ropes and you move on them with the axe in one hand.

The axe as a brake

What is self-arrest?

Self-arrest is stopping your own slide on a snow or ice slope with the ice axe. In outline, the falling climber gets the pick into the snow with the axe across the body, rolls onto it so their body weight presses the pick in, and digs in their boots or crampon points until they stop.

It has to be practised until it is instinctive: that is why it is taught on a safe slope, never first tried on the real one.

Learn it in person, not from a page. The British Mountaineering Council puts it plainly: there is no substitute for skills taught face to face by an experienced instructor. On a guided Himachal climb, your guides run a snow-skills session near base camp before summit day: holding the axe, walking with it, and self-arrest on a safe slope. Borrow · rent · buy

Do you need your own ice axe?

  1. Ask what is on loan.On guided climbs, group technical gear often includes an ice axe, crampons and a helmet on loan. Get the list of what is provided in writing before you pack.
  2. On a course, it is provided.The Nehru Institute of Mountaineering provides technical equipment to its trainees.
  3. Check it before you trust it.Whether the axe is yours, borrowed or rented, look for the UIAA or CE/EN marking and check the head, pick and spike for damage.
  4. Bring it all back down.The Indian Mountaineering Foundation's expedition rules say all ropes and technical gear must be retrieved from the mountain.
FAQ

Ice axe questions beginners ask

What is an ice axe used for?

For balance on snow slopes, for stopping a slide (self-arrest), for cutting steps in hard snow and ice, and as an anchor when buried in the snow.

What are the parts of an ice axe?

The head (with a pointed pick at one end and a flat adze at the other), the shaft, the spike at the base, and often a leash.

What size ice axe do I need?

Most climbers use an axe of 45 to 60 cm for general mountaineering; over 60 cm is usually too unwieldy. Your guide sizes it for the peak.

What is self-arrest?

Stopping your own slide on a snow slope by getting the pick into the snow and pressing your body weight onto it. It has to be practised with an instructor until it is instinctive.

What is the difference between a basic and a technical ice axe?

A basic (B) axe is for walking and general mountaineering; a technical (T) axe is built for steep and vertical ice. Beginners on trekking peaks use a basic axe.

Do I need my own ice axe for Friendship Peak?

Usually not: outfitters commonly lend one on guided climbs. Confirm in the written inclusions before you travel.

Which hand do you hold an ice axe in?

Usually the uphill hand. Your guide shows you on the slope during the snow-skills session.

Do I need an ice axe on a winter trek?

Only where steep, hard snow is expected. Your trek leader decides, based on the route and the conditions.
