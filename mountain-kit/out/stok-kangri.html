Is Stok Kangri open in 2026?

No. Stok Kangri (6,153 m / 20,187 ft), near Leh in Ladakh, has been closed to climbers since 2020. On 21 December 2023 the Ladakh administration closed "all kinds of expeditions" to the peak over the depletion of the Stok glacier, and no reopening had been found as of October 2026. The closest open alternative is Yunam (6,111 m) in Himachal; see our list of India's 6,000 m peaks.

Status, checked 4 October 2026: closed. No reopening order has been found in news or official sources. Any trip still sold as a "Stok Kangri expedition" runs against the Ladakh order, so there is no legitimate trek or climb to book and no honest trip cost to quote.

December 2019. The All Ladakh Tour Operators Association announced a halt to climbs for 2020–2022, citing pollution of the Stok village water source, glacier retreat and over-tourism (The Outdoor Journal).

21 December 2023. The Ladakh administration ordered that "all kinds of expeditions to the region have been temporarily closed and restricted with immediate effect", citing the "depletion of Stok mountain glacier and environmental issues". The order covers the Army, the Air Force, paramilitary forces and travel agencies (The Statesman).

2026. No reopening has been announced. The peak stays on our list of mountains of India as information only; if it reopens, this page will say so with the official source and date.

Route and camps

The former route (closed, for context only)

Before the closure, Stok Kangri was the most climbed 6,000 m peak in India, because the road-head is close to Leh and the climb needs no technical skills. The trail left Stok village, followed the Stok valley (Stok Chu) past the camps of Chang Ma and Mankorma, and reached base camp at about 4,969 m, two days from the village.

Summit day was the hard part: more than 1,000 m of ascent and 8–14 hours on the move, at an altitude where many first-time climbers turned back. The table shows the former camps; the route is closed, and the 2023 order covers "all kinds of expeditions to the region", so treat the base-camp trek as closed too.

What it takes

How hard was Stok Kangri?

When it was open, Stok Kangri was graded a trekking peak: no ropes or technical climbing on the normal route, but very high. The summit day climbed more than 1,000 m from base camp and took 8–14 hours, and the altitude, not the terrain, turned most people back.

That profile is why Yunam is the natural replacement: it is also a non-technical 6,000er where altitude is the main challenge, with a long single-push summit day. If you have never been on snow, start with Friendship Peak.

The best alternatives to Stok Kangri

Yunam (6,111 m), Himachal is the closest like-for-like swap: a non-technical 6,000er with a long summit day, climbed from the Bharatpur camp on the Manali–Leh highway below Baralacha La in June–September. Steep scree and snow near the top and the altitude make it a real test.

Friendship Peak (5,289 m) above Manali is the classic first snow summit, climbed in May–June and September–October; read our Friendship Peak climbing guide. Deo Tibba (6,001 m) near Manali and Chau Chau Kang Nilda (6,303 m) in Spiti are the next steps up.

In Ladakh, Mentok Kangri (6,250 m) above Tso Moriri and Dzo Jongo (6,280 m) in the Markha area are non-technical trekking peaks in our dataset. We give them as information only: Suzu arranges climbs in Himachal, where more peaks are listed on our Himachal peaks page.

Permits

Permits, the 2023 order and the alternatives

The 21 December 2023 order closes all expeditions to the Stok Kangri region, for the armed forces and travel agencies alike, so no outfitter can legitimately run a climb, whatever permit wording is offered.

For the Himachal alternatives, foreign nationals need permission from the Indian Mountaineering Foundation (IMF). For peaks up to 6,500 m, which covers Yunam, Deo Tibba, Chau Chau Kang Nilda and Friendship Peak, the IMF base fee is US$500 for a team of two, plus per-member charges and US$500 liaison-officer equipment hire (IMF peak-fee page, checked 1 Oct 2026). Indian climbers go through a registered Himachal outfitter, which arranges the local permissions. More in our guide to climbing permits in India.

History

History of Stok Kangri

Stok Kangri is the high point of the Stok range of the Zanskar mountains, in Hemis National Park about 15 km south-west of Leh. Wikipedia gives 1951 as its official first ascent; the party is not reliably recorded.

The first official winter ascent was made in March 2002 by a British team. Its height of 6,153 m (20,187 ft) is the figure Wikipedia says a 2007 GPS reading confirmed; other sources give 6,136 m or 6,155 m.

Easy access from Leh made it India's most climbed 6,000 m peak, and that popularity, with pressure on the Stok village water source and a shrinking glacier, led to the closure.

Why was Stok Kangri closed?

Glacier depletion, pressure on the Stok village water source and over-tourism. The 2023 order cites the "depletion of Stok mountain glacier and environmental issues".

When will Stok Kangri reopen?

No date has been announced. We will update this page if an official reopening order appears.

Can I still trek to Stok Kangri base camp?

Treat it as closed. The 2023 order covers "all kinds of expeditions to the region".
