Neelkanth, also spelled Nilkantha, is the 6,596 m (21,640 ft) snow peak that rises about 10 km west of Badrinath temple. Pilgrims see it from the temple town; climbers know it as a hard, technical mountain with knife-edge ridges. How high is Neelkanth Peak, and where is it?

Neelkanth Peak (Nilkantha) is 6,596 m (21,640 ft) high, in the Garhwal Himalaya of Chamoli district, Uttarakhand. It is the snow pyramid you see behind Badrinath temple, about 10 km to its west. Frank Smythe called it the "Queen of the Garhwal". It is a technical climb, first accepted as climbed in 1974. Compare it with other 6,000 m peaks in India.

Neelkanth is named among the 83 peaks Uttarakhand opened for mountaineering on 4 February 2026, and the state Forest Department lists it as an open peak with its check-post at Joshimath. Indian climbers apply on the UKMPS portal; foreign teams apply through the IMF.

The peak behind the temple. For most visitors, Neelkanth is the white pyramid above Badrinath temple. It towers more than 3 km above the town and stands between two glaciers: the Satopanth Glacier on its north and the Panpatia Glacier on its west. Its north-east ridge runs towards Narayan Parbat. Badrinath is also the road-head for Satopanth and Chaukhamba.

The name. Nīlakaṇṭha means "blue-throated", one of Shiva's names. A legend says Shiva raised the mountain to block a priest who walked between Kedarnath and Badrinath in a single day. In 1931 the British climber Frank Smythe called it the "Queen of the Garhwal" for its clean pyramid shape. Do not confuse it with Neelkanth Mahadev temple near Rishikesh or Neelkanth lake in Lahaul.

Route and camps

The west ridge, camp by camp

There is no single standard route. Neelkanth has been climbed by three lines, and each starts from a different side of the mountain. The table and the profile video follow the west ridge, the best documented: Martin Moran's British team made its first ascent between 28 May and 2 June 2000, and Marko Prezelj and Matic Jošt repeated it in November 2001.

The walk-in starts at Hanuman Chatti, on the Joshimath–Badrinath road. A base camp on the Panpatia side, at about 4,100 m (13,450 ft), is three days and about 16 km away, with some 1,600 m of height gain. Camp 1 sits at 5,100 m (16,730 ft), the ridge is gained at about 5,550 m (18,210 ft), and the team bivouacked on the ridge near 6,100 m (20,010 ft) before the summit.

Plan it as a full technical expedition. No neutral source gives a fixed day count. The timings in the table come from the 2000 climb, and camp heights change from year to year.

Day by day

How a Neelkanth expedition unfolds

  1. Stage 1 · Road to Joshimath and Hanuman ChattiDrive up the Alaknanda valley; Joshimath is the Forest Department check-post for the peak. Rest days help you adjust to the height.
  2. Stage 2 · Walk in to base campAbout three days on foot from Hanuman Chatti to a base camp near 4,100 m on the Panpatia side (west ridge), or from Badrinath and Mana for the north-east ridge and north face.
  3. Stage 3 · Camps and ropeFerry loads to Camp 1 and onto the ridge, fixing rope on the steep and corniced sections.
  4. Stage 4 · Summit pushA long day or two along the knife-edge crest to the 6,596 m top, then back down the same way.
  5. Stage 5 · Weather days and the walk outSpare days for storms and fresh snow, then clearing the mountain and walking out.

Itineraries vary with the outfitter, group and weather; rest days are added when needed.

What it takes

How hard is it to climb Neelkanth?

Our dataset grades Neelkanth technical. The Himalayan Journal describes knife-edge ridges, cornices, serac fall and loose pinnacles, and about a dozen attempts have failed, many of them on the south-east arête. The Uttarakhand Forest Department puts it in its high-risk group. In September 1993 six Japanese climbers were killed by an avalanche below the north-east ridge.

It is not a trekking peak, and there is no tourist trek to the summit. If Neelkanth is your long-term goal, Himachal has the steps: start with Deo Tibba (6,001 m) or Yunam (6,111 m), then move to a technical peak such as Indrasan (6,220 m). When to go

Best time to see or climb Neelkanth

For climbers: May to June and September to October. Most summits came in late May and early June (1974, 1993 and 2000); the 2001 repeat of the west ridge was in November.

For pilgrims and sightseers: the clearest views from Badrinath come in the yatra season, May to June and again after the monsoon in September and October. Permits

Permits for Neelkanth in 2026

Indian climbers apply on Uttarakhand's UKMPS portal. Since February 2026 Indian climbers pay no state or IMF expedition fee on the opened peaks, and Neelkanth is named on that list. IMF rules for Indian teams still apply.

Foreign nationals need IMF permission at least 90 days ahead, with a liaison officer and insurance. At 6,596 m Neelkanth is in the 6,501–7,000 m band: IMF base fee US$700 for a team of two, plus US$500 for liaison-officer equipment; per-member charges apply (IMF peak-fee page, as of 1 Oct 2026). More in our IMF permit and peak fees guide and mountaineering in India for foreign climbers.

Inner Line. Badrinath itself is an open pilgrim town, but the border area beyond Mana needs an Inner Line Permit for Indians and a Protected Area Permit for foreigners. Routes that start beyond Mana, such as the north face from the Satopanth Glacier, fall in that area, so check ILP/PAP. A tourist visa covers open areas; confirm visa needs with the Indian mission.

History

Climbing history of Neelkanth

1931–1949: Frank Smythe named it the "Queen of the Garhwal" in 1931 and made the first attempt in 1937. Willi Unsoeld tried in 1949.

13 June 1961 (disputed): Capt. Narinder Kumar's Indian expedition reported that O.P. Sharma and two Sherpas reached the summit by the north face. Jagdish Nanavati later concluded they had turned back well below the top. The IMF ratified the claim in 1963 but asked for a repeat ascent, and the Himalayan Club does not accept it.

3 June 1974: an Indo-Tibetan Border Police (ITBP) team led by S.P. Chamoli reached the summit by the north face, after giving up on the west ridge. This is the first accepted ascent.

30 May–2 June 1993: a multinational Army expedition led by Col. H.S. Chauhan opened a new route by the north-east ridge from the east face, with about 4,000 m of fixed rope; dozens of climbers reached the top. In September 1993 six Japanese climbers died in an avalanche below the same ridge.

28 May–2 June 2000: Martin Moran's British team made the first ascent of the west ridge from the Panpatia Glacier. Marko Prezelj and Matic Jošt repeated it in November 2001.

For pilgrims

Seeing Neelkanth from Badrinath

Where to look

  • From the temple, Neelkanth is the snow pyramid to the west, rising behind the temple.
  • It stands about 10 km away and more than 3 km higher than the town.

When it shows best

  • Clear days in May–June and September–October.
  • In the monsoon months, July and August, cloud often hides the high peaks.

Can you trek to it?

  • Not to the summit: it is a technical climb.
  • Its base camps are expedition approaches, not a tourist trek.

Three places share the name. Neelkanth Peak is this mountain above Badrinath. Neelkanth Mahadev temple is a Shiva shrine near Rishikesh, and Neelkanth lake is in Lahaul, Himachal. This page is about the peak.

True heights

Neelkanth beside the giants around Badrinath

Neelkanth is the peak pilgrims notice, but it is not the highest around Badrinath. Satopanth (7,075 m / 23,212 ft) and Chaukhamba I (7,138 m / 23,419 ft) are higher, so is Mana Peak (7,272 m / 23,858 ft), and Kamet (7,756 m / 25,446 ft) tops them all.

What sets Neelkanth apart is its shape and its place: a clean pyramid only about 10 km from the temple. All five peaks in the chart are in our full list of India's highest peaks.

Neelkanth Peak: questions people ask

How high is Neelkanth Peak?

Neelkanth (Nilkantha) is 6,596 m (21,640 ft) high, in the Garhwal Himalaya of Chamoli district, Uttarakhand.

Where is Neelkanth Peak?

About 10 km west of Badrinath temple. It is the snow peak you see behind the temple.

Why is Neelkanth called the Queen of Garhwal?

The British climber Frank Smythe gave it that name in 1931 for its clean pyramid shape.

What is the legend of Neelkanth?

The name means "blue-throated", one of Shiva's names. A legend says Shiva raised the mountain to block a priest who walked between Kedarnath and Badrinath in a single day.

Who first climbed Neelkanth?

An ITBP team led by S.P. Chamoli, by the north face on 3 June 1974. An Indian claim from 13 June 1961 is disputed and not accepted by the Himalayan Club.

Yes. It is named among the 83 peaks Uttarakhand opened in February 2026. Indian climbers apply on the UKMPS portal; foreign teams pay the IMF fee of US$700 for two plus US$500 for liaison-officer equipment.
