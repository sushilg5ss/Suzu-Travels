Which is the highest peak in Ladakh?

Saltoro Kangri (7,742 m / 25,400 ft), on the Saltoro Ridge in the Siachen area, is the highest peak on ground India administers in Ladakh. India's official map also shows K2 (8,611 m / 28,251 ft) in Ladakh, but K2 stands in Pakistan-administered Gilgit-Baltistan. Second is Saser Kangri I (7,672 m). All 35 peaks are below, from our list of India's highest peaks.

Ladakh is the high, dry corner of the Indian Himalaya. Five ranges cross it from south to north: the Great Himalaya, the Zanskar, the Ladakh Range, the Pangong range and the Karakoram. Its giants are in the far north, in the Saltoro, Saser, Rimo and Siachen groups of the Karakoram; its climbable summits are mostly in the Zanskar range around the Markha valley and the Rupshu plateau near Tso Moriri.

The "highest peak in Ladakh" has two answers. India's official map places K2 (8,611 m) inside the Ladakh union territory, but K2 stands in Gilgit-Baltistan, which Pakistan administers, and is climbed only from Pakistan. On ground India administers, the highest is Saltoro Kangri (7,742 m / 25,400 ft) on the Actual Ground Position Line in the Siachen area, about the 31st-highest mountain in the world. It was first climbed on 24 July 1962 by a joint Japanese–Pakistani team.

Our table covers 35 notable Ladakh peaks from the dataset behind our Indian Himalaya guide: 24 above 7,000 m, 10 in the 6,000s and one in the 5,000s. Wikipedia's Ladakh list names a few more, such as Mt Hardinge, that are not in our dataset. As Wikipedia notes, a large number of Ladakh's peaks are still not open for climbing for security reasons.

Permits

Permits for climbing in Ladakh

Indian nationals no longer need an Inner Line Permit for Ladakh's protected areas, such as Nubra, Pangong and Tso Moriri: the Ladakh administration removed it in August 2021. Local environment and wildlife fees still apply; the amounts change, so check them in Leh.

Foreign nationals still need a Protected Area Permit for Nubra, Pangong, Tso Moriri and Rupshu, Dha-Hanu and similar areas. The same 2021 order extended it from 7 to 15 days. It is issued to groups of two or more through a registered Leh travel agent. Leh town, the highways and Zanskar need none. A tourist visa covers open areas; confirm visa rules with the Indian mission.

Every peak needs Indian Mountaineering Foundation (IMF) permission; foreign teams apply at least 90 days ahead, with a liaison officer and insurance. The IMF base fee for a team of two is US$500 for peaks up to 6,500 m, US$700 for 6,501–7,000 m and US$1,000 above 7,000 m, plus US$500 for liaison-officer equipment; per-member charges apply (IMF, checked 1 Oct 2026). See our IMF permit and fees guide and how to climb in India. Markha peaks also need Hemis National Park entry.

Where they are

Ladakh's peaks, area by area

The peaks fall into six areas. The first two are military and border zones; the last four are where ordinary climbing happens.

Siachen and Saltoro (restricted)

  • Saltoro Kangri (7,742 m): Ladakh's highest, on the AGPL
  • Teram Kangri I (7,462 m) and K12 (7,428 m)
  • Ghent Kangri (7,401 m)
  • Apsarasas Kangri I (7,245 m); its neighbour Apsarasas Kangri II is still unclimbed
  • Singhi Kangri (7,202 m) and Padmanabh (7,030 m)

Closed to ordinary teams. The 2019 opening of Siachen was for Army-arranged tourist visits from Siachen Base Camp to Kumar Post, not for climbing.

Nubra, Saser and Rimo (restricted)

  • Saser Kangri I (7,672 m): first climbed by the ITBP in 1973
  • Mamostong Kangri (7,516 m)
  • Saser Kangri II East (7,513 m): climbed in 2011
  • Rimo I (7,385 m) and Plateau Peak (7,287 m)
  • Shahi Kangri (6,934 m): first climbed in 2022

East Karakoram: the IMF allows only joint expeditions with Indian counterparts.

Markha and Hemis (Leh)

  • Kang Yatse: II (6,250 m) is a walk-up, I (6,400 m) is technical
  • Dzo Jongo (6,280 m)
  • Stok Kangri (6,153 m): closed since 2020

Inside Hemis National Park, which charges its own entry.

Rupshu and Tso Moriri

  • Lungser Kangri (6,666 m): Rupshu's highest, first climbed in 1995
  • Chamser Kangri (6,622 m)
  • Mentok Kangri (6,250 m)

Reached from Karzok on Tso Moriri. Foreigners need a Protected Area Permit for this area.

Kargil and the Suru valley

  • Kun (7,077 m): first climbed by Mario Piacenza in 1913
  • Nun (7,135 m): on the Jammu & Kashmir–Ladakh border
  • Chiling I (6,349 m) on the Zanskar–Kishtwar side

Kun is in Ladakh and Nun is counted in Jammu & Kashmir; both are approached from Kargil via the Suru valley road to Tangol.

Drass

  • Machoi (5,458 m): the only 5,000 m peak in our table, near the Zoji La

Only a short list of Ladakh peaks is climbed by ordinary teams each summer. In our dataset, 10 of the 35 are open to foreigners; the other 25 sit in the restricted Siachen, Nubra and Shyok belt.

When to go

Best time to climb in Ladakh

Ladakh lies in the rain shadow of the Great Himalaya; Leh gets only about 100 mm of precipitation a year. So the climbing season is high summer, when the monsoon soaks the rest of north India.

MonthsWhat to expect
JuneSeason opens; snow-melt rivers run high and crossings can be dangerous
July–SeptemberThe best window for Markha, Rupshu and Kargil peaks
June–AugustThe window given for the Karakoram peaks in our dataset
October–MayCold and snowbound; high passes close

Base camps here start high, so acclimatise in Leh first and read our altitude sickness guide.

FAQ

Ladakh's peaks: your questions

Which is the highest peak in Ladakh?

Saltoro Kangri, 7,742 m (25,400 ft), on the Saltoro Ridge in the Siachen area: the highest on ground India administers. India's official map also places K2 (8,611 m) in Ladakh, but K2 is in Pakistan-administered Gilgit-Baltistan.

Which is the second-highest peak in Ladakh?

Saser Kangri I, 7,672 m (25,171 ft), in the Saser Muztagh above the Nubra valley. An ITBP team first climbed it on 5 June 1973.

How many mountains in Ladakh are above 7,000 m?

24 of the 35 Ladakh peaks in our dataset, almost all of them in the Karakoram. The only one outside it is Kun (7,077 m) in the Great Himalaya.

Is Stok Kangri open for climbing?

No. Climbs stopped in 2020 and the Ladakh administration banned expeditions on 21 December 2023 because of the shrinking Stok glacier. We found no reopening as of October 2026.

Which peaks can I climb in Ladakh?

Kang Yatse II (6,250 m) in the Markha valley and the Rupshu peaks near Tso Moriri, such as Lungser Kangri (6,666 m), are climbed every summer. Kun and Nun are full expeditions from the Kargil side.

Is Nun Kun in Ladakh?

The massif sits on the Jammu & Kashmir–Ladakh border. Kun (7,077 m) is in Ladakh and Nun (7,135 m) is counted in Jammu & Kashmir; both are approached from Kargil through the Suru valley.

Do I need a permit to climb in Ladakh?

Indians have not needed an Inner Line Permit since August 2021. Foreigners still need a Protected Area Permit for areas like Nubra, Pangong and Tso Moriri, and every peak needs IMF permission.

When is the mountaineering season in Ladakh?

July to September, because Ladakh lies in the Himalayan rain shadow. June brings high snow-melt rivers; the Karakoram peaks are climbed from June to August.
