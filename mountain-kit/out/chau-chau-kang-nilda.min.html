What is Chau Chau Kang Nilda, and how do you climb it?

Chau Chau Kang Nilda (6,303 m / 20,679 ft), or CCKN, is a glaciated peak in Spiti, Himachal Pradesh, 13 km north-east of Kaza, with the village of Langza at its foot. It is a moderate climb by scree and the south-west ridge with two high camps, best from June to September. It is one of the 6,000 m peaks of India.

Chau Chau Kang Nilda rises above the high villages of Spiti, the cold desert north of the main Himalayan crest. Its name is usually given as "blue moon in the sky", it also appears on maps as Guan Nelda, and climbers shorten it to CCKN. Langza, a village at about 4,420 m, sits right at its base, so the peak fills the skyline for anyone who drives up from Kaza.

For a climber it is an honest Spiti 6,000er: no technical wall, but snow, scree, a glaciated summit dome and more than 1,800 m of height gain from the village. This page sets out the route with camp altitudes from a documented ascent, the season, the permits and the climbing history. Route and camps

Route from Langza, camp by camp

The road-head is Langza (about 4,420 m), a short drive above Kaza. Teams use Kaza and Langza to acclimatise before moving to a base camp at 5,307 m above the village.

The camp heights below come from an Ordnance Factories team's ascent of the south-west ridge, reported in the American Alpine Journal in 1989. They placed Camp I at 5,547 m and Camp II at 5,883 m, and reached the summit along the west face in about 7½ hours from Camp II. From base camp to the summit took them six days.

What it takes

How hard is Chau Chau Kang Nilda?

CCKN is graded moderate. The normal route climbs scree slopes and the south-west ridge to a glaciated summit dome, with two high camps above base camp. There is no long technical section, but you will wear crampons, move roped on snow and carry loads above 5,500 m. The summit day on the documented ascent was about 7½ hours from Camp II.

The real test is altitude: you start sleeping above 4,400 m within days of arriving. Read our altitude sickness guide, and if this would be your first 6,000er, compare it with Yunam and our guide to choosing a first 6,000 m peak.

  • ExperienceEarlier high-altitude treks; a trekking peak such as Friendship Peak helps.
  • TechniqueCrampons, ice axe, roped glacier travel and basic fixed-rope work.
  • FitnessLoad carrying between camps and a long summit day above 5,800 m.
  • AcclimatisationDays at Kaza and Langza before base camp; no rushing the height gain.
  • TrainingA Basic Mountaineering Course is a good base; Indian expeditions also follow IMF rules on team qualifications.
When to go

Best time to climb Chau Chau Kang Nilda

June to September. Spiti lies in the rain shadow of the main Himalaya, so the summer monsoon reaches it only weakly. The two documented ascents in the American Alpine Journal both summited in September, on the 5th and the 15th, after setting up base camp in late August.

Build weather days into the plan anyway: high camps above 5,500 m can still bring wind, cloud and fresh snow.

Permits

Permits for Chau Chau Kang Nilda

Indian climbers need permission from the Indian Mountaineering Foundation (IMF) for an expedition, and IMF rules set team qualifications, such as the leader's course and summit record. In Himachal, climbs go through an HP Tourism-registered outfitter with registered guides, with pre-trek police registration and life and rescue insurance for every participant under the state's 2021 adventure rules.

Foreign nationals can climb it with IMF permission, applied for at least 90 days ahead, and a liaison officer. For peaks up to 6,500 m the base fee is US$500 for a team of two, plus per-member charges and US$500 for liaison-officer equipment (IMF peak-fee page, checked Oct 2026). Foreigners who reach Spiti through Kinnaur also need a Protected Area Permit for the border belt beyond Jangi. More in our IMF permit and peak fee guide and our guide to mountaineering in India for foreign climbers.

CCKN is in Spiti, so the 2026 Kangra trekker registration, which covered ten Dhauladhar routes, does not apply.

History

Climbing history of Chau Chau Kang Nilda

The first ascent was made in 1939 by J.O.M. Roberts, while he was crossing Spiti from Kullu; Wikipedia calls him the first mountaineer to visit Spiti. The second ascent followed in 1955, by Trevor Braham and Peter Holmes.

Indian expeditions climbed the peak in 1966 and 1981. An Ordnance Factories team led by Keshab Chandra Kar reached the summit by the south-west ridge on 5 September, as reported in the American Alpine Journal in 1989. The record of ascents after 1955 is patchy, so we do not number them.

A Calcutta team led by Alok Basu made the first ascent of the south-east ridge, summiting on 15 September, as reported in the American Alpine Journal in 1991. Its summit party included Yana Banerjee-Bey and Kalpana Ghosh.

Spiti & around

Chau Chau Kang Nilda and its neighbours, side by side

At 6,303 m, Chau Chau Kang Nilda sits in the middle of the high country around Spiti. Below it are Kanamo (5,964 m), whose access is unclear, and Yunam (6,111 m). Above it rise Manirang (6,593 m, first climbed 1952) and Reo Purgyil (6,816 m), the highest peak in Himachal.

Chau Chau Kang Nilda: questions people ask

How high is Chau Chau Kang Nilda?

6,303 m (20,679 ft). A 1989 American Alpine Journal report gives 6,304 m.

What does Chau Chau Kang Nilda mean?

Where is CCKN?

Who first climbed Chau Chau Kang Nilda?
