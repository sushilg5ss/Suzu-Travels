South India tour — 12 days: Mahabalipuram, Thanjavur, Madurai, Thekkady, backwaters and Fort Kochi

Tamil Nadu's great temples, then Kerala's spice hills and a houseboat night — a sample plan we build from; every night can move.

Day by day

DAY 1

Arrive Chennai → Mahabalipuram

Your driver meets you at Chennai airport (name, photo and car number shared with you before the trip). Rather than staying in the city, we drive south along the coast to Mahabalipuram (Mamallapuram) — quieter, by the sea, and the reason most travellers come this way.

DAY 2

Mahabalipuram: the Pallava coast

A UNESCO World Heritage Site carved out of rock by the Pallava kings in the 7th and 8th centuries: the rathas (temples shaped like chariots), cave shrines, the giant open-air relief known as the Descent of the Ganges or Arjuna's Penance, and the Shore Temple above the waves. Go early, before the heat and the crowds.

DAY 3

Mahabalipuram → Puducherry

Further down the coast to Puducherry, 162 km (101 mi) south of Chennai. The old French quarter by the seafront promenade is a grid of straight, quiet streets lined with colonial buildings, some from the 18th century. An afternoon on foot, an evening by the sea.

DAY 4

Puducherry → Thanjavur

A long drive day inland to Thanjavur, the Chola capital. Arrive in time for the Brihadisvara (Big) Temple in the late-afternoon light: begun by the Chola king Rajaraja I around 1003–1004 and consecrated in 1009–1010, it is still a living temple more than a thousand years later.

DAY 5

Thanjavur and Darasuram

The Big Temple again in the morning, then the Airavatesvara temple at Darasuram with its 24 m (79 ft) tower and fine stone carving. Together with Gangaikondacholapuram (completed in 1035) they form UNESCO's "Great Living Chola Temples" — Thanjavur was listed in 1987, the other two added in 2004.

DAY 6

Thanjavur → Madurai

South to Madurai, a city with references going back to the 3rd century BC and called "the city that never sleeps". The Meenakshi Amman Temple has 14 gopuram towers of 45–50 m (148–164 ft) and a hall of a thousand sculpted pillars. Tamil Nadu Tourism lists opening hours of 6:00 am–12:30 pm and 4:00–9:00 pm — we check them on the day. The evening, when the temple is busiest, is the time to go.

DAY 7

Madurai → Thekkady

Across the Western Ghats into Kerala: about 136 km (85 mi) from Madurai airport up to Thekkady, at 900–1,800 m (2,950–5,900 ft). The air cools, the slopes turn to cardamom, pepper, vanilla, cinnamon, tea and coffee. A spice-garden walk in the afternoon.

DAY 8

Thekkady: Periyar Tiger Reserve

The reserve covers 777 sq km (300 sq mi) of forest around a 26 sq km (10 sq mi) lake. A two-hour boat trip, run by the Kerala Tourism Development Corporation, is the classic way to watch elephants, gaur, sambar and otters from a safe distance. Tigers are rarely seen — we never promise one.

DAY 9

Thekkady → the backwaters, houseboat night

125 km (78 mi) west, about 3 hours, to Alappuzha. Board a kettuvallam, a traditional rice barge rebuilt as a houseboat with bedrooms, a living deck and a kitchen; the crew cooks lunch, dinner and breakfast on board. The boat moors for the night on a network of canals, rivers and lakes stretching over 900 km (560 mi).

DAY 10

Backwaters → Fort Kochi

Breakfast on the water, then about an hour north (62 km / 39 mi) to Kochi. Fort Kochi, 10 km (6 mi) from Ernakulam town, is the place to slow down: the Chinese fishing nets, standing for over five centuries, at sunset.

DAY 11

Fort Kochi and Mattancherry

On foot and by car: St. Francis Church — India's oldest European church — Santa Cruz Basilica, Mattancherry Palace and the Jewish Synagogue. In the evening, a Kathakali performance; arrive early to watch the make-up.

DAY 12

Fly out of Kochi

To Cochin International Airport, 82.8 km (51 mi) and about 2 hours from Alappuzha, less from Fort Kochi. Flying on to Delhi for the Golden Triangle, or home? We time the drive around your flight, with a margin.
