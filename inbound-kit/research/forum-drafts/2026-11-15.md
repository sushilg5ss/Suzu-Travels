## 1. Tauck community forum — "Travel during Diwali"
URL: https://forums.tauck.com/discussion/comment/86603 (also https://forums.tauck.com/discussion/comment/86689)
Our page that answers it: https://suzutravels.com/india-tours/diwali-in-india-tour/

**Draft answer (≈180 words):**

Being in India for Diwali is lovely, but it changes how a trip runs, so a few practical points:

- **Check the exact date.** In 2027 the Government of India's holiday list puts Diwali on Friday 29 October, with the festival days running roughly 28–31 October. Local dates can differ by a day.
- **The Taj Mahal is closed every Friday** — so in 2027 it is shut on Diwali itself. See it on the Thursday or Saturday.
- **Expect crowds and fully booked trains/flights** the week before, as Indians travel home. Book transport early and keep a slack day.
- **Smoke and noise.** Fireworks plus the post-monsoon haze can make air quality in North Indian cities poor for several days. The UK FCDO mentions both festival crowds and Oct–Feb air pollution; if you have asthma or a heart condition, ask your doctor before you go.
- **Where:** Varanasi's ghats, Jaipur's lit markets, Udaipur's lakes and Amritsar's Golden Temple (Bandi Chhor Divas) are all special that evening.

I work with a small Himachal travel company; we wrote the dates and the Friday problem up here: https://suzutravels.com/india-tours/diwali-in-india-tour/

## 2. TripAdvisor (India forum, Leh) — "Leh basic queries"
URL: https://www.tripadvisor.in/ShowTopic-g297625-i10473-k9561626-Leh_basic_queries-Leh_Leh_District_Ladakh.html
Our page that answers it: https://suzutravels.com/india-tours/ladakh-trip-guide/

**Draft answer (≈175 words):**

A few things that trip up first-timers in Ladakh:

- **Rest on arrival.** Leh is about 3,500 m (11,500 ft). If you fly in, the district asks visitors to rest for about 48 hours before going higher — Khardung La is 5,602 m. Talk to your doctor before the trip about altitude.
- **Permits.** Foreign nationals need a Protected Area Permit for Nubra, Khardung La, Pangong and Tso Moriri. It is issued through the Leh district's official permit portal (lahdclehpermit.in), usually via a local agent; rules change, so confirm before you go.
- **Road vs flight.** Flying into Leh is the simplest. The Manali–Leh road (about 473 km, open roughly June to September/October) is spectacular but you gain height fast — plan night stops.
- **Phones and cash.** Only post-paid SIMs work in Ladakh, and ATMs thin out past Leh, so carry cash for Nubra and Pangong.
- **Insurance:** check yours covers altitude.

I work with a small Himachal travel company; we put the permits, altitude and seasons in one place here: https://suzutravels.com/india-tours/ladakh-trip-guide/
