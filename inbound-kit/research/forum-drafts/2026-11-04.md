## 1. FlyerTalk › Asia › India — "Setting up UPI payments" (and the older "Payment methods India" thread)
- Thread: https://www.flyertalk.com/forum/india/2168147-setting-up-upi-payments-printerfriendly.html (also https://www.flyertalk.com/forum/india/2103387-payment-methods-india-3.html — older; post only on the newer thread)
- Our page (link only if it helps): https://suzutravels.com/india-tours/upi-and-money-in-india-for-tourists/

> Disclosure: I work with a small Himachal travel company, so I field this question a lot.
>
> Short version: without an Indian bank account you can't link regular UPI apps, but NPCI (the body that runs UPI) has a prepaid wallet for visitors called **UPI One World**. You load it from your foreign card, sign up with passport + visa + selfie, and then scan the same QR codes locals use. The catch: NPCI's own latest release (Feb 2026) describes it as a pilot issued at select points such as Delhi airport, so check npci.org.in before you count on it — don't assume every airport counter has it.
>
> What works for most visitors: a no-foreign-fee card for hotels, restaurants and bigger shops in cities; rupees from ATMs for tea stalls, auto-rickshaws and anything in the hills; and a little cash buffer because mountain towns can have patchy networks. If you carry more than US$5,000 in foreign cash (or US$10,000 including cheques) you fill a currency declaration form on arrival — RBI's FAQ has the details.
>
> I wrote the details up here (what UPI One World is, limits, unused balance, cards vs cash): https://suzutravels.com/india-tours/upi-and-money-in-india-for-tourists/

## 2. Expat.com › India — "Yoga courses in India, best place? recommendations" (#417378)
- Thread: https://www.expat.com/en/forum/asia/india/417378-yoga-courses-in-india-best-place-recommends.html — check the thread date before posting; if it is old, keep this for the next fresh Rishikesh/yoga question on r/IndiaTravel or TripAdvisor.
- Our page: https://suzutravels.com/india-tours/rishikesh-yoga-retreat-guide/

> Disclosure: I work with a small travel company in Himachal, not with any yoga school.
>
> Rishikesh is the usual first choice — Incredible India calls it the yoga capital of the world and it has everything from drop-in classes to 200-hour teacher trainings and silent courses. A few things that save people grief:
>
> 1. Decide the format first: a 1–2 week retreat, a 200-hour TTC (about a month, intense) or a 10-day Vipassana (donation-based, apply on dhamma.org well ahead — acceptance can take two weeks).
> 2. Check claims yourself: look the school up on yogaalliance.org, or ask about Yoga Certification Board (Ministry of Ayush) accreditation. Pay only against a written confirmation.
> 3. Visa: the e-Tourist visa covers a short-term yoga programme. Apply only on indianvisaonline.gov.in — the look-alike sites charge extra.
> 4. Timing: the International Yoga Festival is Feb–Mar; July–August is monsoon.
>
> Getting there: fly to Dehradun (about 20 km / 12 miles away), train, bus or a car from Delhi. Longer write-up if useful: https://suzutravels.com/india-tours/rishikesh-yoga-retreat-guide/
