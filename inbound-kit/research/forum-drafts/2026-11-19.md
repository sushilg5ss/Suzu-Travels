## 1. Rick Steves Travel Forum — "India Holi 2026 tour recommendations"
URL: https://community.ricksteves.com/travel-forum/beyond-europe/india-holi-2026-tour-recommendations
(If this thread is closed, look for a newer "Holi 2027" question there or on r/IndiaTravel — the answer works for both.)
Link to use (only if it helps): https://suzutravels.com/india-tours/india-in-march/

> Disclosure: I work with a small Himachal-based travel company, so treat this as one opinion.
>
> For 2027, Holi is on Tuesday 23 March (it's on the Government of India holiday list), with the Holika Dahan bonfires the evening before. If you want the "big" Holi, Uttar Pradesh Tourism lists Rangotsav in Mathura–Vrindavan from 16 to 30 March 2027 — the Braj towns celebrate for days, not just one. Hotels there fill early, so book well ahead.
>
> Two practical things people underestimate: drinks offered by strangers at Holi can contain bhang (the UK Foreign Office warns about spiked food and drink), and cheap colours can irritate skin and eyes — oil your skin and hair, wear old clothes, and protect phones. Many women prefer to watch the most crowded street play from a rooftop or with a group.
>
> Also, March is the tail of the season: Delhi/Agra/Jaipur are pleasant early in the month but the plains get hot towards the end, while the hills (Shimla is roughly 7–16 °C in March) are lovely.
>
> I put the dates and sources in one place here if useful: [India in March](https://suzutravels.com/india-tours/india-in-march/)

## 2. Fodor's Travel Talk — "Seeking help on Rajasthan Itinerary"
URL: https://www.fodors.com/community/asia/seeking-help-on-rajasthan-itinerary-991644/
Link to use: only if the asker asks about a driver or day-by-day plan — https://suzutravels.com/india-tours/rajasthan-tour/

> Disclosure first: I work with a small travel company in Himachal, so I'm biased toward car-and-driver trips.
>
> A loop that works well without rushing is Jaipur (2 nights) → Jodhpur (2) → Jaisalmer (2) → Udaipur (3), then fly out of Udaipur — roughly 10–12 days from Delhi. Add Agra at the start if the Taj is a must; Agra to Jaipur is about 232 km by road.
>
> The thing to plan around is the desert legs: Jodhpur → Jaisalmer and Jaisalmer → Udaipur are each a full day on the road, so don't schedule sightseeing on those days and break the drive with lunch stops. That's also why most people use one car and one driver for the whole trip rather than trains — the rail links on the western side are fewer and slower.
>
> November to March is the comfortable window; from April the desert gets very hot. Two nights minimum per city is what makes it enjoyable rather than a blur.
>
> Happy to answer anything specific about the route.
