## 1. "Taj Mahal from Delhi — day trip, or stay a night in Agra?"

- **Question found:** Fodor's Asia forum "Taj Mahal Advice?" https://www.fodors.com/community/asia/taj-mahal-advice-821271/ (date not checked — WebSearch title only). Asked every week on r/IndiaTravel and the TripAdvisor Agra forum in Nov–Feb — post on the freshest thread.
- **Our page that answers it:** https://suzutravels.com/india-tours/taj-mahal-tour-from-delhi/

**Draft answer (≈170 words):**

> A day trip works, but plan it around three things people often miss.
>
> 1. **Fridays.** The Taj is closed to general visitors every Friday. Check your Agra day isn't a Friday before you book anything.
> 2. **Train vs car.** Agra is about 204 km (127 mi) from Delhi by road — a long day on the road both ways. The Gatimaan Express from Hazrat Nizamuddin takes about 1 h 40 min, so the train makes the day much less tiring; you then need a car or auto in Agra between the Taj, Agra Fort and the station. Check current timings on IRCTC, not on blogs.
> 3. **Winter fog.** From late December to January, early-morning fog can be thick on the expressway and at the Taj itself. Many "sunrise tours" leave Delhi at 2–3 am — I wouldn't do that drive in fog. If sunrise matters to you, sleep one night in Agra instead.
>

## 2. "Which SIM / eSIM should I get for India as a tourist?"

- **Question found:** Blind "Good eSIM cards for India travel?" https://www.teamblind.com/post/good-esim-cards-for-india-travel-57suhubb (date not checked). Same question is constant on r/IndiaTravel / r/solotravel and the TripAdvisor India forum — post on the freshest thread.
- **Our page that answers it:** https://suzutravels.com/india-tours/sim-card-india-tourist/

**Draft answer (≈170 words):**

> Two routes, and both work:
>
> **Local SIM (cheapest data).** Foreign tourists can get one at an official operator store (or the airport counter) with your passport + visa page copies, a passport photo and your Indian address (the hotel). It isn't instant: activation can take from a few hours to a day or two after a verification call, and a tourist connection can't run beyond your visa period (max 3 months at a time).
>
> **Travel eSIM bought before you fly (easiest).** Data-only, works the moment you land, no paperwork. Costs a bit more per GB. Note you generally can't self-activate an Indian operator's own eSIM from abroad — you need a local number first.
>
> One catch people miss: **prepaid SIMs bought elsewhere in India don't work in Jammu & Kashmir, Ladakh or the North East.** If you're heading there, check before you go. And don't bring a satellite phone or standalone GPS device — illegal without a licence.
>
