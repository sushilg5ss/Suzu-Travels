## 1) TripAdvisor India Forum — "How much spending money to bring for two weeks?"
https://www.tripadvisor.com/ShowTopic-g293860-i511-k4852816-How_much_spending_money_to_bring_for_two_weeks-India.html
(thread date not verified from here — check before posting)

**Draft (≈180 words):**

It depends far more on how you travel than on India itself, so it helps to split the money into two buckets.

Fixed costs you pay whoever you go with: flights, the e-Visa (pay only on indianvisaonline.gov.in — the 30-day e-Tourist fee is lower for April–June arrivals than July–March, and look-alike sites charge extra), travel insurance, and monument tickets. Foreigners pay a separate, higher ticket at places like the Taj Mahal and Humayun's Tomb, so add those up from the official sites once you have your list.

Then the daily spend: transport, hotels, meals, tips. The big levers are hotel standard, whether you take trains or a car with driver, how many internal flights, and whether you travel in the Oct–Mar peak.

Cards work in hotels and bigger shops, but carry some cash for small places and tips.

## 2) Fodor's Asia forum — "2 week tour"
https://www.fodors.com/community/asia/2-week-tour-885856
(check date/status before posting)

**Draft (≈170 words):**

Two weeks is a good length for a first India trip if you don't try to cover the whole country. Most people who enjoy it pick one region and add one contrast.

A common shape: Delhi (1–2 days) → Agra for the Taj at sunrise (note it is closed on Fridays) → Jaipur (2 days) — that's the Golden Triangle in about a week. Then, for the second week, either Rajasthan further west (Jodhpur, Udaipur) or the Himalayan foothills (Shimla, Manali, Dharamshala), which feel completely different and are cooler.

Things that save headaches: book trains early (there is a Foreign Tourist Quota), keep a buffer day before your flight home, and avoid the mountains in the monsoon when landslides close roads.

If you visit Delhi, plan around Mondays — the Red Fort and several museums are closed.
