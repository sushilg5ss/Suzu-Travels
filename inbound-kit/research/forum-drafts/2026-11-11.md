## 1. Reddit r/amritsar — "Visiting Amritsar with my girlfriend"
Question URL: https://www.reddit.com/r/amritsar/comments/1h8wwbf/visiting_amritsar_with_my_girlfriend/
Link to (only if it helps): https://suzutravels.com/india-tours/amritsar-golden-temple-tour/

> Two nights is the sweet spot. Do the Golden Temple twice — once after dark when the sanctum is lit, and once at dawn when it is quietest. Both of you need to cover your heads (scarves are lent free at the entrances), shoes go to the free counter, and you walk clockwise round the pool. Eat at the langar — it's free, everyone sits on the floor together, and it's the best memory most people take home.
>
> For the Attari–Wagah ceremony, leave the city early (it's roughly 30 km) and carry your original passports. Timings move with the season, so confirm on the day. If you're from the UK, read the FCDO page first — it advises against travel near the border except for the Wagah crossing area, and says the ceremony can still be attended.
>
> If you have more days, Dharamshala/McLeod Ganj is a day's drive into the hills and pairs really well.
>
> (Disclosure: I work with a small Himachal travel company. We wrote up the etiquette and the Wagah notes here if useful: https://suzutravels.com/india-tours/amritsar-golden-temple-tour/)

## 2. Fodor's Asia forum — "Southern India in two weeks; Kerala and Tamil Nadu"
Question URL: https://www.fodors.com/community/asia/southern-india-in-two-weeks-kerala-and-tamil-nadu-972703/
Link to: https://suzutravels.com/india-tours/south-india-tour/

> Two weeks is enough if you cross the peninsula only once instead of zig-zagging. A route that works: fly into Chennai, go down the coast to Mahabalipuram (UNESCO-listed Pallava rock temples), then Puducherry (about 162 km / 100 mi south of Chennai), inland to Thanjavur for the Chola temple, Madurai for the Meenakshi temple, up into the hills at Thekkady, then down to a backwater houseboat night and out of Kochi. Every drive moves you forward, and you fly home from a different city.
>
> Timing matters more than people expect: Tamil Nadu's main rains come with the north-east monsoon (October–December), the opposite of Kerala's summer monsoon, so mid-December to February is the comfortable window for both. Keep one flexible day if you go earlier.
>
> Temples: dress modestly, shoes off, and some inner areas are Hindu-only — your guide will tell you.
>
> (Disclosure: I work with a small travel company based in Himachal; we arrange South India trips. Our 12-day sample route is here: https://suzutravels.com/india-tours/south-india-tour/)
