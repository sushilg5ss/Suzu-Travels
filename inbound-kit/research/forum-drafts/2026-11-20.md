## 1. Fodor's Travel Talk — "Solo or group tour for first trip to India?"
URL: https://www.fodors.com/community/asia/solo-or-group-tour-for-first-trip-to-india-1544040/
Link to use (only if it helps): https://suzutravels.com/india-tours/india-solo-travel/

> Disclosure: I work with a small Himachal-based travel company, so weigh this as one opinion.
>
> Both work, and there's a middle way people forget. A small-group tour is the cheapest way to get company and a fixed price, and if you want people to eat with in the evenings it's hard to beat. Fully independent is very doable on a well-trodden first route — Delhi, Agra, Jaipur, then Rishikesh or the Himalayan foothills — if you book your first night and airport transfer before you land, carry a local SIM, and use trains for the longer legs (foreign passport holders can book the IRCTC Foreign Tourist quota themselves, with the original passport on board).
>
> The middle option is a private car and driver for the awkward road legs only. For one person it costs more per head than a coach seat, so ask for a quote before deciding.
>
> Whatever you choose: read your own government's advice first (the UK says avoid hailing taxis on the street, especially at night — use app cabs or pre-paid airport taxis), and save 112 (emergency) and 1363 (Ministry of Tourism 24×7 tourist helpline).
>
> I wrote the details up here: [India solo travel](https://suzutravels.com/india-tours/india-solo-travel/)

## 2. FlyerTalk India forum — "New to India Questions"
URL: https://www.flyertalk.com/forum/india/1687735-new-india-questions.html
Link to use: only if the asker is unsure about the visa — https://suzutravels.com/india-tours/india-e-visa-guide/

> Disclosure: I work with a small travel company in Himachal (we are not a visa agency).
>
> A few first-timer basics that save the most grief:
>
> 1. Visa: apply yourself on the official site, indianvisaonline.gov.in, at least 4 days before arrival. Search ads often show look-alike sites first; they add a service fee on top of the government fee, and there is no official "express" option. Print the ETA and check it says GRANTED.
> 2. Arrival: book your first night and airport transfer in advance. App cabs (Uber/Ola) and pre-paid taxi counters are in the airport terminals; the UK Foreign Office advises not hailing taxis on the street.
> 3. Phone: save 112 for emergencies and 1363 / 1800-11-1363, the Ministry of Tourism's 24×7 multilingual tourist helpline.
> 4. Pace: India rewards slow travel. Two or three bases in 10 days beats six.
> 5. Health: talk to your doctor or a travel clinic, and check CDC or NHS Fit for Travel for vaccines.
>
> Happy to answer anything specific about the North or the Himalaya.
