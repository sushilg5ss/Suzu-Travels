## 1. TripAdvisor India forum — "How do I Book Train Ticket Online from Overseas"
Question URL: https://www.tripadvisor.co.uk/ShowTopic-g293860-i511-k5255252-o10-How_do_I_Book_Train_Ticket_Online_from_Overseas-India.html
Our page (link only if it helps): https://suzutravels.com/india-tours/trains-vs-flights-in-india/

Draft answer (≈170 words):

> Disclosure: I work with a small travel company in Himachal, so I deal with this a lot for guests.
>
> You can book from abroad yourself on the official IRCTC site. Register with your overseas mobile and email, then pick the Foreign Tourist (FT) quota when you search. A few things that trip people up:
>
> - FT quota opens up to 365 days ahead (the normal window is 60 days), but only in the upper classes (1A, 2A, Executive Chair Car).
> - You need your passport number and nationality, and payment has to be an international card.
> - FT fares are higher than the normal fare, and cancellation refunds are lower — so book once your dates are firm.
> - Carry the original passport you booked with on board; the TTE will check it.
> - Agents in India are not allowed to book FT-quota seats for you, so be wary of anyone offering to.
>
> If FT seats are gone, the general quota still works with an international card. For Delhi–Agra–Jaipur a car is often easier anyway. Longer write-up here if useful: https://suzutravels.com/india-tours/trains-vs-flights-in-india/

## 2. Fodor's Asia forum — "Are hired drivers in India safe?"
Question URL: https://www.fodors.com/community/asia/are-hired-drivers-in-india-safe-836656/
(Older thread — post only if replies are still open; otherwise reuse on a fresh r/IndiaTravel or TripAdvisor thread asking how to pick a driver/agency.)
Our page: https://suzutravels.com/india-tours/how-to-choose-a-travel-agency-in-india/

Draft answer (≈160 words):

> I work with a small Himachal-based travel company, so take this with that in mind — but here is what I'd check with any company, ours included:
>
> 1. Registration: ask which state tourism department they're registered with and the number, then look it up yourself. Some agencies are also approved under the Ministry of Tourism's voluntary scheme; many good small ones aren't, so ask rather than assume.
> 2. A real address you can see on a map, and a landline or WhatsApp number that stays the same.
> 3. Everything in writing before you pay: route, car type, what's included (tolls, parking, driver's food and stay) and the cancellation terms.
> 4. Pay a deposit, not the whole trip, and by card or PayPal so you have some protection. Avoid bank transfers to personal accounts.
> 5. Ask for the driver's name, photo and car number before the trip.
>
> The UK FCDO page on India also has a short section on scams worth reading. Our checklist, if helpful: https://suzutravels.com/india-tours/how-to-choose-a-travel-agency-in-india/
