## 1. "Do I need a Restricted Area Permit for the Andamans on an e-Visa?"

- **Question found:** immihelp forum — https://immihelp.com/forum/usa-visitors-visa/171548-restricted-area-permit-with-e-visa-andaman-islands (date not visible in search — check before posting; same question appears regularly on r/IndiaTravel and the TripAdvisor India forum, post there on the next fresh thread).
- **Our page that answers it:** https://suzutravels.com/india-tours/andaman-islands/

**Draft answer (≈170 words):**

> For most nationalities the old permit is no longer needed for the usual tourist islands. The Andaman & Nicobar Tourism notice lists 29 islands excluded from the Restricted Area Permit regime — including Swaraj Dweep (Havelock) and Shaheed Dweep (Neil) — plus 11 islands open for day visits until 31 December 2027. The exceptions: citizens of Afghanistan, China (incl. Hong Kong and Macau) and Pakistan, and people of that origin, still need an MHA-approved permit before arrival (applied at indianfrro.gov.in); Myanmar citizens need one for Mayabunder and Diglipur.
>
> Your normal Indian e-Visa (apply only at indianvisaonline.gov.in — ignore look-alike sites that charge extra) works, and Port Blair (now officially Sri Vijaya Puram) is a designated e-Visa airport. Forests, sanctuaries and tribal reserves still need separate permission, and photographing tribal people is an offence.
>
> Rules change, so read the current notice on the A&N Tourism site before you fly. Disclosure: I work with a small Himachal travel company; we wrote up the rules with sources here if useful: https://suzutravels.com/india-tours/andaman-islands/

## 2. "Is Gulmarg OK for a first-time skier, and when should I go?"

- **Question found:** recurring question on ski forums (e.g. skipass.com 'stations ailleurs' thread https://www.skipass.com/forums/enmontagne/stations_ski_ailleurs/sujet-105835.html — French, date not checked) and TripAdvisor Gulmarg forum each winter. Post on the next fresh English thread (season starts mid-December).
- **Our page that answers it:** https://suzutravels.com/india-tours/gulmarg-ski-holiday/

**Draft answer (≈165 words):**

> Yes, beginners can learn there. The state tourism corporation (JKTDC) lists three lifts suited to beginners, a chair lift for intermediates, trained instructors and a government ski-hire shop where you can rent skis, boots, poles, gloves and goggles. The famous two-stage gondola (to about 3,980 m / 13,000 ft) is really for confident skiers — beginners stay on the lower slopes.
>
> Season: usually mid-December to mid-April, but snow varies a lot year to year, so check recent reports before booking. Gulmarg is roughly 52–56 km (32–35 mi) from Srinagar airport; snow can close the road for hours, so arrive in daylight and keep a spare day.
>
> Two honest caveats: the UK FCDO advises against all travel to Jammu & Kashmir (Gulmarg included) and the US rates it Level 4 — read your own government's advice, and check whether your travel insurance covers both the region and skiing; many policies don't.
>
> Disclosure: I work with a small Himachal travel company; our notes with sources: https://suzutravels.com/india-tours/gulmarg-ski-holiday/
