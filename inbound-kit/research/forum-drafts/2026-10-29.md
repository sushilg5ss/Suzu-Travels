## 1. "Is Sarnath worth a visit?" / "How many days in Varanasi?"
- Thread found: Fodor's — https://www.fodors.com/community/showthread.php?p=13166719 (undated in search; check the date first — if old, use on the next Reddit r/IndiaTravel / r/solotravel "how many days in Varanasi" question)
- Link (only if it helps): https://suzutravels.com/india-tours/varanasi-trip-guide/

> Short answer: yes, if you have two full days or more. Sarnath is about a half-day trip from the ghats, and it became a UNESCO World Heritage Site in July 2026, so it is getting more attention than before — go in the morning.
>
> For a first visit, 2–3 days works well: a sunrise boat on the Ganga, the evening Ganga Aarti at Dashashwamedh Ghat (around 45 minutes, at dusk), Kashi Vishwanath temple (no phones, bags or even pens inside — there are free lockers, so travel light), and Sarnath on the second morning. At the cremation ghats, don't photograph funerals; just watch quietly from a distance.
>
> Timing matters: October to March is the comfortable season. Dev Deepawali falls on 24 November 2026 — spectacular, but book rooms early. July–September the river floods the ghats and boats can be stopped.
>
> Disclosure: I work with a small Himachal travel company; we wrote up a 2- and 3-day plan here if it's useful: https://suzutravels.com/india-tours/varanasi-trip-guide/

## 2. "How do I find a genuine Ayurveda retreat in Kerala?"
- No open recent thread found this run (Reddit/TripAdvisor results were retreat-booking listings). Post on the next r/IndiaTravel, r/Ayurveda or TripAdvisor Kerala forum question asking for a "real" or "authentic" Ayurveda centre.
- Link (only if it helps): https://suzutravels.com/india-tours/ayurveda-retreat-kerala/

> I'd skip "top 10" lists and start from the official ones. Kerala Tourism publishes a list of classified Ayurveda centres (Green Leaf / Olive Leaf, and Ayur Diamond / Gold / Silver) on keralatourism.org, and you can also ask a centre whether it has NABH Ayush accreditation. Then email the centre and ask for the doctor's name, qualification and registration — a genuine place answers that easily.
>
> On timing: the monsoon month (Karkidakam, roughly July–August) is the traditional season, but it's very wet; many people go in the drier months instead.
>
> Visa: if treatment is the only purpose, there's a separate e-Ayush visa; for a holiday with some treatments, check which category fits on the official site, indianvisaonline.gov.in only — there are look-alike sites that charge extra.
>
> And please talk to your own doctor first if you take medicines or have a condition — health agencies have warned about lead in some Ayurvedic products bought abroad.
>
> Disclosure: I work with a small Himachal travel company; our checklist is here: https://suzutravels.com/india-tours/ayurveda-retreat-kerala/
