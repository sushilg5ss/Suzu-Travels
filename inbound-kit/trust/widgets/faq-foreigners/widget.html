What should I know before my first trip to India?

Five things matter most: apply for your e-Visa only on the official site, at least 4 days before you arrive; travel between October and March for Delhi, Agra, Jaipur, Rajasthan, Kerala and Goa (the UK FCDO says the monsoon season usually runs from June to October); start with Delhi, Agra and Jaipur plus one more region; avoid driving at night and in fog; and buy tickets only through official sites and counters.

How do I get an Indian e-Visa without being overcharged?

Apply yourself on indianvisaonline.gov.in. The Government of India says no agent is needed and it charges no express or emergency fee. Your passport needs at least six months’ validity. Apply at least 4 days before arrival, carry the printed ETA showing “GRANTED”, and enter through one of the designated airports or seaports on the official list.

Do I need to fill in an arrival card?

Yes. Since 1 October 2025, foreign nationals and OCI card holders submit the e-Arrival card within 72 hours before arrival — on boi.gov.in, indianvisaonline.gov.in or the “Indian Visa Su-Swagatam” app. It is arrival information, not a visa (Embassy of India notice; checked 18 Oct 2026).

What do the US and UK governments advise about travel in India?

The US State Department rates India Level 2 – Exercise increased caution “due to crime and terrorism. Some areas have increased risk.” Its Level 4 areas are Jammu and Kashmir (except eastern Ladakh and Leh), the India–Pakistan border area, parts of central and east India, and Manipur (issued 16 Jun 2025, checked 23 Oct 2026).

The UK FCDO advises against all travel to Jammu and Kashmir, except air travel to and within Jammu city, and within 10 km (6 mi) of the India–Pakistan border (page updated 7 Oct 2026).

What this means for you: read the current advice for your passport before you book, and ask your insurer whether you are covered in any area your government advises against. We plan around it with you.

How do I avoid scams and ticket touts?

The UK FCDO says: “Be cautious in tourist areas, where scammers, pickpockets and ticket touts target foreign nationals” and “avoid people at railway stations offering tickets and tours as you may be scammed.” It also notes that “app-based taxis like Uber and Ola are widely available, including in all airport terminal buildings” (updated 7 Oct 2026).

Should I travel by car and driver or by train?

Both work. Foreign tourists can book reserved train seats under the Foreign Tourist Quota up to 365 days ahead, online with a passport number and an international card (general bookings open 60 days ahead). For touring door to door, a private car with an English-speaking driver is the simplest — your driver’s name, photo and car number are shared with you before the trip.

The UK FCDO advises: “Avoid travelling at night and in fog as accidents are common due to low visibility”, so plan long drives in daylight. More: private car and driver in India.

Can I pay by card or UPI in India?

UPI options for visitors exist — check the official NPCI site (npci.org.in) for the current rules, and carry a card and some cash as well. Do I need vaccinations for India?

The official e-Visa site says travellers arriving from or through yellow-fever countries need a yellow-fever vaccination card or may face quarantine. For everything else, talk to your doctor or a travel clinic well before you go, and see CDC (US) or NHS Fit for Travel (UK). We do not give medical advice.

When does the monsoon arrive?

India Meteorological Department normals: the monsoon reaches Kerala around 1 June and New Delhi around 27 June, and withdraws from Delhi around 25 September. Weather varies year to year. Month by month, region by region: our best time to visit India guide.
