The route: Jaipur, Jodhpur, Jaisalmer, Udaipur

Rajasthan rewards a loop through the desert rather than a dash between cities. Start with Jaipur, close to Delhi; go west to Jodhpur and the Thar Desert at Jaisalmer; finish slowly by the lakes of Udaipur, where you can fly out.

Delhi → Jaipur → Jodhpur → Jaisalmer → Udaipur, then fly out from Udaipur's Maharana Pratap airport (UDR). There are no official road distances for the desert legs, so we don't print guesses: Jodhpur → Jaisalmer and Jaisalmer → Udaipur are each a full day on the road.

Twelve days in Rajasthan, day by day

A sample we build from, not a fixed package. Nights can move, cities can be dropped or added; the pace stays human.

  1. DAY 1
    Arrive in Delhi

    Your driver meets you at the airport — name, photo and car number are shared with you before the trip. Check in and rest; many international flights land late at night.

  2. DAY 2
    Delhi → Jaipur

    Drive to Jaipur and settle in. If you arrive early enough, a first walk in the walled old city, founded in 1727 and a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 2019.

  3. DAY 3
    Jaipur

    Amber Fort in the morning — one of the six Hill Forts of Rajasthan on UNESCO's list — then the bazaars and palaces of the pink city. Your driver handles parking and waits for you.

  4. DAY 4
    Jaipur → Jodhpur

    A long drive day west towards the desert. We start after breakfast and break the drive with a stop; you arrive in Jodhpur for sunset.

  5. DAY 5
    Jodhpur

    Mehrangarh Fort on its rocky hill about 125 m (410 ft) above the city: seven gates and a museum with more than 15,000 objects, open 9:00–17:00. Below it, the old city's houses in shades of blue.

  6. DAY 6
    Jodhpur → Jaisalmer

    A full day across the Thar to the Golden City. Early start, a lunch stop on the way, and the fort glowing in the late light when you arrive.

  7. DAY 7
    Jaisalmer and the dunes

    Jaisalmer Fort (Sonar Qila), one of India's few 'living forts', where people still live inside the walls; the carved Patwon, Nathmal and Salim Singh havelis; Gadisar Lake. In the evening, the Sam sand dunes and a night at a desert camp that we arrange with a local team.

  8. DAY 8
    Jaisalmer → Udaipur: the drive day

    The longest day on the road. We leave early, stop for meals and breaks, and arrive in Udaipur in the evening. If you'd rather not, we can shorten the plan or skip Jaisalmer — ask us.

  9. DAY 9
    Udaipur, the City of Lakes

    A slow morning, then the City Palace above Lake Pichola — a lake that dates back to 1362.

  10. DAY 10
    Udaipur, or a fort day trip

    Stay in town, or ask us to add a day trip to Kumbhalgarh or Chittorgarh, both part of the UNESCO Hill Forts of Rajasthan.

  11. DAY 11
    Udaipur at your pace

    A free day for the old city and the lakeside, with your driver on call.

  12. DAY 12
    Fly out from Udaipur

    Your driver takes you to Maharana Pratap airport (UDR) for your onward flight home.

When to go: November to March, not in the hot months

Rajasthan is a winter destination. The official monthly figures from Incredible India explain why:

November – March: go

Warm days and cool nights. In January Jaisalmer ranges from about 4.6 °C to 29 °C (40–84 °F), Jodhpur from 5 °C to 30 °C (41–86 °F). Bring a warm layer for desert evenings.

October: good

Jaipur, Jodhpur and Udaipur are pleasant; Jaisalmer's best season starts in November.

April – June: too hot

From April, desert towns pass 40 °C (104 °F) — in May Jaisalmer reaches about 46 °C (115 °F) and Jodhpur about 45 °C (113 °F). We suggest other dates, or the Himalaya instead.

July – September: monsoon

The rainy months. Udaipur's season starts in September; ask us before you plan the desert in these months.

FAQ

Questions travellers ask

How many days do I need for Rajasthan?

Ten to twelve days for the four classic cities — Jaipur, Jodhpur, Jaisalmer and Udaipur — with two nights or more in each. With seven days, keep Jaipur, Jodhpur and Udaipur and leave Jaisalmer for another trip.

What is the best time to visit Rajasthan?

November to March. Udaipur's season starts in September, but Jaisalmer is best from November to March. From April the desert towns pass 40 °C (104 °F), and in May Jaisalmer reaches about 46 °C (115 °F), so we suggest other dates.

Is Jaisalmer worth the drive?

In the cool months, yes: its fort is one of India's few 'living forts', where people still live inside the walls, and the Sam sand dunes are close by. The drives in and out are full days, so give it at least two nights — or skip it in summer and on short trips.

In which order should I visit Jaipur, Jodhpur, Jaisalmer and Udaipur?

Start with Jaipur, closest to Delhi and Agra, then go west to Jodhpur and Jaisalmer, and finish in Udaipur, where you can fly out from Maharana Pratap airport (UDR) instead of driving back.

Can I add the Taj Mahal or a tiger safari?

Yes. Agra to Jaipur is 232 km (144 miles), so start Delhi → Agra → Jaipur. Ranthambore National Park, at Sawai Madhopur, is often added to a Jaipur stay; safari permits are booked only through the Rajasthan Forest Department's official portal.

Is a car and driver better than trains in Rajasthan?

For this loop, a private car is the easiest: the desert legs are long, and a car lets you stop when you like and keeps your luggage with you. Your driver's name, photo and car number are shared with you before the trip.

Is Rajasthan safe for tourists?

Take the same care as anywhere busy. The UK Foreign Office (advice updated 7 October 2026) asks visitors to take care with food and drinks from strangers and to stay alert in crowds; women travellers should read its advice on harassment.
