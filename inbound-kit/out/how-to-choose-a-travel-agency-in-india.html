How do I choose a trustworthy travel agency in India?

Pick a company you can check: ask for its registration certificate and look it up (the Ministry of Tourism's voluntary scheme is searchable on NIDHI; states such as Himachal Pradesh register travel agents under their own Act), find the office on a map, pay by card or PayPal rather than cash, and get the day-by-day plan, inclusions and refund rule in writing before you pay a deposit.

Two kinds of registration — and what each one means

Companies in India often describe themselves as officially approved or registered. That can mean two different things, and neither is a quality rating. Here is what each one is, and how to check it yourself:

TypeWhat it isHow to check it
Ministry of Tourism recognition (national)The Ministry of Tourism "through a voluntary scheme grants recognition/approval to different categories of service providers in the Travel Trade" (PIB, 12 Dec 2024). A company without it is not illegal.Search the Ministry's NIDHI+ database (nidhi.tourism.gov.in) by unit type, state and city. NIDHI holds units under the voluntary scheme and is "not a booking platform" (PIB, 11 Aug 2025).
State registration (e.g. Himachal Pradesh)Many states register travel agents under their own law. In Himachal Pradesh it is the HP Tourism Development and Registration Act, 2002 and the HP Registration of Tourism Trade Rules, 2012. The certificate is renewed every 3 years.Ask for the certificate number and the state that issued it, then check it with that state's tourism department. In Himachal, a registered travel agent must display the certificate "in his office at a conspicuous place".

If a company says it is recognised by the Ministry, ask for its approval certificate and look the company up on NIDHI+ yourself. A registration tells you a company exists legally and can be traced. It is not a promise about the quality of your trip.

The 8-point checklist before you pay anything

Use this for any company — a big one in your own country, a small one in India, or us:

  1. Ask for the registration — and check itGet the certificate number and who issued it (the Ministry of Tourism scheme or a state tourism department). Look it up on NIDHI+ or ask the state department. A real company sends the certificate without fuss.
  2. Find the office on a mapA street address you can see on a map, a landline or a WhatsApp number that answers, and a name of who you are talking to. A mobile number and a logo alone are not enough.
  3. Get a written, day-by-day planEvery night's town, the car type, which hotels or the hotel category, and what each day involves. If the plan is vague, the price will be too.
  4. Read what is included — and what is notFuel, tolls, parking, state taxes, driver's stay, breakfasts, guides, monument tickets. The "not included" list matters as much as the price.
  5. Pay by card or PayPal, not cash or a personal transferCard and PayPal payments leave a record and give you your own provider's dispute rules. Be wary of requests to send money to a personal account or by a money-transfer app.
  6. Know the deposit and the refund rule before you payHow much is the deposit, when is the balance due, and what happens if you cancel? It should be written in the quote or a booking policy page, not just said on the phone.
  7. Know who meets you at the airportThe UK FCDO advises: if a hotel driver collects you at the airport, "make sure they properly identify themselves". Ask for the driver's name and car number before you land.
  8. Ask what happens if something goes wrongA phone number that works during your trip, and who decides if a road closes or a flight is late. In the mountains this is not theory.

Red flags that should make you stop

None of these proves a company is dishonest. Each one means: slow down and check again.

Cash only, or a personal account

No card or PayPal option, or a request to pay a person rather than a company.

Pressure to pay today

"Only two rooms left", "price goes up tomorrow" — with no written reason. A good quote can wait a day while you check.

No written terms

No booking policy, no refund rule, no list of inclusions. If it is not in writing, you cannot hold anyone to it.

A price far below everyone else's

Usually something has been left out — smaller car, fewer nights in the hills, no driver's stay — and appears later as an extra.

Can't or won't show a registration

Or shows a certificate for a different company name or an expired date.

Approached at the station or the street

The FCDO advises: "avoid people at railway stations offering tickets and tours", and warns that "scammers, pickpockets and ticket touts target foreign nationals" in tourist areas.

A company in your own country, or a local one in India?

Both work, and many travellers have good trips either way. A tour company at home gives you consumer protection under your own country's rules and someone in your time zone. It will usually buy the ground services — cars, drivers, hotels — from a local company in India, and that is part of what you pay for.

Booking directly with a local company means you talk to the people who actually plan the roads, cars and hotels, and you can change things more easily. In exchange, you need to do the checks on this page yourself, and pay in a way that leaves you protected — by card or PayPal.

Whichever you choose, check your travel insurance covers the whole trip and the activities you plan, and read your own government's travel advice for India before you book.

If something goes wrong

Card skimming and confidence scams are common, says the UK FCDO; India's scam and cybercrime helpline is 1930. If you paid by card or PayPal, contact your provider quickly about a dispute. Complaints about tourism service providers can also go to the Ministry of Tourism through CPGRAMS (the government's public grievance portal), which sends them "to the authority concerned". FAQ

Questions travellers ask

How do I know if an Indian tour operator is legitimate?

Ask for its registration certificate and check it: the Ministry of Tourism's voluntary recognition scheme can be searched on the NIDHI+ database, and state registrations (for example Himachal Pradesh's) can be checked with that state's tourism department. Then check the office address on a map, read the written terms and pay by card or PayPal.

What does "Ministry of Tourism approved" mean?

It means the company is recognised under the Ministry of Tourism's voluntary scheme for travel trade service providers. The scheme is voluntary (PIB, 12 Dec 2024), so a company without it is not illegal — many companies are registered with their state instead. Neither is a rating of trip quality.

Is it safe to book a tour with a local Indian company?

It can be, if you check the company first: registration, a real office, written plan and terms, and a payment method that leaves a record. Read your own government's travel advice for India too — the UK FCDO warns that scams targeting foreign visitors are common in tourist areas.

Should I book with a company in my country or in India?

Both work. A home-country company gives you your own country's consumer rules and someone in your time zone; a local Indian company lets you talk directly to the people who plan the roads, cars and hotels. If you book locally, do the checks yourself and pay by card or PayPal.

How much deposit is normal?

There is no single rule. What matters is that the deposit, the balance date and the refund rule are in writing before you pay. Ours: a 20% deposit locks your dates and price, with a full refund if you cancel within 48 hours of paying.

How do I pay an Indian travel company safely from abroad?

By card or PayPal to the company itself, so there is a record and your provider's dispute rules apply. Be wary of cash-only requests or transfers to a personal account. We take cards via Razorpay (international cards accepted) and PayPal.

What should be in writing before I pay?

A day-by-day plan with towns and nights, the car type, hotels or hotel category, what is included and not included, the price and currency, the deposit and balance dates, and the cancellation and refund rule.

Who do I contact if I'm scammed in India?

Call India's cybercrime helpline 1930 (listed by the UK FCDO), contact your card provider or PayPal at once, and keep every message and receipt. Complaints about tourism service providers can also be filed with the Ministry of Tourism via CPGRAMS.
