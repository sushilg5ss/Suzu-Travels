What Jaisalmer is — and why it is worth the long trip

Jaisalmer sits far out in the Thar Desert of western Rajasthan. Its story begins in the 12th century — founded by Rawal Jaisal, according to tradition — and its yellow sandstone gives it the name Rajasthan Tourism uses: the Golden City.

The fort is the reason to come. Jaisalmer Fort is one of six forts inscribed together as the UNESCO World Heritage Site 'Hill Forts of Rajasthan' (2013). UNESCO describes it as a hill fort in desert terrain whose township is still inhabited today — Incredible India counts about 3,000 residents inside the walls. That makes it one of the few forts in the world where people still live: you walk lanes of houses, shops and temples, not an empty monument.

It is far from everything else, so it needs planning. You either fly into Jaisalmer, take the train, or drive across the desert from Jodhpur with a break on the way. Two nights are enough for most visitors.

What you see in Jaisalmer

Jaisalmer Fort

The lived-in golden fort on its hill above the town, with lanes of houses, small shops and temples inside the walls. Part of the UNESCO Hill Forts of Rajasthan (2013).

The Jain temples

Seven Jain temples inside the fort, built between the 12th and 15th centuries. Usually open in the mornings only — check locally.

The havelis

Carved merchants' mansions in the old town: Patwon ki Haveli, Salim Singh ki Haveli and Nathmal ki Haveli.

Gadisar Lake

A 14th-century reservoir built by a ruler of Jaisalmer, with boat rides.

Bada Bagh

Sandstone cenotaphs (chhatris) of Jaisalmer's royal family, a short drive from the town; open in the daytime.

Amar Sagar

A lake about 7 km (4 mi) from town with the 17th-century Amar Singh Palace beside it.

The Sam dunes, a desert safari and a night at a camp

The Sam Sand Dunes are about 40 km (25 mi) from Jaisalmer. Camel and jeep rides, sunsets and sunrises over the sand, music in the evening and stargazing are why most visitors stay a night in the desert.

The night rule (Oct 2026)

Since October 2026 the forest department has not allowed visitors on the dunes inside Desert National Park after sunset. Many camps and sunset camel rides are outside the park and carry on as before — rules can change, so we confirm them for your dates.

Camel, jeep or on foot

Camel rides are optional — if you'd rather not ride, a jeep or a walk on the dunes at sunset works too.

Choosing a camp

Camps vary a lot. What to check: proper toilets and showers, heating or heavy blankets in December–January, and how far the tents are from the road. We choose the camp for your dates and comfort level.

Desert National Park

The park around the dunes protects desert wildlife such as the great Indian bustard and the blackbuck.

Desert Festival

Usually late January or February — three days of music and events on the dunes, dates announced by Rajasthan Tourism. Ask us for the dates in your year.

When to go to Jaisalmer

November to March

The best months: sunny days and clear desert nights. Desert nights in December–January can be cold (about 5 °C / 41 °F) — bring warm layers for a camp night.

April to June

From April to June Jaisalmer can pass 45 °C (113 °F). If you have a heart condition or are sensitive to heat, consult your doctor; see CDC or NHS Fit for Travel.

Outside the season

Months outside November–March are hotter and less comfortable for the fort walk and a desert night — ask us whether your dates work.

Desert Festival

Usually late January or February, on the dunes — dates are announced by Rajasthan Tourism each year.

Border advice, temple etiquette and your visa

Jaisalmer district borders Pakistan. The UK FCDO advises against all travel within 10 km of the India–Pakistan border, which is unmarked in places (advice as of Oct 2026 — check before you travel). Jaisalmer town and the Sam dunes are well away from that line, and our routes stay well away from it. The Jain temples are active places of worship: dress modestly, remove shoes, and follow the rules at the entrance (leather items may not be allowed — ask at the gate). Foreign visitors pay separate entry fees in Indian rupees; fees and timings change, so check locally or ask us (as of Nov 2026). For your visa: apply only at the official site indianvisaonline.gov.in — look-alike sites charge extra. We are not a visa agency. In November 2026 the Government of India announced that the Tourist Visa is being renamed the Visitor Visa (PIB, 11 Nov 2026); until the official site changes, apply for the visa it shows there.

FAQ

Questions travellers ask

Is Jaisalmer worth the long trip?

For most travellers, yes. Its golden sandstone fort is part of the UNESCO Hill Forts of Rajasthan (2013) and is still lived in, and a sunset at the dunes is a highlight of many India trips. To save a long road day, fly into Jaisalmer or out of it.

How many nights do you need in Jaisalmer?

Two nights suit most visitors: one in town for the fort, the Jain temples, the havelis and Gadisar Lake, and optionally one at a desert camp.

How far are the Sam Sand Dunes from Jaisalmer?

About 40 km (25 miles). Most visitors drive out in the afternoon for sunset and either return to town or stay the night at a camp.

Can you still go to the Sam dunes at night?

Since October 2026 the forest department has not allowed visitors on the dunes inside Desert National Park after sunset. Many camps and sunset camel rides are outside the park and carry on as before — rules can change, so we confirm them for your dates.

Is a desert camp comfortable?

It depends on the camp — quality varies a lot. Check for proper toilets and showers, heating or heavy blankets in December–January, and distance from the road. We choose the camp for your dates and comfort level.

When is the best time to visit Jaisalmer?

November to March. From April to June the town can pass 45 °C (113 °F), and desert nights in December–January can drop to about 5 °C (41 °F).

Is Jaisalmer close to the Pakistan border — is it safe?

Jaisalmer district borders Pakistan, and the UK FCDO advises against all travel within 10 km of the border, which is unmarked in places (as of Oct 2026 — check before you travel). Jaisalmer town and the Sam dunes are well away from that line, and our routes stay well away from it.

Can I combine Jaisalmer with the Golden Triangle?

Yes. Many guests add Jodhpur and Jaisalmer after Delhi, Agra and Jaipur. The Golden Triangle is from US$299 (4 days) or US$449 (6 days) per person; the Rajasthan extension is quoted on request.
