What Orchha is — and why it is worth a night

Orchha was founded in the 16th century by the Bundela chief Rudra Pratap Singh, on a stretch of land along the Betwa River in what is now Madhya Pradesh. For the next century his successors filled it with palaces, temples and a row of royal cenotaphs by the water.

Today it is a small, quiet town. The palaces and temples stand within walking distance of each other, which makes Orchha an easy, calm stop after the crowds of Agra and before the temples of Khajuraho.

One thing many websites get wrong: "The historic ensemble of Orchha" is on India's UNESCO World Heritage Tentative List (submitted 2019) — it is not (yet) a World Heritage Site. Khajuraho, a day's drive further on, has been a World Heritage Site since 1986.

What you see in Orchha

Raja Mahal

The royal palace inside the fort complex. Construction started in the 16th century and was finished by later rulers, mainly Madhukar Shah.

Jahangir Mahal

Built in the 17th century by the Bundela ruler Veer Singh Deo to honour a visit by the Mughal emperor Jahangir.

Ram Raja Temple

The only temple in India where Rama is honoured as a king rather than as a god, with a ceremonial guard. Open from sunrise to sunset every day.

Chaturbhuj Temple

A tall 16th-century temple dedicated to Vishnu in the heart of Orchha.

Laxmi Narayan Temple

Built by Veer Singh Deo in the early 17th century.

The chhatris

A row of royal cenotaphs (chhatris) of the Bundela rulers on the bank of the Betwa at Kanchan Ghat, from the 16th and 17th centuries. Many visitors come here at sunset.

Beyond the palaces: river, sanctuary and the evening show

Sound and light show

Held every evening at the fort (MP Tourism). Ask us or check locally for the time and language on your date.

Rafting on the Betwa

MP Tourism runs river rafting from Kanchan Ghat to Shiva Ghat, past the chhatris. It is seasonal — ask locally before you plan around it.

Orchha sanctuary

A sanctuary of about 46 sq km (18 sq mi) along the Betwa, good for a quiet walk and birdwatching away from the monuments.

When to go to Orchha

October to March

The comfortable season for walking the palaces. Winter nights can fall to about 13 °C (55 °F) — bring a warm layer.

April to June

April to June is very hot — temperatures can pass 40 °C (104 °F). If you have a heart condition or are sensitive to heat, consult your doctor; see CDC or NHS Fit for Travel.

July to September

The monsoon turns the river country green, but it is humid and some outdoor plans depend on the weather.

Festivals

Ram Navami (March or April) and Ram Vivah (around November–December) bring big crowds to the Ram Raja temple. Dates follow the Hindu calendar — ask us for the dates in your year.

Temple etiquette, fees and your visa

The Ram Raja temple is an active place of worship: dress modestly, remove shoes, and follow the rules on photography at the gate. Foreign visitors pay separate entry fees at the monuments, in Indian rupees; fees and timings change, so check locally or ask us before you go (as of Nov 2026). For your visa: apply only at the official site indianvisaonline.gov.in — look-alike sites charge extra. We are not a visa agency. In November 2026 the Government of India announced that the Tourist Visa is being renamed the Visitor Visa (PIB, 11 Nov 2026); until the official site changes, apply for the visa it shows there.

FAQ

Questions travellers ask

Is Orchha worth visiting?

For travellers who like history and a slower pace, yes. Orchha has 16th- and 17th-century Bundela palaces, the Ram Raja temple and a row of royal cenotaphs on the Betwa River, all within walking distance in a small, quiet town. It fits naturally between Agra and Khajuraho.

How many days do you need in Orchha?

One or two nights. One full day covers the Raja Mahal, Jahangir Mahal, the Ram Raja and Chaturbhuj temples and the chhatris at sunset; a second night adds the sanctuary, the river or simply a slower pace.

How do I get to Orchha?

Many visitors take a train to Jhansi Junction and drive the last 16–17 km (about 10 miles). Orchha also has a small station of its own. If you are touring by private car, Orchha is a stop on the road from Agra to Khajuraho.

How far is Orchha from Khajuraho?

About 172 km (107 miles) by road, according to MP Tourism. Many travellers drive it in a day and see the Khajuraho temples in the late-afternoon light.

Is Orchha a UNESCO World Heritage Site?

Not yet. Orchha is on India's UNESCO World Heritage Tentative List (submitted 2019) — it is not (yet) a World Heritage Site. Nearby Khajuraho is a World Heritage Site, inscribed in 1986.

What is special about the Ram Raja temple?

It is the only temple in India where Rama is honoured as a king rather than as a god, with a ceremonial guard. It is an active place of worship: dress modestly, remove shoes and follow the photography rules at the gate.

When is the best time to visit Orchha?

October to March is the comfortable season. April to June is very hot, often above 40 °C (104 °F), and the monsoon (July–September) is green but humid.

Can I combine Orchha with the Golden Triangle?

Yes. Many guests add Orchha and Khajuraho after Delhi, Agra and Jaipur, then go on to Varanasi. We arrange it by private car with driver; send us your dates for a day-by-day plan and a quote.
