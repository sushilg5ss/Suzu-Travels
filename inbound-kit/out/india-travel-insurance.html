What the UK, US and Australian governments advise

India's official e-Visa pages don't list insurance as an entry requirement, but every major government we checked tells travellers to buy cover.

UK Foreign Office

"Get appropriate travel insurance. Insurance should cover your itinerary, planned activities and expenses in an emergency." Its India health page asks for cover "for local treatment or unexpected medical evacuation" — "particularly important if you have a health condition or are pregnant".

US State Department

"U.S. Medicare and Medicaid do not pay for medical care outside the United States." "The U.S. government does not pay medical costs for U.S. citizens traveling abroad." It advises asking your own health insurer whether you're covered abroad, and recommends medical evacuation insurance for places with limited medical care.

Australia (Smartraveller)

"Our travel advice levels can affect your travel insurance cover." "Standard travel insurance policies are unlikely to cover destinations with a Level 4 'Do not travel' advisory."

Why it matters in India

The UK Foreign Office notes that medical facilities differ from those in the UK, "especially in more remote areas", and that specialised treatment may not be available outside major cities — many of the best India trips go to exactly those mountain areas.

The three kinds of cover, in plain words

The US CDC's Yellow Book (2026 edition) splits travel insurance into three parts. A single policy may include all three — or only one.

Travel disruptionTrip cancellation, lost baggage and similar lossesIt may not cover medical costs at all
Travel health insuranceShort-term cover for healthcare costs while you're abroadPre-existing conditions treated in the 90 days before departure are often excluded

What to check in your policy for an India trip

Six questions to put to your insurer, with your day-by-day plan in hand.

The six things to check, in order. Send your insurer the day-by-day plan we give you — it lists every place, altitude and activity, so they can confirm in writing what is covered.

  1. Medical treatmentHospital care in India, and whether it is paid directly or reimbursed after you pay. The US State Department suggests a policy with a 24-hour help line.
  2. Medical evacuationTransfer to a bigger hospital or a flight home if needed. This is the part governments stress most for remote areas.
  3. Pre-existing conditionsDeclare them to the insurer before you buy, and check the policy covers your current medical conditions.
  4. Altitude and activitiesIf your plan includes high places like Ladakh or Spiti, river rafting in Rishikesh, skiing in Gulmarg or paragliding, check each is named as covered. Limits are policy-specific.
  5. Places under travel adviceSome policies exclude areas your government advises against. Check every place on your itinerary against current advice (see Kashmir below).
  6. Trip cancellationOur 20% deposit is fully refunded only within 48 hours of paying. After 48 hours, our written cancellation terms apply — cancellation cover is what protects you from there.

Kashmir, Ladakh and travel advisories

The UK Foreign Office says your travel insurance could be invalidated if you travel against its advice (advice updated 7 October 2026). Check current advice for every place on your itinerary.

Today the UK advises against all travel to most of Jammu and Kashmir (with exceptions, including Jammu city by air), and the US State Department rates Jammu and Kashmir Level 4 except eastern Ladakh and Leh, while India overall is Level 2. Australia's Smartraveller says standard policies are unlikely to cover Level 4 destinations. So a Kashmir trip may not be covered by a standard policy — check with your insurer before you book, and read our Kashmir seasons page for the dated advisory details.

Ladakh and Leh are treated differently from the Kashmir valley in these advisories, but they are high-altitude places: the UK Foreign Office lists altitude sickness as a risk in parts of India. Consult your doctor or a travel-health clinic before you travel; see NHS Fit for Travel, TravelHealthPro or CDC Travelers' Health.

Travelling from the US: Medicare and your own health plan

Medicare abroad

The US State Department: "U.S. Medicare and Medicaid do not pay for medical care outside the United States." The CDC adds that original Medicare does not cover medical costs incurred outside the United States.

Medigap plans

The CDC notes some Medigap plans cover emergency care abroad in the first 60 days of a trip, with a deductible and a lifetime cap. Check your own plan's terms.

Your employer or private plan

"Check with your U.S. health insurance provider and ask if they cover emergency and routine medical care while abroad" (State Department, updated 11 Aug 2025).

FAQ

Questions travellers ask

Do I need travel insurance for India?

India's official e-Visa pages don't list it as a requirement, but the UK, US and Australian governments all advise cover for medical treatment and medical evacuation. The UK Foreign Office says insurance should cover your itinerary, planned activities and emergency expenses.

Does the Indian e-Visa require travel insurance?

Insurance is not listed among the e-Tourist visa requirements on the official pages we checked. Apply yourself at the official site indianvisaonline.gov.in; beware look-alike sites that charge extra.

Does my US health insurance or Medicare work in India?

The US State Department says: "U.S. Medicare and Medicaid do not pay for medical care outside the United States." Some Medigap plans cover emergency care abroad for the first 60 days (CDC). Ask your own health insurer whether you're covered abroad.

What should travel insurance for India cover?

Medical treatment, medical evacuation, any pre-existing conditions you declare, the altitudes and activities on your plan (rafting, skiing, paragliding), every place on your itinerary under current travel advice, and trip cancellation.

Will travel insurance cover a trip to Kashmir?

It may not under a standard policy. The UK advises against all travel to most of Jammu and Kashmir and says insurance could be invalidated if you travel against advice; Smartraveller says standard policies are unlikely to cover Level 4 areas. Check with your insurer.

Does insurance cover altitude in Ladakh or Spiti, or adventure sports?

It depends on the policy — limits on altitude and activities differ. Check that each activity and high-altitude stay on your plan is named as covered. For health questions, consult your doctor or a travel-health clinic.

How does Suzu's 48-hour refund work with travel insurance?

Your 20% deposit is fully refunded if you cancel within 48 hours of paying. After 48 hours, our written cancellation terms apply — cancellation cover is what protects you from there.

What number do I call in an emergency in India?

Dial 112 and ask for an ambulance (UK Foreign Office). Also save your insurer's 24-hour assistance number and your embassy's number before you travel.
