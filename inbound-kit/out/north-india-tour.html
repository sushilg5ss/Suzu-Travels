Which North India route fits you?

Start from what you most want to see, then from how many days you have.

Short on time, first visit

The Golden Triangle in 4 or 6 days: Delhi, the Taj Mahal in Agra and Jaipur's forts. The Taj Mahal is closed on Fridays and opens 30 minutes before sunrise, so we build the days around it. See the Golden Triangle tour.

Yoga, rivers and temples

+ Rishikesh & Haridwar in 8 days. Rishikesh is often called the yoga capital of the world; Jolly Grant airport near Dehradun is about 20 km away. Our Rishikesh guide explains how to choose a retreat.

Mountains, snow and the toy train

+ Himalayas in 12 days: Shimla is about 370 km (230 mi) from Delhi and Manali 260 km (162 mi) beyond. Ride the UNESCO Kalka–Shimla toy train when it is running. We are a Himachal company — this is home.

Sikh and Tibetan India

Golden Temple & Himalayas in 10 days: the Golden Temple (Sri Harmandir Sahib) at the heart of Sikhism, where visitors can join the Langar community meal; then McLeod Ganj, seat of the Tibetan Government in exile, and Manali.

Lakes and meadows

+ Kashmir in 10 days: Dal Lake, Gulmarg and Pahalgam. Read the UK and US government advice in the section below before you choose this route.

When each route works best

North India spans desert, river plains and the Himalaya, so the best month depends on the route.

Part of the routeBest monthsWatch out for
Delhi, Agra, Jaipur and RajasthanOctober to MarchMay is very hot in Rajasthan (highs of 43–46 °C / 109–115 °F). The monsoon reaches Delhi around late June and withdraws around late September.
Shimla and ManaliMarch to June and September to November; snow December to FebruaryShimla gets about 40 cm (16 in) of snow in each of January and February; nights near 0 °C (32 °F). Rohtang Pass closes with snow from late November until about May — the Atal Tunnel keeps Manali connected.
Rishikesh, Amritsar, DharamshalaOutside the monsoonThe UK FCDO notes the monsoon usually runs from June to October, with landslides in the hills; we plan daytime drives and avoid fog.

Before you choose the Kashmir route

The UK FCDO advises against all travel to Jammu & Kashmir (including Srinagar, Gulmarg and Pahalgam) and the US State Department rates it ‘Level 4: Do not travel’ (FCDO checked 7 Nov 2026; US State Department checked 25 Oct 2026). Your travel insurance may not cover you. Read your government's advice before you choose this route.

If you would rather not go to Kashmir, the Golden Temple & Himalayas route or the Golden Triangle + Himalayas route give you snow, mountain valleys and McLeod Ganj, home of the Dalai Lama, in Himachal Pradesh instead. Our Kashmir guide has the full advisory detail.

FAQ

Questions travellers ask

Which North India tour is best for a first visit?

If you have about a week, the classic 6-day Golden Triangle (Delhi, Agra, Jaipur). If you have 12 days, add the Himalayas — Shimla and Manali — for a complete contrast of Mughal monuments and mountains.

How many days do I need for North India?

Four days is the minimum for the Golden Triangle. Allow 8 days to add Rishikesh and Haridwar, and 10–12 days to add Amritsar, Dharamshala or the Himalayas.

When is the best time for a North India tour?

October to March for Delhi, Agra and Rajasthan. For Shimla and Manali, March to June and September to November, or December to February if you want snow. The monsoon runs roughly late June to late September in Delhi.

How much does a North India tour cost?

Our published private routes start from US$299 per person for 4 days and go up to US$1,099 for 12 days, two sharing, with 3-star hotels, breakfast and a private car and driver. Longer or upgraded trips are quoted on request.

Are these private tours or group tours?

Private only. You have your own car and English-speaking driver for the whole trip, and you choose the start date.

How do I pay, and what if I cancel?

A 20% deposit locks your dates and price; it is fully refunded if you cancel within 48 hours of paying. The balance is due before the trip. You pay by card via Razorpay (international cards accepted) or PayPal.

Is Kashmir safe to visit now?

The UK FCDO advises against all travel to Jammu & Kashmir and the US State Department rates it Level 4: Do not travel (FCDO checked 7 Nov 2026). Travel insurance may not cover you. Read your government's advice first; our Himachal routes are an alternative.
