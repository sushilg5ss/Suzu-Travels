Most first-time solo travellers start on the busiest tourist circuit, where trains, cars and hotels are easiest to arrange, and then slow down in the foothills. This is the shape we suggest; shorten or stretch it to your dates.

How to get around India alone

Three ways to move between cities, plus the short hops in between. Many solo travellers mix them. Our trains vs flights guide compares the first two in detail.

Trains

You can book IRCTC's Foreign Tourist quota yourself with a foreign passport — up to 365 days ahead, paying by international card. Carry your original passport on board. Travel agents cannot book that quota for you.

Flights

For long distances — Delhi to Goa, Kerala or Ladakh — a domestic flight saves a day of travel. Book directly with the airline.

A private car and driver

Your own car and English-speaking driver from city to city: door to door, stops when you want them, and one place for your luggage. Your driver's name, photo and car number are shared with you before the trip. See private car with driver.

Taxis in town

The UK FCDO says app-based taxis like Uber and Ola are widely available, including in all airport terminal buildings, and that pre-paid taxis and hotel transfers are also available. It advises: “Avoid travelling alone and hailing taxis on the street, especially at night.”

Arriving

Australia's Smartraveller says: “Use a pre-paid taxi service when you arrive at the airport.” Easiest of all on a first trip: book your first night's hotel with an airport transfer before you fly.

Is it safe to travel India alone? What the governments say

Read your own government's advice before you book — this is a short summary, not a promise. The US State Department rates India Level 2: “Exercise increased caution in India due to crime and terrorism. Some areas have increased risk.” (issued 16 Jun 2025). Its country page adds: “The U.S. government advises against women traveling alone in India.” The UK FCDO (advice checked 19 Nov 2026) says: “Avoid travelling alone and hailing taxis on the street, especially at night” and “Be wary of accepting drinks or food from strangers or leaving drinks unattended.” Australia's Smartraveller asks you to “exercise a high degree of caution in India overall” and “don't hail taxis on the street.” Travelling alone as a woman? Read our dedicated guide: Is India safe for solo female travellers? Staying at an ashram or retreat? Choose retreats and hosts carefully and read recent independent reviews — Australia's Smartraveller warns of misconduct allegations involving some religious groups.

FAQ

Questions travellers ask

Is India good for a first solo trip?

Yes, if you start on the main tourist circuit — Delhi, Agra and Jaipur — where transport and hotels are easiest, then slow down in Rishikesh or the Himalayan foothills. Book your first night and airport transfer before you land.

Is it safe to travel India alone?

Read your own government's advice first. The US State Department rates India Level 2 (exercise increased caution, issued 16 Jun 2025); the UK FCDO advises avoiding travelling alone and hailing taxis on the street, especially at night. Women travelling alone should read our dedicated guide, Is India safe for solo female travellers?

Where should I go on my first solo trip to India?

Delhi, Agra (the Taj Mahal) and Jaipur for a week; add Rishikesh and McLeod Ganj in the Himalayan foothills for two weeks. All are on the main tourist circuit, so trains, cars and hotels are easy to arrange.

How do I get around India alone?

Trains (book IRCTC's Foreign Tourist quota yourself with your passport, up to 365 days ahead), domestic flights for long distances, or a private car with driver. In cities, the UK FCDO says app taxis like Uber and Ola are widely available.

Can I hire a private car and driver just for myself?

Yes — a private car with an English-speaking driver works for one traveller just as for a group. Your driver's name, photo and car number are shared with you before the trip. Ask us for a solo quote.

What phone numbers should I save in India?

112 for emergencies, 100 for police, 1091 for the women's helpline, and the Ministry of Tourism's 24×7 multilingual tourist helpline 1800-11-1363 (short code 1363). Also save your embassy's number.

Should I join a group tour or travel alone?

Both work. A group gives company and a fixed plan; going fully independent gives total freedom and more planning. A private car and driver sits in between: your own route and pace, with someone local on call.
