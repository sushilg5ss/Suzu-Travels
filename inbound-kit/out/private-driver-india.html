What's included — and what isn't

What a private-driver quote covers, in words: car and driver all days, airport pick-up and drop; hotels and monument guides are optional add-ons; tickets, meals and tips are your own.

Included

Car, fuel, tolls, parking, interstate taxes, the driver's food and stay, airport pick-up and drop. Tourist taxis carry an All India Tourist Vehicle permit, so one car can cross state lines for the whole route.

Not included

Hotels (we can add them to the same quote), monument tickets, licensed guides at monuments, meals, tips, flights and your visa.

Cars

Sedan for 2–3 travellers, SUV for 4–6, a 12-seat minibus for larger groups. We put hill routes in SUVs. Permit rules cap tourist cars at 12 years of age.

Drivers

English-speaking, experienced on hill roads. They are drivers, not guides — we add licensed local guides at the big monuments on request. Your driver's name, photo and car number are shared with you before the trip.

Roads, passes and seasons to know

In our experience hill roads average 30–40 km/h (20–25 mph), so we plan days around hours, not kilometres. The Rohtang Pass is closed from late November to around May; the Atal Tunnel below it stays open year-round. We avoid night driving and fog on hill roads — the UK Foreign Office advises the same for all of India — and we use well-maintained cars with working seat belts.

Kashmir: several governments currently advise against travel to Jammu and Kashmir (UK FCDO, 7 October 2026; US State Department Level 4, except Ladakh). We plan Kashmir road trips only when your own government's advice and your travel insurance allow it — check both before you ask us.

Why book the driver through a local company

We are a registered travel agent based in Himachal Pradesh, not a marketplace. The same small team that quotes your trip answers the phone while you travel, and the driver is accountable to us, not to an app.

You can drive yourself in India with an International Driving Permit and your home licence, but most visitors don't: traffic keeps left, livestock wander onto rural roads, and the hill roads reward someone who drives them every week. A good driver also handles the small things — parking, permits for the Rohtang road, where to stop for a clean lunch — so your days are about the places, not the logistics.

Questions travellers ask

How much does a private driver in India cost?

It depends on the car, the season and how many nights you travel, so we don't print a per-day rate. Send us your route and dates and we reply with a day-by-day plan and a quote in US dollars; a 20% deposit locks it, refundable within 48 hours.

Is it safe to hire a car and driver in India?

A car with a professional driver is how many international visitors travel North India. We use well-maintained cars with seat belts, avoid night driving and fog on hill roads (the UK Foreign Office advises the same), share your driver's name, photo and car number before the trip, and our office number works for the whole trip.

Can I drive myself in India instead?

Yes — you need an International Driving Permit (the 1949 version for UK licence holders) plus your home licence, and rules on how long you may drive on it vary by state. Most visitors still choose a driver because of left-hand traffic, livestock on rural roads and the hill driving.

Is the driver also a guide?

No. Drivers drive and help with logistics. For the Taj Mahal, the Jaipur forts or Delhi's monuments we add licensed local guides on request — quoted separately, so you only pay for the days you want one.

Where does the driver stay overnight?

Near your hotel, at his own arrangement — his food and stay are included in the price. You never pay the driver directly for anything on the route; fuel, tolls and parking are in the quote.

Do I tip the driver?

Tips are optional and given at the end of the trip if you were happy. We never add a service charge.

Can we change the route during the trip?

Yes, within the same region and number of days. Extra nights or kilometres are quoted on the spot, and the same car and driver continue with you.

Which day should we avoid for Agra?

Friday — the Taj Mahal is closed to general visitors on Fridays (it opens 30 minutes before sunrise on other days). We plan the Agra day around it; foreign visitors buy a separate ticket on the spot or online.
