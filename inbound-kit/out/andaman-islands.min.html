Do foreigners need a permit for the Andaman Islands?

Permits for foreigners: what the rules say in 2026

Older blogs still describe a permit stamped on arrival. The current notice from Andaman & Nicobar Tourism is simpler — read it in this order.

  1. Most islands you will visit need no RAPThe notice says foreign nationals 'are not required to obtain a Restricted Area Permit (RAP) for visiting the 29 islands' excluded from the permit regime. The official list includes South Andaman (with Sri Vijaya Puram), Swaraj Dweep (Havelock), Shaheed Dweep (Neil), Baratang, Long Island, Middle and North Andaman and Little Andaman.
  2. Eleven more islands for day visitsA further 11 islands are exempt from the permit for day visits only. The notice says this exemption 'is valid up to 31/12/2027'.
  3. Three nationalities still need a permit in advanceThe relaxation does not apply to citizens of Afghanistan, China (including Hong Kong and Macau) and Pakistan, or to people of that origin. They need a RAP approved by India's Ministry of Home Affairs before arrival — without one, 'entry shall be refused'. Citizens of Myanmar need a RAP for Mayabunder and Diglipur.
  4. Forests, sanctuaries and tribal reserves are separateReserved forests, wildlife sanctuaries and tribal reserves need their own approval, and so do islands that are not on the tourism list. Visits to tribal areas need a Tribal Pass from the Deputy Commissioner. Where a permit is needed, the application is made online at indianfrro.gov.in.
  5. Check before you flyRules can change — check the Andaman & Nicobar Tourism notice before you travel. Your Indian visa is separate: apply yourself at the official site indianvisaonline.gov.in — beware look-alike sites that charge extra. We are not a visa agency.

Rules every visitor should know

These come from the Andaman & Nicobar Tourism page for foreigners. Most protect the islands' reefs, forests and indigenous peoples.

No photos of tribal people

'Taking photographs of the Aboriginal Tribes … in the Tribal Reserves is not permitted. It is an offence punishable under PAT Regulation 1956.' Tribal reserves are closed to visitors without a Tribal Pass.

Off the beach by sunset

'Camping on sea beaches and sleeping in the jungle is strictly prohibited', and 'no one should remain on the sea beach and jungle after sunset.' Plan sunset photos, then head back.

Leave the coral where it is

Scuba diving and underwater photography are allowed in identified areas, but nothing — coral, fish or shells — may be removed from the Marine Park. Marine Park visits are by small or glass-bottom boats.

Driving a scooter?

You need a valid International Driving Licence, and helmets are compulsory for the rider and the pillion. Or let a car and driver take you around the island.

When to go: plan around the monsoon

The southwest monsoon reaches the islands before mainland India. India Meteorological Department (IMD) gives the normal date as 22 May (Kerala: 1 June); in 2026 it arrived on 16 May, six days early.

Beyond the beaches: the Cellular Jail

In Sri Vijaya Puram, the Cellular Jail was built by the British as a colonial prison and completed in 1906. Today it is dedicated to the nation as a National Memorial to the freedom fighters held there, and an evening light-and-sound show tells their story. Check the show times locally — they differ by language and day.

A good plan spends a day in the capital for the jail and the nearby beaches, then takes the ferry to Swaraj Dweep and Shaheed Dweep. Diving and snorkelling are possible in identified areas; choose a dive centre that shows you its certification, and talk to your doctor before you dive — see CDC or NHS Fit for Travel for India.

FAQ

Questions travellers ask

Do foreigners need a permit to visit the Andaman Islands?

Most don't. Under the current Andaman & Nicobar Tourism notice, foreign nationals need no Restricted Area Permit for 29 islands, including Swaraj Dweep (Havelock) and Shaheed Dweep (Neil), and 11 more islands are open for day visits until 31 December 2027. Check the notice before you travel — rules can change.

Which nationalities still need a Restricted Area Permit?

Citizens of Afghanistan, China (including Hong Kong and Macau) and Pakistan, and people of that origin, need a RAP approved by India's Ministry of Home Affairs before arrival. Citizens of Myanmar need one for Mayabunder and Diglipur. Applications are made online at indianfrro.gov.in.

Is Port Blair now called Sri Vijaya Puram?

Yes. The Government of India renamed Port Blair as Sri Vijaya Puram in September 2024. You will still see 'Port Blair' on older maps and some tickets — they are the same place, with the islands' airport.

What is the best time to visit the Andaman Islands?

Plan your beach days before the southwest monsoon, which normally reaches the islands around 22 May (IMD); in 2026 it arrived on 16 May. During the monsoon expect rain and rougher seas. Send us your dates and we check the current situation.

Can I use an Indian e-Visa for the Andamans?

Yes — Sri Vijaya Puram's Veer Savarkar International Airport is a designated e-Visa entry airport, and the main tourist islands need no separate permit for most nationalities. Apply yourself at the official site indianvisaonline.gov.in — beware look-alike sites that charge extra.

Can I go scuba diving in the Andamans?

Yes, in identified areas. Taking coral, fish or anything else from the Marine Park is not allowed. Choose a dive centre that shows you its certification and talk to your doctor before you dive.

Can tourists visit tribal areas or North Sentinel?

No. Tribal reserves are closed without a Tribal Pass from the Deputy Commissioner, and photographing the islands' Aboriginal tribes in the reserves is an offence under the PAT Regulation 1956. We never include tribal areas in a plan.

Can I combine the Andamans with the rest of India?

Yes — you can add the islands to a Golden Triangle, Kerala or Goa trip. We plan the whole route, with one contact from start to finish.
