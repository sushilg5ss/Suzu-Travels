How India celebrates Christmas

Christmas is a Government of India gazetted holiday, so 25 December is a day off for government offices across the country. India's Christian community is small in share but large in number: the 2011 Census — the latest published census — counted about 28 million Christians, 2.3% of the population (Hindus 79.8%, Muslims 14.2%, Sikhs 1.7%).

For a visitor, the easiest place to see Christmas as a living tradition is Goa, where Portuguese rule lasted for 450 years until 1961 and the great churches of Old Goa are still places of worship — UNESCO notes that ceremonies, prayers, weddings and funerals are held in them today. Kerala is the other classic choice for a warm Christmas. In the north, the Himachal hill towns of Shimla and Manali offer a cold, mountain Christmas with a chance of snow.

Churches are places of worship first. Dress modestly, keep quiet during services, and ask before photographing a service or people at prayer.

Old Goa's churches: a UNESCO World Heritage Site

The "Churches and Convents of Goa" were inscribed by UNESCO in 1986. Seven monuments make up the site:

Basilica of Bom Jesus (1605)

Holds the tomb of St Francis Xavier and was made a basilica in 1946. Incredible India lists weekday hours of 09:00–18:30 and Sundays from 10:30 — on 24 and 25 December services take priority, so check hours locally.

Sé Cathedral (1652)

Old Goa's cathedral — like the other churches here, still used for worship, so services come first.

St Francis of Assisi (1517)

The church and convent were rebuilt in 1521 and 1661. Nearby stand the Chapel of St Catherine (1510) and the Chapel of St Cajetan (1661).

Our Lady of the Rosary (1549) and St Augustine (1602)

St Augustine survives as ruins. The whole site lies about 10 km (6 miles) east of Panaji, Goa's capital.

Will it snow at Christmas? An honest answer

We are based in Himachal, so we hear this every December. Snow at Christmas is never guaranteed. In Shimla, December averages about 7 cm of snow (IMD); January and February are much snowier. The India Meteorological Department's long-term figures for Shimla:

Where to spend New Year's Eve in India

We plan your route, car and hotels; we don't sell party or event tickets. Three kinds of New Year's Eve that work:

By the sea in Goa

Warm nights and the beaches at their liveliest. Book your hotel as early as you can, and swim only where lifeguard flags show — never where a red flag flies.

In the mountains

A cold, clear night in Shimla, Manali or Dharamshala, with nights around 0 °C (32 °F) or below. Pack warm layers and allow for snow on the roads.

Somewhere quiet

Kerala's backwaters and tea hills, or a heritage hotel in Rajasthan, for a calmer start to the year. See our India in December guide for the weather region by region.

Crowds, fog and air: what to plan around

Book early

Christmas to New Year (roughly 20 December – 5 January) is one of India's busiest travel periods. Hotels in Goa, Shimla and Manali fill early and many charge peak-season rates — book as early as you can. Our Christmas–New Year trips are quoted on request.

Fog

The UK FCDO advises: "Avoid travelling at night and in fog as accidents are common due to low visibility" (advice checked 21 Oct 2026). In winter we plan daytime drives and keep buffer time around early flights.

Air quality

The UK FCDO says: "Severe air pollution is a major health hazard, especially during the winter months from October to February" (checked 16 Nov 2026). If you have a heart or lung condition, consult your doctor; see CDC or NHS Fit for Travel.

Visa

Apply only at the official site indianvisaonline.gov.in — beware look-alike sites that charge extra. We are not a visa agency. In November 2026 the Government of India announced that the Tourist Visa is being renamed the Visitor Visa (PIB, 11 Nov 2026); until the official site changes, apply for the visa it shows there. Kashmir at Christmas

Gulmarg is popular for winter snow, but the UK FCDO advises against all travel to Jammu and Kashmir except Jammu city, and the US State Department says "Do not travel" to J&K except eastern Ladakh and Leh. Check the latest before you book.

FAQ

Questions travellers ask

Is Christmas celebrated in India?

Yes. Christmas Day, 25 December, is a national (gazetted) holiday. About 2.3% of Indians — some 28 million people, Census 2011 — are Christian. Goa and Kerala are the easiest places for a visitor to see Christmas celebrated in churches and communities.

Is Christmas a public holiday in India?

Yes. 25 December is on the Government of India's list of gazetted holidays. New Year's Day, 1 January, is a normal working day for most offices, and most sights are open.

Where is the best place to spend Christmas in India?

For a Christian Christmas with warm weather, Goa — midnight Mass in Old Goa's UNESCO churches and the beaches — or Kerala. For a cold mountain Christmas with a chance of snow, Shimla or Manali in Himachal. The Golden Triangle works all season.

Will it snow in Shimla or Manali at Christmas?

Maybe, but it is never guaranteed. Shimla averages about 7 cm of snow in December (IMD) against about 40 cm in January and February. Manali is higher and colder, and Solang Nullah usually has winter snow. If snow matters most, come in January.

Is Goa crowded at Christmas and New Year?

Yes. Christmas to New Year is one of India's busiest travel periods, and Goa's hotels fill early, many at peak-season rates. Book as early as you can.

Where can I spend New Year's Eve in India?

Goa's beaches for a lively night, the Himachal hill towns for a cold mountain one, or Kerala's backwaters for a quiet one. We plan the route and hotels; we don't sell party tickets.

Are sights open on 25 December and 1 January?

Most monuments are open, and churches are busy with services. But both 25 December 2026 and 1 January 2027 are Fridays, when the Taj Mahal is closed to visitors — plan Agra for another day.

How early should I book a Christmas trip to India?
