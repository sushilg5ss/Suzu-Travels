The route: a loop through the Himachal hills, never the same road twice

Himachal's hill towns sit in different valleys, and mountain roads are slow — so this plan gives every hill drive a full day and never doubles back. Distances below are the official ones from the state and district tourism sites; where no official figure exists (Manali to Dharamshala, Dharamshala to Dalhousie), we simply say "a full day".

Delhi → Kalka → Shimla (toy train) → Manali → Dharamshala and McLeod Ganj → Dalhousie and Khajjiar → Pathankot. You go up by rail and leave from the far side of the state, so no day is spent driving back.

Ten days in Himachal, day by day

A sample we build from. Swap the order, add a night in Manali, finish at the Golden Temple in Amritsar — the quote changes, the pace stays human.

  1. DAY 1
    Arrive in Delhi

    Your driver meets you at Delhi airport — name, photo and car number are shared with you before the trip. A quiet evening to get over the flight; the mountains start tomorrow.

  2. DAY 2
    Delhi → Kalka → toy train to Shimla

    An early train or drive north to Kalka, then the narrow-gauge Kalka–Shimla railway: 96 km (60 mi) of curves, bridges and tunnels, part of UNESCO's "Mountain Railways of India" since 2008. Your car carries the luggage and meets you at Shimla. Prefer the road? Shimla is 370 km (230 mi) from Delhi — a full day. More in our toy train guide.

  3. DAY 3
    Shimla

    The town on its ridge: the Mall and the Ridge on foot, Christ Church, and a walk up to the Jakhu temple if you have the legs for it. An easy day at altitude before the longer drives.

  4. DAY 4
    Shimla → Manali

    260 km (162 mi) north through the Kullu valley — a full day with stops. We leave after breakfast and arrive before dark.

  5. DAY 5
    Manali: Solang and the Atal Tunnel

    Solang valley is 13 km (8 mi) from Manali. When the road is open, the 9.02 km Atal Tunnel, at about 3,000 m (9,800 ft), takes you through to Sissu in Lahaul, which it keeps connected to Manali all year.

  6. DAY 6
    Manali: Old Manali, Hadimba, or Rohtang in season

    A slower day: Old Manali, the Hadimba temple, cafés by the river. From May to November you can instead ride up to Rohtang Pass, 51 km (32 mi) away — it needs a vehicle permit and is closed by heavy snow from late November until May.

  7. DAY 7
    Manali → Dharamshala

    A full-day drive west into the Kangra valley, into the foothills of the Dhauladhar range. Check in at McLeod Ganj, above Dharamshala.

  8. DAY 8
    McLeod Ganj

    At 2,082 m (6,831 ft), McLeod Ganj is the seat of the Tibetan Government in exile and the Dalai Lama's home since 1959. The main temple, the monasteries, Bhagsu, and Tibetan food in the evening. Our McLeod Ganj guide has the details.

  9. DAY 9
    Dharamshala → Dalhousie and Khajjiar

    A full-day drive to Dalhousie, the hill station founded by Lord Dalhousie in 1850 as a summer retreat, with its Mall Road and old churches. Then about 22–24 km to Khajjiar, a saucer-shaped meadow at about 1,980 m (6,500 ft) ringed by forest. If the drive runs long, we move Khajjiar to the morning of Day 10.

  10. DAY 10
    Down to the plains and home

    To Pathankot, 87 km (54 mi), for the train to Delhi; or to Amritsar, 199 km (124 mi), to fly or to add the Golden Temple; or to Kangra (Gaggal) airport, 110 km (68 mi). We time the drive around your train or flight, with a margin.

When to go — and what we change in each season

The route works most of the year, but not the same way every month. Our full month-by-month table is on the best time to visit Himachal; here is what it means for this trip.

March – June: go

District Shimla calls summer the best season to visit, with days of 15–25 °C (59–77 °F). Himachal Tourism says the same for Dalhousie and Khajjiar. Rohtang reopens by May.

September – November: go

Clear skies after the rains and the best mountain views of the year. From late November Rohtang Pass closes for the winter; Solang and the Atal Tunnel to Sissu take its place on Day 6.

December – February: the snow version

Very cold — in December and January Shimla's nights are around 0 °C (32 °F), with about 40 cm (16 in) of snow in January and February. Snow is likely, never guaranteed. We keep the same order, add a buffer day, and if heavy snow closes the road to Dalhousie we re-route through the Kangra valley.

July – August: we don't recommend it

The monsoon. Shimla gets 346 mm (13.6 in) of rain in July. The UK Foreign Office says the monsoon usually runs from June to October and that flash flooding and landslides can occur with limited warning. If these are your only dates, ask us for a lower-risk plan.

Mountain roads: why we drive in daylight only

The UK Foreign Office advises: "Avoid travelling at night and in fog as accidents are common due to low visibility." Mountain roads are narrow and the weather changes fast, so we never drive the hills after dark, and we plan full days for drives that look short on a map.

Practical notes for Himachal

Visa

Apply yourself for the India e-Visa at the official site indianvisaonline.gov.in — beware look-alike sites that charge extra. We are not a visa agency; our e-Visa guide walks you through it.

Permits

Foreigners need no special permit for any stop on this route. Protected-area permits apply only further east — parts of Kinnaur and Spiti — which this itinerary does not enter. Rohtang needs a vehicle permit, which we arrange if you choose it.

Altitude and health

The overnight stops are modest — McLeod Ganj, at 2,082 m (6,831 ft), is the highest. If you have heart or breathing conditions, consult your doctor before you go and see an official travel-health site (CDC, NHS Fit for Travel).

Flying instead

Manali's nearest airport is Bhuntar, 50 km (31 mi) away; Dharamshala's is Kangra (Gaggal), 14 km (9 mi). Both are small and hill weather can delay or cancel flights, so we keep a road plan ready. More in our Kullu–Manali airport guide.

FAQ

Questions travellers ask

Is 10 days enough for Himachal Pradesh?

Yes, for the classic loop: Shimla, Manali, Dharamshala and McLeod Ganj, Dalhousie and Khajjiar. Hill drives take a full day each, so 10 days gives you rest days in Manali and McLeod Ganj. Spiti or Kinnaur need another week.

Should I go Shimla first or Dharamshala first?

Either works. Shimla first lets you go up on the toy train from Kalka and leave from Pathankot, Amritsar or Kangra at the far end. Dharamshala first suits travellers flying into Amritsar or Kangra. We never plan the same road twice.

What is the best time to visit Himachal?

March to June and September to November. District Shimla calls summer the best season, with days of 15–25 °C (59–77 °F). December to February is cold with likely snow. We don't recommend July and August: the UK Foreign Office warns of monsoon flash floods and landslides.

Will I see snow in Himachal?

Most likely in January and February — Shimla averages about 40 cm (16 in) of snow in each. Nothing is guaranteed. Rohtang Pass is closed from late November to May; when the road is open, the Atal Tunnel takes you from Manali through to Sissu in Lahaul.

Is the Kalka–Shimla toy train worth it?

For most visitors, yes: 96 km (60 mi) of UNESCO-listed mountain railway, slow and scenic. Your car carries the bags and meets you at Shimla. Book early in peak months; our toy train guide explains how.

Do foreigners need permits for this route?

No. Protected-area permits apply only to parts of Kinnaur and Spiti, which this itinerary does not enter. Rohtang Pass needs a vehicle permit, which we arrange if you choose that day.

Is it safe to drive the mountain roads?

The roads are narrow and the weather changes fast. The UK Foreign Office advises avoiding night travel and fog, so we drive in daylight only, with an experienced hill driver, and plan full days for each hill drive.
