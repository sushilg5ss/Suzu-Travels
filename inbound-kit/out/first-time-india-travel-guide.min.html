What should I know before visiting India for the first time?

For a first trip to India: apply for the e-Visa only at indianvisaonline.gov.in, at least 4 days before you arrive; go between October and March for the north; start with Delhi, Agra and Jaipur plus one region; travel by private car with driver or reserved trains, by day; book tickets only officially; and book with a registered company on clear deposit terms.

Getting around: car with driver, trains and taxis

Distances are long and roads are busy, so first-timers do best with transport booked before they land.

Private car with driver

The easiest way for a first trip: the same car and English-speaking driver from the airport to your last day, daytime drives, stops when you want. Your driver's name, photo and car number are shared with you before the trip. See our private car with driver.

Trains

Indian Railways keeps a Foreign Tourist Quota you can book online up to 365 days ahead, with each traveller's passport number and an international card. Other travellers can book only 60 days ahead (since 1 Nov 2024), so the quota helps in busy months.

Taxis in cities

Use app taxis (the FCDO notes Uber and Ola are widely available, including at airports) or a pre-arranged car — not taxis hailed on the street at night.

Mixing both

Many guests take a train for one long leg and a car for the rest. Tell us your route and we suggest the simplest mix.

Money, phones and health: the practical basics

Money

Carry an international card and some cash. UPI payment options for visitors exist — check the official NPCI page for current details before you rely on them.

Health

Talk to your doctor or a travel clinic well before you go, and see CDC (US) or NHS Fit for Travel (UK). We don't give medical advice. Most visitors stick to bottled or filtered water — ask your doctor what suits you.

Yellow fever

If you arrive from or through a country with yellow-fever risk, you need a yellow-fever vaccination certificate (official e-Visa site).

Heat and cold

April to June is very hot on the plains, and December–January nights are cold in the north. See the best time to visit India for temperatures by month.

FAQ

Questions travellers ask

What should I know before going to India for the first time?

Apply for the e-Visa yourself at indianvisaonline.gov.in at least 4 days before arrival, travel between October and March for the north, keep your first route simple (Delhi, Agra, Jaipur plus one region), travel by day, buy tickets only officially, and book with a registered company on clear deposit terms.

Where should I go on my first trip to India?

Most first-timers start with the Golden Triangle — Delhi, Agra and Jaipur — and add one more region: the Himalayan hill towns, Rajasthan, Kerala or Goa. Our India in 2 weeks itinerary shows how it fits together.

How many days do I need in India?

For a first trip to North India, 10 to 14 days is comfortable: about a week for Delhi, Agra and Jaipur, and the rest for one more region. The Golden Triangle alone takes four to six days.

Is India safe for first-time visitors?

The US State Department rates India Level 2 – Exercise increased caution (issued 16 Jun 2025), with some areas at Level 4. The UK FCDO warns about scammers and touts in tourist areas, and advises avoiding night and fog travel and street taxis at night. We plan daytime drives and pre-arranged transport. Check the current advice before you travel.

How do I get an Indian visa?

Apply yourself, online, only at the official site indianvisaonline.gov.in — beware look-alike sites that charge extra. Apply at least 4 days before arrival with a passport valid for six months. We are not a visa agency; our e-Visa guide explains each step.

What is the best time to visit India for the first time?

October to March, when Delhi, Agra, Jaipur and Rajasthan are dry and mild and Kerala and Goa are in season. The monsoon usually runs from June to October.

Should I hire a car and driver or take trains?

Either works, and many guests mix them. A private car with driver is the simplest for a first trip; trains can be booked online up to 365 days ahead under the Foreign Tourist Quota with your passport number.

How do I avoid scams in India?

Apply for your visa only on the official site, buy monument and train tickets only from official sites or counters, ignore offers of tickets and tours at stations (as the UK FCDO advises), use app taxis or pre-arranged cars, and pay a company only against a written quote and clear terms.
