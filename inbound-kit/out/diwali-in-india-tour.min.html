What Diwali is — and what you will see

Diwali, or Deepavali, is the Festival of Lights. Incredible India describes it as the triumph of light over darkness and good over evil, remembering the return of Lord Rama after his 14-year exile. For a visitor it looks like this:

Lamps and rangoli

Homes, streets and temples are lined with diyas — small clay oil lamps — and doorsteps are decorated with rangoli, patterns of coloured powder that welcome good fortune.

Busy, glowing bazaars

In the days before Diwali the markets are at their liveliest: lights, sweets, fabrics and gifts. Johri Bazar in Jaipur and the Diwali Bazaar at Dilli Haat in Delhi are among the places Incredible India lists.

Lakshmi puja and fireworks

On the main night families pray to Lakshmi, the goddess of prosperity, and to Ganesha. After dark the sky fills with fireworks — loud, bright and late.

More than one faith

Jains remember Lord Mahavira's nirvana, and Sikhs mark Bandi Chhor Divas, honouring Guru Har Gobind Ji's release — the Golden Temple in Amritsar is a moving place to be that week.

Where to see Diwali

Diwali is celebrated all over India. Incredible India names these places among them — here is how each fits a first trip:

Jaipur

The Pink City's bazaars, Johri Bazar among them, are strung with lights. Easy to reach on a Golden Triangle route: Agra to Jaipur is 232 km (144 mi) by road.

Udaipur

Lake Pichola and the City Palace reflect the lamps across the water. Incredible India gives September to March as the best time to visit Udaipur.

Varanasi

Dashashwamedh Ghat on the Ganga. About fifteen days after Diwali, Varanasi lights its ghats again for Dev Deepawali — a second festival of lamps if you stay longer (we confirm the 2027 date).

Ayodhya and Delhi

Ayodhya, linked to the story of Rama, is one of the listed venues; in Delhi the festive Diwali Bazaar at Dilli Haat is an easy evening out. In the south, Mysore Palace and Meenakshi Temple in Madurai are listed too — we arrange those regions.

Crowds, smoke and alcohol: what the UK government says

The UK Foreign Office (updated 7 October 2026) asks travellers to take particular care during periods of national or religious significance, such as Diwali (usually in October or November), notes that stampedes have occurred at large religious gatherings, and that there is often a ban on the sale of alcohol during major religious festivals. It also warns that severe air pollution is a major health hazard, especially from October to February, with North Indian cities most affected — and Diwali fireworks add smoke for a night or two. If you are pregnant or have a heart or breathing condition, consult your doctor before you travel; see CDC or NHS Fit for Travel. We keep you out of the densest crowds and can plan the days after Diwali in quieter places.

Before you go

A few practical points for a Diwali trip:

e-Visa

Apply only at the official site indianvisaonline.gov.in — beware look-alike sites that charge extra. We are not a visa agency.

Respect

Diwali is a religious and family festival. Join a puja only when invited, take your shoes off where others do, and ask before photographing people at prayer.

Noise and late nights

Fireworks can go on late, especially on Diwali night. Light sleepers should ask us for a hotel away from the busiest streets.

What to pack

Bring a light layer for the evenings and one dressy outfit — you may be invited to a family celebration or a hotel's Diwali evening.

FAQ

Questions travellers ask

When is Diwali 2027?

Friday 29 October 2027, per the Government of India's 2027 holiday list. Naraka Chaturdasi is Thursday 28 October, Govardhan Puja Saturday 30 October and Bhai Duj Sunday 31 October. Diwali follows the lunar calendar, so it moves every year.

What happens on the five days of Diwali?

Incredible India describes them as: cleaning homes and Dhanteras shopping; lighting diyas and drawing rangoli; Lakshmi puja on the main night; gifts and sweets with family; and Bhai Dooj for brothers and sisters. Fireworks fill the main night.

Is the Taj Mahal open on Diwali 2027?

No — the Taj Mahal is closed every Friday, and Diwali 2027 is a Friday. Visit Agra on Wednesday or Thursday that week and spend Diwali night in Jaipur or Udaipur.

Where is the best place to see Diwali in India?

There is no single best place. Incredible India lists Jaipur's Johri Bazar, Lake Pichola in Udaipur, Dashashwamedh Ghat in Varanasi, Ayodhya, Delhi's Dilli Haat, Mysore Palace and Meenakshi Temple in Madurai. For a first trip, Jaipur or Udaipur fits a Golden Triangle route easily.

Is Diwali a good time to visit India?

Yes, if you plan for it. The UK Foreign Office (7 Oct 2026) asks travellers to take particular care during Diwali, and notes severe air pollution in North Indian cities from October to February. Expect crowds, late fireworks and busy transport; book early and check with your doctor if you have a breathing condition.

Can foreigners join Diwali celebrations?

Yes. Streets, markets and temples are open to everyone. A family puja is private — join only when invited, dress modestly and follow your hosts' lead.

How early should I book a Diwali trip?

Do I need a visa for India?

Most visitors need one. Apply only at the official site indianvisaonline.gov.in — beware look-alike sites that charge extra. We are not a visa agency.
