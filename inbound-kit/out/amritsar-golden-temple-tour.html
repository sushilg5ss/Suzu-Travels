What you will see at the Golden Temple

The temple is open to visitors of every faith. It is a working place of worship, so the best visits are slow ones.

Sri Harmandir Sahib

Guru Ram Das, the fourth Sikh Guru, built the first pool here and founded Amritsar around it. The temple stands on a 67-ft (20 m) square of marble, two storeys high; Maharaja Ranjit Singh had the upper half covered with about 400 kg of gold leaf.

The Amrit Sarovar

The sacred pool — the “Pool of Nectar” — that surrounds the temple. You walk the marble path (the parikrama) around it, barefoot, with the gold reflected in the water.

The Akal Takht

Facing the temple across the walkway: established by Guru Hargobind and one of the five seats of authority in Sikhism.

Guru Ka Langar

The community kitchen serves free food to around 20,000 people every day, and up to 100,000 on special occasions. Visitors can join the community meal.

How to visit respectfully

The official rule is short; the rest is common courtesy that the temple volunteers will happily help with.

  1. Remove your shoes and cover your headRemove your shoes and cover your head before entering the temple complex (Punjab Tourism). A scarf or bandana works for everyone; we tell you where to leave your shoes.
  2. Dress modestlyDress modestly; follow the instructions of temple volunteers (sevadars). Shoulders and knees covered is a good rule for everyone.
  3. Go early, then come back at nightPunjab Tourism notes the temple is less crowded in the early mornings on weekends. It is open from early morning until late evening — confirm the hours locally — and many visitors return after dark to see it lit up.
  4. Eat at the langarVisitors can join the free community meal. Sit where the volunteers show you and take only what you will eat.
  5. PhotosBe discreet, and ask before you photograph people. Follow any signs or volunteer instructions about where cameras are not welcome.

The Beating Retreat at the Attari–Wagah border

Every evening India's Border Security Force (BSF) and the Pakistan Rangers lower their flags in a drill ceremony at the Attari–Wagah joint check post, near Border Pillar No. 102 on the Grand Trunk Road. It starts in the late afternoon — about 4:15 pm in winter and 5:15 pm in summer (Incredible India); the time moves with sunset, so arrive early. The Attari–Wagah ceremony is run by the Border Security Force; timings and access rules can change at short notice (security, weather, official decisions). Carry your passport, keep bags to a minimum, and confirm on the day.

The border is 32 km (20 miles) from Amritsar bus stand and 36 km (22 miles) from the airport; your driver takes you there and back, so you can do it on the same day as a morning at the temple.

UK travellers: the FCDO advises against all travel within 10 km of the India–Pakistan border, except for the Wagah–Attari border crossing; it says that while the crossing is currently closed, visitors can still attend the daily border retreat ceremony (FCDO advice updated 7 Oct 2026, checked 11 Nov 2026). Always read gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/india or your own government's advice before you travel.

Jallianwala Bagh is the memorial garden where Indians were shot by British troops on 13 April 1919; it commemorates the 2,000 Indians who were killed or wounded. You can see the Martyrs' Gallery, the bullet-marked wall and the memorial well. Please check its opening hours locally.

From Amritsar into the Himalaya

Golden Temple & Himalayas, 10 days: Delhi → Amritsar → Attari–Wagah → Dharamshala → Manali → Delhi. Town positions are approximate.

Amritsar is the natural gateway to Himachal Pradesh, our home state. From the plains of Punjab the road climbs to Dharamshala and McLeod Ganj (2,082 m / 6,831 ft), the seat of the Tibetan Government in exile, under the snowy Dhauladhar range. Kangra (Gaggal) airport is 14 km (9 miles) away if you would rather fly out from there. Our McLeod Ganj guide covers the town.

Next comes Manali, in the Kullu valley. In winter the Rohtang Pass is closed (late November to May), but the Atal Tunnel is open all year, so the high valley beyond stays within reach on most days.

Prefer a quieter hill town? Dalhousie is 199 km (124 miles) from Amritsar. Either way, our best time to visit Himachal guide explains what each month is like.

FAQ

Questions travellers ask

How many days do you need in Amritsar?

Two nights is enough for most visitors: the Golden Temple by day and after dark, a meal at the langar, Jallianwala Bagh and the evening ceremony at the Attari–Wagah border. Add a day if you want to slow down.

Can tourists and non-Sikhs visit the Golden Temple?

Yes. Visitors of every faith are welcome. Remove your shoes and cover your head before entering the temple complex (Punjab Tourism), dress modestly and follow the instructions of the temple volunteers (sevadars).

What should I wear to the Golden Temple?

Modest clothes that cover shoulders and knees, and a scarf or bandana to cover your head. You leave your shoes outside before you enter.

Is the langar free, and can visitors eat there?

Yes. The Guru Ka Langar serves free food to around 20,000 people every day, and up to 100,000 on special occasions (Punjab Tourism). Visitors can join the community meal.

What time is the Wagah border ceremony?

It starts in the late afternoon — about 4:15 pm in winter and 5:15 pm in summer (Incredible India) — and moves with sunset, so arrive early. The ceremony is run by the Border Security Force; timings and access rules can change at short notice. Carry your passport and confirm on the day.

How far is the Wagah border from Amritsar?

About 32 km (20 miles) from Amritsar bus stand and 36 km (22 miles) from the airport (District Amritsar). With a private car it fits easily into an afternoon.

How do I get from Delhi to Amritsar?

By fast train (the station is 2 km from the temple), by a short flight (the airport is 13 km from the temple), or a full day by private car. We help you choose by your dates and the rest of your route.
