What do I need to know about skiing in Gulmarg?

Gulmarg skiing runs from about mid-December to mid-April, 52–56 km (32–35 mi) from Srinagar in Kashmir. A two-stage gondola climbs to about 3,980 m (13,000 ft); there are beginner lifts, instructors and a government ski-hire shop. Before anything else: what governments advise about Kashmir

The UK FCDO advises against all travel to the Union Territory of Jammu and Kashmir, and names Gulmarg, Srinagar and the Jammu–Srinagar highway; the only exceptions are flying to and from Jammu city and travel within Jammu city (updated 7 Oct 2026). The US State Department rates Jammu and Kashmir 'Level 4: Do Not Travel', except eastern Ladakh and Leh (checked 23 Oct 2026). The FCDO also warns: 'Your travel insurance could be invalidated if you travel against advice from the Foreign, Commonwealth & Development Office (FCDO).' Check your own government's latest advice and your insurer's terms before you book anything — if they rule Gulmarg out, we will happily plan snow in Himachal instead.

Gulmarg has a long ski history: the Ski Club of India was set up here in 1927, and India's first National Winter Games were held here in 1998 (JKTDC).

The Gulmarg gondola: two stages up the mountain

The gondola is run by the J&K Cable Car Corporation (JKCCC), a government corporation, and opened in 1998. Heights and ride times below are from JKTDC and Incredible India; the two sources round slightly differently.

Lifts, lessons and ski hire

Beginners

JKTDC lists three ski lifts suited to beginners and says trained instructors are available.

Intermediates

JKTDC lists one chair lift for intermediate skiers. From the top of the gondola there is a downhill run of about 3 km (2 mi); Incredible India also notes snowboarding down from Kongdoori.

Ski hire

Skis, boots, sticks, gloves and goggles can be hired on the spot from the government-run ski shop (JKTDC). Bring your own if you prefer — we'll make room in the car.

Ski training

The Indian Institute of Skiing and Mountaineering (IISM) was set up at Gulmarg in 1968 by India's Ministry of Tourism. Course places and eligibility are set by IISM — check with them directly.

Off-piste

Gulmarg is known for deep powder off the marked runs. We do not arrange off-piste or backcountry skiing; if you plan it, go only with a qualified local mountain guide and the right avalanche equipment.

FAQ

Questions travellers ask

When is the ski season in Gulmarg?

JKTDC says the ski season usually starts around mid-December and runs to mid-April. Snow varies each winter, so we confirm conditions for your dates before you pay.

Is Gulmarg good for beginners?

Yes, it has a beginner area: JKTDC lists three ski lifts suited to beginners, one chair lift for intermediates, trained instructors and a government ski-hire shop. Experienced skiers come for the gondola and the powder.

How high does the Gulmarg gondola go?

Phase 1 runs from Gulmarg to Kongdoori at about 3,100 m (10,200 ft); Phase 2 continues to Apharwat at about 3,980 m (13,000 ft). Incredible India gives ride times of about 9 and 12 minutes and winter hours of 10 am to 5 pm, weather permitting.

Can I hire ski equipment in Gulmarg?

Yes. JKTDC says skis, boots, sticks, gloves and goggles can be hired on the spot from the government-run ski shop, and trained instructors are available.

Is it safe to go skiing in Gulmarg?

We don't make safety promises. The UK FCDO advises against all travel to Jammu and Kashmir, naming Gulmarg (updated 7 Oct 2026), and the US State Department rates it Level 4: Do Not Travel (checked 23 Oct 2026). Read your own government's latest advice before deciding.

Will my travel insurance cover Gulmarg?

Check with your insurer in writing. The UK FCDO says your travel insurance could be invalidated if you travel against its advice, and skiing is often an add-on. We ask every guest to confirm cover before booking.

How do I get to Gulmarg?

Gulmarg is about 52–56 km (32–35 mi) south-west of Srinagar (JKTDC). We meet you at the airport and drive you in a private car, in daylight when there is snow on the road.
