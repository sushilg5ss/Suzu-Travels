In short

Is India safe for solo female travellers?

There is no yes-or-no answer. Many women travel in India, but the UK, US and Australian governments all advise women to take extra care: harassment is common, sexual assault on foreign visitors has been reported, and travelling alone at night carries more risk. Planning with a registered local company, daylight road travel and known transport lower the risk — they never remove it.

Practical steps that lower the risk

These come straight from the official advice, plus how we plan trips for women travelling on their own:

  1. Arrive with a pick-up already bookedThe FCDO notes that pre-paid taxis and app taxis such as Uber and Ola are available at airports and that many hotels offer transfers. If someone collects you, make sure they properly identify themselves before you set off. On our trips you know your driver's name, photo and car number before you land.
  2. Travel by road in daylightThe FCDO advises: "Avoid travelling at night and in fog as accidents are common due to low visibility", and notes a higher risk of crime at night. We plan every long drive to finish before dark.
  3. Never hail a taxi on the streetBoth the FCDO and Smartraveller say this. Use a pre-paid booth, a hotel car, an app taxi or your arranged car.
  4. Watch food and drinksThe FCDO warns: "Be wary of accepting drinks or food from strangers or leaving drinks unattended" — there have been reports of spiked drinks and food.
  5. Avoid isolated places when aloneThe FCDO's advice covers beaches too, at any time of day. Visit quiet viewpoints, ghats and trails with a guide or in company.
  6. Know who your hosts areSmartraveller reports allegations of sexual misconduct involving some religious groups and their leaders, and advises: "If you're visiting India for religious reasons, find out about your hosts before travelling." This matters for ashram and retreat stays — see our Rishikesh retreat guide.
  7. Keep someone informedShare your day-by-day plan and your driver's details with family at home, and keep your phone charged with a local or roaming connection.

FAQ

Questions travellers ask

Is India safe for solo female travellers?

There is no simple yes or no. Many women travel in India, but the UK, US and Australian governments all advise extra caution for women, and the US and Australian advice says women should avoid travelling alone. Planning transport and stays in advance, travelling by road in daylight and avoiding isolated places lower the risk.

What does the UK government say about women travelling in India?

The FCDO says there is a risk of sexual assault, including attacks on female foreign visitors in tourist areas and cities, and that female travellers often experience verbal and physical harassment. It advises avoiding isolated areas, including beaches, when alone, and not hailing taxis on the street, especially at night (page updated 7 Oct 2026).

Does the US advise women not to travel alone in India?

Yes. The US State Department's India page says: "The U.S. government advises against women traveling alone in India." India's overall US advisory is Level 2, exercise increased caution (issued 16 Jun 2025). Check the current advice before you travel.

Is it safe to take a taxi alone in India?

The official advice is to avoid hailing taxis on the street, especially at night. Use pre-paid airport taxis, app taxis such as Uber and Ola, hotel transfers or a car arranged in advance, and check the driver identifies himself before you set off.

What is the emergency number in India?

112 is the national emergency number. Police is 100, the women helpline is 1091 and the cybercrime helpline is 1930 (UK FCDO). Also save your own embassy's emergency number.

Is a private driver a good idea for a woman travelling alone?

It removes street taxis and night-time transfers from your trip and gives you one known contact on the road. With Suzu, your driver's name, photo and car number are shared with you before the trip, and our office is a phone call away. It lowers risk; it does not remove it.

What should I wear in India?

Dress modestly, especially at religious sites — we tell you what each one expects before you visit. Smartraveller notes that physical contact between men and women in public might be considered inappropriate.
