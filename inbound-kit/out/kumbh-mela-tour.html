Nashik–Trimbakeshwar Simhastha: the other 2027 Kumbh

Maharashtra's Simhastha Kumbh is held at Nashik and Trimbakeshwar, on the Godavari river. The Maharashtra Government announced its schedule in June 2025 (reported by PTI):

Opening: 31 October 2026

The Kumbh opens with flag hoisting (Dhwajarohan) at Trimbakeshwar and at Ramkund in Nashik.

What is the Kumbh Mela?

UNESCO added the Kumbh Mela to its Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity in December 2017, describing it as the largest peaceful congregation of pilgrims on earth. It is held in turn in Prayagraj (Allahabad), Haridwar, Ujjain and Nashik.

The holy dip

Hindus believe that bathing in the sacred river during the Kumbh brings spiritual merit. Pilgrims, families and sadhus come from all over India to bathe, pray and listen to teachers.

Amrit Snan

The most important bathing days, when the akharas — the orders of sadhus — go to the river in procession. In Haridwar in 2027 these are 6 March, 8 March and 14 April. They are also the most crowded days.

Kumbh, Ardh Kumbh and Maha Kumbh

Haridwar 2027 is an Ardh Kumbh — a 'half' Kumbh, held between the full Kumbhs. Uttarakhand's Chief Minister has said it will be held as grandly as a full Kumbh.

Har Ki Pauri

Haridwar's most famous ghat on the Ganga and the centre of the Kumbh. Haridwar railway station is the nearest station, and Jolly Grant airport at Dehradun has daily flights to Delhi.

Crowds and safety: an honest answer

The UK Foreign Office (advice updated 7 October 2026) asks visitors to take particular care at times of religious significance and notes that stampedes have happened at large religious gatherings. NHS Fit for Travel's advice for the 2025 Kumbh said that accidents and injuries, such as slips, falls, stampedes and road accidents, are common. What we suggest: if crowds worry you, avoid the main Amrit Snan mornings and visit on a quieter bathing day; agree a meeting point with your group; keep copies of your passport and visa and a charged phone; follow police barriers and one-way routes; and stay outside the mela core.

We don't promise any event is safe or unsafe. We share the official advice with dates and plan your visit around it with you.

What to wear, bring and know

A Kumbh is a religious gathering first and a sight second. A few things make the day easier for you and the pilgrims around you:

Clothes

Modest clothing that covers shoulders and knees, closed shoes you don't mind getting wet, and warm layers for January and February nights.

Food, water and health

NHS Fit for Travel's advice for the 2025 Kumbh: take safe food and water precautions at all times. We're not doctors — consult your doctor or a travel-health clinic; see NHS Fit for Travel or CDC Travelers' Health.

Photographs

Ask before you photograph sadhus or pilgrims, and never photograph women bathing. Your guide will tell you where cameras are not welcome.

Visa

Most visitors need an Indian e-Visa. Apply yourself at the official site indianvisaonline.gov.in; beware look-alike sites that charge extra. We are not a visa agency — see our India e-Visa guide.

Registration

Check the official site haridwarkumbh.uk.gov.in for any visitor registration or rules for 2027 before you travel.

Questions travellers ask

When is the next Kumbh Mela?

In 2027 there are two. Haridwar's Ardh Kumbh runs from 14 January to 20 April 2027, with Amrit Snan days on 6 March, 8 March and 14 April. The Nashik–Trimbakeshwar Simhastha opens on 31 October 2026 and has its main baths on 2 August, 31 August and 11–12 September 2027. Confirm dates on the official sites before you book flights.

Can foreigners attend the Kumbh Mela?

Yes. The Kumbh is open to everyone, and many international visitors come to watch the bathing days and the processions. You need a valid Indian visa or e-Visa — apply yourself at the official site indianvisaonline.gov.in and beware look-alike sites that charge extra. What is an Amrit Snan?

The most important bathing days of a Kumbh, when the akharas (orders of sadhus) go to the river in procession. In Haridwar in 2027 they fall on 6 March (Mahashivratri), 8 March (Phalguna Amavasya) and 14 April (Mesh Sankranti). They are also the most crowded days.

Is the Kumbh Mela safe for tourists?

The UK Foreign Office (advice updated 7 October 2026) asks visitors to take particular care at times of religious significance and notes that stampedes have happened at large religious gatherings. What is the difference between Kumbh, Ardh Kumbh and Maha Kumbh?
