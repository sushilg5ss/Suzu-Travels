Five festivals worth planning a trip around

Most visitors come for one of these. Each fits a different part of India and a different season:

Holi — 23 March 2027

Colour, music and crowds. Braj (Mathura, Vrindavan, Barsana) celebrates for about two weeks — UP Tourism's Rangotsav 2027 is 16–30 March — and Braj sits between Delhi and Agra, so Holi fits a Golden Triangle trip. See our Holi guide.

Diwali — 29 October 2027

The festival of lights, at the start of the cool, dry season in the north. Lovely to see, but it is a family festival: trains and flights are full around it and some shops close for a day or two. Arrive a few days before or stay on after.

Kumbh Mela — Haridwar, 14 Jan – 20 Apr 2027

The Ardh Kumbh at Haridwar on the Ganga, with main bathing days (Amrit Snan) on 6 March, 8 March and 14 April. Holi 2027 falls inside the window. See our Kumbh Mela guide.

Pushkar Camel Fair — October–November

Camel traders, decorated camels and folk music on the edge of the Thar desert, an easy add-on from Jaipur or Udaipur on a Rajasthan tour. The organisers publish the year's dates nearer the time.

Hornbill Festival — 1–10 December

Nagaland's showcase of Naga tribal dance, music, food and crafts at the Naga Heritage Village, Kisama, about 12 km (7 mi) from Kohima. We arrange North-East trips on request.

Which trip fits which festival

India's festival year lines up with its travel seasons. A simple way to choose:

  1. January – March: North India's best weatherMakar Sankranti, the Haridwar Kumbh, Maha Shivratri and Holi all fall in the cool, dry months — the classic time for the Golden Triangle, Rajasthan, Varanasi and Rishikesh. See the best time to visit India.
  2. April – June: the HimalayaThe plains get hot, so Vaisakhi in Amritsar and Buddha Purnima in Dharamshala pair well with the hill towns of Himachal. Srinagar's tulip garden usually opens in March — check the opening dates.
  3. July – September: monsoon festivalsJanmashtami in Mathura, Ganesh Chaturthi in Mumbai and Onam in Kerala. Expect rain in most regions; we plan around it or suggest a shorter trip.
  4. October – December: the big festival seasonDurga Puja, Dussehra, Diwali, Guru Nanak's birthday, Pushkar and Hornbill — with the return of clear skies in the north. The busiest and most colourful time of year: book early.

Travelling during a festival: what to know

Festivals are the best memories of many trips. A few practical points help:

Book early

Trains, flights and hotels fill around Diwali, Holi and Christmas–New Year. Hill stations such as Shimla ask visitors to book early for Christmas and New Year.

Dates can move

Islamic festivals depend on the moon, and fairs such as Pushkar or Dev Deepawali are fixed by the organisers each year. We confirm the dates before you book flights.

Crowds

The UK Foreign Office (India travel advice, 7 Oct 2026) says to take particular care during periods of national or religious significance, and notes that stampedes have occurred at large religious gatherings. Go with a local guide, agree a meeting point and leave early if a crowd feels too tight.

Closures

On big festival days some shops, offices and markets close. Monuments usually stay open, but check — the Taj Mahal is closed on Fridays, and Diwali 2027 is a Friday.

Respect

Ask before photographing people at worship, dress modestly at temples, gurdwaras and mosques, and take your shoes off where others do.

Your visa

Apply only at the official site indianvisaonline.gov.in — beware look-alike sites that charge extra. See our India e-Visa guide.

FAQ

Questions travellers ask

When is Diwali 2027?

Diwali 2027 falls on Friday 29 October, per the Government of India's 2027 holiday list (DoPT, 16 July 2026). The festive days around it on the same list are Naraka Chaturdasi on 28 October, Govardhan Puja on 30 October and Bhai Duj on 31 October.

When is Holi 2027?

Holi 2027 is on Tuesday 23 March, with Holika Dahan bonfires on the evening of Monday 22 March. In Braj the celebrations start earlier: UP Tourism lists Rangotsav 2027 in Mathura and Vrindavan as 16–30 March.

When is the next Kumbh Mela?

Haridwar's Ardh Kumbh runs from 14 January to 20 April 2027, with main bathing days (Amrit Snan) on 6 March, 8 March and 14 April. The Nashik–Trimbakeshwar Simhastha began on 31 October 2026, with main baths on 2 August, 31 August and 11 September 2027.

When is the Pushkar Camel Fair?

It is held in October–November in Pushkar, Rajasthan. The 2027 dates were not yet published when we checked (9 Nov 2026); we confirm them for you before you book.

Is it a good idea to travel in India during Diwali or Holi?

Yes, if you plan for it. Both are joyful to see, but trains, flights and hotels fill up and some shops close. Book early, keep a local guide with you at Holi, and arrive a day or two before the main day.

Do Indian festival dates change every year?

Yes. Most Hindu, Sikh, Buddhist and Islamic festivals follow lunar calendars, so the dates move each year. Islamic festival dates also depend on the sighting of the moon and can move by a day. Use the official list for the year you travel.

Which festival is best for a first trip to India?

There is no single best one. Holi in March fits the classic Golden Triangle in good weather; Diwali in October–November opens the cool season in the north. Tell us your dates and interests and we suggest what fits.

How far ahead should I book a festival trip?

For Diwali, Holi, the Kumbh bathing days and Christmas–New Year, plan a few months ahead — hotels near the main sites fill first.
