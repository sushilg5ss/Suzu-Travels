The route: the Golden Triangle first, the Himalaya second

The plains are best seen first, while you adjust to India's pace and time zone; the mountains are the reward. This order also puts the long hill drives in the second week, when jet lag is gone.

Delhi → Agra (204 km / 127 mi) → Jaipur (232 km / 144 mi) → back via Delhi → Shimla (370 km / 230 mi from Delhi) → Manali (260 km / 162 mi from Shimla) → Dharamshala → fly out from Kangra airport. Official road distances; hill roads are slow, so each hill drive gets a full day.

Fourteen days in India, day by day

A sample we build from, not a fixed package. Every night can move; the quote changes, the pace stays human.

  1. DAY 1
    Arrive in Delhi

    Your driver meets you at the airport — name, photo and car number are shared with you before the trip. Check in and rest; international flights often land late at night.

  2. DAY 2
    Old and New Delhi

    Old Delhi's lanes in the morning, then New Delhi and Humayun's Tomb in the afternoon light. A good first day to learn how traffic, tickets and tipping work, with your driver close by.

  3. DAY 3
    Delhi → Agra

    204 km (127 mi) by road. Agra Fort in the afternoon — it is open sunrise to sunset every day. Agra has three UNESCO World Heritage Sites: the Taj Mahal, Agra Fort and Fatehpur Sikri.

  4. DAY 4
    Taj Mahal at sunrise → Jaipur

    The Taj Mahal opens 30 minutes before sunrise — the calmest hour. It is closed on Fridays, so we never plan this day on a Friday. Then Fatehpur Sikri on the road west and on to Jaipur, 232 km (144 mi) from Agra.

  5. DAY 5
    Jaipur, the Pink City

    Jaipur's old walled city is UNESCO-listed. Start at Hawa Mahal's pink latticed facade and spend a full day in the old city at your own speed.

  6. DAY 6
    Jaipur → Delhi → north

    Back to Delhi by train or car (check current train timings on IRCTC), then onwards towards Chandigarh and Kalka, or a last night in Delhi. This is the one long transfer day of the trip.

  7. DAY 7
    Up to Shimla

    Ride the Kalka–Shimla toy train — 96 km (60 mi) of narrow-gauge line, part of the UNESCO-listed Mountain Railways of India — or go up by car; Shimla is 119 km (74 mi) from Chandigarh by road. Your driver takes the luggage either way.

  8. DAY 8
    Shimla

    Christ Church on the Ridge, the town strung along deodar-forested ridges, and slow walks. A gentle first full day in the hills.

  9. DAY 9
    Shimla → Manali

    260 km (162 mi), a full day: we plan around 30–40 km/h (20–25 mph) averages on hill roads, in our experience, and stop for lunch and tea on the way up the Kullu valley.

  10. DAY 10
    Manali: Solang and the Atal Tunnel

    Solang valley is 13 km (8 mi) from Manali. When the road is open, the 9.02 km Atal Tunnel at about 3,000 m takes you through to Sissu in Lahaul. Rohtang Pass, 51 km (32 mi) away, needs a vehicle permit and is closed from late November until May — it is not part of this plan.

  11. DAY 11
    Old Manali and Naggar

    A slow day: the old village above the town, and Naggar further down the valley.

  12. DAY 12
    Manali → Dharamshala

    A full-day drive west across Himachal to Dharamshala, under the snow line of the Dhauladhar range.

  13. DAY 13
    McLeod Ganj

    The upper town at 2,082 m (6,831 ft) is the seat of the Tibetan Government in exile, where the Dalai Lama settled in 1959. Namgyal Monastery and the Tsuglagkhang complex, St John in the Wilderness church, Bhagsunag falls, the tea gardens and the Norbulingka Institute.

  14. DAY 14
    Kangra → Delhi → home

    Kangra airport at Gaggal is 14 km (9 mi) from Dharamshala: a short flight to Delhi for your connection, or a drive back. Check airline schedules — in our experience, hill airports can cancel in bad weather, so we keep a margin before your international flight.

Shorter, longer, or in winter: how the plan changes

Only 10 days?

Keep days 1–10 and drop Dharamshala and one Manali day: from Manali we drive you back down to Chandigarh or Delhi for your flight.

Three weeks?

Add Amritsar and the Golden Temple after Dharamshala, a few slower days in Rishikesh — see our Rishikesh yoga retreat guide — or more nights in the Himachal valleys, our home region.

Travelling December to February?

Snow can close the roads above Manali and slow the Shimla–Manali drive, and Rohtang Pass is closed from late November until May. In winter we keep Shimla and Dharamshala, and we never promise snow.

Warmth instead of mountains?

Swap the second week for the South: fly from Delhi to Kochi for our 8-day Kerala tour — tea hills, spice forests and a houseboat night.

When to go on a two-week North India trip

Our recommendation, built on the official season facts:

October – November: go

The monsoon has withdrawn (its normal final withdrawal over south India is around 15 October, per the India Meteorological Department), the plains are pleasant and the hill roads are open.

March – April: go

Spring in the plains and clear days in the hills; the high pass above Manali stays closed until May, but everything on this plan is open.

December – February: the winter version

Snowfall is common around Dharamshala in winter and roads above Manali can close; fog can slow the plains. We plan the winter version with buffer days.

Late June – September: monsoon

The IMD's normal monsoon onset in New Delhi is 27 June. We usually suggest other dates for this route.

Practical notes before you book

Visa

Apply yourself only at the official site indianvisaonline.gov.in — beware look-alike sites that charge extra. We are not a visa agency. Our India e-Visa guide walks you through it.

Safety

The UK FCDO advises against travel within 10 km of the India–Pakistan border and to Jammu and Kashmir (except Jammu city) — this itinerary does not go there (advice as of 7 Oct 2026; check before you travel). It also advises avoiding travel at night and in fog; we plan daytime drives.

Altitude and health

Manali and McLeod Ganj are above 2,000 m. If you have a heart or lung condition, consult your doctor; see CDC or NHS Fit for Travel.

The Taj on a Friday

The Taj Mahal is closed on Fridays. If your dates put Agra on a Friday, we move Agra Fort and Fatehpur Sikri around it rather than skip the Taj.

FAQ

Questions travellers ask

Is two weeks enough for India?

Two weeks is enough for North India: the Golden Triangle (Delhi, Agra, Jaipur) plus a week in the Himalaya of Himachal Pradesh. It is not enough to add the South as well without spending days in transit — if you want Kerala, swap it for the mountain week.

What is the best 2-week itinerary for India?

For a first visit we suggest Delhi (2 nights), Agra (1), Jaipur (2), a night on the way north, Shimla (2), Manali (3) and Dharamshala (2), then a flight from Kangra to Delhi. It joins India's most famous sights with its calmest mountain towns, and every leg is a day drive.

What is the best month for a 2-week trip to India?

October–November and March–April for this route. December–February is cold in the hills, with snow that can close roads above Manali. The monsoon normally reaches New Delhi around 27 June (IMD), so late June to September is the wettest time.

Can I see the Taj Mahal and the Himalayas in one trip?

Yes — that is exactly this plan. The Taj Mahal is on day 4 at sunrise, and from day 7 you are in the Himalayan foothills at Shimla, then Manali and McLeod Ganj at 2,082 m (6,831 ft).

Is it better to travel India by train or by car?

Both, in the right places. A private car with one driver is the most flexible way through Agra, Jaipur and the hill roads, and it carries your luggage. Trains are good for some legs — the Kalka–Shimla toy train is a highlight in itself, a UNESCO-listed mountain railway. Check current timings on IRCTC.

How long are the drives?

Delhi–Agra is 204 km (127 mi), Agra–Jaipur 232 km (144 mi), Delhi–Shimla 370 km (230 mi) and Shimla–Manali 260 km (162 mi). In the hills we plan around 30–40 km/h (20–25 mph) averages in our experience, so each hill drive is a full day with stops.

Do I need a visa for India?

Plan on needing one. Apply yourself only at the official site indianvisaonline.gov.in — beware look-alike sites that charge extra. We are not a visa agency; our e-Visa guide explains the steps.
