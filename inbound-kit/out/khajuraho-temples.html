What Khajuraho is — and why people make the detour

The Khajuraho Group of Monuments is a UNESCO World Heritage Site, inscribed in 1986. The temples were built by the Chandella dynasty between about 950 and 1050 and are split into western, eastern and southern groups. Tradition says there were once 85; about 20–25 are standing today.

What you come for is the stone. Tall sandstone spires rise in layers like mountain peaks, and almost every surface is carved: gods and goddesses, celestial dancers, musicians, warriors, animals and scenes of everyday life.

Yes, some carvings are erotic, and they are the part most people have heard about. In reality they are a small share of the sculpture. Most visitors leave talking about the craftsmanship, not the headlines.

Khajuraho is a quieter, smaller town than Agra or Varanasi. That is part of its appeal: one or two calm days of temples and evening light between two of India's busiest stops.

What you see in one or two days

Western Group

The best-known temples stand together in the Western Group. Most visitors spend a full morning or late afternoon here; the Dance Festival and the sound-and-light show also take place here.

Eastern Group

A second cluster of temples elsewhere in town — calmer, and good for a second visit on day two.

Southern Group

A few temples further out of town. Worth adding if you stay two nights and want the full picture.

Sound and light show

Held at the Western Group complex, with a show in English and one in Hindi. English show: 6:30–7:05 pm from October to February, 7:30–8:05 pm from March to September (MP Tourism, Jul 2026).

When to go: season, festival and safari

October to March

The best months for walking around the temples. It gets hot after March, with summer heat from April to June.

Khajuraho Dance Festival

The 53rd festival runs 20–26 February 2027 at the Western Group: classical Indian dance performed against the temples, a tradition since 1975. Entry is free. Hotels fill early — plan ahead.

Panna Tiger Reserve

Its Madla gate is about 25 km (15 miles) from Khajuraho. The best time is 15 October to 15 June; the park closes in the monsoon. Tiger sightings are never guaranteed.

Winter evenings

From October to February the English sound-and-light show starts at 6:30 pm — easy to fit in after a day at the temples.

Fees, timings and your visa

The temples are open from sunrise to sunset. Foreign visitors pay a separate, higher entry fee, charged in Indian rupees; children under 15 enter free. Entry fees and show times change — check the ASI and MP Tourism sites before you go (as of Nov 2026). For your visa: apply only at the official site indianvisaonline.gov.in — look-alike sites charge extra. We are not a visa agency. FAQ

Questions travellers ask

Is Khajuraho worth the detour?

For most culture travellers, yes. It is a UNESCO World Heritage Site with some of India's finest temple sculpture, built between about 950 and 1050, and it sits naturally between Agra or Orchha and Varanasi. If you only have a week and dislike long road days, Agra and Varanasi alone may suit you better — we'll tell you honestly.

How many days do I need in Khajuraho?

One or two nights. One night covers the Western Group and the evening show; a second night adds the Eastern and Southern groups or a visit to Panna Tiger Reserve.

How do I get from Agra or Varanasi to Khajuraho?

From Agra, most travellers go by private car with a stop at Orchha — a long day's drive, so we plan breaks. Between Khajuraho and Varanasi there are seasonal flights and trains; check current schedules on airline sites or IRCTC, or ask us.

What is the best time to visit Khajuraho?

October to March. It gets hot after March. The 53rd Khajuraho Dance Festival runs 20–26 February 2027, with free entry at the Western Group.

Are all the Khajuraho carvings erotic?

No. The erotic carvings are a small share of the sculpture. Most of it shows gods and goddesses, dancers, musicians, animals and everyday life.

What are the opening hours and the entry fee for foreigners?

The temples are open from sunrise to sunset. Foreigners pay a separate, higher fee in Indian rupees; children under 15 enter free. Fees change, so check the ASI site before you go.

Can I add Panna Tiger Reserve?

Yes. Its Madla gate is about 25 km (15 miles) from Khajuraho, and the best time is 15 October to 15 June. Sightings are never guaranteed.
