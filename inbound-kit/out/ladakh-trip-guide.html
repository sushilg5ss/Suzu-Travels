A sample 8-day Ladakh trip by car

A summer shape that respects the district's acclimatisation rule and keeps the highest night short:

Fly in to Leh (Days 1–3, with 48 hours' rest and Thiksey) → over Khardung La to the Nubra valley (Days 4–5) → Pangong lake (Day 6) → back to Leh and fly out (Days 7–8).

  1. Days 1–2: arrive in Leh and restFly in and take it slowly — the district asks every visitor to rest at least 48 hours before going to higher places like Khardung La or Pangong, though nearby Leh is fine. Easy visits: the Shanti Stupa (built 1991) above town and the old bazaar.
  2. Day 3: monasteries of the Indus valleyThiksey monastery, 19 km (12 mi) from Leh, founded in 1433 — visitors can join the morning prayers. Add Hemis or Shey nearby, or a day trip to Lamayuru's 11th-century monastery (115 km / 71 mi, at 3,510 m).
  3. Days 4–5: Khardung La and the Nubra valleyOver Khardung La at 5,602 m (18,379 ft), 40 km (25 mi) from Leh, down into Nubra, about 120 km (75 mi) north of Leh: Diskit's 33 m (108 ft) Maitreya Buddha, the Hunder dunes with double-humped Bactrian camels, and Turtuk village if the road and your permit allow.
  4. Day 6: Pangong lakePangong Tso lies at about 4,350–4,400 m (14,300–14,500 ft) and is about 130 km (80 mi) long, with roughly a third in India. We plan the route that is open that day and keep the stay short because of the height.
  5. Days 7–8: back to Leh and fly outReturn to Leh and fly to Delhi. With more time, add Tso Moriri (240 km / 150 mi from Leh, above 4,000 m) or drive out over the Manali road in summer.

The permit foreign visitors need

Visitors register on the Leh district's official permit system and pay its fees. Foreign nationals need a Protected Area Permit for areas such as Nubra, Khardung La, Pangong and Tso Moriri. It is issued through that system (lahdclehpermit.in), which encourages visitors to apply through local travel agents and collects the district's environmental (green) fee, Red Cross and wildlife fees. Citizens of Pakistan, Afghanistan, China, Bangladesh, Myanmar and Sri Lanka, and holders of diplomatic, official, UN or journalist passports, must instead get the permit from the Ministry of Home Affairs or Ministry of External Affairs in New Delhi. We arrange it with you; rules can change, so we confirm when we quote. (Leh district permit portal, checked 15 Nov 2026.)

Best time for a Ladakh trip

The Ladakh Police call May to September pleasant, at about 10–25 °C (50–77 °F) by day with cold nights; there is little rain. What each part of the year means:

May

Leh is open by air; the district says the Manali road opens in May or June, so it may still be shut. A quieter month if you fly in and out.

June to September

The main season: both highways normally open, all permit circuits running, and festivals such as Hemis Tsechu (June) and the Ladakh Festival in Leh (September). The Ladakh Police warn that landslides and snow can block the highways, especially in June–July — we keep a spare day in every road plan.

October

Clear and cold, fewer visitors. The Manali road usually closes in September or October, so most trips fly in and out.

November to April

Winter: Leh is reached by air only for most practical purposes. District Leh says nights can fall to −30 °C (−22 °F); the Ladakh Police say −40 °C in places. Pangong freezes completely. Winter monastery festivals and Losar (Ladakhi New Year, between 8 and 30 December) are special — for well-prepared cold-weather travellers only.

Altitude affects people differently — consult your doctor before you book and see CDC or NHS Fit for Travel. District Leh asks all visitors to rest at least 48 hours in Leh before going higher, and the Ladakh Police advise people with heart or breathing conditions to see a doctor first. The UK Foreign Office says your insurance should cover altitudes over 2,400 m and mountain rescue and helicopter costs — see our travel insurance guide.

Is Ladakh safe to visit? What governments say

Ladakh is a separate Union Territory from Jammu and Kashmir. The UK FCDO advises against all travel to the Union Territory of Jammu and Kashmir, including Srinagar and Sonamarg; it does not name Ladakh. The US State Department rates Jammu and Kashmir 'Level 4: Do Not Travel' except visits to eastern Ladakh and its capital, Leh (FCDO advice read 15 Nov 2026; State Department checked 14 Nov 2026).

That is why we fly guests to Leh or drive the Manali road, and do not plan the Srinagar–Kargil road. Advice can change, so check the latest from your own government before you book — your travel insurance could be invalidated if you travel against FCDO advice. Ladakh is close to sensitive borders, so permits, checkpoints and the routes open to foreigners are set by the authorities; we follow them. For the Kashmir side, see our Kashmir guide.

What to see on a Ladakh trip

Thiksey monastery

19 km (12 mi) from Leh, founded in 1433, with a 15 m (48 ft) Maitreya Buddha; visitors can attend the daily morning prayers.

Nubra valley

Over Khardung La, about 120 km (75 mi) north of Leh: the Diskit Buddha, the Hunder dunes and their Bactrian camels, and Turtuk village.

Pangong Tso

The long blue lake at about 4,350–4,400 m (14,300–14,500 ft), about 130 km (80 mi) long, a third of it in India. It freezes completely in winter.

Tso Moriri

A quieter high lake, 240 km (150 mi) from Leh — the district says 6–7 hours — and a Ramsar wetland.

Lamayuru

The 'moonland' monastery, 115 km (71 mi) west of Leh at 3,510 m, possible as a day trip.

Hemis National Park

Snow-leopard country near Leh; the district calls it the largest national park in South Asia. Sightings are rare and never promised.

Before you go

Visa

Apply yourself only at the official site indianvisaonline.gov.in — beware look-alike sites that charge extra. We are not a visa agency. Our e-Visa guide explains the steps. The e-Visa does not replace the Ladakh permit.

Phones

The Ladakh Police note that only post-paid mobile connections work in Ladakh and prepaid SIMs from other states are unreliable; data is good in Leh and limited outside. We share your driver's details and our office number before the trip.

Money

There are ATMs in Leh but few elsewhere; many Leh hotels take cards, but carry cash for Nubra and Pangong.

The car and the roads

The Ladakh Police recommend SUVs and say sedans are not suited to roads like Manali–Leh. We use an SUV with a driver who knows mountain roads and drive in daylight. Your driver's name, photo and car number are shared with you before the trip. See private driver in India.

Health

Consult your doctor before you book, especially with heart or breathing conditions; see CDC or NHS Fit for Travel. Bring warm layers even in summer — nights are cold.

FAQ

Questions travellers ask

Do foreigners need a permit for Ladakh?

For most of the famous places, yes. Foreign nationals need a Protected Area Permit for areas such as Nubra, Khardung La, Pangong and Tso Moriri. It is issued through the Leh district's official permit system, which encourages visitors to apply through local travel agents. We arrange it with you; rules can change, so we confirm when we quote.

What is the best time for a Ladakh trip?

June to September, when both highways are normally open and days are about 10–25 °C (50–77 °F). May and October work if you fly in and out. From November to April Ladakh is very cold — District Leh says nights can reach −30 °C (−22 °F) — and the Manali road is closed.

Should I fly to Leh or drive from Manali?

Most overseas guests fly from Delhi (about an hour) and rest 48 hours on arrival. The Manali–Leh road, 473 km (294 mi), is a spectacular two-day drive usually open from about May/June to September/October; we often use it one way in summer. We don't plan the Srinagar road because of the UK FCDO advice on Jammu and Kashmir.

Is altitude sickness a risk in Ladakh?

Yes, Ladakh is high: Leh is at about 3,500 m (11,500 ft) and Khardung La at 5,602 m (18,379 ft). District Leh asks every visitor to rest at least 48 hours in Leh before going higher. Altitude affects people differently — consult your doctor before you book and see CDC or NHS Fit for Travel.

Is Ladakh safe for tourists?

Ladakh is separate from Jammu and Kashmir. The UK FCDO advises against all travel to Jammu and Kashmir (including Srinagar) but does not name Ladakh; the US State Department's 'Do Not Travel' for J&K excepts eastern Ladakh and Leh. Check the latest advice before you book; your insurance could be invalidated if you travel against FCDO advice.

How many days do I need for Ladakh?

About 8 days for Leh, Nubra and Pangong, including two rest days on arrival. Add 2 days for Tso Moriri, or 3–4 if you drive in or out via Manali. Add travel days to and from Delhi.

Will my phone work in Ladakh?

Probably not on a prepaid SIM. The Ladakh Police say only post-paid mobile connections work in Ladakh and prepaid SIMs from other states are unreliable; data is good in Leh and limited outside. Our office number and your driver's details are shared before the trip.
