Why Darjeeling and Sikkim are worth the journey

Darjeeling sits at about 2,200 m (7,200 ft) in the hills of West Bengal, in India's far east. On a clear morning the snow peaks of the Kanchenjunga range fill the northern sky. Around the town, more than 80 working tea gardens produce the tea that carries Darjeeling's name around the world.

The hill town is also home to the Darjeeling Himalayan Railway — the original Indian 'toy train', a 2-ft (610 mm) gauge line opened in 1881 and listed by UNESCO in 1999. Beyond Darjeeling lies Sikkim, a small Himalayan state with Gangtok as its capital.

This corner of India is far from Delhi and the Golden Triangle, so many of our guests add it as a week of its own — often after Varanasi, with a flight to Bagdogra. It rewards slow travel: early starts for the views, short drives, and time to sit with a cup of tea.

The Sikkim permit for foreigners — what changed in 2026

Foreign visitors need a Restricted Area Permit (RAP) to enter Sikkim, and a Protected Area Permit (PAP) for areas such as Tsomgo Lake, Nathu La and Yumthang. Since January 2026 these are issued online only through the Government of India's e-FRRO system — no paper permits at the border. Apply yourself on the official portal and check the current rules before you travel; we help you plan the dates but we do not issue permits. The news reports differ slightly on timing: apply online through e-FRRO (indianfrro.gov.in) once you are in India and before you reach Sikkim — and check the portal for the current process. Some protected areas may not be open to foreigners. The Sikkim permit is only for Sikkim — Darjeeling is in West Bengal. What to see in Darjeeling and around Gangtok

The toy train joy ride

Daily tourist trains run from Darjeeling to Ghum — India's highest railway station — and back via the Batasia Loop. The short ride is the easiest way to travel on the UNESCO line.

Batasia Loop

The most famous loop on the railway, where the line turns a full circle — and a good spot to look for Kanchenjunga.

Tiger Hill at sunrise

A viewpoint at 2,590 m (8,497 ft), about 13 km (8 miles) from Darjeeling. Leave before dawn and dress warmly.

Tea gardens

More than 80 working tea gardens surround Darjeeling. Walk the slopes and taste the tea where it grows.

Mountaineering and wildlife

The Himalayan Mountaineering Institute and the Padmaja Naidu Himalayan Zoological Park, both in Darjeeling.

Gangtok and Sikkim

Sikkim's capital is your base for monasteries and mountain views. Day trips into protected areas such as Tsomgo Lake need a separate PAP.

When to go — weather, views and the monsoon

Spring and autumn

Many travellers prefer these months for clearer mountain views. That is our experience — no official page names a single best season.

Winter

In January Darjeeling ranges from about −0.4 °C to 15 °C (31–59 °F). Cold nights, often clear mornings — pack warm layers.

Monsoon

The UK FCDO notes India's monsoon usually runs from June to October, with flash flooding and landslides that can come with little warning. Avoid driving at night and in fog.

Altitude

Darjeeling is at about 2,200 m (7,200 ft). If you have a heart or lung condition, consult your doctor; see CDC or NHS Fit for Travel.

Your India visa comes first

The Sikkim permit is separate from your India visa. Apply only at the official site indianvisaonline.gov.in — beware look-alike sites that charge extra. We are not a visa agency. In November 2026 the Government of India announced that the Tourist Visa is being renamed the Visitor Visa (PIB, 11 Nov 2026); until the official site changes, apply for the visa it shows there.

FAQ

Questions travellers ask

Do foreigners need a permit for Sikkim?

Yes. Foreign visitors need a Restricted Area Permit (RAP) to enter Sikkim, and a Protected Area Permit (PAP) for areas such as Tsomgo Lake, Nathu La and Yumthang. Since January 2026 these are issued online only through the Government of India's e-FRRO system — no paper permits at the border. Check the official portal for the current rules.

How do I get a Sikkim permit online?

Apply yourself through e-FRRO (indianfrro.gov.in) once you are in India and before you reach Sikkim, and check the portal for the current process. We help you plan the dates, but we do not issue permits.

Do I need a permit for Darjeeling?

The Sikkim permit is only for Sikkim. Darjeeling is in West Bengal, so the Sikkim RAP is not about Darjeeling itself — your India visa is what you need there.

How many days do I need for Darjeeling and Sikkim?

Six to seven days: about three in Darjeeling and three around Gangtok, plus the drives from and to Bagdogra. Keep one day spare for weather.

How do I get to Darjeeling?

Fly to Bagdogra (IXB), about 90 km (56 miles) away, or take a train to New Jalpaiguri (NJP). From there it is a drive with your driver, or the toy train for the heritage experience.

Is the Darjeeling toy train worth it?

For many visitors, yes. The Darjeeling Himalayan Railway has been a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 1999. The short joy ride from Darjeeling to Ghum via the Batasia Loop is the easiest way to ride it.

When is the best time to visit Darjeeling and Sikkim?

Many travellers prefer spring and autumn for clearer views. Winter is cold but often clear. Avoid the heaviest monsoon weeks: mountain roads can close after landslides, especially in July and August.
