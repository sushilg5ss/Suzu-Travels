How foreigners book Indian trains: the Foreign Tourist Quota

Indian Railways keeps a quota of seats for foreign tourists, bookable online on IRCTC. These are the rules, from IRCTC's own terms.

  1. Register on IRCTC with an international mobileForeign users add and verify an international mobile number in their IRCTC profile, then use the Foreign Tourist ticket booking option.
  2. Book up to 365 days aheadForeign tourists can book up to 365 days in advance. The general window for everyone else was cut to 60 days from 1 November 2024; the 365-day limit for foreign tourists stayed (PIB, 17 Oct 2024). If you book beyond the general window, your berth is allotted when that window opens.
  3. Enter every passportThe passport number and nationality of each passenger are mandatory at booking. Names cannot be changed later.
  4. Pay by international cardPayment is only by international debit or credit card. The quota fare is 1.5 times the normal fare, plus a service charge per ticket; there are no concessions.
  5. Carry the original passport on boardBring the passport you booked with, with your valid visa. Without it you are treated as travelling without a ticket and charged a penalty of three times the fare. No visa yet? Apply only at the official site indianvisaonline.gov.in — beware look-alike sites that charge extra (our e-Visa guide walks you through it).
  6. Know the cancellation ruleIf you cancel, a flat 50% of the fare is deducted on top of the cancellation charges — online only, up to 4 hours before departure, refunded to the card you paid with.

Which train class to book

The Foreign Tourist Quota covers seven classes, depending on the train. For a first trip we suggest an air-conditioned class.

EC and CC — day trains

Executive Chair Car (EC) and AC Chair Car (CC) are reclining seats on fast day trains — the usual choice for Delhi–Agra or Delhi–Kalka.

1A, 2A, 3A — overnight AC

First AC, AC 2-tier and AC 3-tier are sleeping berths in air-conditioned coaches, from the most private (1A) to the most shared (3A).

SL and 2S — non-AC

Sleeper (SL) and Second Sitting (2S) are the cheapest and busiest. Fine for short hops if you travel light; not our pick for a first trip.

Domestic flights in India — and the winter fog

For long legs a domestic flight is the time-saver. The UK Foreign Office's practical airport advice is to allow enough time to complete check-in and security procedures.

In winter, fog in North India delays both flights and trains. The Ministry of Civil Aviation tells airlines to warn passengers in advance about delays and cancellations caused by low visibility, and to cancel upcoming flights if the delay will be more than 3 hours (PIB, 1 Jan 2025). Delhi has runways equipped for low-visibility (CAT III) landings, and in 2024 six airports were certified for them: Delhi, Lucknow, Jaipur, Amritsar, Bengaluru and Kolkata.

What it means for your plan: in the fog season, avoid a tight connection on the same day as an international flight, keep the first morning flexible, and give your driver the flight number so pick-ups follow the real arrival time. Is train travel in India safe?

The UK Foreign Office says: "Train travel in India is generally safe, however accidents happen and have previously resulted in death and serious injury. There are police or guards on trains and stations if you need help. You must carry valid ID." It also advises: do not accept food or drinks from strangers, take care of your passport and valuables as thefts are common, and "avoid people at railway stations offering tickets and tours as you may be scammed."

On the road it warns that "buses are often driven fast and recklessly" and advises avoiding travel at night and in fog. That is why our cars drive in daylight, and why we suggest booking trains online on IRCTC rather than with anyone at the station.

FAQ

Questions travellers ask

Can foreigners book Indian train tickets online?

Yes. Foreign tourists can book on IRCTC under the Foreign Tourist Quota after adding and verifying an international mobile number. You need each passenger's passport number and pay by international debit or credit card.

How far in advance can tourists book trains in India?

Up to 365 days ahead under the Foreign Tourist Quota. The general advance booking window has been 60 days since 1 November 2024, but Indian Railways kept the 365-day limit for foreign tourists (PIB, 17 Oct 2024).

Is the Foreign Tourist Quota more expensive?

Yes — IRCTC charges 1.5 times the normal fare plus a service charge per ticket, with no concessions. Check the current fare on IRCTC when you book.

What ID do I need on Indian trains?

Your original passport — the one whose number you entered at booking — with your valid visa. Without it you are treated as travelling without a ticket and charged a penalty of three times the fare.

What if I cancel a Foreign Tourist Quota ticket?

IRCTC deducts a flat 50% of the fare plus the cancellation charges. You cancel online, up to 4 hours before departure, and the refund goes back to the card you paid with.

Is train travel in India safe for tourists?

The UK Foreign Office calls train travel in India generally safe, notes that accidents happen, and advises guarding your passport and valuables, not accepting food or drinks from strangers and avoiding people at stations offering tickets or tours (page updated 7 Oct 2026).

Do winter flights in India get delayed by fog?

They can. In North India's winter fog season airlines are told to warn passengers of delays and cancellations and to cancel flights delayed by more than 3 hours. Keep a buffer before international connections.
