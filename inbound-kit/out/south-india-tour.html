The route: east coast to west coast, temples first, water last

South India is two different trips joined by the Western Ghats: Tamil Nadu on the east coast, with temples that have been in worship for over a thousand years, and Kerala on the west, green and watery. This route crosses from one to the other once, so every drive moves you forward.

Chennai → Mahabalipuram → Puducherry → Thanjavur → Madurai → Thekkady → Alappuzha backwaters → Kochi. One open-jaw route: you fly into Chennai and out of Kochi, so you never drive back.

Twelve days in South India, day by day

A sample we build from. Add Munnar's tea hills (two more nights), a beach, an Ayurveda stay or the Golden Triangle — the quote changes, the pace stays human.

  1. DAY 1
    Arrive Chennai → Mahabalipuram

    Your driver meets you at Chennai airport (name, photo and car number shared with you before the trip). Rather than staying in the city, we drive south along the coast to Mahabalipuram (Mamallapuram) — quieter, and right by the sea.

  2. DAY 2
    Mahabalipuram: the Pallava coast

    A UNESCO World Heritage Site carved out of rock by the Pallava kings in the 7th and 8th centuries: the rathas (temples shaped like chariots), cave shrines, the giant open-air relief known as the Descent of the Ganges or Arjuna's Penance, and the Shore Temple above the waves. Go early, before the heat and the crowds.

  3. DAY 3
    Mahabalipuram → Puducherry

    Further down the coast to Puducherry, 162 km (101 mi) south of Chennai. The old French quarter by the seafront promenade is a grid of straight, quiet streets lined with colonial buildings, some from the 18th century. An afternoon on foot, an evening by the sea.

  4. DAY 4
    Puducherry → Thanjavur

    A long drive day inland to Thanjavur, the city of the Chola king Rajaraja I. Arrive in time for the Brihadisvara (Big) Temple in the late-afternoon light: begun by him around 1003–1004 and consecrated in 1009–1010, it is still a living temple more than a thousand years later.

  5. DAY 5
    Thanjavur and Darasuram

    The Big Temple again in the morning, then the Airavatesvara temple at Darasuram with its 24 m (79 ft) tower and fine stone carving. Together with Gangaikondacholapuram (completed in 1035) they form UNESCO's "Great Living Chola Temples" — Thanjavur was listed in 1987, the other two added in 2004.

  6. DAY 6
    Thanjavur → Madurai

    South to Madurai, a city with references going back to the 3rd century BC and called "the city that never sleeps". The Meenakshi Amman Temple has 14 gopuram towers of 45–50 m (148–164 ft) and a hall of a thousand sculpted pillars. Tamil Nadu Tourism lists opening hours of 6:00 am–12:30 pm and 4:00–9:00 pm — we check them on the day.

  7. DAY 7
    Madurai → Thekkady

    Across the Western Ghats into Kerala: about 136 km (85 mi) from Madurai airport up to Thekkady, at 900–1,800 m (2,950–5,900 ft). The air cools, the slopes turn to cardamom, pepper, vanilla, cinnamon, tea and coffee. A spice-garden walk in the afternoon.

  8. DAY 8
    Thekkady: Periyar Tiger Reserve

    The reserve covers 777 sq km (300 sq mi) of forest around a 26 sq km (10 sq mi) lake. A two-hour boat trip, run by the Kerala Tourism Development Corporation, is the classic way to watch elephants, gaur, sambar and otters from a safe distance. Tigers are rarely seen — we never promise one.

  9. DAY 9
    Thekkady → the backwaters, houseboat night

    125 km (78 mi) west, about 3 hours, to Alappuzha. Board a kettuvallam, a traditional rice barge rebuilt as a houseboat with bedrooms, a living deck and a kitchen; the crew cooks lunch, dinner and breakfast on board. The boat moors for the night on a network of canals, rivers and lakes stretching over 900 km (560 mi).

  10. DAY 10
    Backwaters → Fort Kochi

    Breakfast on the water, then about an hour north (62 km / 39 mi) to Kochi. Fort Kochi, 10 km (6 mi) from Ernakulam town, is the place to slow down: the Chinese fishing nets, standing for over five centuries, at sunset.

  11. DAY 11
    Fort Kochi and Mattancherry

    On foot and by car: St. Francis Church — India's oldest European church — Santa Cruz Basilica, Mattancherry Palace and the Jewish Synagogue. In the evening, a Kathakali performance; arrive early to watch the make-up.

  12. DAY 12
    Fly out of Kochi

    To Cochin International Airport, 82.8 km (51 mi) and about 2 hours from Alappuzha, less from Fort Kochi. Flying on to Delhi for the Golden Triangle, or home? We time the drive around your flight, with a margin.

When to go: two coasts, two monsoons

This is the part most South India itineraries skip. Tamil Nadu and Kerala get their rain at different times of year, so a trip that joins them has a best window of its own.

Mid-December – February: go

Both coasts are at their best. Tamil Nadu Tourism recommends December to February for Madurai; Kerala Tourism names November–February as its peak season with pleasant weather, and the best months for houseboats and the hills.

October – December: Tamil Nadu's monsoon

The India Meteorological Department calls the north-east monsoon, October to December, Tamil Nadu's chief rainy season — it brings 48% of the state's annual rain, and the season also matters for cyclones on the coast. Possible, but we build late-autumn trips with a flexible day.

June – September: Kerala's big monsoon

The south-west monsoon brings most of Kerala's rain. Green and dramatic, but the UK Foreign Office warns of flash flooding and landslides that can occur with limited warning during the monsoon.

March – May: hot

The plains of Tamil Nadu and the Kerala coast are hot and humid. The hills stay cooler, and it is the best season for wildlife at the Periyar lake — but long temple days in the heat are hard work.

Practical notes for South India

Visa

Apply yourself for the India e-Visa at the official site indianvisaonline.gov.in — beware look-alike sites that charge extra. We are not a visa agency; our e-Visa guide walks you through it.

Safety, from the UK Foreign Office

Avoid travelling at night. Be cautious in tourist areas, where scammers, pickpockets and ticket touts target foreign nationals. Women travelling alone: avoid isolated areas, including beaches, at any time of day. On the coast there are strong currents and most beaches have no flags or lifeguards. Daytime drives, your driver and our office WhatsApp number are how we plan around this.

Temples

Many South Indian temples are living places of worship: dress modestly, leave your shoes at the entrance and follow the rules posted at each temple — access to inner areas and photography rules vary. Your driver or guide will tell you before you go in.

Health

Consult your doctor or an official travel-health site (CDC, NHS Fit for Travel) before you go. Carry water, sunscreen and insect repellent; the plains are warm even in January.

FAQ

Questions travellers ask

How many days do you need for a South India tour?

Ten to fourteen. Twelve days lets you see the Tamil Nadu temples — Mahabalipuram, Thanjavur, Madurai — and Kerala's Thekkady, backwaters and Fort Kochi without rushing. With 8 days or fewer, pick one state: Kerala alone, or the temple towns of Tamil Nadu.

What is the best time to visit South India?

Mid-December to February for a trip that joins Tamil Nadu and Kerala. Tamil Nadu's chief rainy season is the north-east monsoon, October to December (India Meteorological Department); Kerala's big monsoon is June to September. Tamil Nadu Tourism recommends December to February for Madurai, and Kerala Tourism calls November to February its peak season.

Tamil Nadu or Kerala — which one if I have less time?

Tamil Nadu if you love architecture and temple life: the Pallava rock temples of Mahabalipuram, the Chola temples of Thanjavur and Madurai's Meenakshi Temple. Kerala if you want slower days: spice hills, wildlife boats at Periyar, a houseboat night and Fort Kochi. Twelve days gives you both.

Which airport do I fly into and out of?

This route flies into Chennai and out of Cochin International Airport (Kochi), so you never drive back the way you came. It works the other way round too. If you start with the Golden Triangle, you fly from Delhi to Chennai.

Can I combine South India with the Golden Triangle?

Yes — it is a classic two-week India. Spend 4–6 days on Delhi, Agra and Jaipur, then fly south to Chennai and follow this route. We quote both halves together, with one point of contact for the whole trip.

What should I wear in South Indian temples?

Dress modestly, take off your shoes at the entrance and follow the rules posted at each temple — access to inner areas and photography rules vary from temple to temple; your driver or guide will tell you before you go in.

Is South India safe for foreign tourists?

The UK Foreign Office advises avoiding travel at night, being careful of scammers and touts in tourist areas, and — for women alone — avoiding isolated areas, including beaches. Many beaches have strong currents and no lifeguards. We plan daytime drives and you have our office WhatsApp number throughout.
