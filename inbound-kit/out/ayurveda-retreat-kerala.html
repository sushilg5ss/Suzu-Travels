How to choose an Ayurveda centre in Kerala — five steps

Kerala has a great many Ayurveda resorts, centres and hospitals. We don't sell or recommend any of them — this is the checklist we would use ourselves.

  1. Start from the official listKerala Tourism (the Department of Tourism, Government of Kerala) publishes a searchable list of Ayurveda centres at keralatourism.org/ayurveda-centres. You can filter by district, destination and classification, and download the list of approved centres.
  2. Read the classificationUnder the revised scheme the Government of Kerala sanctioned in 2019, Ayur Silver is the category with the minimum facilities and Ayur Diamond the one with the highest. The list also shows Green Leaf and Olive Leaf centres. A classification tells you about facilities, not about results.
  3. Check for NABH accreditationIndia's National Accreditation Board for Hospitals runs an Ayush accreditation programme with the Ministry of Ayush, started in 2009, with standards for Ayurveda hospitals, Panchakarma clinics and wellness centres. Ask the centre whether it is accredited, and for which standard.
  4. Ask who the doctor isIn India only practitioners on the national register may practise Ayurveda. Ask for the doctor's name, qualification and registration, and whether you will see the same doctor throughout.
  5. Ask what the fee coversConsultation, treatments per day, room, vegetarian meals, yoga, airport transfer. Get it in writing, pay the centre directly and keep its confirmation.

When to go: the monsoon season or the dry months?

Kerala has two rainy seasons — the southwest monsoon arrives in June/July with most of the year's rain, and the northeast monsoon brings rain in October/November.

The UK Foreign Office notes that India's monsoon usually runs from June to October, with flash flooding and landslides that can occur with limited warning. Check India Meteorological Department (mausam.imd.gov.in) before you travel.

Health first: what to ask your doctor

Ayurveda treatments are not a substitute for medical care from your own doctor. Consult your doctor before you book — especially if you are pregnant, take regular medication or have a health condition — and tell the centre's doctor about every medicine you take. See CDC or NHS Fit for Travel for India.

Medicines to take home: be careful with herbal medicines you are offered to continue at home. US and Australian health authorities have reported lead in some Ayurvedic products bought abroad (US CDC, 2012; NSW Health, 2014). Show anything you plan to keep taking to your own doctor.

Which visa for an Ayurveda retreat?

Apply yourself at the official site indianvisaonline.gov.in — beware look-alike sites that charge extra. We are not a visa agency.

Holiday with some Ayurveda

A short wellness stay alongside a holiday is usually planned on the e-Tourist visa (30 days, 1 year or 5 years). Indian missions list 'attending a short-term yoga programme' among e-Visa purposes.

Treatment is the sole purpose

India has an Ayush visa for a foreigner 'whose sole purpose is to seek treatment through Ayush systems', including 'therapeutic care and wellness' at an accredited and registered centre. It is on the e-Visa system as the e-Ayush Visa, with an e-Ayush Attendant Visa for a companion.

Not sure which?

Read the purposes listed for each e-Visa type on indianvisaonline.gov.in, or ask the nearest Indian embassy. Our India e-Visa guide explains the steps.

What to expect when you arrive

At a dedicated centre, expect to begin with a consultation with its Ayurveda doctor, who suggests a treatment plan and how long it should run. Ask for this plan in writing, with the number of treatment days, before you commit to a long stay.

Food is part of the programme: Kerala Tourism notes that Ayurveda recommends 'a healthy vegetarian diet', and in the monsoon month a herbal rice gruel (Karkidaka kanji) is traditional. Panchakarma, which Kerala Tourism describes as 'a therapeutic way extolled by Ayurveda, for eliminating toxic elements from the body', is usually a longer, structured programme — ask the doctor whether it suits you.

Leave a free day at each end of the stay: one to arrive and rest, one before a long flight home.

FAQ

Questions travellers ask

What is the best time for an Ayurveda retreat in Kerala?

Traditionally the monsoon month of Karkidakam (July–August): Kerala Tourism writes that the body is considered highly receptive to Ayurveda treatments then. Expect daily rain. December–February is mild and comfortable and Kerala's peak season, a good time to combine a stay with sightseeing.

How long should an Ayurveda retreat be?

There is no single answer: the centre's Ayurveda doctor suggests the length after a first consultation, and longer programmes such as Panchakarma take more time than a few massages. Ask for the plan, with the number of treatment days, in writing before you book your flights.

How do I know an Ayurveda centre in Kerala is genuine?

Look it up on Kerala Tourism's official list of classified Ayurveda centres (keralatourism.org/ayurveda-centres), ask whether it holds NABH Ayush accreditation, and ask for the doctor's name, qualification and registration. A listing is a starting point, not a guarantee of results.

Do I need a special visa for Ayurveda treatment in India?

If treatment is the sole purpose of your trip, India has a separate Ayush visa, available as the e-Ayush Visa for accredited centres. A short wellness stay during a holiday is usually planned on the e-Tourist visa. Check the purposes on the official site indianvisaonline.gov.in — beware look-alike sites. We are not a visa agency.

What is Panchakarma?

Kerala Tourism describes Panchakarma as 'a therapeutic way extolled by Ayurveda, for eliminating toxic elements from the body'. It is a structured programme planned by the centre's doctor, usually with a set diet. Ask the doctor whether it suits you, and talk to your own doctor first.

Is Ayurveda safe?

Ayurveda is not a substitute for care from your own doctor. Consult your doctor before you book, tell the centre's doctor about every medicine you take, and be careful with herbal medicines to take home — US and Australian health authorities have reported lead in some Ayurvedic products bought abroad.

Can I combine an Ayurveda retreat with a Kerala tour or the Golden Triangle?

Yes. You can spend a few days on the backwaters or in the tea hills before or after your stay, or start in Delhi, Agra and Jaipur and fly south. We plan the car, driver and hotels around the dates your centre gives you.
