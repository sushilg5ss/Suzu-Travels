Quick answer

Serolsar Lake trek, at a glance

Serolsar Lake is a small sacred lake about 5 km from Jalori Pass (about 3,120 m) in Kullu district, reached on an easy forest trail with a 360-degree viewpoint on the way. The walk takes about three hours each way and needs no permit. March to November is best; in winter snow and ice cover the trail and the Jalori road can close.

How the day runs

Your Serolsar Lake day, step by step

  1. Send your dates

    WhatsApp us your dates and group. We check the Jalori road status for those days.

  2. Stay in Jibhi or Tirthan

    A cottage or homestay by the river — we book it together with your cab.

  3. Drive up to Jalori Pass

    Morning drive Jibhi → Jalori Pass, about 12 km on a steep hill road.

  4. Walk to the lake

    With a local guide: the 360° viewpoint, then the forest trail to the lake and the Budhi Nagin temple — about 3–3.5 hours up.

  5. Lunch and turn back

    Carry your own tiffin and water (no shops on the trail). Turn back by early afternoon — about 3 hours down.

  6. Back to Jibhi

    Down to Jalori and on to your stay. Next day: Raghupur Fort, Jibhi waterfall or a trout-fishing day in Tirthan.

Jibhi → Jalori → Serolsar

  • By cab → Jibhi to Jalori Pass, about 12 km of steep hill road.
  • On foot → about 2 km to the 360° viewpoint.
  • On foot → about 3.3 km more to the lake and the Budhi Nagin temple.
  • Back → the same way; leave the lake by early afternoon.

Safety

What we check before we book your walk

Suzu Travels is an HP Tourism registered travel agent (Reg. No. DTO-MND-11-243/2022). We arrange the guide with registered local operators and plan around these checks:

  • Road status firstIn winter we check the Jalori road the same morning; after snowfall the highway is often open to 4x4 only at first, or closed.
  • Local hill driverNo self-drive on the Jibhi–Jalori stretch in snow or ice — a local driver who knows the road.
  • Guide and spikes in winterWinter walks only with a guide and microspikes; the trail has block ice and drop-offs in places.
  • Turn-back timeLeave the lake by about 3 pm to reach Jalori before dark. Carry 2 litres of water — there is no source until the temple.
  • Respect the lakeNo touching the water, no camping by the lake, and carry all litter back.
  • Warm layersEven in summer — the pass is above 3,000 m and the weather turns fast.

FAQ

Serolsar Lake trek — questions

How long is the Serolsar Lake trek?

About 5 km one way from Jalori Pass, so about 10 km return. Most people take 3–3.5 hours up and about 3 hours back.

Is the Serolsar Lake trek easy?

Yes. It is an easy forest walk with gentle ups and downs and one short steep climb to the 360-degree viewpoint. Good shoes help.

How high is Serolsar Lake?

About 3,100 m (10,170 ft). Jalori Pass, where the walk starts, is about 3,120 m.

Can I do the Serolsar Lake trek in winter?

Only when the Jalori road is open. From December to February the trail has snow and ice and the highway over Jalori (NH-305) can close after heavy snow, so go with a guide and check that morning.

Do I need a permit for Serolsar Lake?

No permit is needed for the walk. Please do not touch the lake water — it is sacred and fenced.

Can I camp at Serolsar Lake?

Camping is not allowed around the lake. Stay in Jibhi or Tirthan and do it as a day trek.

How far is Jalori Pass from Jibhi?

About 12 km by a steep hill road, roughly 45 minutes to an hour in good conditions.
