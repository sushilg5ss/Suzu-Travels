Quick answer

Lamadugh trek, at a glance

Lamadugh is a high meadow at about 3,000 m (9,900 ft) above Manali, reached on a steep forest trail from Old Manali in 4–6 hours up and 2–3 hours down. It is an easy-to-moderate day hike with views of the upper Beas valley and the snow peaks around it. Carry all your water, register on the Kullu trekking portal and start by 9 am.

Where
Above Old Manali and Dhungri, Kullu district — forest trail to an open meadow
Height
Meadow about 3,000 m (HP trekking portal: 3,018 m); about 950 m of climbing from Manali
Distance & time
About 6 km one way; 4–6 hours up, 2–3 hours down
Best time
Apr–Jun and Sep–Nov; Dec–Feb snow on the upper trail — guide and clear day only
Rules
Register on the Kullu trekking portal; no plastic, no fires; back before dark

How the day works

A Lamadugh hike with Suzu, hour by hour

  1. Night before

    We check the weather and snow on the trail and register your group on the Kullu trekking portal.

  2. 8–9 am: meet your guide

    At your Old Manali or Manali hotel. Fill 2 litres of water each — there is none on the trail.

  3. The climb

    Steady, often steep forest trail through deodar, pine and oak above Dhungri, with short rests.

  4. Around 12–1 pm: the meadow

    Lunch on the open Lamadugh meadow with views across the upper Beas valley to the snow peaks.

  5. Start down by 2 pm

    The descent is quicker but slippery — take it slowly, especially on wet or icy sections.

  6. 5–6 pm: back in Manali

    Cab back to your hotel if you stay outside Old Manali, or on to the rest of your trip.

What to carry

  • 2 litres of water each and a packed lunch
  • Shoes with good grip; microspikes in winter (arranged on request)
  • Warm layers and a rain shell, even in May
  • Sun cap, sunscreen and a small first-aid kit

Polythene is banned in Himachal — take all your waste back down. Add a paragliding flight or a snow day to the same trip.

Going in winter?

From about December to February the upper part of the trail is under snow. We go only on clear days with a local guide, start early and turn round by early afternoon — even if that is short of the meadow. After heavy snowfall the upper trail is not attempted.

FAQ

Lamadugh trek — questions

How long is the Lamadugh trek?

About 6 km one way on the usual route from Old Manali. Most people take 4–6 hours up and 2–3 hours down, so plan a full day. Some operators count other routes and quote longer round-trip distances.

How high is Lamadugh?

The meadow is at about 3,000 m (around 9,900 ft); the HP trekking portal lists 3,018 m. You climb roughly 950 m from Manali town.

Where does the Lamadugh trek start?

From Old Manali or from the Dhungri / Hadimba forest above Manali. Both trails meet in the forest below the meadow; your guide picks the one that suits your hotel.

Can I do the Lamadugh trek in winter?

From about December to February the upper trail is under snow. Go only with a local guide on a clear day and be ready to turn back. For a sure snow day, pick Sethan or snow activities in Manali.

Is there water on the trail?

No. Carry at least 2 litres each from Manali; there are no shops or taps on the way.

Do I need a permit for Lamadugh?

There is no separate permit, but trekkers in Kullu district are asked to register on the HP trekking portal before they start. We do it for our guests.

Is Lamadugh good for beginners?

Yes, if you are fit enough for a steep 5–6 hour climb. Young children and anyone with knee trouble should choose the shorter Jogini Waterfall trek.

Can we camp at Lamadugh?

Yes, from about April to November, with a guide and tents arranged on request. There is no camping in the snow season. See also camping in Manali.
