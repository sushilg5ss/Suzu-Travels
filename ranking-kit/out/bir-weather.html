Bir (landing fields, about 1,400 m) and Billing (take-off, about 2,400 m, 14 km up the hill) sit below the Dhauladhar in Kangra district. Bir has mild days for most of the year and cold winter nights; Billing is about 1,000 m higher and several degrees colder, with snow in some winters. For paragliding, October–November and March–May are the best months; December–February flights run when the weather allows; and flying is closed from 15 July to 15 September every monsoon by district order. July and August are by far the wettest months.

  • Best flyingMid-September–November and March–May.
  • Closed15 July – 15 September (monsoon, district order).
  • WinterDecember–February: open when weather allows; cold at take-off.
  • WettestJuly–August — very heavy rain.
  • Daily patternMornings are best; cloud and wind often build after midday.

Flying day

How the weather decides your flight

You ride 14 km up a narrow road to Billing, take off at about 2,400 m and glide down to the Chowgan fields at Bir, about 1,000 m lower, in 15–30 minutes. Mornings are the safest bet: cloud and wind often build after midday, and on some days flights stop early — on 3 October 2026, for example, no flights ran after 11 am when cloud rolled in.

Plan two days, so the second morning is a spare if the weather grounds the flight. Tandem flights need riders aged 12+ and 30 kg+ under HP Aero Sports rules. Wear closed shoes and a warm layer — it is much colder at take-off than in Bir.

FAQ

Bir Billing weather: frequently asked questions

What is the weather in Bir Billing today?

The top of this page shows the current temperature and the next days' forecast for Bir, Billing and Palampur (MET Norway data). The pilots make the final weather call each morning.

When is paragliding closed in Bir Billing?

From 15 July to 15 September every monsoon, by district order.

What is the best time for paragliding in Bir?

October–November and March–May.

Can you paraglide in Bir in winter?

Yes, from December to February when the weather allows, but it is cold at take-off and the days are short — keep a spare morning.

Does it snow in Bir?

Rarely in Bir itself; Billing, about 1,000 m higher, gets snow in some winters, and the Dhauladhar behind is white.

Why do flights stop after midday?

Cloud and wind often build in the afternoon, so mornings are the safest time to fly.
