The Library of Tibetan Works and Archives (LTWA) is a Tibetan library and study centre founded by the Dalai Lama in 1970, in the Gangchen Kyishong compound between lower Dharamsala and McLeod Ganj — about 20 minutes' walk down Jogibara Road from McLeod Ganj. It holds Tibetan books, manuscripts and artefacts, and runs Buddhist philosophy classes for the public: taught by qualified geshes in Tibetan with English translation, on weekdays (Wednesday is logic and debate), each with a short meditation. The 2026 year opened on 11 March, with a summer break from 14 to 31 July and a winter break from 11 December. Timings change around the Dalai Lama's teachings, so confirm with the library before you go.

Classes have usually been held mid-morning, in two one-hour sessions, but the schedule moves around the Dalai Lama's public teachings — check with the library on arrival.

Visiting

Visiting the library

The library sits inside Gangchen Kyishong, the compound of the Central Tibetan Administration, on the road between lower Dharamsala and McLeod Ganj. Alongside the reading rooms and archives it keeps Tibetan manuscripts, thangkas and other artefacts. Dress modestly, keep voices low, and ask before taking photographs inside.

Getting there

How to get there

On foot: about 20 minutes downhill from McLeod Ganj on Jogibara Road; the walk back is uphill. By taxi or auto-rickshaw: a few minutes from McLeod Ganj or the lower town. From further away: fly into Kangra (Gaggal) airport or take the overnight bus from Delhi — see Delhi to Dharamshala for foreign visitors.

More study

Other places to learn in Dharamsala

For a residential course, see Tushita Meditation Centre above McLeod Ganj and the Vipassana centre next to it. For Tibetan arts, visit the studios at Norbulingka Institute. The Dalai Lama's public teachings are held at the main temple in McLeod Ganj a few times a year.

FAQ

Library of Tibetan Works and Archives: common questions

Where is the Library of Tibetan Works and Archives?

In Gangchen Kyishong, the Central Tibetan Administration compound between lower Dharamsala and McLeod Ganj, about 20 minutes' walk from McLeod Ganj.

Can tourists attend classes at the Tibetan Library?

Yes, the Buddhist philosophy classes are open to the public, with English translation; confirm the current schedule with the library.

When are classes held?

On weekdays, except official holidays; the 2026 year opened on 11 March, with breaks from 14 to 31 July and from 11 December.

Who founded the Library of Tibetan Works and Archives?

The 14th Dalai Lama, in 1970.

Is there a certificate?

Yes, for completing a semester's topic with at least 80% attendance.
