Badrinath lies at about 3,300 m on the Alaknanda, between the Nar and Narayan ranges, about 45 km beyond Joshimath — and unlike Kedarnath it is reached by road. In the yatra season (May–October) days are about 12–16 °C and nights cold, near freezing early and late in the season. From about November to April it lies under deep snow.In 2026 the temple doors close on 16 November at 9:37 pm; worship then moves down the valley for the winter. May–June and September–October are the best months; July–August bring heavy rain and landslides on the road from Rishikesh.

Opening, closing and Mana

When Badrinath opens and closes, and the road

The doors of Badrinath open each year in late April or May, on a date announced on Basant Panchami, and close in November; in 2026 they close on 16 November at 9:37 pm. Through the winter, worship moves down the valley to Pandukeshwar and Joshimath, and Badrinath and the villages above it are snowbound.

The road from Rishikesh runs through Devprayag, Rudraprayag, Karnaprayag and Joshimath — around 295 km, usually two days. In the monsoon, landslides often stop traffic for hours; late in the season, the first snow can arrive in October. Mana, about 3 km beyond Badrinath, is open in the yatra season.

Before you go

Badrinath yatra: what the weather means for you

Clothing: a warm jacket, thermal layers, cap and gloves in every month; a rain jacket from late June to September.

Altitude: Badrinath is at about 3,300 m. Break the drive at Joshimath or Pipalkoti, walk slowly, and drink plenty of water.

Buffer: keep a spare day in the monsoon and late in the season; landslides and snow can close the highway at short notice. Cab: Char Dham yatra taxi.
