Hemkund Sahib is a Sikh shrine beside a glacial lake at about 4,630 m in Chamoli district, reached on foot from Govindghat via Ghangaria (about 3,050 m). It is open only in the warmer months — usually from late May to early October (in 2026 it closed on 10 October) — and is under deep snow the rest of the year. Even in the season it is cold: days at the lake are only around 5–8 °C and nights near or below freezing, with snow lying beside the path well into June. The monsoon (July–August) brings rain, fog and landslide risk on the road to Govindghat, but it is also when the nearby Valley of Flowers is at its best. June and September usually give the best balance of open paths and clear weather.

The route and the season

Hemkund Sahib yatra: the route, snow and the Valley of Flowers

From Govindghat, about 20 km beyond Joshimath on the Badrinath road, the route climbs to Ghangaria, the base village, and then about 6 km — and more than 1,500 m of height — up a steep path to the lake and gurdwara. Most pilgrims stay the night at Ghangaria and go up and back in a day, starting early before the afternoon cloud. Ponies are available, and a helicopter service runs between Govindghat and Ghangaria in the season, weather permitting.

Early in the season, snow lies along the upper path and the lake may be partly frozen; the opening date is announced each spring once the path is cleared. The Valley of Flowers is a separate walk of about 3–4 km from Ghangaria and is usually open from June to October, with the most flowers in July and August. In the monsoon, landslides often hold up the road between Joshimath and Govindghat — keep spare days.

Before you go

Hemkund Sahib yatra: what the weather means for you

Clothing: warm layers, a waterproof jacket, gloves and a cap even in June; good walking shoes with grip for the wet, steep path.

Altitude: Hemkund Sahib is at about 4,630 m. Spend the night at Ghangaria, climb slowly, drink water, and come down if you feel unwell. Start early — cloud and rain usually build after midday.

Getting there: drive from Rishikesh to Govindghat via Joshimath (a long day, often two); keep spare days in the monsoon. Cab: Char Dham yatra taxi.

Hemkund Sahib weather: frequently asked questions

What is the weather at Hemkund Sahib today?

The top of this page shows the current temperature and the next days' forecast for Hemkund Sahib, Ghangaria and Govindghat (MET Norway data).

When does Hemkund Sahib open and close?

Usually from late May to early October; in 2026 it closed for winter on 10 October. The opening date is announced each spring.

How cold is Hemkund Sahib in June?

Days at the lake are around 7 °C and nights near freezing (an estimate), with snow beside the path.

Is there snow at Hemkund Sahib in the yatra season?

Yes, especially in late May and June, when snow lies along the upper path and the lake may be partly frozen.

When is the best time to visit the Valley of Flowers?

July and August for the most flowers; it is usually open from June to October.

How far is Hemkund Sahib from Ghangaria?

About 6 km on a steep path, climbing more than 1,500 m.
