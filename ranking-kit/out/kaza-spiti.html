Kaza: base camp for the whole of Spiti

Kaza is the main town of Spiti, at about 3,650 m on the Spiti river. It has most of the valley's hotels and homestays, restaurants, ATMs, a fuel pump and a hospital, so it is the natural base for day trips to Key Monastery (~14 km), Kibber and Chicham (~18–25 km), Langza, Hikkim and Komic (~16–22 km), Dhankar (~32 km), Tabo (~47 km) and the Pin valley. Kaza is about 420 km from Shimla via Kinnaur (open all year, two days) and about 200 km from Manali over Kunzum Pass (open roughly June–October). Plan at least 3 nights in Kaza. In winter (December–February) nights fall to around −20 °C, many hotels close and water freezes, but heated homestays stay open for snow and snow leopard trips.

The town

Things to do in Kaza

Kaza is split by a stream into old Kaza, with whitewashed houses and the bazaar, and new Kaza, with government offices and newer hotels. The colourful Sakya Tangyud monastery in town is worth a visit in the evening, and the old lanes, chortens and willow groves along the river are good for a slow walk on your first, acclimatising day.

Kaza is also where you top up fuel, cash and supplies before heading to the villages. Mobile coverage is good in town but patchy beyond; tell family your plans before you leave for the high villages.

Winter

Kaza in winter

From December to February Kaza is deep in the cold: daytime temperatures often stay below freezing and nights drop to around −20 °C. The Manali–Kunzum road is closed, so Kaza is reached only via Shimla and Kinnaur. Many hotels shut, but a few heated homestays stay open, and this is the season for snow leopard trips from Kibber and for frozen-river landscapes. Travel with a driver experienced on ice, carry serious winter clothing and allow spare days. Read Spiti in winter and winter Spiti guide 2026-27.

Getting there

How to reach Kaza

Via Shimla and Kinnaur (all year): Shimla → Narkanda → Rampur → Reckong Peo/Kalpa → Nako → Tabo → Kaza, about 420 km over two or three days. Via Manali (about June–October): Manali → Atal Tunnel → Gramphu → Batal → Kunzum Pass → Losar → Kaza, about 200 km in 7–9 hours. The Shimla route allows gradual acclimatisation. See Spiti via Shimla or Manali and the Himachal distance chart. Foreign nationals need an Inner Line Permit for the Kinnaur–Spiti border stretch.

FAQ

Kaza: frequently asked questions

How high is Kaza?

About 3,650 m above sea level.

How far is Kaza from Manali?

About 200 km over Kunzum Pass, 7–9 hours, open roughly from June to October.

How far is Kaza from Shimla?

About 420 km via Kinnaur, usually over two or three days; this route is open all year.

Are there ATMs and fuel in Kaza?

Yes, Kaza has ATMs and a fuel pump, but fill up and carry some cash before heading to the villages.

Can I visit Kaza in winter?

Yes, via Shimla and Kinnaur, but it is extremely cold; many hotels close and heated homestays are the main option.

How many days should I stay in Kaza?

At least three nights to see Key, Kibber, Langza and Dhankar without rushing.
