Nako is a small village at about 3,660 m in upper Kinnaur, built around a willow-ringed lake just off the Shimla–Kaza highway. The lake reflects the surrounding peaks on calm days, turns gold with the willows in October and is often frozen in mid-winter. Beside it stands the Nako monastery, a group of old temples linked by tradition to Rinchen Zangpo's time in the 11th century, with old murals inside. Most travellers spend a night here between Kalpa / Reckong Peo and Tabo / Kaza (Kaza is about 105 km on), which also helps with acclimatisation. The road via Kinnaur is usually open through the year, with closures after heavy snow or landslides.

What to see

Nako lake, the monastery and the village

Nako lake is small — you can walk around it in a few minutes — but on a still morning it holds a perfect reflection of the snow peaks. In October the willows and poplars turn gold; from about December to February the lake is usually frozen.

The Nako monastery is a cluster of old, low temples beside the lake. Tradition links it to the time of the translator Rinchen Zangpo in the 11th century, and the dim halls keep old wall paintings and clay images. Visit respectfully, without flash.

The village itself is a maze of stone-and-timber houses, prayer wheels and stacked firewood. A short climb above it gives a view over the lake, the fields and the Hangrang valley.

Where Nako fits on a Kinnaur–Spiti trip

Before you go

Altitude, roads and winter

At 3,660 m, take it easy on the first evening, drink plenty of water and avoid alcohol. The stretch around Malling is prone to landslides and slides of loose rock; travel in daylight with a driver who knows the road.

In winter Nako is cold and very quiet; most guesthouses close, but a few homestays stay open. Snow can close the road for a day or two at a time. Foreign nationals need an Inner Line Permit for parts of this border area — we arrange it as part of the trip.

FAQ

Nako: frequently asked questions

How high is Nako?

About 3,660 m above sea level.

Does Nako lake freeze?

Usually yes, from about December to February.

How far is Nako from Kaza?

About 105 km, via Tabo; Tabo is about 60 km from Nako.

Is Nako worth a night's stay?

Yes — it breaks the long drive into Spiti and helps you acclimatise.

Can I visit Nako in winter?

Yes, the Kinnaur road usually stays open, but it is very cold, few places are open and snow can close the road for a day or two.

Do I need a permit for Nako?

Indian citizens do not; foreign nationals need an Inner Line Permit for parts of this border area, which we arrange.
