Langza, Hikkim and Komic are three high villages on the plateau above Kaza in the Spiti valley, joined by a single loop road that most travellers drive in half a day.Langza is known for its tall Buddha statue facing Chau Chau Kang Nilda and for marine fossils found in the surrounding slopes. Hikkim (about 4,400 m) has one of the highest post offices in the world (PIN 172114), open since 1983, where you can post a letter or card home. Komic, a few kilometres on, is often described as one of the highest villages connected by a motorable road and has the old Sakya-school Tangyud Monastery on the edge of a canyon. All three are roughly 15–20 km by road from Kaza. Go between June and October; in winter the road is often snowbound and the post office closes.

The three villages

What to see in Langza, Hikkim and Komic

Langza sits on a wide grassy bowl below the peak of Chau Chau Kang Nilda. A large, colourful statue of the Buddha looks out over the valley from the edge of the village, and the slopes around it are known for fossils of ammonites and other sea creatures from the time this land lay under the ancient Tethys sea. Fossils are part of the landscape and the local economy — look, photograph, but do not dig or carry them away.

Hikkim, at about 4,400 m, is a small farming village famous for its post office — one of the highest in the world, with PIN code 172114, run by the same postmaster since it opened in 1983. Visitors post cards to themselves and their families; letters go down the valley on foot or by vehicle, so they take time. Hikkim was also once listed as the world's highest polling station.

Komic, about 4 km beyond Hikkim, is one of the highest villages in Asia reached by road. Its Tangyud Monastery (also called Komic Lundup Chemmo) belongs to the Sakya school — one of only two Sakya monasteries in Spiti — and sits on the lip of a canyon with wide views of the plateau.

Plan the loop

How to visit: route, time and distances

Most people drive the loop from Kaza as a half-day trip: up to Langza, across to Hikkim and Komic, and back down to Kaza (or the other way round). Allow 3–5 hours with stops. Distances are approximate and the road is narrow, partly unpaved and steep in places.

Before you go

Altitude, weather and winter access

At 4,400–4,500 m the air holds much less oxygen than in Delhi. Spend at least one night at Kaza (or two nights in the valley) before driving up, walk slowly, drink plenty of water, and come down if you get a severe headache, breathlessness at rest or confusion.

June to October is the season: dry, sunny days and cold nights. From about November to April snow often blocks the loop road, the post office closes for the winter, and Spiti itself is reached only from the Shimla–Kinnaur side. Winter trips are possible with a local driver used to snow, but plan for closed roads. See Spiti in winter and the 2026-27 winter guide.

Getting there

Reaching Kaza

Via Shimla and Kinnaur (open most of the year): Shimla – Rampur – Reckong Peo – Nako – Tabo – Kaza, usually over 3–4 days with stops, which also helps you acclimatise. FAQ

Langza, Hikkim and Komic: frequently asked questions

Is Hikkim the highest post office in the world?

It is one of the highest in the world, at about 4,400 m, with PIN code 172114. It has been open since 1983 and closes in winter.

How far are Langza, Hikkim and Komic from Kaza?

Roughly 15–20 km each by road; the loop through all three takes 3–5 hours from Kaza with stops.

Can I send a postcard from Hikkim?

Yes, in season. Post travels down the valley slowly, so expect it to take days or weeks.

Is Komic the highest village in the world?

It is often described as one of the highest villages reached by a motorable road, at about 4,500 m or more.

Can I take fossils from Langza?

No — look and photograph only. Removing fossils damages the site and the villagers' heritage.

When can I visit Langza, Hikkim and Komic?

June to October is best. In winter the road is often blocked by snow and the post office is closed.
