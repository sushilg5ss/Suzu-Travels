Yamunotri, the source shrine of the Yamuna, stands at about 3,290 m in Uttarkashi district — about 6 km on foot (or by pony or palki) from the road head at Janki Chatti. In the yatra season (May–October) days are about 12–16 °C and nights cold, near freezing early and late in the season. From about November to April it is under snow.In 2026 the temple doors close on 11 November at 10:28 am (Bhai Dooj); the goddess spends the winter at Kharsali village, and the doors reopen on Akshaya Tritiya, usually in late April or May. May–June and September–October are the best months; July–August bring heavy rain and slippery paths.

Opening, closing and the walk

When Yamunotri opens and closes, and the walk up

The doors of Yamunotri open each year on Akshaya Tritiya, usually in late April or May, and close on Bhai Dooj in the Diwali festival days; in 2026 that is 11 November at 10:28 am. Through the winter the goddess is worshipped at Kharsali, a village across the river from Janki Chatti.

Most people sleep at Barkot, drive about 45 km to Janki Chatti early in the morning, and walk the last 6 km — steep and stony in places — or go by pony or palki. Beside the temple is the hot spring of Surya Kund. In the monsoon the path is slippery and landslides can block the road below; in winter the path is under snow.

Before you go

Yamunotri yatra: what the weather means for you

Clothing: a warm jacket, thermal layers, cap and gloves in every month; a rain jacket from late June to September; good walking shoes for the 6 km path.

Start early: leave Barkot at dawn so you are back down at Janki Chatti before the afternoon cloud and rain.

Buffer: keep a spare day in the monsoon and late in the season; landslides and snow can close the road at short notice. Cab: Char Dham yatra taxi.
