Malana is a village of a little under 2,000 people at roughly 2,650 m in a side valley of the Parvati, reached by a narrow road from Jari (between Bhuntar and Kasol) and then a steep 1–1.5 hour walk. It governs itself through a village council under the local deity, Jamlu, and speaks its own language, Kanashi. Visitors must not touch houses, temples, walls or people, must ask before taking photographs, and cannot stay overnight inside the village — plan it as a day trip from Kasol or Jari. Cannabis is illegal in India under the NDPS Act, whatever you may have heard about Malana. Go in May–June or September–November; in winter the path is snowy and the village very cold.

Before you go

Malana village rules for visitors

Malana's rules come from its council and its deity, and the villagers take them seriously. Signboards at the entry repeat them. Follow them and you will be welcome; break them and you may be asked to pay a penalty set by the village.

RuleWhat it means in practice
Do not touchDo not touch houses, walls, temples, sacred stones or people. Keep to the paths.
Temples are off-limitsLook from outside; do not enter or lean on temple buildings.
Paying for thingsPut money on the counter or where the seller shows you, and take goods the same way.
PhotographsAsk before photographing people; some places may not be photographed at all.
No overnight staysVisitors are not allowed to stay inside the village; leave in time to reach Jari or Kasol before dark.
Respect the councilDisputes are settled by the village council; stay polite and follow instructions.

On cannabis: Malana is often linked with charas, but possessing, buying or using cannabis is an offence in India under the Narcotic Drugs and Psychotropic Substances (NDPS) Act, with heavy penalties. Do not carry or buy anything.

The village

What makes Malana different

Malana is one of the most isolated old villages in Himachal. Its people speak Kanashi, a language found nowhere else, and decide village matters through a council that acts in the name of the deity Jamlu. The houses are tall wood-and-stone buildings with slate roofs, built in the traditional kath-kuni style of the Kullu hills. Local stories link the villagers to the soldiers of Alexander the Great — a legend, not history, but one you will hear often. The Chanderkhani pass above the village is an old crossing to Naggar on the Kullu side and is now a trekking route — see our Chandrakhani pass trek guide.

Day plan

A simple day trip from Kasol

Leave Kasol by 8–9 am, drive to Jari and on to the road-end, and walk up to Malana by late morning. Spend an hour or two walking through the village on its paths, eat at one of the small places outside the restricted area, and start down by early afternoon to be back in Kasol before dark. Add Manikaran or Tosh on another day.

FAQ

Malana village: common questions

How do I reach Malana village?

Go to Jari on the Bhuntar–Kasol road, take a local taxi up the Malana valley road to the road-end, then walk about 1–1.5 hours up a steep path.

Can tourists stay overnight in Malana?

No, visitors are not allowed to stay inside the village; plan a day trip and sleep in Kasol, Jari or Manikaran.

Why can't you touch things in Malana?

The village council and its deity, Jamlu, treat outsiders touching houses, temples and people as a breach of purity; visitors who do so may be asked to pay a penalty.

Is cannabis legal in Malana?

No. Possessing, buying or using cannabis is an offence in India under the NDPS Act, in Malana as anywhere else.

What language is spoken in Malana?

Kanashi, a language spoken only in Malana; most villagers also understand Hindi.

When is the best time to visit Malana?

May–June and September–November; avoid the monsoon and go in winter only with a local guide in clear weather.
