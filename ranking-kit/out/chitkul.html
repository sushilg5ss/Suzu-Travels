Chitkul is a village at about 3,450 m at the head of the Baspa valley in Kinnaur, about 28 km beyond Sangla and roughly 250 km (8–10 hours) from Shimla. It is the last inhabited village on this road before the Indo-Tibet border; the area beyond is controlled by the ITBP and closed to visitors. See the wooden Mathi Devi temples, walk along the Baspa, and eat at the dhabas that call themselves India's last. Indian visitors need no permit. Go in April–June or September–October. In winter the Sangla–Chitkul road usually closes after heavy snow from about mid-December until March, and most guesthouses shut — for a winter Kinnaur trip, base yourself at Kalpa instead.

Getting there

How to reach Chitkul

There is one road in: up the Sutlej from Shimla to Karcham, then up the Baspa valley through Sangla and Rakchham. Most people break the journey for a night at Sarahan, Sangla or Kalpa — both for the long drive and to get used to the height.

Karcham → Sangla~18 kmNarrow, steep road up the gorge; road work in places

By bus: HRTC runs buses from Reckong Peo and from Sangla to Chitkul, usually two or three a day in season, and back in the morning and afternoon — timings change by season, so check locally. By car: use a hill-experienced driver; the Karcham–Sangla stretch is the hardest. Route details: Shimla to Kalpa · Delhi to Kalpa.

Stay and health

Where to stay, altitude and practical tips

Stay: Chitkul has simple guesthouses and homestays, plus camps in summer; Sangla and Rakchham have more choice. Many places close from late November to March. Altitude: at about 3,450 m, the air is thin — come up slowly, ideally after a night at Sangla or Kalpa, drink water and avoid alcohol on the first night; if you have a medical condition, ask your doctor before going. Money and fuel: no ATM in Chitkul and no petrol pump on the last stretch — fill up and draw cash at Reckong Peo or before. Phone: expect weak or no data.

FAQ

Chitkul: common questions

Where is Chitkul?

At the head of the Baspa valley in Kinnaur district, Himachal Pradesh, about 28 km beyond Sangla.

Is Chitkul the last village of India?

It is the last inhabited village on this road before the Indo-Tibet border; beyond it the area is controlled by the ITBP and closed to visitors.

How far is Chitkul from Shimla?

About 250 km, usually 8–10 hours by road via Narkanda, Rampur, Karcham and Sangla.

Can I visit Chitkul in December or January?

Usually not: heavy snow normally closes the road beyond Sangla from about mid-December until March, and most guesthouses shut. Visit Kalpa in winter instead.

Do I need a permit for Chitkul?

Indian visitors need no permit to reach the village; going beyond towards the border is not allowed.

What is the best time to visit Chitkul?

April to June and September to mid-October.
