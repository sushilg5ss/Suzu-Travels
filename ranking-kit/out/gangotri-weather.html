Gangotri stands at about 3,100 m on the Bhagirathi, in Uttarkashi district — a road shrine, about 100 km beyond Uttarkashi. In the yatra season (May–October) days are about 13–17 °C and nights cold, near or below freezing at the start and end of the season. From about November to April it is under snow, and the road above Harsil is often closed. In 2026 the temple doors close on 10 November (Annakoot), and the goddess spends the winter at Mukhba, near Harsil; the doors reopen on Akshaya Tritiya, usually in late April or May. May–June and September–October are the best months; July–August bring heavy rain and landslides.

Opening, closing and the road

When Gangotri opens and closes, and the road

The doors of Gangotri open each year on Akshaya Tritiya, usually in late April or May, and close on Annakoot (Govardhan Puja) in the Diwali festival days; in 2026 that is 10 November. Through the winter the goddess is worshipped at Mukhba (Mukhwa) village, near Harsil, which is on a motorable road.

The road from Rishikesh runs through Chamba and Uttarkashi — about 145 km to Uttarkashi and another 100 km to Gangotri, so most people stop a night in Uttarkashi or Harsil. In the monsoon, landslides often hold up traffic; in winter, snow usually closes the road somewhere above Harsil. The Gaumukh trek (about 18 km each way from Gangotri) needs a Gangotri National Park permit and is only open in the warmer months.

Before you go

Gangotri yatra: what the weather means for you

Clothing: a warm jacket, thermal layers, cap and gloves in every month; a rain jacket from late June to September.

Altitude: Gangotri is at about 3,100 m. Break the drive at Uttarkashi or Harsil, walk slowly, and drink plenty of water.

Buffer: keep a spare day in the monsoon and late in the season; landslides and snow can close the highway at short notice. Cab: Char Dham yatra taxi.
