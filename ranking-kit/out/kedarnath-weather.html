Kedarnath stands at about 3,583 m, at the head of the Mandakini valley, a trek of about 16 km from Gaurikund. It is cold in every month: in the yatra season (May–October) days are roughly 10–15 °C and nights near or below freezing early and late in the season. From about November to April it lies under deep snow. The temple doors close for winter on 11 November 2026 (Bhai Dooj), at 8:30 am, and Kedarnath's deity is worshipped at the Omkareshwar temple in Ukhimath until the doors reopen, usually in late April or May. May–June and September–October are the best months; July–August bring heavy monsoon rain and landslides.

Opening, closing and snow

When Kedarnath opens and closes

The doors of Kedarnath open each year in late April or May, on a date announced on Mahashivratri, and close on Bhai Dooj after Diwali. In 2026 they close on 11 November at 8:30 am. Through the winter the deity is carried down to the Omkareshwar temple at Ukhimath, where it is worshipped until the spring opening.

Snow can fall at Kedarnath in any month, and heavy falls are common from November. In May, walls of old snow still line parts of the trek; by October the sky is at its clearest and the nights are freezing. In July and August, rain, cloud and landslides on the road to Sonprayag and on the trek cause frequent delays.

Before you go

Kedarnath yatra: what the weather means for you

Clothing: a warm jacket, thermal layers, cap and gloves in every month; a rain jacket or poncho and waterproof shoes, especially from late June to September.

Altitude: Kedarnath is at about 3,583 m. Spend a night at Guptkashi or Sonprayag on the way, walk slowly, and start the trek early — afternoon cloud and rain are common.

Buffer: keep a spare day in the monsoon and late in the season; landslides and snow can close the road or the trek at short notice. Cab: Char Dham yatra taxi.
