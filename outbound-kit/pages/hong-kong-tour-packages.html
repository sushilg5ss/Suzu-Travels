Hong Kong & Macau

Why Hong Kong and Macau work so well for Indian travellers

Hong Kong packs a lot into a short holiday: a skyline harbour you cross by ferry, a tram up to Victoria Peak, beaches and hiking trails, the Big Buddha on Lantau, two theme parks and street markets that run late into the night. Macau, a short ferry or bridge-bus ride away, adds Portuguese-era squares, temples and the big resorts of Cotai. Entry for Indian passport holders is simple — a free online registration for Hong Kong and no visa for Macau.

Visa & entry

Hong Kong and Macau entry rules for Indian passport holders

What the official rules say

  • Hong Kong: Indian nationals must apply for and successfully complete Pre-arrival Registration (PAR) online before they can visit visa-free. It is free of charge, needs an Indian passport valid for at least six months, and covers a stay of up to 14 days per visit.
  • The result is shown instantly. You must print the notification slip on blank A4 white paper, sign it and carry it with the same passport — airlines check it before boarding and Hong Kong Immigration checks it on arrival.
  • A registration is valid for six months and can be used for multiple visits of up to 14 days each — useful for a Hong Kong → Macau → Hong Kong trip.
  • If the registration is unsuccessful, or you want to stay longer than 14 days, you need a visit visa from the Hong Kong Immigration Department (normally about four weeks).
  • Macau: the Macao SAR Government Portal lists India among the nationalities that do not need a visa, with a stay of up to 30 days for holders of a valid passport.

Checked 13 November 2026 · sources: Hong Kong Immigration Department — Pre-arrival Registration for Indian Nationals · Macao SAR Government Portal — immigration clearance of non-residents

Please note: admission at the border, and any visa, is always the decision of the immigration authorities of Hong Kong and of Macau. The registration slip is not a guarantee of entry. We help you prepare correctly, but we never promise an outcome we do not control.

FAQ

Common questions about a Hong Kong tour

Do Indians need a visa for Hong Kong?

Indian passport holders can visit Hong Kong visa-free for up to 14 days, but only after they successfully complete the free online Pre-arrival Registration (PAR) of the Hong Kong Immigration Department. Your passport must be valid for at least six months. The result is shown instantly; you print the notification slip on blank A4 white paper, sign it and carry it with the same passport, because airlines check it before boarding and immigration checks it on arrival. A registration is valid for six months and covers multiple visits of up to 14 days each. If the registration is unsuccessful, or you want to stay longer than 14 days, you need a visit visa from the Immigration Department, which normally takes about four weeks. The slip is not a guarantee of entry — admission is always the decision of Hong Kong Immigration.

Do Indians need a visa for Macau?

The Macao SAR Government Portal lists India among the nationalities that do not need a visa for Macau, with a stay of up to 30 days for holders of a valid passport. Admission is decided by the Macau immigration authorities at the border. If you go from Hong Kong to Macau and back to Hong Kong, your Hong Kong Pre-arrival Registration covers multiple visits within its six-month validity, so you re-enter Hong Kong on the same slip.

How many days are enough for Hong Kong and Macau?

Five nights is a comfortable first trip: three or four nights in Hong Kong for the harbour, Victoria Peak, Lantau and the Big Buddha and one theme-park day, then one night (or a long day trip) in Macau. With three or four nights, keep to Hong Kong and do Macau as a day trip.

Which is the best time to visit Hong Kong?

October to December is usually the most comfortable — cooler, drier days that suit walking and the Peak views. March and April are also pleasant. June to September is hot, humid and rainy, and it is the typhoon season, so we keep plans flexible in those months. Chinese New Year (January or February) is festive but busier and more expensive.

Is Hong Kong good for a family trip with children?

Yes. Hong Kong Disneyland and Ocean Park are the two big theme-park days, the Ngong Ping 360 cable car and the Star Ferry are fun for children too, and the city is compact with good public transport. We plan one park day (or both) around your children's ages and keep the rest of the days easy-paced.

Is vegetarian and Indian food available in Hong Kong?

Yes. Hong Kong has many Indian restaurants, especially around Tsim Sha Tsui in Kowloon, and vegetarian Chinese food is easy to find. Macau has fewer options, so we note vegetarian and Jain needs with hotels in advance and suggest where to eat.

Hong Kong costs depend on your dates, hotel area and category, theme-park tickets, the Macau add-on and the airfare from your city. Instead of a teaser price that changes when you ask, we send a costed, day-by-day quote for your dates — usually within 24 hours.
