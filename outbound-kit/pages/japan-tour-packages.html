Why Japan is worth the planning for Indian travellers

Japan puts a giant modern city, old temple towns and mountain scenery within a few hours of each other, joined by the Shinkansen bullet train. Tokyo has the Shibuya crossing, Asakusa's Senso-ji temple and Tokyo Tower; Mount Fuji rises over the lakes of Kawaguchiko and the hills of Hakone; Kyoto keeps the Golden Pavilion, Kiyomizu-dera, the bamboo grove of Arashiyama and the torii gates of Fushimi Inari; Nara's deer wander around Todai-ji; and Osaka is Japan's street-food city. It is clean, safe and punctual — the visa just needs to be started early.

Where to stay — it shapes your whole trip

Shinjuku or Ginza, Tokyo

Big stations, shopping and food on the doorstep. Shinjuku is the easiest base for day trips to Mount Fuji; Ginza and Tokyo Station suit the bullet train.

Lake Kawaguchi or Hakone

One night near Mount Fuji — lake-side hotels or a ryokan with hot-spring baths. Fuji is often hidden by cloud, so a night here gives you a second chance to see it.

Central Kyoto

Stay near Kyoto Station or Shijo and Gion for easy buses and trains to the temples. Kyoto fills up fast in cherry blossom and autumn weeks.

Namba, Osaka

Next to Dotonbori's food streets and well connected to Kansai airport, Universal Studios Japan and Nara.

Visa & entry

Visa and entry for Indian passport holders

What the official rules say

  • Indian passport holders need a visa for a holiday in Japan. Since 1 April 2024, Indian nationals and foreign nationals living in India can get a single-entry short-term visa for tourism as an eVISA.
  • Applications from India are made through the Japan Visa Application Centres (VFS Global), which are accredited by the Embassy and Consulates of Japan. The eVISA is issued electronically — there is no sticker in the passport.
  • Japan's eVISA FAQ says examination normally takes 5 working days, but it can take more than a month at busy times or if more documents are needed. The eVISA is valid for 3 months after issue and for one entry; the permitted stay is up to 90 days, as decided by the issuing office.
  • At the airport you show the visa issuance notice on your phone with internet access. PDFs, screenshots and printouts are not accepted.
  • Visa appointments drive the timeline, so we suggest starting at least four to six weeks before travel — earlier for cherry blossom and autumn dates.

Checked 22 October 2026 · sources: Ministry of Foreign Affairs of Japan — JAPAN eVISA · JAPAN eVISA — frequently asked questions · Consulate-General of Japan in Bengaluru — the JAPAN eVISA system in India

Please note: visa approval and entry at the border are always the decision of the Japanese authorities. We guide you through the documents and timelines honestly, but we never promise an outcome we do not control.

Apply only through the official channel — the Japan Visa Application Centre for your region — and keep your hotel bookings and return ticket handy for the immigration desk.

FAQ

Common questions about Japan

Do Indians need a visa for Japan?

Yes. Indian passport holders need a visa for a short holiday in Japan. Since 1 April 2024, Indian nationals and foreign nationals living in India can get a single-entry short-term tourist visa as an eVISA, applied for through the Japan Visa Application Centres (VFS Global). Japan's eVISA FAQ says examination normally takes 5 working days but can take more than a month in busy periods, and the eVISA must be used within 3 months of issue. At the airport you show the visa issuance notice on your phone with internet access — screenshots and printouts are not accepted. Approval is always the decision of the Japanese authorities.

How many days are enough for Japan?

Seven to eight nights covers the classic first trip: Tokyo, a night near Mount Fuji, Kyoto with a day in Nara, and Osaka. With ten to twelve nights you can add Hiroshima and Miyajima, the Japanese Alps, or more relaxed days in Kyoto. Hokkaido in winter needs its own four to five nights.

When is the best time to visit Japan from India?

Late March to early April for cherry blossom and October to November for autumn leaves are the favourite seasons; both are busy, so plan early. May and early October are pleasant and calmer. June to mid-July is the rainy season on the main island, July and August are hot and humid, and typhoons are most likely from August to September. December to February is cold, with snow in Hokkaido and the mountains.

When is cherry blossom season in Japan?

In Tokyo and Kyoto the cherry blossoms usually open in late March and early April, but the dates move every year with the weather and the trees stay at their best for only about a week. Japan publishes blossom forecasts from early in the year; we follow them and build your dates with a little flexibility.

Is vegetarian food easy to find in Japan?

It needs planning. Many Japanese dishes use dashi, a fish stock, even when they look vegetarian. Tokyo, Kyoto and Osaka have Indian and vegetarian restaurants, and Kyoto's Buddhist temple cuisine (shojin ryori) is fully vegetarian. We note vegetarian and Jain needs with hotels and suggest restaurants for every day of your trip.

Is Japan good for a family trip with children?

Very. Japan is clean, safe and runs on time. Tokyo Disneyland and DisneySea, Universal Studios Japan in Osaka, the deer park in Nara and a ride on the Shinkansen bullet train are favourites with children. We keep travel days short and book hotel rooms that fit your family, since many city rooms in Japan are small.

Japan costs depend heavily on your dates (cherry blossom and autumn weeks cost more), hotel category, rail passes and the airfare from India. Instead of a teaser price that changes when you ask, we send a costed, day-by-day quote for your dates — usually within 24 hours.
