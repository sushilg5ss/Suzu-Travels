Turkey (Türkiye)

Why Turkey works so well for Indian travellers

Few countries pack so much variety into one trip: Istanbul's mosques, palaces and bazaars on two continents, the fairy-chimney valleys and cave hotels of Cappadocia, the travertine pools of Pamukkale and the Roman city of Ephesus — with the Mediterranean and Aegean coast if you want beach days. Domestic flights keep the long distances manageable, and Turkish food has plenty of vegetarian choices.

Where to stay — it shapes your whole trip

Sultanahmet, Istanbul

Old Istanbul — walk to the Hagia Sophia, the Blue Mosque, Topkapı Palace and the Grand Bazaar. The easiest base for a first visit.

Taksim, Galata & Karaköy

The livelier, modern side: cafés, the Galata Tower, İstiklal Avenue and Bosphorus views. Good for couples and friends.

Göreme & Uçhisar, Cappadocia

Cave and stone hotels among the fairy chimneys, close to the valleys and the balloon launch areas. Book early for terrace rooms.

Coast — Antalya, Bodrum or Kuşadası

Beach and resort stays to end the trip slowly. Kuşadası is close to Ephesus; Antalya and Bodrum suit longer beach breaks.

Visa & entry

Visa and entry for Indian passport holders

What the official rules say

  • Indian ordinary passport holders need a visa to enter Turkey (Türkiye).
  • e-Visa — only with a supporting document. Indian citizens who hold a valid Schengen, USA, UK or Ireland visa or residence permit may get a single-entry e-Visa valid for one month, if they meet the conditions. The original supporting visa or permit must be with you when you enter. E-visas of other countries are not accepted as supporting documents.
  • The e-Visa is applied for only on the official website evisa.gov.tr; the Turkish Embassy says no company in India is authorised to issue or facilitate it. It is valid only for tourism or business travel.
  • Without such a visa or permit, you apply for a visa through the Turkish Embassy or Consulate process in India — we guide you through the documents and the timeline.
  • Your passport must be valid at least 60 days beyond your permitted stay; the Ministry advises at least 6 months from arrival.

Checked 19 October 2026 · sources: Republic of Türkiye Ministry of Foreign Affairs — Visa information for foreigners · Turkish Embassy in New Delhi — e-Visa announcement

Please note: visa approval is always the decision of the Turkish authorities. We guide you through the documentation and timelines honestly, but we never promise an outcome we do not control.

Apply with enough lead time — embassy appointments get busy before the summer and Diwali holidays.

FAQ

Common questions about Turkey

Do Indians need a visa for Turkey?

Yes. Indian ordinary passport holders need a visa for Turkey (Türkiye). If you hold a valid Schengen, USA, UK or Ireland visa or residence permit, you may be eligible for a single-entry e-Visa valid for one month, applied for only on the official site evisa.gov.tr — you must carry the original supporting visa or permit. Everyone else applies for a visa through the Turkish Embassy or Consulate process in India. Approval is always the decision of the Turkish authorities.

How many days are enough for Turkey?

Seven to eight nights covers the classic circuit — Istanbul, Cappadocia, Pamukkale and Ephesus — at a comfortable pace. With five or six nights we suggest Istanbul and Cappadocia only, so you are not spending the holiday in transfers.

When is the best time to visit Turkey from India?

April to June and September to November: mild weather for walking in Istanbul and the ruins, and good conditions in Cappadocia. July and August are hot and busy but fine for the coast. Winter is cold inland and Cappadocia can get snow.

Is the Cappadocia hot-air balloon ride guaranteed?

No. Balloon flights depend on the weather on the morning and are cancelled when winds or visibility are not safe. We plan two mornings in Cappadocia where we can, so there is a second chance if the first flight is called off.

Is vegetarian food easy to find in Turkey?

Yes, with a little planning. Turkish cuisine has many vegetarian dishes — mezes, lentil soup, vegetable dishes, pide and fresh bread — and Istanbul has Indian restaurants. Jain meals need advance notice, which we arrange with hotels where possible.
