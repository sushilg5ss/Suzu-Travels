Why the Philippines is worth it for Indian travellers

More than 7,000 islands, and the best of them are made for an easy island-hopping holiday: the limestone lagoons of El Nido and Coron in Palawan, the long White Beach of Boracay, and Bohol with its Chocolate Hills, tarsiers and river cruises. English is widely spoken, Manila adds Spanish-era history in Intramuros, and since June 2025 eligible Indian tourists can enter without a visa for up to 14 days.

Visa & entry

Philippines entry rules for Indian passport holders

What the official rules say

  • Visa-free for up to 14 days for tourism. Since 8 June 2025, Indian nationals may enter the Philippines without a visa for a non-extendible, non-convertible 14 days. You need a passport valid for at least six months beyond your stay, a confirmed hotel booking, proof of financial capacity and a return or onward ticket.
  • Up to 30 days with certain visas. If you hold a valid and current visa or residence permit of the United States, Japan, Australia, Canada, the Schengen area, Singapore or the United Kingdom, you may enter without a Philippine visa for up to 30 days for tourism (not extendable), with a passport valid six months beyond the stay and a return or onward ticket.
  • Any port of entry. Visa-free entry can be used at any Philippine port of entry. Travellers must not have any derogatory record with the Bureau of Immigration.
  • Longer stays, transit or other purposes need a Philippine visa. Eligible Indian nationals can apply on the official e-Visa site evisa.gov.ph.
  • eTravel declaration: register free on the official site etravel.gov.ph within 72 hours before arrival, and show the eTravel QR code when boarding.

Checked 17 November 2026 · sources: DFA Philippines — visa-free privileges for Indian nationals · Philippine Embassy, New Delhi · eTravel (official)

Please note: admission is always the decision of the Philippine immigration authorities at the port of entry, and any visa is the decision of the Philippine government. We help you carry the right documents, but we never promise an outcome we do not control.

We give you the hotel confirmations, return ticket and day-by-day itinerary in one set, keep your trip within the visa-free days, and remind you to complete eTravel in time. If you want to stay longer than your visa-free period, we guide you to the right visa before you book.

FAQ

Common questions about a Philippines tour

Do Indians need a visa for the Philippines?

Not for a short holiday, if you meet the conditions. When we checked the official advisory of the Philippine Department of Foreign Affairs on 17 November 2026, Indian nationals could enter the Philippines without a visa for up to 14 days for tourism, with a passport valid for at least six months beyond the stay, a confirmed hotel booking, proof of financial capacity and a return or onward ticket. The 14 days cannot be extended or converted. Whether you are admitted is always the decision of the Philippine immigration authorities at the port of entry.

Can Indians stay 30 days in the Philippines without a visa?

Yes, according to the same DFA advisory, if you hold a valid and current visa or residence permit of the United States, Japan, Australia, Canada, the Schengen area, Singapore or the United Kingdom. You can then enter without a Philippine visa for up to 30 days for tourism (not extendable), with a passport valid for at least six months beyond the stay and a return or onward ticket. For longer stays or other purposes you need a Philippine visa; eligible travellers can apply on the official e-Visa site, evisa.gov.ph.

What is eTravel and do I need it?

eTravel is the Philippine government's free online travel declaration. The official eTravel site says you can register only within 72 hours before your arrival, and you are asked to show your eTravel QR code when boarding your flight. Use only the official site etravel.gov.ph — it is free, so ignore websites that charge for it. We remind you when your 72-hour window opens.

How many days are enough for a Philippines trip?

Seven or eight days is a comfortable first trip: a day or two in Manila, two or three days in Bohol for the Chocolate Hills, the tarsiers and the Loboc River, and three days on a beach island such as Boracay. With 10 to 12 days you can add Palawan — El Nido or Coron. Remember that the visa-free stay for most Indian travellers is 14 days.

Which is better for a first trip — Boracay, Palawan or Bohol?

Boracay is the easiest beach holiday, with its long White Beach, sunsets and plenty of hotels and restaurants. Palawan (El Nido and Coron) is for island-hopping between limestone cliffs and lagoons, and suits travellers happy with boat days. Bohol mixes countryside sights — the Chocolate Hills, tarsiers and river cruise — with Panglao's beaches. Many couples and families combine two of them; we suggest the mix that fits your dates and flights.

Which is the best time to visit the Philippines?

The dry season, from about December to May, is the most popular time: December to February is the most pleasant, and March to May is hotter and good for beaches. From about June to November it is the rainy season, and typhoons can disrupt boats and domestic flights, especially in the second half of the year. Trips are still possible then — we build in buffer days and choose islands and dates carefully.

Is vegetarian and Indian food available in the Philippines?

Yes, in Manila and in tourist areas such as Boracay, Cebu and El Nido you will find Indian restaurants and vegetarian options; on remote islands and boat tours it takes more planning. We note vegetarian and Jain needs with hotels and island-hopping operators in advance.

A Philippines trip cost depends on your dates, the islands you choose, the domestic flights and boat transfers between them, hotel category, tours and the airfare from your city.
