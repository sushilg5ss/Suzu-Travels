Why Uzbekistan works so well for Indian travellers

Uzbekistan packs some of the finest Islamic architecture in the world into one compact trip: the madrasas of Registan and the tiled lanes of Samarkand, the Kalyan Minaret and old trading domes of Bukhara, and Khiva’s walled inner town, all linked by trains and short transfers. The e-visa is applied for online, the cities are walkable, and the old Silk Road history feels close to home for Indian travellers.

Visa & entry

Uzbekistan visa for Indian passport holders

What the official rules say

  • Indian passport holders need a visa for Uzbekistan. India is on the Ministry of Foreign Affairs’ list of countries whose citizens can obtain an electronic entry visa through e-visa.gov.uz.
  • The Ministry says the tourist visa is issued for up to one month. The Embassy of Uzbekistan in New Delhi describes the e-visa as valid for 90 days from issue, for a single stay of up to 30 days.
  • The Embassy advises applying at least 3 working days before your planned travel. You fill in the online form and upload a photo and your passport page; the e-visa is sent to you by email, and a personal interview may be requested.
  • Your passport should be valid for at least 3 months (90 days) beyond your visa and your stay, with a blank page.

Checked 29 October 2026 · sources: Ministry of Foreign Affairs of Uzbekistan — Visa to the Republic of Uzbekistan · Embassy of Uzbekistan in India — Consular section · Embassy notice on the e-visa system · e-visa.gov.uz

Please note: whether a visa is issued, and whether you are admitted at the border, is always the decision of the authorities of the Republic of Uzbekistan. We help you prepare a complete, honest application, but we never promise an outcome we do not control.

FAQ

Common questions about an Uzbekistan tour

Do Indians need a visa for Uzbekistan?

Yes, Indian passport holders need a visa, and India is on the official list of countries whose citizens can get an electronic entry visa through e-visa.gov.uz. The Embassy of Uzbekistan in New Delhi advises applying at least 3 working days before you travel; the e-visa is sent by email, and a personal interview may be asked for. Your passport should be valid for at least 3 months beyond your visa. Whether a visa is issued is always the decision of the Uzbek authorities. We checked these rules on 29 October 2026 and re-check them before every booking.

How long can we stay in Uzbekistan on an e-visa?

Uzbekistan's Ministry of Foreign Affairs says the tourist visa is issued for up to one month, and the Embassy in New Delhi describes the e-visa as valid for 90 days from issue for a single stay of up to 30 days. A classic Tashkent, Samarkand, Bukhara and Khiva trip takes 7 to 9 nights, well inside that.

How many days are enough for Uzbekistan?

Seven to nine nights covers the classic Silk Road circuit: Tashkent, Samarkand, Bukhara and Khiva. With five or six nights, keep to Tashkent, Samarkand and Bukhara. Add a night or two for a mountain day near Tashkent, or for a slower pace in the old towns.

Which is the best time to visit Uzbekistan from India?

April to early June and September to October are the most comfortable months, with pleasant days for walking around the old towns. July and August are very hot, especially in Bukhara and Khiva, so sightseeing is best in the early morning and evening. Winter is cold but quiet, with fewer visitors.

How do we travel between Tashkent, Samarkand, Bukhara and Khiva?

Fast trains link Tashkent, Samarkand and Bukhara, which is the easiest way to travel between them. For Khiva we use a train, a road transfer or a domestic flight via Urgench, depending on your dates and pace. We book seats in advance and arrange station and airport transfers.

Is vegetarian and Indian food available in Uzbekistan?

Uzbek food is meat-heavy — plov, kebabs and samsa — but fresh bread, salads, fruit, nuts and vegetable dishes are easy to find, and Tashkent has Indian restaurants. We note vegetarian and Jain needs with your hotels and guides in advance and suggest suitable restaurants on touring days.
