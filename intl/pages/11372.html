The most underrated destination in the region

Malaysia rarely tops anyone’s wishlist, and that is precisely why it delivers. You get skyscrapers and rainforest, colonial port towns and island beaches, all at prices well below Thailand and a fraction of Singapore. For Indian travellers there is a particular advantage: Malaysia has a substantial Indian-origin population, so Tamil is widely spoken in parts of Kuala Lumpur, Batu Caves is one of the most important Hindu sites outside India, and finding good vegetarian food is never a problem. It is comfortable in a way that surprises people.

Where to go

Kuala Lumpur

The Petronas Twin Towers, the KL Tower, Merdeka Square and the limestone caves and 272 coloured steps at Batu Caves. Excellent shopping at Bukit Bintang and Pavilion, and street food at Jalan Alor. Two to three nights covers it well. KL is easy to navigate and considerably cheaper than Singapore for the same kind of city holiday.

Genting Highlands

An hour from Kuala Lumpur and around 1,800 metres up, reached by one of the world’s longest cable cars. Cool air, a theme park, and a casino complex. Families like it for the temperature change and the rides; it is an easy overnight or even a day trip from KL.

Langkawi

An archipelago in the Andaman Sea with good beaches, the SkyBridge across a mountain gorge, island-hopping trips and duty-free shopping. This is where Malaysia competes directly with Thailand’s islands, usually at a lower price. Two to three nights.

Penang

George Town’s colonial shophouses and street art are UNESCO-listed, and the food is widely considered the best in Malaysia. A strong Indian quarter too. The most interesting stop for travellers who prefer culture and eating to beaches and malls.

Combining Malaysia with Singapore

These two pair naturally and travellers who combine them get noticeably better value than those who do either alone. Singapore is polished and expensive; Malaysia is relaxed and affordable. A week split four nights Kuala Lumpur and three nights Singapore — or the reverse — covers two countries, two very different atmospheres, and brings the average daily cost down. The two are connected by short flights and by road across the causeway. Note that Singapore requires a visa for Indians while Malaysia currently does not, so plan the Singapore paperwork early.

Common questions about Malaysia

Do Indians need a visa for Malaysia?

Malaysia currently allows visa-free entry for Indian passport holders on short tourist stays, under a scheme that has been extended in stages. A digital arrival card is normally needed before you travel. We confirm the current rule for your dates rather than relying on what was true last season.

Is Malaysia cheaper than Thailand?

Broadly yes, particularly on hotels and food, and dramatically cheaper than Singapore. Flights are comparable. For families watching the budget it is usually the best-value option in the region.

How many days do I need?

Three days covers Kuala Lumpur and Genting. Six to seven lets you add Langkawi or Penang properly. Ten days if you want to combine Malaysia with Singapore without rushing either.

Is Batu Caves worth visiting?

Yes, and it is free. It is one of the most significant Murugan temples outside India, with the enormous golden statue and the 272 steps. Go early before the heat and the crowds. Modest dress is expected.

Langkawi or Penang?

Langkawi for beaches, island hopping and duty-free. Penang for heritage streets, street art and the best food in the country. Beach holiday, Langkawi. Interesting holiday, Penang. With seven days you can fit both, though it makes for a busy week.

Is vegetarian food easy to find?

Very. Malaysia’s Indian community means pure vegetarian restaurants are common in Kuala Lumpur and Penang, and banana-leaf meals are a local institution. Jain requirements are manageable with advance notice.
